[[ACE-priv]]
== Book 3. Privileged Architecture

The ACE Privileged Architecture consists of CSRs and exception causes.

We refer to the ACE Unprivileged Architecture for the definitions of ACE instructions and the categories of _configuration_ and _usage_ instructions.

The ACE Privileged Architecture is _implemented_ on a hart when the hart implements the `ACES` field and those exception causes of <<ACE-exceptions>> that apply to its configuration. It is mandatory on every hart that is not M-mode-only. On an M-mode-only hart it is optional; where it is not implemented: `ACES` is not present and is treated as Initial; no ACE exception cause exists and the behaviors of <<ACE-exception-error-correspondence>> apply; `Smacecrstatus` and `Ssacevbootscrt` are not implemented; and the `macecsk`, `macephysbootscrt` and `macelocality` groups may exist, each where its extension is implemented, with the accessibility that <<ACE-CSR-macecsk>> and <<ACE-CSR-locality-groups>> prescribe.

On an M-mode-only hart none of the extensions in the above table is mandatory.
`Smacecsk` is allowed in every configuration.
If `Smacepbootscrt` is implemented, in any configuration, the `macephysbootscrt`
CSR group is implemented to set the Physical Boot Secret Locality.
The rest are:

The ACE Privileged Architecture depends on the Indirect CSR Access mechanism of `Smcsrind`/`Sscsrind` for the CSR groups marked (Indirect) in <<ACE-CSRs-priv>>, unless the ARC prefers direct access for these CSRs; the `*iselect` values are to be assigned by the ARC.

[[ACE-exceptions]]
=== Exceptions Raised by ACE

With reference to <<ACE-State-field>>, we discuss here error conditions that raise exceptions.

When an exception is raised, the CSR `*cause` will reflect the error condition, as described in <<ACE-exception-codes>>.
The error codes are listed in decreasing priority order.
The actual numbers are TBD.
`ace_exc_fatal` precedes every exception that depends on the state of the ACE unit, including the address-misaligned, access-fault and page-fault exceptions of ACE memory instructions, whose transfer bounds are computed from that state (<<ACE-instruction-load>>); among the ACE-specific conditions it is preceded only by the illegal-instruction grounds decided without accessing the ACE unit.

.Exception causes in decreasing priority order

|===
|Value | Mnemonic/RISC-V type           | Examples in ACE (((ACE, error codes)))
|      | Instruction address breakpoint | {empty}
|      | Instruction page fault, instruction access fault | As ordered by the Privileged Specification: first during instruction address translation, then on the physical address.
|      | Illegal instruction, or virtual instruction | The ACE grounds decided without accessing the state of the ACE unit, listed first in <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>. They are defects in the encoding or the operands of the instruction. A virtual-instruction exception is raised in place of the illegal-instruction exception where `Smstateen` or the CSR access rules of the hypervisor extension so prescribe (<<ACE-CSRs-priv>>); the `Smstateen` check precedes the `ACES`-Off check, which precedes the CSR privilege check.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_fatal`                | Fatal system failure in the ACE unit. Not delegable below M-mode.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_no_csk`               | Use of the ACE unit while no CSK is active: either none has been established, or the `macecsk` group is only partially written (see <<ACE-CSK-requirements>>, <<ACE-CSR-macecsk>>). Not delegable below M-mode: only M-mode can establish a CSK.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_CR_off`               | Any access, as defined in <<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>>, to a CR whose `*lcrstatus` field is _Off_. Not delegable below M-mode, whichever field is _Off_: the hardware does not know which mode holds the authoritative copy of the CR, so M-mode receives the exception and forwards it to the mode that owns the _Off_ field (see <<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>>). Enables on-demand (lazy) CR loading and trap-and-emulate. `ace_exc_unsupported` arises only from an opening `ace.mgmt`, which <<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>> exempts from this gate: an unsupported Algorithm provisioned or imported into an _Off_ CR is reported to the emulator by `ace_exc_unsupported`, and every later access to the CR that is not exempted by `ace_exc_CR_off` (<<ACE-error-architecture>>).
|      | Illegal instruction            | The ACE grounds that require the state of the ACE unit, listed second in <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>. They are raised only once no fatal condition is pending, and after `ace_exc_no_csk` and `ace_exc_CR_off`.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_unsupported`          | Unsupported Metadata (<<ACE-Metadata-validity>>) when opening a provisioning or import.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_privilege_violation`  | Attempt to execute any _usage_ instruction that would violate the Usage Control policies as per <<ACE-Metadata>>. | TBD  | `ace_exc_out_of_memory`        | Insufficient CRF capacity when opening a provisioning or import, cloning, or raising _SCProtection_ with `ace.restrict*`.
| TBD  | `ace_exc_unconfigured_buffer`  | Use of ACEIOBUF as an input or an output while unconfigured.
|      | Load/store address breakpoint  | On the address of a component access of `ace.load`, `ace.store`, `ace.input` or `ace.output`, when that access is about to be performed; for the first component access, before the address-misaligned check.
|      | Load/store address misaligned  | On the effective address of `ace.load`, resp. `ace.store`, before any component access (<<ACE-Memory-Alignment>>); for `ace.input` and `ace.output`, as for an ordinary access on the implementation.
|      | Load/store page fault, load/store access fault | Per component access, in offset order, as ordered by the Privileged Specification.
|===

The relative order of the standard exceptions is that of the Privileged Specification and is reproduced for reference only; the load/store address breakpoint and the load/store faults are evaluated per component access because the component addresses of an ACE memory instruction are determined from the state of the ACE unit.

The `medeleg` and `hedeleg` bits corresponding to `ace_exc_fatal`, `ace_exc_no_csk` and `ace_exc_CR_off` are read-only zero: these three causes are never delegated below M-mode. Those corresponding to `ace_exc_unsupported`, `ace_exc_out_of_memory`, `ace_exc_privilege_violation` and `ace_exc_unconfigured_buffer` are writable wherever `medeleg`, resp. `hedeleg`, is implemented, so that these causes can be delegated to S-mode and, with the H extension, to VS-mode.

[NOTE]
====
ACE memory instructions are allowed to trap so that implementations have more
flexibility when loading/storing large ACE contexts. Simpler implementations
may defer the load/store process to software using scalar or vector
loads/stores followed/preceded by an `ace.mv` instruction, whereas more performant
implementations may have dedicated hardware to speed up the instructions.
The hardware then raises an illegal-instruction exception for the emulated instruction, before any condition of this table is evaluated (<<ACE-extensions-overview-unpriv>>). An M-mode emulator of an ACE instruction reproduces this table in full: it evaluates the `Smstateen` bits, the `ACES` fields, the CSK state and the `*lcrstatus` gates, then the second-group grounds and the remaining conditions, in this order, sets the `*lcrstatus` and `ACES` fields the instruction would set, and raises or forwards the resulting exception as the hardware and the M-mode handler would.
====

=== CSRs

ACE extends `*status` and `misa`, and provides three direct CSRs — six on RV32, counting the
high shadows — and up to 32 indirect CSRs on RV32, 16 on RV64.

| 0xXXX       | MRW        | `mlcrstatush`             | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `mlcrstatus`

| (Indirect)  | SRW        | `sacelocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate the OS's processes; `vsacelocality` substitutes for it when V=1. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | HRW        | `vsacelocality{0..3}`     | Locality: the guest OS's `sacelocality`. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.

Similarly to <<ACE-CSR-unpriv>>,
on RV32 each of the 64-bit CSRs `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus` and `vslcrstatus`, whenever implemented,
is accompanied by a 32-bit CSR named `mlcrstatush`, resp. `slcrstatush`, `vslcrstatush`,
giving access to bits [63:32]; a read or write of the CSR itself then accesses bits [31:0].

These CSRs, including the `macecsk` group (if present),
are not available if the ACE extension is not present or if an ACES field is Off
(see <<ACE-ACE-field-in-misa>>, <<ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus>>, and <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>>).
An access to one of these CSRs while an `ACES` field in effect is Off raises an illegal-instruction exception; this condition is evaluated before the privilege check and after the `Smstateen` check below, where implemented. Otherwise an access from a privilege mode below the CSR's privilege raises an illegal-instruction exception, except that, as the hypervisor extension prescribes, an access from VS-mode or VU-mode to a CSR that would be accessible in HS-mode, or from VU-mode to a CSR that would be accessible in VS-mode, raises a virtual-instruction exception.
Where `Smstateen` is implemented, a bit of `mstateen0`, `hstateen0` and `sstateen0` (to be allocated) gates every ACE instruction and every ACE CSR, including the read-only identification CSRs, in the modes below the register's own: a clear bit makes the instruction or access raise an illegal-instruction exception (`mstateen0`, `sstateen0`) or a virtual-instruction exception (`hstateen0`), as the `Smstateen` specification prescribes. These exceptions are decided without accessing the ACE unit, belong to the first group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>, and are evaluated before every other ACE condition.

[[ACE-ACE-field-in-misa]]
==== ACE field in `misa`

{empty}
(((CSR, ACE field in `misa`)))
Bit `L` (11) of `misa` indicates that the ACE extension is present.
If ACE is implemented, this bit must not be read-only zero.

If an emulated ACE unit is provided by M-mode firmware, this bit can be set to 1 by the latter.
M-mode firmware must not clear `misa.L` without first zeroizing all ACE state, emulated or in hardware (`ace.clear.all` and the CSRs of <<ACE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>); while `misa.L` is clear the ACE state is inaccessible and is not required to be retained.

[WARNING]
====
This allocation is provisional and has not been agreed with the RISC-V Architecture
Review Committee. Presence of ACE may instead be discovered by other mechanisms, in
particular the unified discovery mechanism, which is the direction RISC-V has taken for
recent extensions and which does not consume a `misa` bit; bit `L` (11) may also be
allocated to another extension. Implementers should expect this subsection to change, and
should not treat `misa.L` as a stable interface until the question is settled.
====

[[ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus]]
==== ACE Context Status in `mstatus` and `sstatus`

{empty}
(((CSR,ACES field in mstatus)))
(((CSR,ACES field in sstatus)))
An ACE context status field, `ACES`, is added to `mstatus`[26:25] and shadowed in `sstatus`[26:25].
It is defined analogously to the floating-point context status field, `FS`, and the vector context status field, `VS`,
and tracks the dirtiness of the CRs, of the ACEIOBUF and of every writable ACE-specific CSR other than the `macecsk` group.
`mstatus.ACES` is always in effect; when V=1 the `vsstatus.ACES` field of <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>> is
in effect as well.

[WARNING]
====
The choice of `mstatus`[26:25], and of the corresponding bits of `sstatus` and
`vsstatus`, is provisional and has not been agreed with the RISC-V Architecture Review
Committee. Those bit positions are contested by other extensions, and ACES may end up at a
different offset, in a different register, or be carried by a mechanism other than a
`*status` context-status field. Nothing in this specification depends on the particular
offset: only on there being a two-bit context-status field with the semantics of
<<ACE-aces-encoding>>, defined analogously to `FS` and `VS`.
====

[[ACE-aces-encoding]]
.Encoding of the ACES field
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<8%,<84%",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Name |Meaning
|   0   | Off     | ACE disabled. The ACE state is inaccessible. Executing any ACE instruction, or accessing any ACE-specific CSR other than the read-only identification CSRs of <<ACE-CSRs-unpriv>>, raises an illegal-instruction exception.
|   1   | Initial | ACE enabled. No CR or the ACEIOBUF is configured. The hart may issue any ACE instruction.
|   2   | Clean   | ACE enabled. At least one CR or ACEIOBUF is configured; state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty   | ACE enabled. At least one CR, ACEIOBUF, or ACE-specific CSR may have changed since the last context swap.
Unconfigured CRs that were previously configured also cause ACES to become Dirty, since context-switching code must erase any stale saved state.
|===


The following holds for every `ACES` field, at the privilege modes at which it is in effect.
Any ACE instruction, including an access to an ACE CSR other than the read-only identification CSRs (<<ACE-CSRs-unpriv>>), raises an illegal-instruction exception if a
field in effect is Off. Where none is Off, an instruction that modifies ACE state sets every field
in effect to Dirty, and an implementation may set a field to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any
time, even in the absence of a state change. While a field is Dirty, the `SD` bit of the register
holding it is 1; otherwise that `SD` is set in accordance with the existing specifications. A hart
whose `misa.L` is writable implements the `ACES` field in every `*status` register it implements, and
the field is present and writable while `misa.L` is clear, analogously to `FS` and `VS`; an emulated
ACE unit relies on it. The `*lcrstatus` fields and `ACES` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are set before any trap is taken on or after that instruction and before any later instruction executes, including when the instruction continues in the background (Rule <<ACE-IRR-long-running-no-data>>) or is discarded and restarted (Rule <<ACE-IRR-uninterruptible-instructions>>). An implementation may set a `*lcrstatus` field to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, as it may an `ACES` field; setting a field to Dirty is not a change of ACE state for the purposes of the state change rule for exceptions of <<ACE-error-architecture>>. A software write to a `*lcrstatus` CSR or to a Locality CSR sets the `ACES` fields in effect to Dirty; context-restoring software therefore writes `ACES` after those CSRs.

NOTE: Precise management of `mstatus.ACES` is an optimization that software may use to reduce context-switch overhead.

NOTE: Writes to an `ACES` field do not modify any ACE state.
Setting a field to Off, Initial, or Clean does not clear CRs, ACEIOBUF, or ACE CSRs;
any state changes must be explicit and must precede such a write.

[[ACE-CSR-lcrstatus]]
==== `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` for Dirtiness Tracking

{empty}
(((CSR, `mlcrstatus`)))
(((CSR, `slcrstatus`)))
(((CSR, `vslcrstatus`)))
The 64-bit registers `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` track per-CR status, including dirtiness, at all levels below M-mode, S-mode, and VS-mode, respectively.

For each of these 3 CSRs:::
`*lcrstatus`(__i__) = `*lcrstatus`[2{interpunct}__i__+1:2{interpunct}__i__] is a two-bit field that encodes four possible status values for ``K(``__i__``)``, as described in the following table.
+
--
[[ACE-lcrstatus-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `*lcrstatus` bits
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<12%,<80%",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Name           | Behavior
|   0   | Off            | The CR is inaccessible.
|   1   | Initial        | The CR is accessible and in an initial state as defined by software.
|   2   | Clean          | The CR is accessible and its state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty          | The CR is accessible and its state may not match the last context swap.
|===
--
+
`*lcrstatus` is evaluated after the illegal-instruction grounds that do not require ACE unit state (the first group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>), `ace_exc_fatal`, and `ace_exc_no_csk`, but before all other conditions, including the second group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>> and Rule <<ACE-SGR-gate-order>>. The hardware state of a CR with an _Off_ field in effect is never consulted: conditions that depend on its contents are evaluated only after lazy loading or by the M-mode code that emulates the CR.
+
An ACE instruction _accesses_ ``K(``__i__``)`` if it names the CR as an operand, directly or through a GPR, or if the instruction is `ace.clear.all`. An access to a CR with an _Off_ field in effect raises `ace_exc_CR_off`, with the following exceptions: an `ace.mgmt` opening a provisioning or import, `ace.clear`, `ace.clear.all`, and `ace.clone` as a destination access. These operations do not depend on the CR's contents and set every field in effect for that CR to _Dirty_, even if the CR was already _Unconfigured_. However, an `ace.clone` with identical source and destination executes without setting any field to _Dirty_ (<<ACE-instruction-clone>>). An exempted instruction that raises an exception before its zeroizing step likewise sets no field: an instruction replaces _Off_ with _Dirty_ only if it has zeroized the CR.
+
A mode whose _Off_ mark has been replaced by _Dirty_ treats the hardware CR as authoritative: a mode that held the authoritative image of the CR for lazy loading discards it, and M-mode, which alone emulates a CR, discards the emulated copy it held for it (<<ACE-error-architecture>>).
+
`ace.getmd*` on a CR with an _Off_ field in effect traps like any other access. An implementation may return the MDH without trapping only if the result matches what the trap and subsequent restore would have produced.
+
A mode marks a CR _Off_ only while it holds the authoritative image or emulates the CR; the hardware contents of an _Off_ CR are never the sole copy of a CC. A handler that has not yet saved a CR leaves its field _Clean_ or _Dirty_.

For `mlcrstatus`:::
+
--
When the privilege mode is (H)S, VS or U/VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when `mlcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `mlcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `slcrstatus`(__i__) or `vslcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`mstatus.ACES` is also in effect.
--


For `slcrstatus`:::
+
--
When the privilege mode is VS, U, or VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `slcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `slcrstatus`(__i__) and `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `slcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `vslcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`mstatus.ACES`, `vsstatus.ACES` when V=1, and `mlcrstatus` are also in effect.
--


For `vslcrstatus`:::
+
--
`vslcrstatus` exists only when the H extension is implemented.

When V=1, `vslcrstatus` substitutes for `slcrstatus`: CSR accesses that would target `slcrstatus` instead access `vslcrstatus`, analogously to `vsstatus` and `sstatus`.

When the privilege mode is VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when any one of `mlcrstatus`(__i__), `vslcrstatus`(__i__), or `slcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `vslcrstatus`(__i__), `slcrstatus`(__i__) and `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `vslcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `slcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`mstatus.ACES`, `vsstatus.ACES`, `slcrstatus`, and `mlcrstatus` are also in effect.
--

The `*lcrstatus` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are those in effect, that is those owned by the privilege modes above the one in which it executes; an instruction never writes the field owned by its own mode.

`ace_exc_CR_off` is a single exception cause and is not delegable below M-mode, whichever `*lcrstatus` field is _Off_: raising it, the hardware does not know which mode holds the authoritative copy of the CR. The M-mode handler examines the fields in effect in the order `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, `vslcrstatus`, resolves the condition itself if `mlcrstatus`(__i__) is _Off_, and otherwise forwards the exception to the most privileged mode whose field is _Off_, which owns that field and can write it.
A handler therefore never services `ace_exc_CR_off` for a field it cannot write, and an instruction it issues to resolve the condition is not gated by a higher level's field.

[NOTE]
====
The _Off_ value and the `ace_exc_CR_off` exception provide an architectural hook for on-demand (lazy) CR loading. At context restore, a handler may leave a CR unrestored, set its `*lcrstatus` field to _Off_, and retain the authoritative SCC in memory. A subsequent access from a lower-privileged mode traps with `ace_exc_CR_off` to M-mode, which forwards it to the mode that owns the _Off_ field; that handler then imports the SCC before resuming execution. The architecture defines no separate "lazy" state: Software that marks a CR _Off_ is responsible for ensuring that the authoritative SCC is restored.
====

[[ACE-CSR-macecsk]]
==== `macecsk` Group

{empty}
(((CSR, `macecsk`)))
The optional `macecsk` group consists of XLEN-bit M-mode RW CSRs: eight in RV32, four in RV64.

A write to the __i__-th CSR supplies bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of the value that becomes the CSK once the group has been written in full.

This CSR group is accessible in M-mode whenever `ACES` is not Off and `misa.L` is set, and not in Debug mode (<<ACE-interaction-with-debug>>); any other access raises an illegal-instruction exception.
Out of reset `ACES` is Off (<<ACE-out-of-reset-priv>>), so M-mode firmware enables `ACES` before it establishes the CSK, and until then the ACE unit is not usable.

A CSK can only become active if all eight (respectively, four) CSRs have been written to.
A new CSK becomes active only once every CSR of the group has been written since the previous activation; from the first such write until then the CSK is _partially written_ and the ACE unit is unavailable (`ace_exc_no_csk`, <<ACE-CSK-requirements>>).
Entering M-mode discards a partial write, so that a CSK is never assembled from the writes of two sequences.
One mechanism that ensures this is the following (informative):

* The implementation maintains a per-hart set of flags indicating which CSRs in the group have been written to.
* These flags are initially unset.
* When M-Mode firmware writes to a CSR in the `macecsk` group, the implementation sets the corresponding flag, and keeps a copy of the value written so far.
* Once all flags have been set, the values written by M-mode firmware are activated and replace the old values atomically, and the flags are cleared. The new CSK can now be used for cryptographic operations.
* Any mode change to M-mode resets the flags and discards the partially written value, so that M-mode always begins a configuration attempt from a clean state and a partial key is never activated.
* A transition of the hart out of M-mode does *not* clear the flags: a partially written CSK persists, and the ACE unit stays unavailable, until M-mode is next entered.
* Any ACE unit reset clears the write-tracking flags and discards the partially written value.
* While any of the flags is set, that is while a CSK is partially written, the ACE unit is not available: any attempt to execute an ACE instruction, or to access an ACE CSR other than those named in <<ACE-CSK-requirements>>, raises ACE exception `ace_exc_no_csk`. Because activation clears the flags, the unit becomes available again as soon as the group has been written in full.

Reading a register of the group returns either the corresponding part of the active CSK, or zero if none is established, or always zero, at the implementation's choice; the choice is fixed per implementation and does not depend on whether a write sequence is in progress.

A CSK whose value is `zeros(256)` is unconfigured under every model of <<ACE-CSK-requirements>>: writing that value in full through the `macecsk` group discards any established CSK and leaves the unit without a CSK; an established CSK that is zeroized (<<ACE-interaction-with-debug>>) becomes unconfigured; under the fourth model an all-zero output of the RBG is discarded and regenerated.

[NOTE]
====
The flags survive a transition out of M-mode, so an incomplete CSK write sequence is not
silently lost. The next ACE access from any mode traps with `ace_exc_no_csk`, notifying M-mode.
Entering M-mode resets the flags, allowing the handler to restart CSK provisioning.
====

The ACE unit is unavailable until a CSK is established as described in <<ACE-CSK-requirements>> (immediately in the case of a hardwired CSK; as soon as it has been generated in the case of a boot-generated one).

[[ACE-CSR-locality-groups]]
==== Locality Secret CSR Groups

{empty}
(((CSR, `macephysbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `hacevirtbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `macelocality`)))
(((CSR, `hacelocality`)))(((CSR, `sacelocality`)))
(((CSR, `vsacelocality`)))
Each group listed in <<ACE-locality-csr-groups>> holds one 128-bit Locality Secret
(<<ACE-locality-indexes>>) in XLEN-bit RW CSRs accessed via Indirect CSR Access: four in RV32 and
two in RV64, the CSRs numbered "2" and "3" existing only on RV32.
A write to the __i__-th CSR of a group sets bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of its secret.
Access below the group's privilege, or in non-authenticated Debug mode, raises an
illegal-instruction or, as <<ACE-CSRs-priv>> prescribes, a virtual-instruction exception. The groups are accessible whether or not a CSK has been established (<<ACE-CSK-requirements>>): no ordering between CSK and Locality configuration is enforced.

[[ACE-locality-csr-groups]]
.Locality Secret CSR groups
[width="100%",cols="<25%,<18%,^11%,<46%",options="header"]
|===
| Group | Locality Secret | Privilege | Present when

| [[ACE-CSR-macephysbootscrt,macephysbootscrt]]`macephysbootscrt{0..3}` | _PhysBootScrt_ |  M   | `Smacepbootscrt` is implemented.
| [[ACE-CSR-hacevirtbootscrt,hacevirtbootscrt]]`hacevirtbootscrt{0..3}` | _VirtBootScrt_ |  HS  | `Ssacevbootscrt` and the H extension are both implemented.
| [[ACE-CSR-macelocality,macelocality]]`macelocality{0..3}`             | _MLocality_    |  M   | `Smacelclt` is implemented.
| [[ACE-CSR-hacelocality,hacelocality]]`hacelocality{0..3}`             | _HLocality_    |  HS  | `Ssacelclt` and the H extension are both implemented.
| [[ACE-CSR-sacelocality,sacelocality]]`sacelocality{0..3}`             | _SLocality_    |  S   | `Ssacelclt` and S-mode are both implemented.
| [[ACE-CSR-vsacelocality,vsacelocality]]`vsacelocality{0..3}`          | _SLocality_ (guest) |  HS, and VS through `sacelocality`  | `Ssacelclt` and the H extension are both implemented.
|===

When the H extension is implemented, `vsacelocality` substitutes for `sacelocality` when V=1:
CSR accesses that would target `sacelocality` instead access `vsacelocality`, analogously to
`vsstatus` and `sstatus` and to `vslcrstatus` (<<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>>), and _SLocality_ then
denotes its value (<<ACE-Localities>>). Like the other VS CSRs, `vsacelocality` is accessible
from HS-mode and M-mode, and a hypervisor saves and restores it with them. Software that exports CRs on behalf of a lower privilege level configures every SW Filter Group register it owns before doing so: an export whose CC names an unconfigured entry yields an SCC that no import accepts (<<ACE-Localities>>).

The Locality CSR groups are readable at their respective privilege levels.
Context-switching software saves and restores them like any other CSR.

[NOTE]
====
`sacelocality` is the OS's Locality, separating its processes whether the OS runs at S-mode or, through `vsacelocality`, at VS-mode; a hypervisor uses its own `sacelocality` for its host processes and configures it before it saves guest ACE state (<<ACE-Localities>>).
`hacelocality` is the hypervisor's tag on its virtual machines: it enters the bindings of every export and import a guest performs, and the hypervisor carries it with the virtual machine.
====

[[ACE-out-of-reset-priv]]
=== ACE Privileged State out of Reset

This section and <<ACE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> are the authoritative statement of ACE state out of
reset.

Out of ACE unit reset:

. The value of the bit `L` of `misa` is 1 if the ACE unit and the ACE ISA are at least partially implemented in hardware.
   In the case of an emulated ACE unit, `L` is 0.
. The `ACES` field of `mstatus` (visible in `sstatus`) and, where implemented, of `vsstatus` is Off.
. The CSK is unconfigured, unless it is hardwired or randomly regenerated.
. `macephysbootscrt` and `hacevirtbootscrt` are zero.
. `macelocality`, `hacelocality`, `sacelocality`, and `vsacelocality` are zero.
. All fields of `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` are set to _Initial_.

An ACE unit reset caused by a fatal condition (<<ACE-error-architecture>>) differs in items 1, 2 and 6: `misa.L` is unchanged, an `ACES` field that is Off stays Off and every other `ACES` field becomes Dirty, and every `*lcrstatus` field is set to _Dirty_.
