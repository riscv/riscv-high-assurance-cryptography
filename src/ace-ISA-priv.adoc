[[ACE-priv]]
== Book 3. Privileged Architecture

The ACE Privileged Architecture consists of CSRs and optional custom instructions to implement parts of the unprivileged ISA.

We refer to the ACE Unprivileged Architecture for the definitions of ACE instructions and the categories of _configuration_ and _usage_ instructions.

The ACE Privileged Architecture is _implemented_ on a hart when the hart implements the `ACES` field and those exception causes of <<ACE-exceptions>> that apply to its configuration. It is mandatory on every hart that is not M-mode-only. On an M-mode-only hart it is optional; where it is not implemented: `ACES` is not present and is treated as Initial; no ACE exception cause exists and the behaviors of <<ACE-exception-error-correspondence>> apply; `Smacecrstatus` and `Smacevbootscrt` are not implemented; and the `macecsk`, `macephysbootscrt` and `macelocality` groups may exist, each where its extension is implemented, with the accessibility that <<ACE-CSR-macecsk>> and <<ACE-CSR-locality-groups>> prescribe.

On an M-mode-only hart none of the extensions in the above table is mandatory.
`Smacecsk` is allowed in every configuration.
If `Smacepbootscrt` is implemented, in any configuration, the `macephysbootscrt`
CSR group is implemented to set the Physical Boot Secret Locality.
The rest are:

.Privileged requirements, per system configuration

|===
| Extension {rightarrow} +

| M-mode only
| Optional: If implemented, `mlcrstatus` is implemented and shadowed in `slcrstatus`.
| Optional: If implemented, `macelocality` configures MLocality
| {empty}

| M+U
| Mandatory: `mlcrstatus` is implemented and shadowed in `slcrstatus`.

and the Localities they set (<<ACE-Localities>>); an empty cell means the extension has no effect in
that configuration.

[[ACE-exceptions]]
=== Exceptions Raised by ACE

With reference to <<ACE-State-field>>, we discuss here error conditions that raise exceptions.

When an exception is raised, the CSR `*cause` will reflect the error condition, as described in <<ACE-exception-codes>>.
The error codes are listed in decreasing priority order.
The actual numbers are TBD.
`ace_exc_fatal` precedes every exception that depends on the state of the ACE unit, including the address-misaligned, access-fault and page-fault exceptions of ACE memory instructions, whose transfer bounds are computed from that state (<<ACE-instruction-load>>); among the ACE-specific conditions it is preceded only by the illegal-instruction grounds decided without accessing the ACE unit.

=== CSRs

ACE extends `*status` and `misa`, and provides three direct CSRs — six on RV32, counting the
high shadows — and up to 28 indirect CSRs on RV32, 14 on RV64.

[[ACE-CSRs-priv]]
.ACE Privileged CSRs.
[cols="^12%,^12%,<26%,<50%"]
[float="center",align="center",options="header"]
|===
| Address     | Privilege  | Field                     | Description
| 0xXXX       | MRW        | `mlcrstatus`              | Per-CR dirtiness and accessibility tracking below M-mode; supports on-demand CR loading
| 0xXXX       | MRW        | `mlcrstatush`             | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `mlcrstatus`
| 0xXXX       | SRW        | `slcrstatus`              | Per-CR dirtiness and accessibility tracking below S-mode; supports on-demand CR loading
| 0xXXX       | SRW        | `slcrstatush`             | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `slcrstatus`
| 0xXXX       | HRW        | `vslcrstatus`             | Per-CR dirtiness and accessibility tracking below VS-mode; supports on-demand CR loading
| 0xXXX       | HRW        | `vslcrstatush`            | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `vslcrstatus`
| (Indirect)  | MRW        | `macecsk{0..7}`           | ACE Context Sealing Key.  CSRs "4" to "7" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | MRW        | `macephysbootscrt{0..3}`  | Locality: Physical (host) Boot Session Secret. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | HRW        | `hacevirtbootscrt{0..3}`  | Locality: Virtual (guest) Boot Session Secret. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | MRW        | `macelocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate processes below M-mode. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | HRW        | `hacelocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate processes below HS-mode. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
| (Indirect)  | SRW        | `sacelocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate U-mode processes. CSRs "2" and "3" are RV32 only.
|===

Similarly to <<ACE-CSR-unpriv>>,
on RV32 the 64-bit ACE CSRs `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus` and `vslcrstatus`, whenever implemented,
are accompanied by a 32-bit CSR with `h` appended to the same name
giving access to bits [63:32] of the original CSR which, when accessed, gives access only to bits [31:0].

These CSRs, including the `macecsk` group (if present),
are not available if the ACE extension is not present or if an ACES field is Off
(see <<ACE-ACE-field-in-misa>>, <<ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus>>, and <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>>).
An access to one of these CSRs while an `ACES` field in effect is Off raises an illegal-instruction exception; this condition is evaluated before the privilege check and after the `Smstateen` check below, where implemented. Otherwise an access from a privilege mode below the CSR's privilege raises an illegal-instruction exception, except that, as the hypervisor extension prescribes, an access from VS-mode or VU-mode to a CSR that would be accessible in HS-mode, or from VU-mode to a CSR that would be accessible in VS-mode, raises a virtual-instruction exception.
Where `Smstateen` is implemented, a bit of `mstateen0`, `hstateen0` and `sstateen0` (to be allocated) gates every ACE instruction and every ACE CSR, including the read-only identification CSRs, in the modes below the register's own: a clear bit makes the instruction or access raise an illegal-instruction exception (`mstateen0`, `sstateen0`) or a virtual-instruction exception (`hstateen0`), as the `Smstateen` specification prescribes. These exceptions are decided without accessing the ACE unit, belong to the first group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>, and are evaluated before every other ACE condition.

[[ACE-ACE-field-in-misa]]
==== ACE field in `misa`

{empty}
(((CSR, ACE field in `misa`)))
Bit `L` (11) of `misa` indicates that the ACE extension is present.
If ACE is implemented, this bit must not be read-only zero.

If an emulated ACE unit is provided by M-mode firmware, this bit can be set to 1 by the latter.
`misa.L` cannot be cleared by M-mode firmware without first zeroizing all (emulated) ACE state.

[WARNING]
====
This allocation is provisional and has not been agreed with the RISC-V Architecture
Review Committee. Presence of ACE may instead be discovered by other mechanisms, in
particular the unified discovery mechanism, which is the direction RISC-V has taken for
recent extensions and which does not consume a `misa` bit; bit `L` (11) may also be
allocated to another extension. Implementers should expect this subsection to change, and
should not treat `misa.L` as a stable interface until the question is settled.
====

[[ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus]]
==== ACE Context Status in `mstatus` and `sstatus`

{empty}
(((CSR,ACES field in mstatus)))
(((CSR,ACES field in sstatus)))
An ACE context status field, `ACES`, is added to `mstatus`[26:25] and shadowed in `sstatus`[26:25].
It is defined analogously to the floating-point context status field, `FS`, and the vector context status field, `VS`,
and tracks the dirtiness of the CRs, of the ACEIOBUF and of every ACE-specific CSR.
`mstatus.ACES` is always in effect; when V=1 the `vsstatus.ACES` field of <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>> is
in effect as well.

[WARNING]
====
The choice of `mstatus`[26:25], and of the corresponding bits of `sstatus` and
`vsstatus`, is provisional and has not been agreed with the RISC-V Architecture Review
Committee. Those bit positions are contested by other extensions, and ACES may end up at a
different offset, in a different register, or be carried by a mechanism other than a
`*status` context-status field. Nothing in this specification depends on the particular
offset: only on there being a two-bit context-status field with the semantics of
<<ACE-aces-encoding>>, defined analogously to `FS` and `VS`.
====

[[ACE-aces-encoding]]
.Encoding of the ACES field
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<8%,<84%",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Name |Meaning
|   0   | Off     | ACE disabled. The ACE state is inaccessible. Executing any ACE instruction or accessing any ACE-specific CSR raises an illegal-instruction exception.
|   1   | Initial | ACE enabled. No CR or the ACEIOBUF is configured. The hart may issue any ACE instruction.
|   2   | Clean   | ACE enabled. At least one CR or ACEIOBUF is configured; state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty   | ACE enabled. At least one CR, ACEIOBUF, or ACE-specific CSR may have changed since the last context swap.
Unconfigured CRs that were previously configured also cause ACES to become Dirty, since context-switching code must erase any stale saved state.
|===


The following holds for every `ACES` field, at the privilege modes at which it is in effect.
Any ACE instruction, including an access to an ACE CSR, raises an illegal-instruction exception if a
field in effect is Off. Where none is Off, an instruction that modifies ACE state sets every field
in effect to Dirty, and an implementation may set a field to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any
time, even in the absence of a state change. While a field is Dirty, the `SD` bit of the register
holding it is 1; otherwise that `SD` is set in accordance with the existing specifications. In
implementations with a writable `misa.L`, an `ACES` field may be present even when `misa.L` is
clear, analogously to `FS` and `VS`. The `*lcrstatus` fields and `ACES` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are set before any trap is taken on or after that instruction and before any later instruction executes, including when the instruction continues in the background (Rule <<ACE-IRR-long-running-no-data>>) or is discarded and restarted (Rule <<ACE-IRR-uninterruptible-instructions>>). An implementation may set a `*lcrstatus` field to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, as it may an `ACES` field; setting a field to Dirty is not a change of ACE state for the purposes of the state change rule for exceptions of <<ACE-error-architecture>>.

NOTE: Precise management of `mstatus.ACES` is an optimization that software may use to reduce context-switch overhead.

NOTE: Writes to an `ACES` field do not modify any ACE state.
Setting a field to Off, Initial, or Clean does not clear CRs, ACEIOBUF, or ACE CSRs;
any state changes must be explicit and must precede such a write.

[NOTE]
====
ACE memory instructions are allowed to trap so that implementations have more
flexibility when loading/storing large ACE contexts. Simpler implementations
may defer the load/store process to software using scalar or vector
loads/stores followed/preceded by an `ace.mv` instruction, whereas more performant
implementations may have dedicated hardware to speed up the instructions.
====

[[ACE-CSR-lcrstatus]]
==== `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` for Dirtiness Tracking

{empty}
(((CSR, `mlcrstatus`)))
(((CSR, `slcrstatus`)))
(((CSR, `vslcrstatus`)))
The 64-bit registers `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` track per-CR status, including dirtiness, at all levels below M-mode, S-mode, and VS-mode, respectively.

For each of these 3 CSRs:::
`*lcrstatus`(__i__) = `*lcrstatus`[2{interpunct}__i__+1:2{interpunct}__i__] is a two-bit field that encodes four possible status values for ``K(``__i__``)``, as described in the following table.
+
--
[[ACE-lcrstatus-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `*lcrstatus` bits
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<12%,<80%",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Name           | Behavior
|   0   | Off            | The CR is inaccessible.
|   1   | Initial        | The CR is accessible and in an initial state as defined by software.
|   2   | Clean          | The CR is accessible and its state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty          | The CR is accessible and its state may not match the last context swap.
|===
--
+
`*lcrstatus` is evaluated after the illegal-instruction grounds decided without accessing the state of the ACE unit (the first group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>>), `ace_exc_fatal` and `ace_exc_no_csk`,
and before every other condition, including the second group of <<ACE-illegal-instruction-grounds>> and the conditions of Rule <<ACE-SGR-gate-order>>: the hardware state of a CR whose field in effect is _Off_ is never consulted, since its contents are available only once it has been lazily loaded, or to the M-mode code that emulates it, and those conditions are evaluated there. An ACE instruction _accesses_ ``K(``__i__``)`` when ``K(``__i__``)`` is one of its CR operands, named directly or through a GPR, or when it is `ace.clear.all`; an access to a CR whose field in effect is _Off_ raises `ace_exc_CR_off`, except that an `ace.mgmt` opening a provisioning or import of ``K(``__i__``)``, `ace.clear`, `ace.clear.all`, and an `ace.clone` naming ``K(``__i__``)`` as destination, whose results do not depend on the contents of ``K(``__i__``)``, execute and set every field in effect to _Dirty_ even if ``K(``__i__``)`` was already _Unconfigured_. A mode whose _Off_ mark on ``K(``__i__``)`` has been replaced by _Dirty_ treats the hardware CR as authoritative and discards the image or emulated copy it held for it. `ace.getmd*` on such a CR traps like any other access; an implementation may return the MDH without trapping only where the result is the one the trap and the ensuing restore would have produced.

For `mlcrstatus`:::
+
--
When the privilege mode is (H)S, VS or U/VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when `mlcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `mlcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `slcrstatus`(__i__) or `vslcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`mstatus.ACES` is also in effect.
--


For `slcrstatus`:::
+
--
When the privilege mode is VS, U, or VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `slcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `slcrstatus`(__i__) and `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `slcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `vslcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`sstatus.ACES`, `mstatus.ACES`, and `mlcrstatus` are also in effect.
--


For `vslcrstatus`:::
+
--
`vslcrstatus` exists only when the H extension is implemented.

When V=1, `vslcrstatus` substitutes for `slcrstatus`: CSR accesses that would target `slcrstatus` instead access `vslcrstatus`, analogously to `vsstatus` and `sstatus`.

When the privilege mode is VU:

* Accesses to ``K(``__i__``)``
when any one of `mlcrstatus`(__i__), `vslcrstatus`(__i__), or `slcrstatus`(__i__) is 0 (Off) raise an `ace_exc_CR_off` exception.

* Any instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `vslcrstatus`(__i__), `slcrstatus`(__i__) and `mlcrstatus`(__i__) to Dirty.

Any direct write to `vslcrstatus`(__i__) does not change either `mlcrstatus`(__i__) or `slcrstatus`(__i__) (if defined).

`sstatus.ACES`, `vsstatus.ACES`, `mstatus.ACES`, `slcrstatus`, and `mlcrstatus` are also in effect.
--

`ace_exc_CR_off` comprises three exception causes, one per owning level: `ace_exc_CR_off_m` for `mlcrstatus`, `ace_exc_CR_off_hs` for the HS-level `slcrstatus` while V=1, and `ace_exc_CR_off_s` for `slcrstatus` while V=0 and for `vslcrstatus`.
The fields in effect are examined in the order `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, `vslcrstatus`, and the first found _Off_ selects the cause, so that the exception is taken by the most privileged mode whose field is _Off_:
`ace_exc_CR_off_m` is not delegable below M-mode and `ace_exc_CR_off_hs` is not delegable below HS-mode, whereas `ace_exc_CR_off_s` may be delegated to the mode that owns the register.
A handler therefore never receives `ace_exc_CR_off` for a field it cannot write, and an instruction it issues to resolve the condition is not gated by a higher level's field.

[NOTE]
====
The _Off_ value and the `ace_exc_CR_off` exception provide an architectural hook for on-demand (lazy) CR loading. At context restore, a handler may leave a CR unrestored, set its `*lcrstatus` field to _Off_, and retain the authoritative SCC in memory. A subsequent access from a lower-privileged mode traps with `ace_exc_CR_off` to the mode that owns the _Off_ field; the handler then imports the SCC before resuming execution. The architecture defines no separate "lazy" state: Software that marks a CR _Off_ is responsible for ensuring that the authoritative SCC is restored.
====

[[ACE-CSR-macecsk]]
==== `macecsk` Group

{empty}
(((CSR, `macecsk`)))
The optional `macecsk` group consists of XLEN-bit M-mode RW CSRs: eight in RV32, four in RV64.

A write to the __i__-th CSR sets bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of the CSK.

This CSR group is accessible in M-mode whenever `ACES` is not Off and `misa.L` is set, and not in Debug mode (<<ACE-interaction-with-debug>>); any other access raises an illegal-instruction exception.
Out of reset `ACES` is Off (<<ACE-out-of-reset-priv>>), so M-mode firmware enables `ACES` before it establishes the CSK, and until then the ACE unit is not usable.

A CSK can only become active if all eight (respectively, four) CSRs have been written to.
A mechanism for ensuring this is the following:

* The implementation maintains a per-hart set of flags indicating which CSRs in the group have been written to.
* These flags are initially unset.
* When M-Mode firmware writes to a CSR in the `macecsk` group, the implementation sets the corresponding flag, and keeps a copy of the value written so far. While any of the flags is set, reading any of the registers in this group will return the old value.
* Once all flags have been set, the values written by M-mode firmware are activated and replace the old values atomically, and the flags are cleared. The new CSK can now be used for cryptographic operations, and reading any of the registers in this group will return the new value.
* Any mode change to M-mode resets the flags and discards the partially written value, so that M-mode always begins a configuration attempt from a clean state and a partial key is never activated.
* A transition of the hart out of M-mode does *not* clear the flags: a partially written CSK persists, and the ACE unit stays unavailable, until M-mode is next entered.
* Any ACE unit reset clears the write-tracking flags and discards the partially written value.
* While any of the flags is set, that is while a CSK is partially written, the ACE unit is not available: any attempt to execute an ACE instruction, or to access an ACE CSR other than those named in <<ACE-CSK-requirements>>, raises ACE exception `ace_exc_no_csk`. Because activation clears the flags, the unit becomes available again as soon as the group has been written in full.

[NOTE]
====
The flags survive a transition out of M-mode, so an incomplete CSK write sequence is not
silently lost. The next ACE access from any mode traps with `ace_exc_no_csk`, notifying M-mode.
Entering M-mode resets the flags, allowing the handler to restart CSK provisioning.
====

The ACE unit is unavailable until a CSK is established as described in <<ACE-CSK-requirements>> (immediately, in the case of a fixed or randomly regenerated hardwired CSK).

[[ACE-CSR-locality-groups]]
==== Locality Secret CSR Groups

{empty}
(((CSR, `macephysbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `hacevirtbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `macelocality`)))
(((CSR, `hacelocality`)))
(((CSR, `sacelocality`)))
Each group listed in <<ACE-locality-csr-groups>> holds one 128-bit Locality Secret
(<<ACE-locality-indexes>>) in XLEN-bit RW CSRs accessed via Indirect CSR Access: four in RV32 and
two in RV64, the CSRs numbered "2" and "3" existing only on RV32.
A write to the __i__-th CSR of a group sets bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of its secret.
Access below the group's privilege, or in non-authenticated Debug mode, raises an
illegal-instruction or, as <<ACE-CSRs-priv>> prescribes, a virtual-instruction exception. The groups are accessible whether or not a CSK has been established (<<ACE-CSK-requirements>>): no ordering between CSK and Locality configuration is enforced.

// When the H extension is implemented, a `vsacelocality` group exists alongside it, holding a
// separate Locality Secret for the guest. When V=1, `vsacelocality` substitutes for `sacelocality`:
// CSR accesses that would target `sacelocality` instead access `vsacelocality`, analogously to
// `vsstatus` and `sstatus` and to `vslcrstatus` (<<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>>). Out of ACE unit reset the
// value of the guest SLocality is zero.

The Locality CSR groups are readable at their respective privilege levels.
Context-switching software saves and restores them like any other CSR.

[NOTE]
====
`sacelocality` is the operating system's Locality, used by S-mode and, with H, by VS-mode.
A hypervisor isolates guests with `hacelocality` and saves and restores `sacelocality` with the other supervisor CSRs.
====

[[ACE-out-of-reset-priv]]
=== ACE Privileged State out of Reset

This section and <<ACE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> are the authoritative statement of ACE state out of
reset.

Out of ACE unit reset:

. The value of the bit `L` of `misa` is 1 if the ACE unit and the ACE ISA are at least partially implemented in hardware.
   In the case of an emulated ACE unit, `L` is 0.
. The `ACES` field of `mstatus`, of `sstatus` and, where implemented, of `vsstatus` is Off.
. The CSK is unconfigured, unless it is hardwired or randomly regenerated.
. `macephysbootscrt` and `hacevirtbootscrt` are zero.
. `macelocality`, `hacelocality`, and `sacelocality` are zero.
. All fields of `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus`, and `vslcrstatus` are set to _Initial_.
