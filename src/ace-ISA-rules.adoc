[[ACE-common-rules]]
== Common Rules

_This chapter is normative. It is not yet included by `ace.adoc`._

This chapter collects statements that the specification currently repeats at many sites. Each rule
is written so that it can be stated once here and deleted from the places listed under *Replaces*.
The counts are occurrences in the current text of Books 1, 2 and 3.

Three of the defects found in review — a GCM footnote that described a `last_blk_len` range
belonging to other modes, an `Smstateen` paragraph duplicated in two Books, and a transfer-counter
description given twice with different wording — arose from copies of one statement drifting apart.
Single-sourcing them is the point of this chapter; the reduction in length is secondary.

A rule is listed under <<ACE-rules-unverified>> instead of below when the sites it would replace are
*not* known to agree. Those must be checked site by site before the rule is adopted, because
adopting one of them silently changes behaviour wherever a site differs.

*Rules already applied.* R1, R2 and R4 are no longer listed in this chapter, which is why the
numbering has gaps. R3, R5, R7, R8, R9, R10, R12, R13, R16, R17, R18, R20, R22, R23, R24, R25, R26 and R28 are kept below, each with a *Status* paragraph, because
their consolidated wording is worth consulting. Their landing places are:

[[ACE-rules-instructions]]
=== Rules for the Instruction Descriptions


[[ACE-rule-cr-operand]]
==== R3. Direct and indirect CR addressing

An instruction that names one CR carries a selector bit `r` at the position shown in its encoding
diagram: `r` = 0 when the CR index is a 5-bit immediate, `r` = 1 when it is held in the GPR named by
the same field. An instruction that names two CRs carries a two-bit field `R` instead, encoded as in
<<ACE-instruction-clone>>. Using `X0` as an indirect CR index raises an illegal-instruction
exception except where an encoding assigns it a meaning, and an indirect index outside [0..31] does
likewise.

*Replaces:* the selector-bit sentence in `ace.exec`, `ace.setst`, `ace.mgmt`, `ace.getmd*` and
`ace.restrict*`, and the two `ace.mv` sentences that restate it.

*Status: applied.* Merged into `GR1`, which was previously “Indirect CR index” and carried only the
validity constraints. Encoding first, validity second, in one rule: “indirect” now has a definition
before `GR1` constrains it, and nothing had to be renumbered. The term is *addressing*, not indexing
and not indirect addressing, because the rule covers `r` = 0 as well and because that is the word
already used where CRs are introduced.

Five sentences went outright and two `ace.mv` sentences lost a clause. The five had drifted apart in
wording — “the context is encoded as an immediate”, “the context is an immediate”, “takes the value
0 if the context is encoded as an immediate in Bits [11:7]” — and each named a bit position that the
encoding diagram directly above it already showed.

Three things stayed. `ace.clone` keeps its definition of the two-bit field `R` and the four values it
takes, since that is what `GR1` points at; `ace.derive` keeps its one-line pointer to it. `ace.setst`
keeps the reason its selector sits at bit 20, reworded to stand alone now that the sentence it
referred to is gone: “The selector occupies bit 20 here rather than bit 26, as in `ace.exec`, because
bits [31:25] are taken by `#immed7`.” And the conceptual sentence where CRs are first introduced —
that a CR is addressed directly via an immediate or indirectly via a GPR — stays, since it
introduces the distinction rather than restating the encoding.


[[ACE-rule-mgmt-bookkeeping]]
==== R5. Bookkeeping common to every `ace.mgmt`

Every `ace.mgmt` first checks that `acemanagedcr` holds either the target CR's number or 32, and
raises an illegal-instruction exception if it holds anything else. An `ace.mgmt` that *opens* a
management operation then writes the CR's number to `acemanagedcr`; one that *completes* one writes
32. Both clear `acestart`.

*Replaces:* the first, second-to-last and last step of each of the five step lists in
<<ACE-instruction-manage>>. Each list keeps only the steps peculiar to its operation.

*Status: applied.* Twenty lines removed; the rule is stated in the lead-in of
<<ACE-instruction-manage>>. Applying it also resolved a divergence: four lists ended with
“`acestart` is cleared” and the fifth with “`acestart` is set to 0”. The complete-provisioning
list, left with one step, became a sentence.

[[ACE-rule-cr-transfer]]
==== R23. The CR transfer model, once for `ace.load` and `ace.store`

`ace.load` and `ace.store` move the serialized image of a CR that follows the MDH, in the two
directions. The implementation obtains the payload length `size` from the first 8 bytes of the MDH:
_ConfigStatus_ identifies the payload as a PI or an SCC, and the fields of <<ACE-length-rule>> give
its length. `size` excludes the MDH but, for an SCC, includes the SIV and, where present, SIV2 and
IMPQUAL. Either instruction may move more than XLEN bits. `acestart` is a byte offset into that
payload — the MDH bytes software transfers on its own are not counted in it — it is always a
multiple of 16, and an implementation may halt a transfer only at a 16-byte boundary. The transfer
index advances the memory address and the offset within the image together, beginning at the current
`acestart`, so a resumption re-transfers no byte and displaces no part of the remainder.

*Replaces:* in `ace.load` and in `ace.store` alike, the three sentences that determine `size`, the
“larger than the effective XLEN” sentence, the sentences on 16-byte chunking, the sentence on
16-byte halting, the paragraph on advancing both offsets together, and the remark that the opening
`ace.mgmt` left `acestart` at zero — which the lead-in of <<ACE-instruction-manage>> already states
under R5.

Each instruction keeps what is its own: for `ace.load`, that `%offset(Xs1)` addresses the first byte
*after* the MDH and that a system-defined format makes its semantics implementation-dependent; for
`ace.store`, the *Bound.* paragraph, its NOTE, and the requirement to read the MDH out with
`ace.getmd*` beforehand.

Two things to settle when it is applied. Each of the two instructions states the 16-byte fact twice,
once as “`acestart` … is a multiple of 16, in other words … in 16-byte chunks” and once as
“implementations may halt only at 16-byte multiples”; the rule states it once, as a property of
`acestart` and a constraint on halting. And the sentence raising `ace_exc_complete_cr_data_access`,
which `ace.load`, `ace.store` and `ace.mv` each carry, is already stated with its full instruction
list in <<ACE-error-architecture>> and again in the footnote of <<ACE-instruction-properties>>;
whether the three local copies go is a judgement call, since each is a complete
condition-and-consequence pair rather than a dangling condition.

*Status: applied.* The rule is stated in <<ACE-instruction-load>>, as the paragraph
<<ACE-cr-transfer-model>>, and `ace.store` refers to it in one sentence. It is not in
<<ACE-management-operations>>, which precedes the two instructions and was the first candidate:
that subsection is informative, and, more to the point, the specification already anchors a shared
rule at the primary instruction of its group rather than in a section above it. `ace.mv` took its
transfer length “exactly as `ace.load` determines it” and its bound from the reason given in
`ace.store`; the first of those cross-references now points at the rule itself.

Book 1 lost 26 lines and gained 15, and `ace.store` lost nineteen consecutive lines to a single
sentence. Two divergences closed: each instruction had stated the 16-byte property twice in
different words, once as a chunk size and once as a halting constraint, and only `ace.store` had
said that `acestart` excludes the MDH bytes. The device that lets one paragraph serve both
instructions is `end`, the offset at which the transfer stops — `ace.load` sets it to `size`,
`ace.store` to min(`size`, `imglen`), which is what its *Bound.* paragraph exists to state.

Left in place as foreseen: the three copies of the `ace_exc_complete_cr_data_access` sentence, each
a complete condition-and-consequence pair, and the six `<<ACE-resumability>>` pointers, which are
R28's business. One redundancy inside `ace.load` also survives, which R23 had not counted: it says
twice that the MDH is passed separately to `ace.mgmt`, at the head of its description and again in
its precondition sentence. Folding the operand detail — a GPR pair, a quartet on RV32, or a vector
register — into the first would retire the second.

[[ACE-rule-iobuf-window]]
==== R24. The ACEIOBUF window transfer, once for `ace.input` and `ace.output`

`ace.input` and `ace.output` transfer the bytes of the ACEIOBUF window in the two directions: for
each `j` from `acestart` to min(`Xl`, `aceiobuftop`) − 1, byte `j` of the ACEIOBUF is exchanged with
the byte at memory address `%offset(Xs)` + `j`. Bytes below `acestart`, and bytes at or beyond
min(`Xl`, `aceiobuftop`), are not accessed on either side. Upon successful completion `acestart` is
set to 0. If `Xl` = 0, or `acestart` {ge} min(`Xl`, `aceiobuftop`), the window is empty: nothing is
transferred, and `acestart` is still set to 0, exactly as it would be had the transfer run to the
end of the window (<<ACE-CSR-acestart>>). If `Xl` is greater than `aceiobuftop`, only the bytes of
the window are transferred, which software can detect by comparing `Xl` with `aceiobuftop`. Either
instruction may be used only while the ACEIOBUF is enabled and `aceiobuflen` is nonzero; otherwise
ACE exception `ace_exc_unconfigured_buffer` is raised.

*Replaces:* nine consecutive lines of `ace.input` and the same nine lines of `ace.output`, which
differ only in the direction of the copy and in the name of the instruction. This is the largest
verbatim duplication left in Book 1.

Each description then keeps one sentence: `ace.input` its opening, that inputs may be read from
memory when the vector registers are too short or absent, and `ace.output` that it is the
corresponding output operation. The non-normative reason for setting `acestart` to 0 on an empty
window — that leaving it unchanged would make a resumption loop retrying on an empty window fail to
terminate — is worth keeping, and is currently given twice.

*Status: applied.* The rule is stated in <<ACE-instruction-input>>, as the paragraph
<<ACE-iobuf-window-transfer>>, and `ace.output` refers to it in one sentence — the same shape as
R23, and for the same reason: the shared statement is anchored at the primary instruction of the
pair rather than in the window model of <<ACE-CSR-aceiobuftop>>, which was the first candidate.

Thirteen lines of `ace.output` became two, and the description now reads in full: “This is the
output operation corresponding to `ace.input`. <<ACE-iobuf-window-transfer>> of
<<ACE-instruction-input>> applies, with the direction reversed: byte `j` of the ACEIOBUF is written
to memory address `%offset(Xs)` + `j`.” `ace.input` keeps its opening, on why the two instructions
exist, its own direction sentence, and its pointer to <<ACE-usage-input-output>>, which `ace.output`
never carried.

The rule is written with the direction as its only free variable, which is what made the two copies
identical in the first place: the window bounds, the empty-window rule, the truncation rule and the
`ace_exc_unconfigured_buffer` condition never depended on it. Two sentences that began with a
lowercase “bytes below `acestart`” were repaired in passing, and the non-normative reason for
setting `acestart` to 0 on an empty window, which was given twice, is now given once, in the
sentence that states the behaviour.

[[ACE-rule-mv-variants]]
==== R25. The four `ace.mv` variants in one table

The four variants of `ace.mv` differ in four values — the `Form` field, the sub-opcode in the
register field that Form leaves unused, the direction of the copy, and the admissible
_ConfigStatus_ — and in nothing else. They become a table with those four columns, preceded by the
three statements they share: a variant is valid only when _ConfigStatus_ has the value its row
gives, and raises an illegal-instruction exception otherwise; a vector variant requires
`vl` {times} (`SEW`/8) to be a multiple of 16, and transitions the CR to Error State _Invalid_ if it
is not; and a vector variant raises an illegal-instruction exception when V is not enabled.
`acestart` advances by the number of bytes transferred, 16 in a scalar variant and
`vl` {times} (`SEW`/8) in a vector one.

*Replaces:* the four RV64 bullets and the four RV32 bullets of <<ACE-move>>. Two of the RV32 bullets
say only “No changes”, and the other two write out the quartet layout that `GR2` and R26 supply.

This is the R4 pattern rather than the R14 one: the table sits in the section whose text it
replaces, so nothing sends the reader elsewhere. A reader of `ace.mv` had to compare four nearly
identical bullets to see what distinguishes the variants, which is what a table shows at a glance.

*Status: applied.* Forty-three lines became thirty-three: six sentences of shared preamble, the
four-column table <<ACE-mv-variants>>, and one sentence on the GPR operand. The four bullets had
between them thirteen sentences that said the same four things, and the “For RV32 the differences”
list is gone entirely — two of its four items said only “No changes”.

The `ConfigStatus` values are now a column rather than a sentence repeated four times, which is what
makes the table earn its place: the reader sees at a glance that the two `Form` = `0b01` variants
take `ace_cfgst_provisioning` or `ace_cfgst_importing` and the two `Form` = `0b10` ones
`ace_cfgst_exporting`, a pattern the bullets buried.

One sentence is held locally pending R26: “The 128-bit GPR operand is the pair `X[s+1] @ X[s]` on
RV64 and the quartet `X[s+3] @ X[s+2] @ X[s+1] @ X[s]` on RV32 (`GR2`)”. `GR2` gives the pair and
quartet but not their order, so deleting the two RV32 bullets without it would have lost the layout.
When R26 lands, that sentence moves into `GR2` and `ace.mv` keeps nothing of it.

[[ACE-rule-pair-layout]]
==== R26. The layout of a GPR pair or quartet operand

`GR2` gains a second sentence: a 64-bit operand held in a GPR pair `Xs` is `X[s+1] @ X[s]`, and a
128-bit operand held in a GPR quartet is `X[s+3] @ X[s+2] @ X[s+1] @ X[s]`; the same layout applies
whether the operand is a source or a destination.

*Replaces:* the RV32 bullets of `ace.mv`, the two RV32 bullets of `ace.getmd*`, the Form B item of
`ace.mgmt`, the RV32 Form B bullet of `ace.size`, and the corresponding clause of `ace.setst`, each
of which writes the concatenation out in full.

One clause must survive the deletion: the RV32 bullets of `ace.getmd*` also require the copies to be
*atomic*, which `GR2` does not say and which is a property of that instruction rather than of the
layout.

*Status: applied.* `GR2` is rewritten and the concatenation is now written out in exactly one place
in Book 1. Seven sites were cleared: the sentence `ace.mv` was holding from R25, the auxiliary-input
clause of `ace.setst`, the Form B item of `ace.mgmt`, the two RV32 bullets of `ace.getmd*` — which
became one — the RV32 paragraph of `ace.restrict*`, the RV32 Form B bullet of `ace.size`, and the
second sentence of `ace.avail`. The last two were not in the count above; `ace.restrict*` and
`ace.avail` state the same layout and were found only when the sites were visited.

The rule had to be widened to do the work. `GR2` covered the 128-bit case alone — “On RV64 a 128-bit
operand is a pair, on RV32 a quartet” — which does not reach `ace.setst`'s 64-bit auxiliary input or
`ace.getmdl`'s 64-bit destination, both of which are one GPR on RV64 and a pair on RV32. It now
states both widths, both directions, and the order, so every site it replaces is derivable from it:

`{fournbsp}` “a 64-bit operand is one GPR on RV64 and the pair `X[s+1] @ X[s]` on RV32, and a
128-bit operand is the pair `X[s+1] @ X[s]` on RV64 and the quartet `X[s+3] @ X[s+2] @ X[s+1] @
X[s]` on RV32”.

Atomicity survives where it was stated, in `ace.getmd*` and in `ace.restrict*`, since `GR2` says
nothing about it. Two smaller things were repaired in passing: the rule was headed “Register pairs
and quartet”, and `ace.restrict*` called its `Xs` operand “the output” where it is read, not
written.

<<ACE-instruction-derive>> was left alone although it matches the search. Its RV32 bullet does not
merely give the layout: it assigns the transfer length to `X[s2+1]` and the two field indices to
`X[s2]`, which is a field placement a reader implementing it wants in front of them, even though it
follows from `GR2` together with the RV64 formula.

[[ACE-rule-h-v-bits]]
==== R27. The `h` and `v` selector bits

`ace.getmd*` and `ace.restrict*` share an encoding shape and are distinguished only by `func2`.
Bit 28, `h`, selects between the two GPR variants: `h` = 0 is the variant whose mnemonic ends in
`l`, which names bits [63:0] of the MDH, and `h` = 1 the other one. Bit 29, `v`, selects the operand
class: `v` = 0 a GPR, a GPR pair on RV32, and `v` = 1 the lowest 128 bits of a vector register or
group, which carries the whole MDH. A vector operand therefore requires `h` = 0, and the combination
`v` = `h` = 1 is an illegal instruction.

*Replaces:* four sentences in the _Encoding_ of <<ACE-instruction-getmd>> and four in the _Encoding_
of <<ACE-instruction-restrict>>.

The rule cannot say that `h` = 1 selects bits [127:64], although both encodings read as though it
did: `ace.restricth` writes bits [127:64] only, while `ace.getmd` reads the *whole* 128-bit MDH into
a GPR pair, `ace.getmdl` being the one that reads bits [63:0]. Each instruction therefore keeps one
line saying what its `h` = 1 variant names. The asymmetry follows from the direction — `ace.restrictv`
already writes the whole MDH at once, so no wide GPR write is needed — and is not a defect, but it
is why the shared rule has to be phrased in terms of the mnemonics.

[[ACE-rule-resumability-pointer]]
==== R28. “Note that the rules in <<ACE-resumability>> apply.”

Delete the sentence. <<ACE-resumability>> already opens its *Resumable instructions* item with the
list of the instructions it governs — `ace.exec`, `ace.derive`, `ace.load`, `ace.store`,
`ace.input`, `ace.output` — and that list is exactly the set of six descriptions that carry the
pointer.

*Replaces:* six occurrences, one each in `ace.load`, `ace.store`, `ace.exec`, `ace.derive`,
`ace.input` and `ace.output`.

Nothing is left in their place and nothing is lost that was not already stated elsewhere, so this is
the one rule in the chapter that is a pure deletion. It also removes a maintenance hazard: the
pointer and the list are two records of the same membership, and a seventh resumable instruction
would have to be entered in both.

*Status: applied by the author.* All six are gone, and the membership is now recorded once, in the
*Resumable instructions* item of <<ACE-resumability>>, which still opens with the same list of six
instructions. Six lines removed with no replacement, the smallest and cleanest change in the
chapter.

[[ACE-rule-size-return]]
==== R29. What `ace.size` returns, stated once

`ace.size` returns in `Xd` the size in bytes of the memory buffer needed to hold the exported SCC or
the partially configured PI: for an SCC the figure includes the authentication tag or tags and the
additional variable-length data, and for a PI it is the size of the whole PI. It returns 0 when the
CC is unconfigured, when the _Algorithm_, _AlgorithmPolicy_ or _SCProtection_ of the supplied MDH is
unsupported, or when the MDH is otherwise malformed. The three Forms then differ only in where the
MDH comes from: the CR named by `Ks` or `K{Xs}`, bits [63:0] of the MDH in a GPR, or the whole MDH
in a vector register.

*Replaces:* the repetition inside <<ACE-instruction-size>> itself, where the “size of the memory
buffer … in the first case … in the second case” passage appears three times and the zero return
twice, and once more in the closing paragraph.

This one is contained in a single section, so it costs the reader nothing at all: four bullets
become one statement and a three-line list of where each Form takes its MDH. The closing paragraph
on the return of 32, which is not an error indication, stays as it is.

[[ACE-rule-mgmt-forms]]
==== R30. The Form encodings of `ace.mgmt` follow from those of `ace.setst`

`ace.mgmt` already states that its encoding “is identical to that of `ace.setst` except for the most
significant bit of `funct3`, which is set to `1`”. Its own three-item list of Form encodings
therefore follows from `ace.setst`'s and can go.

*Replaces:* the “The encodings of the three Forms are” list of <<ACE-instruction-manage>>.

The Form *meanings* are not shared and stay where they are: `ace.setst`'s three Forms differ in how
the auxiliary input is passed, `ace.mgmt`'s in how the MDH is passed. Only the `funct3` values are
mechanical.

[[ACE-rule-vector-mdh]]
==== R31. A 128-bit MDH carried in a vector operand

Where a Form of an ACE instruction carries the whole 128-bit MDH in a vector register or group, that
operand must satisfy `VL` {times} `SEW` {ge} 128; otherwise an illegal-instruction exception is
raised. This is the requirement that <<ACE-extensions-overview-unpriv>> records as one of `Zvl128b`,
`Zvl256b`, `Zvl512b` or `Zvl1024b`.

*Replaces:* the requirement as stated at `ace.getmdv`, at Form C of `ace.size`, and in the mnemonic
list of `ace.size`; and it supplies the requirement where it is missing, at Form C of `ace.mgmt`, at
`ace.restrictv`, and at the vector variants of `ace.mv`.

This is a divergence rather than plain repetition. `ace.getmdv` states the bound and the exception;
`ace.size` states the bound in its mnemonic list and names the four `Zvl` extensions in its
description; Form C of `ace.mgmt` says only that “the MDH is in the 128 lowest bits of `Vs`” and
says nothing about a short vector. Stating it once decides the open case rather than restating the
settled ones.

[[ACE-rule-rv32-shadows]]
==== R7. RV32 high shadows

On RV32, a 64-bit ACE CSR `X` is accompanied by a 32-bit CSR `Xh` that shadows bits [63:32] of `X`,
so that those bits can be accessed. The shadow has the same privilege as the register it shadows.

*Replaces:* the shadow sentence in `acesivl`/`acesivh`, `acesiv2l`/`acesiv2h`, `aceiql`/`aceiqh` and,
in Book 3, the one covering `mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus` and `vslcrstatus`.

*Status: applied.* Four sentences removed, three in Book 1 and one in Book 3, and replaced by one
sentence after each of the two CSR tables — six registers in the Book 1 copy, three in the Book 3
copy, which opens “Similarly to <<ACE-CSR-unpriv>>”.

I first wrote a single sentence in Book 1 scoped to both Books. That was wrong, and the general
point applies to every rule in this chapter whose sites straddle the Book boundary: Book 1 is
destined for the Unprivileged Architecture Part of the complete RISC-V specification and Book 3 for
the Privileged Architecture Part, so a rule written in one that governs the other's registers would
end up in a different document from the registers it governs. A cross-reference between the two
copies is welcome; a dependency is not.

Each copy is quantified over *every 64-bit ACE CSR of its own Book*, which is exactly the set that
has shadows: the six SIV, SIV2 and IMPQUAL halves in Book 1, the three `*lcrstatus` registers in
Book 3. Every other ACE CSR is XLEN-bit or 32-bit read-only, so neither quantifier over- or
under-reaches. Both state what the four original sentences left implicit — that a read or write of
the base register itself accesses bits [31:0] on RV32.

Contrary to what this rule first proposed, the shadow *rows* stay in the two CSR tables. Those tables
are register enumerations, and each row carries an address; dropping the rows because the names are
mechanical would leave nine CSRs without one.

[[ACE-rule-secret-csr-groups]]
==== R8. Secret-bearing CSR groups

A *secret CSR group* is a set of XLEN-bit RW CSRs, accessed through Indirect CSR Access, that
together hold one 128-bit or 256-bit secret: four CSRs on RV32 and two on RV64 for a 128-bit secret,
eight and four for a 256-bit one. A write to the __i__-th CSR of a group sets bits
[(_i_+1){middot}XLEN−1 : _i_{middot}XLEN] of the secret. Access from a mode lower than the group's,
or in non-authenticated Debug mode, raises an illegal-instruction exception. Out of ACE unit reset
every such secret is zero, unless it is hardwired or regenerated by hardware.

The groups are then given by one table with columns: group name, secret, privilege, and any rule
peculiar to it.

*Replaces:* five near-identical subsections in Book 3 — `macephysbootscrt`, `hacevirtbootscrt`,
`macelocality`, `hacelocality`, `sacelocality`. `macecsk` keeps a subsection of its own, because its
activation rules and its readback behaviour are not shared with the others.

*Status: applied.* The five subsections became one, <<ACE-CSR-locality-groups>>, plus the table
<<ACE-locality-csr-groups>>; Book 3 lost 38 lines. Two points needed care. The five anchors are
referenced from <<ACE-priv-extensions>> and from <<ACE-locality-indexes>> in Book 1, so each is kept
as an inline anchor with a reftext in its table row rather than deleted with its heading. And the
condition under which each group exists, which the subsections stated unevenly — only `hacelocality`
said so explicitly — is now a column of the table, so the five are stated in the same terms.

[[ACE-rule-reset-values]]
==== R9. Reset values are stated only in the reset sections

<<ACE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> and <<ACE-out-of-reset-priv>> are the authoritative statements of ACE
state out of reset. No CSR description restates its own reset value.

*Replaces:* the per-CSR reset sentences in Books 1 and 3.

*Status: applied.* Eight sentences removed, three from Book 1 — `aceiobuftop`, `acestart`,
`acemanagedcr` — and five from Book 3 — `misa.L`, `mstatus.ACES`, `vsstatus.ACES`, the CSK, and the
Locality Secrets. Both reset sections now open by declaring themselves authoritative. Three points
needed care.

One reset value was *not* in either reset section and had to be added before its statements could be
deleted: that the `ACES` field is Off out of reset, which only the two `*status` subsections gave.
Removing them first would have lost it.

Two statements of the `misa.L` reset value disagreed: its own subsection said “1 if the ACE unit is
implemented and 0 otherwise”, while <<ACE-out-of-reset-priv>> distinguishes a unit implemented in
hardware from an emulated one, for which `L` is 0. The latter is the one that survives, and it
agrees with the sentence immediately below it about M-mode firmware setting the bit for an emulated
unit.

One sentence was kept although it matched the search: “Their values out of ACE unit reset identify
the version of the ACE unit. Software cannot modify these values”, in <<ACE-CSR-impl-ids>>. It
states what those values *mean* and that they are read-only, not what they are, so the reset
sections do not cover it.

[[ACE-rule-warl-clamp]]
==== R32. Clamping WARL CSRs

Writing to an ACE CSR that has an upper bound a value larger than that bound sets the CSR to the
bound. Software must therefore read such a CSR back after writing it.

*Replaces:* the three-sentence WARL paragraph of <<ACE-CSR-aceiobuflen>>, whose bound is
`acemaxiobuflen`, and the three-sentence WARL paragraph of <<ACE-CSR-aceiobuftop>>, whose bound is
`aceiobuflen`. Each description keeps one clause naming its own bound.

`aceiobuflen` also states twice, once in its own text and once in the NOTE that follows it, that
context-switching code must save and restore the ACEIOBUF up to `aceiobuflen`. The normative
sentence stays; the NOTE keeps only its recommendation, that software set `aceiobuflen` to zero once
the buffer is no longer needed.

[[ACE-rule-aces-fields]]
==== R33. The `ACES` field, once for the `*status` registers

An `ACES` field occupies bits [26:25] of `mstatus` and, where the H extension is implemented, of
`vsstatus`, and is defined analogously to `FS` and `VS`. It tracks the dirtiness of the CRs, of the
ACEIOBUF and of every ACE-specific CSR. For each of them: any ACE instruction, including an access
to an ACE CSR, raises an illegal-instruction exception if a field in effect at the current privilege
mode is Off; where none is Off, an instruction that modifies ACE state sets every field in effect to
Dirty; an implementation may set a field to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, even with no
state change; while a field is Dirty the `SD` bit of its own register is 1, and otherwise `SD` is
set in accordance with the existing specifications; and where `misa.L` is writable the field may be
present even when `misa.L` is clear. A write to an `ACES` field modifies no ACE state.

The fields in effect at each privilege mode are then given by one table.

*Replaces:* the shared half of <<ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus>> and <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>>, which state
the Dirty-setting rule, the may-set-Dirty-at-any-time rule, the `SD` rule and the writable-`misa.L`
rule twice each.

This is the R8 pattern applied to a second family, and each register keeps what is peculiar to it:
that `vsstatus.ACES` is not itself ACE context state, so writing it does not set `mstatus.ACES` to
Dirty; and that when V=1 both `vsstatus.ACES` and `mstatus.ACES` are in effect.

One divergence has to be resolved first, and it is a substantive one rather than a wording
difference. <<ACE-ACES-field-in-mstatus>> says flatly that an instruction modifying ACE state sets
`mstatus.ACES` to Dirty when the field is Initial or Clean; <<ACE-ACES-field-VS>> guards the same
statement with “If neither field is Off”. The wording above follows the second, since an instruction
issued while a field is Off raises an exception and modifies nothing, but the two texts should be
compared before the rule is adopted.

[[ACE-rule-lcrstatus]]
==== R34. The three `*lcrstatus` registers, described once

`mlcrstatus`, `slcrstatus` and `vslcrstatus` have the same two-bit-per-CR layout and the same
encoding. For each of them, at the privilege modes at which it is in effect: an ACE instruction that
uses ``K(``__i__``)`` raises `ace_exc_CR_off` when the __i__-th field of *any* register in effect is
Off; an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets the __i__-th field of every register in
effect to Dirty; and a direct write to one register's field changes neither of the other two. The
privilege modes at which each register is in effect, the registers in effect with it, and the
`*status` fields in effect with it, are given by one table.

*Replaces:* the three `For` items of <<ACE-CSR-lcrstatus>>, which state the same three rules with
only the register lists varying, including the sentence “Any direct write to … does not change
either … or … (if defined)”, which appears three times.

`vslcrstatus` keeps its two peculiar sentences: that it exists only when the H extension is
implemented, and that when V=1 it substitutes for `slcrstatus` in CSR accesses, analogously to
`vsstatus` and `sstatus`.

The register sets are nested — `mlcrstatus` alone, then with `slcrstatus`, then with `vslcrstatus` —
so the table has one row per register and one column per set, which is how the three subsections
already read; nothing is moved away from the reader of any one of them.

[[ACE-rule-priv-config-table]]
==== R35. The privilege-configuration requirements in one table

The four bullet lists of <<ACE-priv-extensions>> — for an M-mode-only hart, an M+U system, an M+S+U
system, and an M+S+U system with the H extension — state the same requirements with different
values. They become one table with a row per system configuration and a column per extension:
`Smacecrstatus`, `Smacecsk`, `Smacelclt`, `Smacepbootscrt` and `Smacevbootscrt`.

*Replaces:* the four lists, seventeen bullets in all, of which “`Smacecsk` is allowed” appears four
times verbatim and “If `Smacepbootscrt` is implemented, the `macephysbootscrt` CSR group is
implemented to set the Physical Boot Secret Locality” four times verbatim.

A reader of this section is choosing a configuration and asking what it requires, which is what a
row gives; the comparison across configurations, which the bullet lists make the reader assemble by
hand, is what the table adds. This is therefore not the case R14 warns against, where a value
belonging to one Algorithm would have to be looked up in a section about all of them.

[[ACE-rules-algorithms]]
=== Rules for the Algorithm Descriptions

[[ACE-rule-block-iteration]]
==== R10. Block iteration

In a state that processes whole blocks, an `ace.exec` whose `ACELEN` is a whole multiple of the
block size `b` performs the state's per-block operation `ACELEN`/`b` times, taking the blocks of
`INPUT`, and producing the blocks of `OUTPUT`, from the least significant position upwards. A state
that this specification describes as processing a single block — every last-block, finalize, verify
and output state — is excluded and processes exactly one.

*Replaces:* the restatements across ECB, CTR/XCTR, XEX, GCM, GCM-SIV, OCB, CMAC, Ascon-AEAD128 and
Ascon-Hash256.

*Status: applied.* Seventeen restatements removed, not the fourteen first counted — two were written
`ACELEN > b` inside a single pair of backticks and escaped the search. Book 2 lost 62 lines. The
rule is item 4 of <<ACE-general-rules-for-algorithms>>, and it absorbed the `foreach` loop that <<ACE-ECB-mode>>
used to carry, which was the only place the block indexing was written out.

The sites stated their condition in three incompatible ways: “If `ACELEN` > `b`”, “If `ACELEN` is a
multiple of `b`”, and, in one case, “If `ACELEN` {ge} 128”. The first is strictly wrong — it
excludes `ACELEN` = `b`, the ordinary single-block case — and the rule now covers all of them by
giving the count as `ACELEN`/`b`. GCM's _Decrypt_ sentence also mixed the two notations, decrypting
“each 128-bit block of `INPUT` into the corresponding `b`-bit block of `OUTPUT`”.

Each of the states that lost the sentence now carries the marker “(multi-block)” before the Form it
expects, so that `AGR4` is visibly the thing that governs it. There are 18 such states: the 17 from
which text was removed, plus State _Encrypt_/_Decrypt_ of the generic tweakable block cipher, which
iterates by its Parameters but never carried the sentence.

The states left unmarked divide cleanly: either they process exactly one block, or they iterate by a
different mechanism — Subalgorithm `process_VLI`, the output-squeeze loop of the generic hash and
MAC section, or the long-field transfer counter of R17. `AGR4` governs none of those.

One sentence of the same shape was kept: <<ACE-Ascon-Hash256>>'s _Hash_Finalize_ writes successive
64-bit fields of `OUTPUT` and restarts at `OUTPUT`[63:0] when a further `ace.exec` is issued. That
is a different rule, not block iteration over an input.

[[ACE-rule-input-truncation]]
==== R11. An over-long input is truncated

Where a state consumes a single value of `X` bits, an `ACELEN` greater than `X` is truncated: only
the `X` least significant bits of `INPUT` are considered, and the rest are ignored. `X` is the width
of the field being set, which is not necessarily `b`.

*Rejected: not useful.* This was applied and reverted. The nine restatements were replaced by the
sentence “Rule AGR6 of <<ACE-general-rules-for-algorithms>> applies.”, and the result read worse
than what it replaced: the reader of a state lost a short, complete clause and gained a pointer to
another section, for no saving in length.

The general lesson, which applies to the rules still open in this chapter: consolidation is worth
doing when the restatement can simply be *deleted*, because a general rule already implies it and
the reader loses nothing — R10 and R13 are of that kind. It is not worth doing when a pointer has to
be left behind in its place. A cross-reference is only an improvement over a repeated sentence when
the sentence is long, or when the copies have drifted apart; here each was one clause, and they
agreed.

The nine sites were also more than the seven first counted: two are embedded mid-sentence, in the
generic tweakable block cipher and in CMAC's _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, and CMAC writes the words in
the other order. Should this ever be revisited, those are the two to notice.

[[ACE-rule-output-clearing]]
==== R12. Unwritten output bits are cleared

When a state writes fewer than `ACELEN` bits to `OUTPUT`, the remaining bits of `OUTPUT` are
cleared.

*Replaces:* nothing. The rule is stated, the local statements stay.

*Status: applied as a rule only.* `AGR7` gives the property a single normative home, but the three
Algorithms that state it keep their sentences, at the author's instruction: each is a clause in the
middle of a state description, exactly the case the R11 episode showed is worse off for being
replaced by a pointer.

What the rule did buy is concision, because the three sites had been cross-referencing *each other*
in the absence of a general rule. <<ACE-hash-functions>> ended with “This matches the rule stated for
CMAC in <<ACE-CMAC-mode>>”, and Ascon-Hash256 with “as in <<ACE-CMAC-mode>> and
<<ACE-hash-functions>>”. Both pointers are gone, and CMAC's sentence lost its redundant
`ACELEN` > `b` guard.

*Open: Ascon-AEAD128 says something else.* Its _Enc_Last_Block_ and _Hash_Output_ each end
“(Only the 128 lsbs of `OUTPUT` are written to.)”, which is not what `AGR7` says: not written is not
the same as cleared, and with `ACELEN` > 128 the two give different results. These two sentences were
counted among this rule's sites when it was first drafted, wrongly. They are untouched, and the
question of which behaviour is intended is open.

[[ACE-rule-ready]]
==== R13. State _Ready_ accepts no `ace.exec`

No `ace.exec` may be issued in State _Ready_; issuing one transitions the CR to Error State
_Invalid_, as <<ACE-general-rules-for-algorithms>> prescribes for any instruction a state does not allow.

*Replaces:* the per-Algorithm restatements.

*Status: applied.* Eleven restatements removed, not the nine first counted, and the rule is `AGR3`.
It is worded “unless the Algorithm explicitly allows it”, so it is a default rather than an
absolute; the _Ready_ row of the valid-states table in Book 1 was softened to match, since it
previously said flatly that no `ace.exec` may be executed in that state.

The eleven sites stated it in four different ways, and only one of them — the generic hash and MAC
section — gave the consequence, that the CR transitions to Error State _Invalid_. The rule states it
once and derives it from `AGR1`.

Applying the rule also supplies the statement where it was *missing*: CTR/XCTR, SHA-2, SHA-3, KMAC,
HMAC, ECC and ML-DSA never said anything about `ace.exec` in _Ready_, so eleven copies covered ten
Algorithms and left seven uncovered.

Two sites needed more than a deletion. In ECB the sentence was the whole of the _Ready_ behaviour
item, which therefore went with it. In ML-KEM it was the first line of an item whose second line
clears the state fields, so the item was rewritten to keep that clause.

[[ACE-rule-last-block-length]]
==== R14. Entering a last-block state

*Rejected: not useful.* The proposal was to state the shape of the transition once — a Form B
`ace.setst` whose `Xs` is the bit length of the final block, a value outside the Algorithm's range
transitioning the CR to Error State _Invalid_ — and to gather the five admissible ranges into one
table.

It fails for the same reason as R11, in a slightly different form: a reader of GCM or of OCB would
have to look at a table in another section to find the range that applies, instead of reading it in
place. The comparison the table would offer is worth less than the value being where it is used.
Each Algorithm therefore keeps its own sentence in full.

[[ACE-rule-verification]]
==== R15. Verification outcomes

A state whose purpose is to verify a tag, a MAC or a signature transitions to _Success_ when the
value matches and to _Failure_ when it does not. Neither outcome is an error: no exception is
raised, the CR is not invalidated, and the Algorithm has performed as specified.

It would have replaced eight restatements, in GCM, GCM-SIV, OCB, CMAC, Ascon-AEAD128, <<ACE-ECC>>,
<<ACE-EdDSA>> and <<ACE-PQC-ML-DSA>>; the final sentence is in any case already stated once, under
*Algorithmic Errors* in <<ACE-error-architecture>>.

*Discarded.* Two reasons, and the second is the one that decides it.

The eight sites are each a clause in the middle of a state description, giving a *consequence*
alongside the condition that produces it. Replacing them with a pointer is the R11 case, and each
Algorithm keeps its outcomes where a reader of that Algorithm will read them.

More fundamentally, this is a rule about how an Algorithm is *designed* — that verification is
modelled as a transition to _Success_ or _Failure_ rather than as an error — and the behaviour
follows from the design. It is not a rule about what the architecture does when an Algorithm runs,
which is what the `AGR` set states. The distinction is the same one that disqualified R19, seen from
the other side: R19 bound the caller instead of the architecture, and this binds the specification of
the Algorithms instead of their execution.

[[ACE-rule-derived-fields]]
==== R16. Derived fields are recomputed on import

A field that an Algorithm derives from other fields of the same CC is not carried in the Serialized
Context. It is recomputed when the import completes, that is by the `ace.mgmt` with
`#ace_CR_management_end`, and likewise when the value it derives from is set or replaced.

*Replaces:* the intra-Algorithm duplication of the statement, and the timing clause in the
behaviour bullets that state each derivation.

*Status: applied.* The rule is `AGR5`. The case for it was not the number of sites but that three
Algorithms stated it *twice each*, hundreds of lines apart: GCM and GCM-SIV in a Data Structures
NOTE and again in a behaviour bullet, OCB in a table cell and again in a behaviour paragraph. The
two NOTEs are gone — GCM's keeps its unrelated first sentence, about the AD and plaintext lengths
being the caller's business — and the two behaviour bullets shed the timing that `AGR5` now supplies,
keeping the formula, which is the part worth having in place:

`{fournbsp}` `** \`auth_key\` is a derived field (AGR5): \`auth_key <- enc_blk(key, zeros(128))\`.`

Four sites were deliberately left alone, because each says something `AGR5` does not. OCB's
paragraph gives an implementation trade-off — cache the `L` values in the Additional Data Section or
recompute them, the latter likely faster. ML-DSA's derivation of `tr` is conditional on `HasPrivKey`
and comes with the argument that a verification-only CC is functional immediately after import.
EdDSA's says the clamped scalar, `prefix` and public key may be cached rather than recomputed, and
are never exported. OCB's table cell is a one-clause annotation in a field table.

This is the pattern the R11 episode established: delete where the local text adds nothing, keep it
where it does, and state the rule once either way.

[[ACE-rule-long-field-transfer]]
==== R17. Loading and emitting a long field

A state that loads a long field from `INPUT`, or emits one to `OUTPUT`, over several `ace.exec`
instructions records its progress in the _AlgorithmUse_ field of the MDH, as a count of bits. The
count is zeroed on entry to the state, rises monotonically, and is never reset by the completion of
the field. When it reaches the size of the field, no further `ace.exec` is allowed in that state;
issuing one transitions the CR to Error State _Invalid_. On the input side, bits in excess of the
field size in the last transfer are ignored.

`ace.restrict*` may not write _AlgorithmUse_ while an Algorithm uses it in this way
(<<ACE-instruction-restrict>>).

*Replaces:* the counter descriptions in the ML-KEM and ML-DSA `_*_Input_` and `_*_Output_` states.

*Status: applied.* The rule is `AGR9`, and Book 2 lost about forty lines — the largest single
reduction of any rule so far, because here the local text really was bulk: ML-KEM spent a
`process_VLI` invocation plus a nine-line paragraph justifying why `cumul_len` and not `block_base`
carries the count, and ML-DSA repeated the same argument in different words. Each site is now one
sentence of the form asked for: “the fields `encapsk`, `decapsk` and `ciphertext` are loaded using
(multi-block) `ace.exec` instructions of Form B, following rule AGR9”.

Three divergences had to be settled, and two of them resolved themselves.

ML-DSA stated the overflow behaviour on both sides — excess bits of a final input transfer ignored,
an `ace.exec` issued past the end invalidating the CR — and ML-KEM stated neither. `AGR9` adopts
ML-DSA's, which is the complete pair and matches `AGR6`'s treatment of an over-long input, so ML-KEM
gains rules it was missing rather than losing any.

ECC was at first left out of scope, then brought in at the author's suggestion by widening _W_ from
“a field of the MDH” to “a state field _W_, usually _AlgorithmUse_ of the MDH”. Subsuming it took
three further generalisations, each of which the rule now carries: the unit is bits *or* bytes, as
the state says, since ECC counts bytes in `block_base`; the `process_VLI` invocation applies only
“where the loading is performed by `process_VLI`”, since ECC's set states do not use it; and
completion admits an exception — “unless the state defines a transition of its own” — because ECC's
_Output_ transitions to _Success_ where the other states forbid a further `ace.exec`.

That brought in four more sites: ECC's set/output lead-in, its _Set_Generator_, its _Set_ states for
`Scalar`, `Hash`, `SecondPt` and `Signature`, and EdDSA's _Set_Ctx_, which had described itself by
reference to ECC's. Two now-redundant “`block_base` <- 0” steps went with them, since `AGR9` zeroes
_W_ on entry.

The widened rule is worth watching. Three “unless” clauses is close to the point at which a general
rule states less than the sites it replaces; if a fourth exception appears, splitting `AGR9` into an
MDH-counter rule and a byte-counter rule would be better than adding it.

Two smaller things fell out. ML-DSA carried the “entering an _*_Input_ or _*_Output_ state zeroes
_AlgorithmUse_” bullet twice, once in the shared statement and once in its own; `AGR9` absorbs both.
And each site's surviving sentence keeps what is genuinely local: for ML-KEM that its field sizes
come from the size table, for ML-DSA that entering _pubkey_Output_ without `HasPubKey` invalidates
the CR.

[[ACE-rule-progress]]
==== R18. Progress of a long-running operation

Where an implementation interrupts and resumes a long-running operation that transfers no data —
a scalar multiplication, a signature operation, a key generation — it records progress in the
_AlgorithmUse_ field in an implementation-defined way, not in `acestart` (<<ACE-CSR-acestart>>). It
must then export a mandatory Additional Data Section holding the Implementation Qualifier and
whatever else resumption needs, and an importing implementation that does not recognise the
Implementation Qualifier restarts the operation instead of resuming it.

*Replaces:* the restatements in the ECC and PQC sections and the general half of the statement in
Book 1.

*Status: applied.* Six statements, not the three first counted: three paragraphs in the ECC section,
one trailing bullet each in ML-KEM and ML-DSA, two sentences in ML-DSA's Internal State, and four
lines in the `acestart` CSR description. The rule is `AGR8`.

The sites did not agree. ECC required the exporting implementation to provide the Additional Data
Section and told the *importer* to compare `acemvendorid`, `acemarchid` and `acemimpid` against
`IMPQUAL`; ML-KEM and ML-DSA required the Section but said nothing about the comparison. That
comparison is now performed by the import procedure itself, so `AGR8` states the consequence — an
operation interrupted on one implementation and imported on another always restarts — instead of
telling the importer to do the work again.

ECC also gave the importer discretion to discard even on a match, which the PQC sections did not.
`AGR8` resolves this in ECC's favour: an importing implementation may always discard the Section and
restart. Discarding is never unsafe, and ECC has a reason for it that survives as the only text left
in that section — that keeping the Section also means trusting the random number generator of the
originating implementation, and that an importer unwilling to do so discards `RndNum` with it.

[[ACE-rule-zero-padding-caller]]
==== R19. Zero-filling a final partial block is the caller's obligation

*Discarded.* Two reasons, and the second disqualifies it outright.

It fails the readability test of R11 and R14: the three statements, in <<ACE-GCM-mode>>,
<<ACE-GCM-SIV-mode>> and <<ACE-Ascon-AEAD128>>, are two or three lines each, and each names its own
states and its own standard's requirement, so almost nothing would be deleted.

More fundamentally, it is not the kind of thing a `GR` or an `AGR` states. Those rules bind the
*architecture* — what the ACE unit does, what a CR transitions to, what an instruction is required
to produce. An obligation on the *caller*, such as zero-filling the last block of associated data,
belongs with the Algorithm that imposes it and nowhere else. This distinction is worth applying to
any future proposal here: if the sentence would begin “the caller must”, it is not a candidate for
the general rules.

[[ACE-rule-mdh-row]]
==== FIXED: R20. The MDH is not listed

Every Provisioning Input and every Serialized Context begins with the 128-bit MDH, and every
Internal State contains it. The field tables do not list it.

*Replaces:* the leading `| i | 128 | MDH` row of the per-Algorithm tables and the leading
“Metadata Header.” bullet of the Internal State lists.

*Status: applied by the author.* A short section on data structures common to all Algorithms now
carries the two MDH rows once, and the per-Algorithm tables keep their original lettering, starting
at Position ii. That choice matters: every position reference in Book 2 — `SCC_content_size()` from
Position ii to Position viii, `RndNum` at Position viii, the padding at Pos. x of Ascon-AEAD128 that
Ascon with set Nonce replaces, and EdDSA's Positions xi and xiii — remains valid, which renumbering
from i would have broken. I verified all of them, and that no table now begins at Position i with a
field other than the MDH.

The Metadata Header is retained in an Internal State only where a field of it carries Algorithm
state: _AlgorithmUse_ and _AuxInfo_ in ML-DSA, _AlgorithmUse_ in ML-KEM, _StateExtension_ in ECC.

Two things the pass left behind, now fixed: SHA-2 carried two `*Internal State*` bullets, the first
a one-line summary still naming the Metadata Header, and the surviving list had lost its indentation
where the removed bullet used to sit.

[[ACE-rule-skid-row]]
==== R21. The System Key Identifier alternative is not listed

*Discarded: not useful.* For the same reason as R14. The proposal was to drop the
“`k` or 64 {vbar} `key` or System Key Identifier” form from 25 table rows and let
<<ACE-rules-system-keys>> carry it. But the width of a key field is exactly what a reader consults a
field table for, and the two admissible widths are what determine the length of the PI or SCC in
that row. Sending the reader to another section for the alternative would cost more than the
repetition does.

[[ACE-rule-padding-row]]
==== R22. Padding is implicit

The Provisioning Input and the Serialized Context are each implicitly padded at the end so that
their length is a multiple of 128 bits.

*Replaces:* the trailing padding row of each field table, and the paragraphs that enumerated the
padding width and the block count for each key size.

*Status: applied.* Sixteen trailing padding rows removed, across GCM, GCM with Set IV, GCM-SIV, OCB,
CMAC, the generic hash and MAC section, HMAC, SHA-3, KMAC, Ascon-AEAD128, Ascon with set Nonce, Ascon
with Nonce Masking, EdDSA and ML-DSA, together with nine paragraphs. Book 2 lost 58 lines.

Four padding rows stayed, because they are *internal* rather than trailing and the implicit rule
does not cover them: one bit in OCB between `N_len` and `L*`, 32 bits in the generic hash section
between `block_base` and `cumul_len`, 48 bits in KMAC before `cshake_block`, and 16 bits in ECC
after `block_base`.

Two sites needed more than a deletion. Ascon with set Nonce described `budget` as replacing “the
final 32-bit Padding field (Pos. x of <<ACE-Ascon-AEAD128>>)”, a row that no longer exists; it now
says `budget` is appended and occupies exactly what the implicit padding would have, so the total
length is unchanged. And <<ACE-ECC>>, whose padding was a formula rather than a row, keeps the part
that matters — that the length is still derivable from the MDH alone, because the presence of its
four optional fields is given by _StateExtension_ — while the sentence appending the padding field
became a statement of what the implicit padding is.

*The two PQC families keep their sizes.* Their Serialized Contexts run to kilobytes, so both
<<ACE-PQC-ML-KEM>> and <<ACE-PQC-ML-DSA>> state theirs, in bytes and in 128-bit blocks and with the
MDH counted in: 2448, 3536 and 4784 bytes for the three ML-KEM subtypes, 6672, 9664 and 12496 for
the three ML-DSA ones. ML-DSA's figures had gone with its padding paragraph and were restored. No
other Algorithm states its size, since for the rest the field table is short enough to add up and
`ace.size` answers the question at run time.

[[ACE-rule-granularity-once]]
==== R36. The granularity constraint is stated once per Algorithm

An Algorithm states its granularity constraint in the `Granularity:` item of its *Parameters* list
and nowhere else. `AGR2` gives the consequence of violating it.

*Replaces:* the clause “For this Algorithm, `ACELEN` must be an integer multiple of `b`” appended to
the block-size item of six Algorithms — <<ACE-tweakable>>, <<ACE-GCM-mode>>, <<ACE-GCM-SIV-mode>>,
<<ACE-OCB-mode>>, <<ACE-Ascon-AEAD128>> and <<ACE-Ascon-Hash256>>.

Three of the six say the same thing twice within the same list, in the block-size item and again in
the `Granularity:` item two lines below. The other three — GCM-SIV, OCB and Ascon-Hash256 — have no
`Granularity:` item at all, so applying the rule means adding one to each. That is the point of the
rule rather than a cost of it: the granularity of an Algorithm is then always in the same place, and
`AGR2`, which speaks of “the granularity constraints of the Algorithm in its current state”, has
something to point at in every Algorithm.

Two smaller divergences go with it. <<ACE-tweakable>> writes its `Granularity:` item as a sentence,
“`ACELEN` must be a multiple of `b` (including `b`)”, where the others give a value; and
<<ACE-CMAC-mode>> writes “Granularity = `b`.” where every other Algorithm writes “Granularity: …”.

[[ACE-rule-shared-functions]]
==== R37. Functions shared by several Algorithms are defined once

The functions used by more than one Algorithm — the block-cipher primitives `enc_blk` and `dec_blk`
above all — are defined once, with the other material common to the Algorithm descriptions. An
*Algorithm-Specific Functions* list then holds only what is specific to its Algorithm, which is what
its title promises.

*Replaces:* six definitions of `enc_blk`, in <<ACE-ECB-mode>>, <<ACE-XEX-XTS-modes>>,
<<ACE-GCM-mode>>, <<ACE-GCM-SIV-mode>>, <<ACE-OCB-mode>> and <<ACE-CMAC-mode>>, and three of
`dec_blk`, in the first, second and fifth of those.

The nine differ only in wording — “encrypts plaintext block `p`” against “encrypts block `p`”, `K`
against `key` for the parameter — which is the argument for single-sourcing them: they carry no
information that the name does not, and a reader who has reached the third Algorithm has read the
definition twice already. `double` shows the shape the rest should take. It is defined in
<<ACE-OCB-mode>> and <<ACE-CMAC-mode>> merely refers to it, which is right for a function that two
Algorithms share but would be the R11 mistake for one that six use, since there the definition can
simply move rather than leave a pointer behind.

[[ACE-rules-unverified]]
=== Candidate Rules Requiring Per-Site Verification

The following are patterns rather than established rules. Each looks uniform but has at least one
site that may not conform, so none may be adopted before the sites are checked.

. *Entry to State _Ready_ clears the Algorithm's working state and retains its key material.*
  *Not applicable.* Which fields _Ready_ clears differs from Algorithm to Algorithm —
  <<ACE-PQC-ML-DSA>> deliberately retains `privkey`, `pubkey` and their flags, <<ACE-ECC>> makes
  retention selectable bit by bit through the auxiliary argument of a Form B `ace.setst`, and the
  symmetric Algorithms differ in which working fields they name. Gathering those lists into a table
  would cost readability exactly as R14 would: the fields cleared on entry to _Ready_ are what a
  reader of that Algorithm's _Ready_ state wants in front of them. They stay in place.

. *A transition to a state that sets an auxiliary value uses a Form B `ace.setst` when the value
  fits in a GPR and a Form C one when it does not.* Holds at most sites, but GCM uses Form B for a
  length and then Form B `ace.exec` for the value itself, and Ascon-AEAD128 uses Form B for
  `tag_len` without changing the _State_ field at all.

. *An out-of-range auxiliary argument transitions the CR to Error State _Invalid_.* The single most
  repeated statement in Book 2, 36 occurrences, and `AGR1` arguably implies all of them.
  *Discarded on the author's instruction.* The clause states the *consequence* of a condition given
  in the same sentence — “a value of zero, or larger than 127, causes the CR to transition to Error
  State _Invalid_” — and a consequence belongs with its condition. Deleting it would leave the
  reader with a condition and no outcome, and send them to `AGR1` to find one.

. *`ace.exec` in a state that does not allow the Form issued.* *Resolved, not consolidated.* I had
  recorded the three statements — in the definition of an Algorithm, in `AGR1`, and in the note of
  <<ACE-usage-input-output>> — as disagreeing on whether an *unavailable* Form raises an
  illegal-instruction exception instead of invalidating the CR. On rereading they do not: the first
  and third draw exactly that distinction and `AGR1` was simply silent on it, which is not a
  disagreement.
+
The author's ruling is that a Form the current state does not allow transitions the CR to Error
State _Invalid_, because issuing it is literally an attempt to manipulate the state of the CR, so
the CR shuts down. All three said that already. `AGR1` was given a sentence saying it in those terms
together with the availability exception, and the statement in the definition of an Algorithm, which
had restated both, keeps only its OCB example and defers to `AGR1` for the consequence. The note in
<<ACE-usage-input-output>> is unchanged: it sits where the exceptions are described, and a
consequence belongs with its condition.

What `AGR1` states of the Form case is the rule and not the reason for it: “This covers a `ace.exec`
or `ace.setst` issued in a Form that the current state does not allow”, with the clause explaining
why — that issuing it is literally an attempt to manipulate the state of the CR, so the CR shuts
down — commented out. The three sites therefore stand as intended: the consequence is in the general
rule, the definition of an Algorithm defers to it and keeps its OCB example, and the note in
<<ACE-usage-input-output>> keeps the availability case beside the exceptions it belongs with.

[[ACE-rules-not-proposed]]
=== Patterns Considered and Not Proposed

The second pass also turned up repetitions that should be left alone. They are recorded so that a
later pass need not re-derive the reasons.

. *The instruction-format sentence.* “`ace.load` is an I-type instruction”, and its seven
  counterparts, could become a column of <<ACE-instruction-properties>>. They should not: each sits
  immediately above the encoding diagram it describes, which is where a reader looking for the
  format already is, and the table is in another subsection. This is R14's case.

. *“The *Provisioning Input* contains only the MDH.”* Five occurrences, one clause each, and
  <<ACE-MDH-in-all-algorithms>> arguably implies it. Deleting it would leave the *Provisioning
  Input* item of those five Algorithms empty, and the reader would have to infer the emptiness from
  a rule elsewhere instead of reading it. This is R11's case.

. *`SCC_Encrypt` and `SCC_Decrypt`.* The two pseudocode functions of <<ACE-SCC-AEAD>> share their
  length check, their key derivation and their keystream loop. They are inverse operations, and
  pseudocode that a reader checks line by line against cite:[RFC8452] must be readable end to end;
  neither should be written as a delta on the other.

. *“In State _X_, (multi-block) `ace.exec` instructions are expected to be of Form A.”* Nine
  occurrences, with the state and the Form varying. This is per-state data, not a repeated rule, and
  `AGR4` already governs the part of it that is general.

[[ACE-rules-effect]]
=== Estimated Effect

Books 1, 2 and 3 are about 9300 lines. Applying R1–R22 removes on the order of 400 lines outright
and replaces perhaps as many again with four tables — the extension matrix already exists, and R4,
R8 and R14 add one each. The proportion is modest; the reason to do it is that each rule closes a
class of divergence rather than an instance of it.

The largest single item is R1, at about 216 lines, and it is also the safest: the blocks it removes
are already declared subordinate to the matrix that would replace them.

R23–R37 remove on the order of 150 further lines and add three tables — the `ace.mv` variants, the
three `*lcrstatus` registers, and the privilege configurations of Book 3 — each of them in the
section whose text it replaces. Their largest single items are R24, nine lines duplicated exactly
between `ace.input` and `ace.output`, and R35, seventeen bullets in four lists. R28 is the cheapest
to apply and the only pure deletion in the chapter. Four of them close a divergence rather than a
repetition and are worth applying for that alone: R31, where three descriptions state a vector
length bound and three others that need it do not; R33, where the `mstatus` and `vsstatus` texts
disagree on whether an Off field suppresses the transition to Dirty; R36, where three Algorithms
constrain `ACELEN` in a place no general rule points at; and R23, where each of two instructions
states the same 16-byte property twice in different words.
