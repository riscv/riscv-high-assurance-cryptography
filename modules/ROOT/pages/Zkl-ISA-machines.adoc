_This chapter is normative._

All values, bit slices, concatenations, and finite-field operations in this chapter follow the conventions of <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>. Specifically, the byte at the lowest address occupies the least significant bits, and the `@` operator places its left operand in the more significant bits—opposite to the `{vvert}` used by referenced standards. No Machine may deviate from these conventions.

This chapter specifies KLE instruction behavior for supported Machines. Some Machines are defined generically for primitives meeting certain properties (e.g., GCM works with AES or SM4), while others are tied to specific primitives (e.g., Ascon).

<<KLE-exec-encodings>> gives the encoding of each Machine of this specification in the MDH field _Machine_,
which has two sub-fields: _Type_ in bits [11:4] and _Mode_ in bits [3:0].
For some classes of Machines, _Mode_ specifies a subtype;
other groups of Machines may subdivide _Machine_ differently.

== Definition of a Machine in KLE

The following information fully defines a Machine:

Parameters::
+
--
These include:

* the _block size_ `b`, the bit length of the basic unit of information that the Machine processes, usually the block size of its main underlying primitive;
* the _key size_ `k`, also in bits, for Machines that hold a key;
* the _granularity_, usually equal to `b`. “Last blocks”, such as those of the AD or of the plaintext or ciphertext, may be shorter than `b`. Where the length of such a block is set explicitly, a longer input that carries it is truncated: its excess most significant bits are ignored; and
* any other parameter needed to instantiate the Machine with compatible primitives.
--

Data Structures::
+
--
These include:

. *Provisioning Input*:
its fields, in the order in which they appear in the PI,
each with its length.
The PI is implicitly zero-padded at the end to a multiple of 128 bits; provisioning ignores the padding bits.
+
Every PI starts with the MDH, at position i.
The *Provisioning Input* tables of the Machines omit it and start at position ii:
+
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|   i  | 128           | MDH
|===

. *Internal State*:
the information kept in the internal state of a CC, which is needed to define the Machine and its semantics.
It must exist internally, but its representation and format are not prescribed.
The Internal State always contains the MDH.
A key field of the Internal State holds the key itself, even when the PI or the SCC carries a System Key Identifier in its place (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-system-keys>>).

. *Serialized Content*:
the plaintext of the `Content1` of the SCC:
its fields, in the order in which they appear in an SCC, each with its length.
It is implicitly zero-padded at the end to a multiple of 128 bits,
and does not include the MDH.
--

Machine-Specific Functions::
Any other function that is specific to the Machine.

State Machine::
+
--
The state machine is defined by:

. the list of the *States* that the Machine may assume, each with its representation as <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-State-field>> specifies;
. the complete list of *allowed state transitions*, with the prerequisites of each, the native `kl.setst` Form and the meaning of any auxiliary parameter to it, and any other condition that triggers a transition without a `kl.setst`; and
. the *allowed* or *expected* instructions in each State, and their semantics.
  Besides configuration and export sequences, a State typically permits only certain Forms of `kl.exec`, `kl.setst` and `kl.derive`.
With `Zklio` and the required KLIOBUF setup, a substitution may replace any native Form, however the Machine words it
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-usage-input-output>>, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-form-mismatch>>):
a Form D `kl.exec` replaces a Form A, B or C `kl.exec`, and a Form A `kl.setst` replaces a Form C `kl.setst`.
Other Forms are prohibited.
`kl.input` and `kl.output` access no locker, so the state machine does not restrict them.
Rule <<KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>> in <<KLE-Machine-rules>> states the behavior precisely.
--

[[KLE-algo-notation]]
=== Notation in the Machine Descriptions

The following nomenclature is specific to the Machine specifications:

* `INPUT` is the input data of `kl.exec` or `kl.setst`: the input vector register,
  or the KLIOBUF (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-usage-input-output>>).

* `OUTPUT` is the output data of `kl.exec`: the output
  vector register, or the KLIOBUF (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-usage-input-output>>).

* `KLLEN` is the operand length in bits: `vl` {times} `SEW` for a vector operand, and
  `kliobuftop` {times} 8 for a KLIOBUF operand. `kliobuftop`, like every
  other KLIOBUF CSR and like `klstart`, is a number of bytes.
  For a KLIOBUF operand, `kliobuftop` plays the role of `VL`, and the transfer
  window is bytes [0, `kliobuftop`-1] (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-kliobuftop>>).
  For either operand of `kl.exec`, `klstart` plays the role of `vstart`: it is the byte
  position within the transfer window at which processing starts or resumes
  (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-klstart>>).
  `kl.setst` does not honor `klstart`: a native Form C one reads its whole operand, and a substituted one reads bytes
  [0, `kliobuftop`-1] (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-usage-input-output>>).

[[KLE-Machine-rules]]
== General Rules for Machines

Besides the General Rules (GR) of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-unpriv>>, in particular those governing the _State_ field and its transitions (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-State-management>>), the following Machine Rules (*MR*) apply to all Machines, unless stated otherwise.

[horizontal]
[[KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions,MR1]]MR1:: A usage instruction, or a Form of it, that may modify the state of a locker and that the current State does
not allow transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_. It raises no illegal-instruction exception: only the grounds
of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-illegal-instruction-grounds>> do, such as a reserved encoding or an unimplemented extension.
+
`kl.clearads` is allowed in every Valid State (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>); a `kl.setst` naming _Success_ or
_Failure_ in none (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>).
A wrong Form, including a substitution that the State does not admit or that is issued without `Zklio`, is not allowed
(Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-substitution-needs-zklio>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-form-mismatch>>).
A `kl.setst` naming _Unconfigured_ (`kl.clear`) or an Error State, and `kl.clearall`, are not usage instructions
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-properties>>), so no State restricts them.

[[KLE-MGR-granularity,MR2]]MR2:: If `KLLEN` violates the granularity constraints of the Machine in its current State, the instruction performs no operation and the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_, with its output window zeroed
(Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zero-unwritten-output>>).
The granularity constrains the transfers of whole blocks, for instance to a whole multiple of the block size.
In a State that processes a final block or piece of `last_block_len` bits, the constraint is `KLLEN` {ge} `last_block_len`.
A fixed-width input follows Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>, and the Machine defines the constraint on a tag that it admits truncated.

[[KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block,MR3]]MR3:: _Single- and Multi-block `kl.exec`._
In a State that processes whole blocks of `b` bits, one `kl.exec` applies the per-block operation of the State
`KLLEN`/`b` times, for `i` = 0, `b`, `2b`, {ellipsis}, `KLLEN` − `b` in that order, consuming `INPUT[i+b-1:i]` if the State
takes an input and producing `OUTPUT[i+b-1:i]` if it produces an output. Blocks are thus taken and produced from the least
significant position upwards, here and throughout this specification.
A resumed `kl.exec` performs only the iterations from `i` = 8 {interpunct} `klstart` onwards (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-klstart>>).
+
A State that the Machine describes as processing a single block, such as a last-block, tag-finalize or verify State, processes
exactly one block whatever `KLLEN` is, unless its transfer window is invalid (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>) or empty (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transfer-window-checks>>).
Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>> governs a further `kl.exec` in a last-block State.
The `last_block_state` of <<KLE-process-VLI>> instead processes exactly its final piece, which may first complete the current block.
The only interruption point of a `kl.exec` in a State that processes a single block or a final piece is 0
(Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-interruption-point>>).

[[KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields,MR4]]MR4:: A field that a Machine can derive inexpensively
from other fields of the same CC may be omitted from the Serialized Content.
It is then recomputed when an import completes, by the `kl.mgmt` with `#kl_cfg_management_end`, whatever the format,
a system-specific one (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-sysfmt-completion>>) included.
It is also recomputed whenever a field it depends on is modified, for instance when `kl.derive` overwrites a key field,
or when an explicit operation of the Machine regenerates a field it depends on.
A Machine states only the derivation itself, and marks such a field “(derived)” in its *Internal State*.

[[KLE-MGR-atomic-randomness,MR5]]MR5:: _Randomness:_
+
--
* A Machine operation that needs random bits from an RBG or DRBG, such as the generation of a nonce, an ephemeral scalar or a signature into the locker, is an instruction of its own.
  That instruction works only on the locker and on scalar GPR operands captured at issue, transfers no data to or from memory, vector registers, the KLIOBUF or another locker, and may be long-running (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-no-data>>).
  If the RBG or DRBG cannot supply the bits, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>> applies.
* An instruction that transfers data never waits or fails for lack of random bits, even when resumed, restored or restarted; further randomness that its Machine needs comes from another instruction.
* Randomness that the implementation needs, such as masks, is supplied in advance, without weakening the required protection.
* An internal algorithmic random value, such as a signing nonce or an ephemeral scalar, serves only the one instance of the scheme that requires it, and no other instance or purpose.
  It is never reused, not even when an interrupted operation restarts (Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>).
  Resuming copies of one interrupted operation, restored from one image, completes that one instance on the same inputs and is not a reuse.
--

[[KLE-MGR-progress-discard,MR6]]MR6:: _Implementation-defined interruption of long-running operations._
A State in which a `kl.exec` or `kl.derive` may be interrupted under Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-preemption>> admits only that one instruction and Form, besides the `kl.setst` instructions that its Machine allows in the State, `kl.clearads` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>) and configuration instructions.
The following sub-rules govern interruption:

. Each such Machine and State designates one progress field _P_.
_P_ is non-zero from the first precise halt of the operation until its completion, and zero whenever no such operation is interrupted.
. The encoding of the progress within _P_ is implementation-defined.
. If _P_ is non-zero, the instruction issued in such a State either resumes the interrupted operation or restarts it; a restart first resets _P_ to zero internally.
. Every random value and every intermediate value generated for the operation (the “material”), kept in a state field or in the ADS, is meaningful for resumption only if _P_ is non-zero.
. Any material *must* be regenerated if the operation is restarted instead of resumed.
. If _P_ is non-zero, _P_ is zeroed and the material destroyed by:
** every transition of the locker's _State_ other than the moves of a management operation into and out of a Configuration State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-management-no-state-operations>>), including a same-State `kl.setst` and a transition to _Ready_ or to an Error State;
** `kl.clearads`, which leaves _State_ unchanged; and
** an import that completes without `Content2`, whatever the reason (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-restart>>).
. A `kl.clone` restarts the operation in its destination (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-restart>>): the copy's _P_ is zeroed and its material destroyed, while the source keeps both.
. _P_ is not cleared, and the material is not destroyed, by an interrupt; an export, or an import that completes with `Content2`, clears and destroys them only where Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-restart>> lets it restart the operation.
. When the operation completes, _P_ is set to zero; the algorithm of the Machine determines whether the material is kept or destroyed.
. A long-running `kl.setst` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-no-data>>) is not resumable: on preemption it is restarted
under option 5 of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-resumability-options>>, keeping no _P_ and no material, and its random values
are drawn again (Rule <<KLE-MGR-atomic-randomness>>).

[[KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input,MR7]]MR7:: Where a State consumes from `INPUT` a single value of fixed width, that of the field it assigns or of the
operand it is defined over, the width need not be the block size `b`.
If `KLLEN` exceeds that width, only that many least significant bits of `INPUT` are used, and the rest are ignored.
A smaller `KLLEN` violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

[[KLE-MGR-clear-output,MR8]]MR8:: A State writes `OUTPUT` only within its transfer window, bits [8 {times} `klstart`, `KLLEN`).
An output longer than `KLLEN` is truncated to its `KLLEN` least significant bits, unless it is meant to be emitted over
several `kl.exec` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>); a `KLLEN` below a minimum that the Machine sets violates the
granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).
When a State writes fewer bits than the window holds, it clears the remaining bits of the window.
This concerns the bits *within* the operand; the tail-agnostic policy of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLV>>
governs the elements beyond `VL`.

[[KLE-MGR-load-long-field,MR9]]MR9:: _Long fields transferred by several `kl.exec`._
A State _X_ that loads a long field from `INPUT`, or emits one to `OUTPUT`, over several `kl.exec` names a state field _W_, such as _MachineUse_ of the MDH, and the unit of _W_, bits or bytes.
A digest or XOF output that a State computes as it emits it is not a long field under this Rule: the Machine defines its progress and the outcome of an excess `KLLEN`, as in <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>> and <<KLE-Ascon-Hash256>>.
_W_ records the amount transferred so far:

. *Entry.* Entering _X_ zeroes _W_; entering a loading State also zeroes the field, so that reloading replaces it.
. *Progress.* _W_ rises monotonically by the amount of data actually transferred, in its unit: by `KLLEN`, or `KLLEN`/8, for a `kl.exec` that completes, at most up to the size of the field, and by the committed prefix for one that halts (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-precise-halt>>), so that its resumption adds only the remainder.
. *Completion.* The transfer is complete once _W_ reaches the size of the field; _W_ is not reset then, but a transition that the Machine makes at that point zeroes it.
  Unless the Machine defines another transition at that point, an emitting State then transitions to _Success_,
  and a loading State admits no further `kl.exec` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>>).
. *Excess.* Excess data in the final loading transfer is ignored; an emitting `kl.exec` that would carry _W_ past the field size transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
. *Incomplete load.* A `kl.setst` that leaves a loading State _X_ before the transfer is complete leaves the field unconfigured: the field, and any length of it that the Machine set on entering _X_, are zeroed, and the flag, if any, that records that the field holds a value stays clear.
  A same-State `kl.setst`, which restarts the load (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>), and `kl.clearads`, which leaves the _State_ unchanged, do not leave _X_.
  A Machine whose next State needs the field states this as an entry condition (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
. *Flags.* A Machine may keep in _StateExtension_ a flag per loaded field, recording that the field holds a configured value, such as the `Has` flags of the ECC, ML-KEM and ML-DSA Machines and the `HasKey` flag of HMAC.
  Unless the Machine states otherwise, such a flag is cleared on entering the loading State of its field, and set when the load completes and passes the checks of Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>, or when an operation of the Machine computes the field; `kl.getstx` reads it.
  Each Machine lists its flags, their bit positions, and any departure from this pattern.
. *`PROCESS_VLI`.* A load performed by `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>) invokes it with `max_len` = the size of the field and `cumul_len` = _W_.
  A field stored as loaded is passed as `block` = `state`, with `b` = `n` = its size, `mode` = `assign`, and `process_block` = `finalize` = `None`.
  A field processed as it is loaded, such as the IV of <<KLE-GCM-mode>>, is passed with the functions and `mode` that its Machine states.
. *`kl.restrict*`.* Where _W_ is a field of the MDH, `kl.restrict*` cannot write it (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>>).

[[KLE-MGR-single-last-block,MR10]]MR10:: _Single final block._
A State that processes a single final block, such as _Enc_Last_Block_, _Dec_Last_Block_ or _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, admits at most one `kl.exec` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>).
Unless the Machine defines a transition of its own on its completion, the locker stays in the State.
A further `kl.exec` then transitions it to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>>), with its output window zeroed (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zero-unwritten-output>>).
An empty final input needs no `kl.exec`, as for an empty final block of the AD of OCB (<<KLE-OCB-mode>>; Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transfer-window-checks>>).
+
NOTE: A second final block is not a continuation of the input but an attempt to manipulate the internal state of the Machine.

[[KLE-MGR-policy-gate,MR11]]MR11:: _Policy gate and entry conditions._
+
--
Each Machine states, for each of its States:

* the bit of _MachinePolicy_, if any, that enables the operation of the State (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Machine-field>>):
** _MachinePolicy_[0] where the Machine says “if encryption is allowed”, and for signature or MAC generation;
** _MachinePolicy_[1] where it says “if decryption is allowed”, and for signature or MAC verification;
** each bit gates the States of its operation and every State that follows uniquely from them:
*** encryption: _Encrypt_, _Enc_Last_Block_, _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ and, where it emits the tag of the encryption, _Hash_Output_;
*** decryption: _Decrypt_, _Dec_Last_Block_, _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ and, in <<KLE-GCM-mode>> and <<KLE-OCB-mode>>, _Hash_Verify_;
*** signature generation: _Sign_Generate_, _Output_ with a signature, and _Sign_Output_;
*** signature verification: _Sign_Input_ and _Sign_Verify_; and
* its _entry conditions_: what the State requires, such as the `Has` flags, the mode, and the other configuration of the CC that the State uses.

A `kl.setst`, `kl.exec` or `kl.derive` is checked at two moments:

. *At issue, before it acts, against _MachinePolicy_.* The locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_, and the
  instruction performs no operation, if:
.. the instruction is issued in a State whose operation _MachinePolicy_ disables, or from which every operation that
   the Machine can complete is disabled (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>); a State that
   still leads to an enabled operation is not affected; or
.. the instruction would transition the locker into a State whose operation _MachinePolicy_ disables, whether it names
   that State (`kl.setst`) or the Machine makes the transition on its completion (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
. *After it acts, against the entry conditions of the State that it transitions into.* They are checked after the
  operations of leaving the current State, against the content that these operations and the instruction itself
  produce, such as the last part of the IV that the completing `kl.exec` of <<KLE-GCM-mode>> writes.
  If they do not hold, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ instead of entering that State.

An instruction executed in the State checks a condition only if the condition can change while the locker is in the State, or if the check is one of Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-read>>.
The Machine states such a check with the instruction.
A failed check also transitions the locker to _Invalid_, unless it detects a data error (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>).
--

[[KLE-MGR-config-data-error,MR12]]MR12:: _Configuration errors and data errors._
Each Machine states which of its checks detect which of these two kinds of error:

* A _configuration error_ transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_, so the CC cannot be used until it is reconfigured. It is either:
** a private key, or another field that sets a parameter or a key of the CC (not the data of an operation), that the referenced standard rejects, such as a malformed private key; or
** a value in any field, a peer point or a signature included, whose encoding violates a constraint of its representation that is checked when the field is written, such as the zero most significant bits of secp521r1 (<<KLE-ECC>>); a constraint that the referenced standard checks only when the operation decodes the value, such as the hint encoding of an ML-DSA `signature` (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 21), is checked by that operation and detects a data error.
* A _data error_ transitions the state machine to _Failure_, which is a Valid State. It is either:
** a ciphertext, tag, signature, message representative or public key that is otherwise malformed or does not verify, such as an ML-KEM encapsulation key that fails its check; or
** a point that an operation uses (a peer's point, a public key or a generator) that is not a valid point of the curve.

[[KLE-MGR-check-on-read,MR13]]MR13:: _Checks when a field is read._
An operation that reads a field performs, before it changes any state, the checks that depend on the operation or on more than one field, such as the validity of a point in the role the operation gives it, the range of `Scalar` against the group order, or the consistency of a public key with a private key; Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>> classifies a failure.
A check that depends only on the field is made when the field is written (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>), and need not be repeated when it is read, since the KLF guarantees the integrity of the locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>).
A Machine may nevertheless repeat such a check on a value that an interrupted operation resumes with, as secp521r1 does (<<KLE-ECC>>); a repeated check that fails transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
A corruption of the KLF is a KLE fatal error (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>).

[[KLE-MGR-check-on-write,MR14]]MR14:: _Checks when a field is written._
A check that a Machine attaches to a field and that depends on that field alone is performed whenever the field is written: at the completion of its load (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>), of a `kl.derive` into it, of the provisioning that carries it, and of an import that restores it.
Examples are the zero most significant bits of secp521r1, the well-formedness of an ML-DSA `privkey`, and the input checks of cite:[nist-fips-203] on an ML-KEM key.
Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>> classifies a failure.
The validity of a curve point is not checked when the point is written, since it depends on the role that an operation gives the point: it is checked when the point is read (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-read>>).

[[KLE-MGR-tag-verify,MR15]]MR15:: _Verification of a tag._
A Machine that verifies a tag or a MAC without disclosing the value it computes performs the verification in constant time.
It then transitions the state machine to _Success_ if the verification succeeds, and to _Failure_ otherwise (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>).
The instruction that provides the tag or MAC to verify performs the verification. This instruction is:

* by default, a Form C `kl.setst` naming _Hash_Verify_, whose auxiliary operand carries the expected value;
* where a `kl.setst` naming _Hash_Verify_ sets the number of bits to compare instead, as for CMAC (<<KLE-CMAC-mode>>), or where the Machine enters _Hash_Verify_ by itself, as OCB and Ascon-AEAD128 do (<<KLE-OCB-mode>>, <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>), a single Form B `kl.exec` in _Hash_Verify_, which supplies the expected value;
* where the expected value is supplied first, as the SIV of GCM-SIV is (<<KLE-GCM-SIV-mode>>), the `kl.exec` that completes the internal tag computation.

[[KLE-truncation-vs-length]]
== Input and Output Padding, Truncation, Byte Granularity

Unless the Machine specifies otherwise and pads internally, the caller must pad an input whose length the granularity of the State does not admit to a `KLLEN` that the Machine admits, and pass its actual length to the Machine, typically through `kl.setst`.

Cryptographic inputs are normally supplied through vector registers or the KLIOBUF, in whole bytes. For a standard that permits arbitrary bit lengths, the caller specifies the bit length of the final input piece, usually as a `kl.setst` parameter, and the Machine truncates that input to it. The Machine clears the output bits beyond the final piece, also within its last byte (Rule <<KLE-MGR-clear-output>>).

This occurs, for instance, with the `last_block_len` parameters in <<KLE-GCM-mode>>, <<KLE-GCM-SIV-mode>>, <<KLE-OCB-mode>>, <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>, and <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>. Such a parameter gives the length to which the Machine truncates the passed input, not the length `KLLEN` of that input. Macro `PROCESS_VLI` receives field lengths in bits, but `kl.exec` always transfers whole bytes, and the last input may be truncated internally.

Where a standard's formula requires the bit count of data that the Machine receives in whole bytes, it is the byte count multiplied by 8, as with the length block of GHASH in <<KLE-GCM-mode>>.

[horizontal]
[[KLE-MGR-last-blk-len,MR16]]MR16::
+
--
A `last_block_len`, or any other parameter giving the bit length of a final input piece, is a multiple of 8, unless the Machine either:

* states that its referenced standard orders the bits within a byte as <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>> does, so that a truncation of the last byte to its least significant bits is the bit string that the standard defines (as for <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>); or
* defines which bits of the partial final byte carry the piece (as for the nonce of <<KLE-OCB-mode>>).

A value that the Machine does not admit transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
Whether zero is admissible depends on the Machine.
--

[[KLE-MGR-caller-limits,MR20]]MR20:: A Machine enforces only the per-message limits that its correctness needs at practical lengths, such as the

+

* the length of a message is limited by the referenced standard, such as the 2^20^ blocks of a data unit of

[[KLE-nonce-reuse,MR21]]MR21:: Keystream and nonce discipline is the caller's, as in the referenced standards: no Machine prevents the reuse of

or the Machine lists that `kl.setst`.

[[KLE-ECB-mode]]
=== Electronic Codebook Modes (Direct Encryption/Decryption)

ECB mode cite:[nist-SP-800-38A-1] is the simplest way to use a block cipher.
(((KLE, Machine, ECB)))

Parameters::

* `b` = bit length of a block (8|`b`), with `b` {ge} 64.
* `k` = bit length of a key (8|`k`).
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::
* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64      | Key or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key`: `k` bits.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  i    | `k` or 64      | Key or System Key Identifier
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::
* `enc_blk(K,p) -> c` encrypts plaintext block `p` using the key `K`, and
  returns the ciphertext result `c`.
* `dec_blk(K,c) -> p` decrypts ciphertext block `c` using the key `K`, and
  returns the plaintext result `p`.

State Machine::

* *States:*
 ** _Ready_: The CC is ready.
 ** _Encrypt_: Encrypting blocks of input.
 ** _Decrypt_: Decrypting blocks of input.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ or _Decrypt_ -> _Encrypt_, if encryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Ready_ or _Encrypt_ -> _Decrypt_, if decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** From _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_ to _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** The `kl.setst` into _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_ is of Form A: it takes no auxiliary operand.
 ** In _Encrypt_ and _Decrypt_, (multi-block) Form A `kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT` instructions are expected.
    For each block, `OUTPUT <- enc_blk(key, INPUT)` in _Encrypt_, and `OUTPUT <- dec_blk(key, INPUT)` in _Decrypt_.

[[KLE-keystream-modes]]
=== CTR/XCTR Keystream Generation

In CTR mode, the IV and the counter are concatenated to form the input block of the underlying keyed PRP or PRF, so their lengths sum to its block size.
(((KLE, Machine, keystream generation)))

In XCTR mode, the IV has the block size, and its XOR with the counter, zero-extended to the block size, forms the input block.

Parameters::
* `b` = block size (8|`b`), with `b` {ge} 64.
* `k` = key size (8|`k`).
* `j` = counter size: 32 in CTR mode, 64 in XCTR mode.
* `n` = IV size (8|`n`).
* In CTR mode, `b = n + j`; in XCTR mode, `b = n`.
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::
* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key`: `k` bits.
 ** `IV`: `n` bits.
 ** `ctr`: `j` bits.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|    i  | `k` or 64       | `key` or System Key Identifier
|   ii  | `n`             | `IV`
|  iii  | `j`             | `ctr`
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::
* `keystream_block(p) -> c`: `enc_blk(key, p)` (<<KLE-ECB-mode>>), the encryption of the counter block `p` under `key`.
* `tick_ctr()`: increases `ctr` by one, modulo 2^`j`^.

State Machine::
* *States:*
  ** _Ready_: The CC is ready.
  ** _Operate_: The CC produces a keystream.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Operate_, if encryption or decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Operate_ -> _Ready_.

* *Behavior:*
 ** When entering _Ready_, `IV` {leftarrow} 0, and `ctr` {leftarrow} 0 in CTR mode and 1 in XCTR mode
    (as in the XCTR of HCTR2 cite:[cryptoeprint-2021-1441]).

 ** When entering _Operate_, the Form C ``kl.setst Kn, \#kl_state_operate, INPUT``
    sets `IV` {leftarrow} `INPUT`, with `INPUT` truncated to its `n` least significant bits
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>), and leaves `ctr` unchanged.
    The Machine allows a same-State `kl.setst` into _Operate_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>), which
    does the same: it replaces `IV` and keeps `ctr`.

 ** In State _Operate_, (multi-block) Form C ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected.
    For each block, "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn```" performs:
+
--
  . In CTR mode, `tmp <- keystream_block(bswap(ctr) @ IV)`;
    in XCTR mode, `tmp <- keystream_block(IV xor (zeros(b - j) @ ctr))`.
  . `tick_ctr()`.
  . `OUTPUT <- tmp`.
--
+
As a `kl.derive` source (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>), _Operate_ supplies the keystream as Form C `kl.exec` instructions of
whole blocks would; the unused bytes of the last block are discarded.

 ** In States _Ready_ and _Operate_, the Form B
    ``kl.setst Kn, \#kl_state_set_aux_value, Xs`` sets the counter:
    `ctr` {leftarrow} `bin(Xs, j)`. It is an operation, not a State
    (Rule <<KLE-operation-immediates>>).

NOTE: Setting the counter gives random access into a keystream, for example to resume an interrupted stream.
No check detects `ctr` wrapping around (Rule <<KLE-nonce-reuse>>).

Example pseudocode is given in <<Zkl-pseudocode.adoc#KLE-pseudocode-keystream-modes>> (for the case `b` = `KLLEN` = 128).

[[KLE-XEX-XTS-modes]]
=== XEX/XTS modes

The XTS mode was designed for storage confidentiality cite:[DBLP-journals-ieeesp-Martin10], and NIST made it a Recommendation in 2010 cite:[nist-SP-800-38E].
XTS builds on the XEX construction of Phillip Rogaway cite:[DBLP-conf-asiacrypt-Rogaway04], which turns a block cipher into a _Tweakable Block Cipher_ (TBC) cite:[DBLP-conf-crypto-LiskovRW02].
A TBC takes a tweak besides the key and the plaintext or ciphertext.
Key and tweak together select the permutation that the cipher computes.

The two-key form of the XEX construction that cite:[nist-SP-800-38E] uses builds a TBC `Ẽ` from a block cipher `E`,
with keys `K1` and `K2`, tweak `T`, and plaintext `P`, as follows:

`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `Ẽ(K1, K2, T, P) = E(K1, P xor M) xor M` `{nbsp}{nbsp}` where `{nbsp}{nbsp}` `M = E(K2, T)` `{nbsp}{nbsp}` is called a _mask_.

The single-key XEX of cite:[DBLP-conf-asiacrypt-Rogaway04] starts from the mask `x {interpunct} E(K, T)` instead, and is not architected.

In XEX mode, the mask is computed once per tweak, by the `kl.setst` that sets the tweak.
Each further block updates it by a multiplication in GF(2^b^), instead of encrypting a new tweak.

KLE uses the two-key form: `key1` is `K1`, which encrypts the data, and `key2` is `K2`, which encrypts the tweak.
(((KLE, Machine, XEX)))
The Machine cannot check that the two keys are independent: the provisioner must supply two independently generated keys,
and a System Key Identifier must resolve to two independently generated keys in the SKS.

Parameters::

* `b` = 128 = block size.
* `k` = key size (8|`k`).
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)     | Field
|  ii   | `k` or 64       | `key1` or System Key Identifier
| iii   | `k` or 0        | `key2`, or empty if _KeyType_ = 1 (a single SKID retrieves both keys, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-single-skid>>)
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key1`: `k` bits.
 ** `key2`: `k` bits. `key2` = `key1` is a configuration error (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>), checked when either key is written (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
 ** `mask`: `b` bits.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  i  | `k` or 64     | `key1` or System Key Identifier
| ii  | `k` or 0      | `key2`, or empty if _KeyType_ = 1 (a single SKID retrieves both keys, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-single-skid>>)
| iii  | `b`           | `mask`
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `enc_blk(K, p) -> c` encrypts block `p` using the key `K`, and returns the result `c`.
* `dec_blk(K, c) -> p` decrypts block `c` using the key `K`, and returns the result `p`.
* `update_mask(mask) -> mask` updates the mask by multiplication with a generator
of GF(2^128^): multiplication by `x` modulo `x^128^ + x^7^ + x^2^ + x + 1`, where `V[i]` is the coefficient of `x^i^`,
as in cite:[nist-SP-800-38E] and cite:[IEEE-1619-2018] {sect}5.2. That is,
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `update_mask(V)` = `V << 1` `{fournbsp}` if `V[127]` = `0`
+
and
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `update_mask(V)` = `(V << 1) xor (zeros(120) @ 0b10000111)`
`{nbsp}{nbsp}` otherwise.

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Encrypt_, and _Decrypt_ (defined as before, cf. <<KLE-ECB-mode>>).

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ or _Decrypt_ -> _Encrypt_, if encryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Ready_ or _Encrypt_ -> _Decrypt_, if decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Encrypt_, _Decrypt_ -> _Ready_.

* *Behavior:*
 ** When entering _Ready_, `mask` {leftarrow} 0.
 ** When entering _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_, the Form C ``kl.setst`` takes the tweak as `INPUT`
    and computes the initial mask:
  . `mask <- INPUT`, with `INPUT` truncated to its `b` least significant bits
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>);
  . `mask <- enc_blk(key2, mask)`;
  . a same-State `kl.setst`, which the Machine allows (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>), or one from the other of the two States,
    repeats these steps with a new tweak.

 ** In States _Encrypt_ and _Decrypt_, (multi-block) Form A ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected.
    For each block, "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" performs:
+
--
  . In State _Encrypt_, `OUTPUT <- mask xor enc_blk(key1, INPUT xor mask)`;
    in State _Decrypt_, `OUTPUT <- mask xor dec_blk(key1, INPUT xor mask)`.
  . `mask <- update_mask(mask)`.
--
//

Encryption pseudocode is provided in <<Zkl-pseudocode.adoc#KLE-pseudocode-basic-IV-mode>>;
decryption is similar.


[[KLE-XTS-from-XEX]]
==== Procedure: XTS and Ciphertext Stealing from an XEX Context

cite:[nist-SP-800-38E] adds two things to the XEX construction above: the tweak is the
data unit sequence number, and a data unit whose length is not a multiple of `b` is
completed by ciphertext stealing.
Software obtains both from the XEX CC, with no further architectural support, as this
subsection describes.
A data unit has at most 2^20^ blocks (cite:[nist-SP-800-38E]); the caller keeps this limit, which the Machine does not
count (Rule <<KLE-MGR-caller-limits>>).

*The tweak.* The `INPUT` of the `kl.setst` into _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_ is `bin(i, b)`
(<<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>): the data unit sequence number `i` as a `b`-bit
little-endian value.

*Ciphertext stealing.* Let the data unit be `m` {ge} 1 full blocks followed by a final partial
block of `s` bits, with `0` < `s` < `b` and `s` a multiple of 8, and number the blocks
`P~0~` {ellipsis} `P~m-1~`, `P~m~`.
The CC is at _mask index_ `j` when `mask` is `update_mask` applied `j` times to `enc_blk(key2, T)`, `T` being the tweak.
Each block uses the current `mask` and then advances it, blocks being processed in order
(Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>), so the `j`-th block processed after the tweak was set, counting
from 0, is processed at mask index `j`.

Encryption needs no reordering, because SP 800-38E consumes the mask indices in their
natural order:

  . Issue `kl.exec` for `P~0~` {ellipsis} `P~m-2~`, obtaining `C~0~` {ellipsis} `C~m-2~`.
  . Issue `kl.exec` for `P~m-1~`, obtaining `CC` at mask index `m-1`.
  . `C~m~` {leftarrow} `CC[s-1:0]` and `CP` {leftarrow} `CC[b-1:s]`.
  . Issue `kl.exec` for `CP @ P~m~`, obtaining `C~m-1~` at mask index `m`.
  . The ciphertext is `C~0~` {ellipsis} `C~m-2~`, `C~m-1~`, `C~m~`, in that order, with
    `C~m~` the final `s` bits.

Decryption consumes mask index `m` before mask index `m-1`, and a mask only ever advances.
The two are obtained from one context by cloning it once, immediately before the last two blocks:

  . Issue `kl.exec` for `C~0~` {ellipsis} `C~m-2~`, obtaining `P~0~` {ellipsis} `P~m-2~`.
    The `mask` of the locker is now at index `m-1`.
  . `kl.clone` the locker into a second locker, which is therefore also at mask index `m-1`.
  . On the clone, issue one `kl.exec` on any single block (`KLLEN` = `b`), and discard its output.
    This consumes mask index `m-1` and leaves the clone at index `m`.
  . On the clone, issue `kl.exec` for `C~m-1~`, obtaining `PP` at mask index `m`.
  . `P~m~` {leftarrow} `PP[s-1:0]` and `CP` {leftarrow} `PP[b-1:s]`.
  . On the original locker, still at mask index `m-1`, issue `kl.exec` for `CP @ C~m~`,
    obtaining `P~m-1~`.
  . Clear the clone.

With stealing, decryption costs one `kl.clone` and one discarded block operation per
data unit, whatever its length. `CP @ P~m~` and `CP @ C~m~` place the
partial block in the least significant bits, where the first `s`/8 bytes of the
string live under <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>.

A data unit of whole blocks needs no stealing: both directions are the plain XEX sequence over
its blocks.

[[KLE-tweakable]]
=== Generic Tweakable Block Ciphers

The specification of a generic tweakable block cipher resembles that of a block cipher in ECB mode.
Since `kl.exec` takes a single input, a separate instruction sets the tweak.
That instruction truncates its input to the `t` bits of the tweak (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>), so a tweak shorter than the block needs no change of vector length.
A tweak longer than the block may need the vector length reconfigured between setting the tweak and processing blocks.

Some commonly used TBCs are `Deoxys` cite:[Deoxys-141], `Skinny` cite:[DBLP-conf-crypto-BeierleJKL0PSSS16], `QARMA` cite:[DBLP-journals-tosc-Avanzi17], and `QARMAv2` cite:[DBLP-journals-tosc-AvanziBDEGNR23].

Parameters::

* `b` = block size (8|`b`), with `b` {ge} 64.
* `k` = key size (8|`k`).
* `t` = tweak size (8|`t`).
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key`: `k` bits.
 ** `tweak`: `t` bits.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|   i  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|  ii  | `t`           | `tweak`
|===
--

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Encrypt_, and _Decrypt_ (cf. <<KLE-ECB-mode>>).
Setting the tweak is an operation, not a State (Rule <<KLE-operation-immediates>>).

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ or _Decrypt_ -> _Encrypt_, if encryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Ready_ or _Encrypt_ -> _Decrypt_, if decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Encrypt_, _Decrypt_ -> _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** When entering _Ready_, the `tweak` field is cleared.
 ** The `kl.setst` into _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_ is of Form A.
 ** In States _Ready_, _Encrypt_ and _Decrypt_, the set-tweak operation, the Form C
    `kl.setst Kn, \#kl_state_set_aux_value, INPUT`, performs `tweak <- INPUT`, with `INPUT` truncated
    to its `t` least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
 ** In _Encrypt_ and _Decrypt_, (multi-block) Form A `kl.exec` instructions are expected.
    For each block, "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" encrypts, resp. decrypts, `INPUT` into `OUTPUT` under `key` and `tweak`.
    The Machine never updates `tweak` by itself: every block uses the same `tweak` until the next set-tweak operation.

[[KLE-process-VLI]]
=== Macro: Processing Variable Length Inputs into a Fixed Length Output

Many Machines process variable-length inputs into fixed-length outputs, for instance to hash, or to derive an internal fixed-length IV from a variable-length external one. They do so in specific States, such as _Hash_Absorb_.
This section describes the semantics of such a State, called _Current_State_ here.
`PROCESS_VLI` is a macro, not a subroutine: an invocation splices the behavior below into the State Machine of the
calling Machine, with each parameter replaced by the variable or function that it names.
The macro defines how the `kl.exec` instructions of _Current_State_ and `last_block_state` consume their `INPUT`.
A `kl.setst` passes it no data: on entering _Current_State_ it may set `max_len` (Form B), and on entering
`last_block_state` it may set `last_block_len`. Any other operand of a `kl.setst` is defined by the invoking Machine.

The macro follows the granularity of the calling Machine, which can simplify the inner loop and the computation of `amount`.

A Machine invokes it as:

```
PROCESS_VLI(max_len,
            block,           b,               state,           n,
            input_base,      block_base,      state_offset,    cumul_len,
            process_block(), finalize(),      mode,            granularity,
            last_block_state, process_last_block(),            last_block_len).
```
where each parameter is a _reference_ to a variable or function of the calling Machine, or `None` where the calling Machine does not define it:

. `max_len` is the total length of the data to be absorbed, *in bits*. If neither software nor the Machine sets it (see *State Machine Behavior* below), `max_len` is zero.
. `block` contains the current block being filled internally before processing. Its size is `b` bits.
. `state` is the output buffer. It may be the same as `block`. Its size is `n` bits.
. `input_base` is the offset, within the `INPUT` of the `kl.exec`, of the next data to absorb.
. `block_base` is the offset, within the current block, of the next data to absorb: within `block` if `mode` is
   `assign`, and within `state` from `state_offset` if `mode` is `xor_accumulate` (steps 3.b and 3.c).
   Each time it reaches `b`, the block is processed (step 3.g).
. `state_offset` is the offset within `state` at which the input is XORed, for instance in sponge constructions.
. `cumul_len` is the cumulative length of the data absorbed so far by the macro, or `None` if not tracked.
. `mode` is either `assign` or `xor_accumulate`, and selects how incoming data is combined with
   the destination, as specified in steps 3.b and 3.c below. It is *not* inferred from whether `block` and
   `state` denote the same variable.
. `last_block_state` is the State in which a final piece of the input is absorbed, or `None`.
. `last_block_len` is the variable of the caller Machine that contains the bit length of the final
   piece still to be absorbed, or `None` if `last_block_state` is `None`.

[IMPORTANT]
====
`input_base`, `block_base`, `cumul_len` and `last_block_len`, the last two whenever they are
given, shall denote distinct storage locations.
They are updated independently: `input_base` is set to 8 {interpunct} `klstart` at the start of every `kl.exec` (step 2), whereas
`block_base` and `cumul_len` persist across instructions.
An invocation that binds two of them to the same location is malformed: the macro would update
that location several times per iteration.

When `mode` is `xor_accumulate`, the caller shall satisfy `state_offset` + `b` {le} `n`. The
destination of step 3.c ends at bit `block_base` + `amount` + `state_offset` − 1, and
`block_base` + `amount` can reach `b`, so a larger `state_offset` would write past the end of `state`.

`mode` is an explicit parameter because the two combining rules are not interchangeable.
Sponge constructions require `xor_accumulate`: they XOR the input directly into the rate portion
of the state. A field that is *loaded* rather than absorbed, in particular key and key-like
material, requires `assign`. Selecting `xor_accumulate` where `assign` is meant combines the
incoming value with whatever the field already held. For a key field, this silently yields a key
that neither party chose.
====

The macro does not initialize the persistent state variables `block_base` and `cumul_len`, so that several invocations of it can concatenate their results.
The invoking Machine zeroes them where needed.
A Machine that enters a state with `mode` = `assign`, and whose destination field must hold a
defined value once loading completes, shall zero that field on entry to the state.

*Units.* `max_len`, `b`, `n`, `input_base`, `block_base`, `state_offset`, `cumul_len`, `last_block_len`, and `amount` are
all counted in bits, to support values of `max_len` that are not multiples of 8 bits.
However, the macro only supports values of `b` and `n` that are multiples of 8 bits,
and all `kl.exec` instructions transfer whole bytes and never a fraction of a byte.
As a consequence, `input_base` and `block_base` are multiples of 8, except after the final transfer of a
`max_len` that is not a multiple of 8, which ends the instruction (step 3.h), and after a final piece absorbed in
`last_block_state`.

Finally, three parameters are functions of the calling Machine:

* `process_block()=process_block(block, state)` processes the current block into `state`.
  It may be given as `None`, in which case no such call is made, and the macro uses `state` only in step 3.c.
* `finalize()` finalizes the processing of the input data, completing the current block where the Machine's rules require it. It may also be given as `None`, in which case no such call is made.
* `process_last_block()=process_last_block(block, state)` processes the block that holds the final bit of
  a piece absorbed in `last_block_state`, in place of `process_block()`. It may be given as `None`, in
  which case that block is processed as any other.

The last three parameters may be omitted, and are then `None`: the steps for `last_block_state` below do
not apply.

Passing `None` for both `process_block()` and `finalize()` reduces the macro to its accounting of partial progress across
`kl.exec` instructions, which is what a Machine needs when it merely has to fill a long
field from memory and has no per-block processing to perform.

A Machine whose final piece needs a State of its own passes `last_block_state`.
It also passes `process_last_block()` if the block that holds the final bit needs processing of its own.

The optional parameter `granularity` specifies the transfer unit for inputs.
Every `kl.exec` transfer in a sequence, except possibly the last, must have a `KLLEN` that is a whole multiple of `granularity`.
The final transfer may be shorter or internally truncated to `max_len` − `cumul_len` (<<KLE-truncation-vs-length>>); consequently, `max_len` need not be a multiple of `granularity`.
Where `max_len` != 0, a transfer whose `KLLEN` is not a whole multiple of `granularity` violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>) if `cumul_len` + `KLLEN` < `max_len`, with `cumul_len` as the instruction finds it, also when it resumes.
For example, with `granularity` = 128, a 96-bit IV is supplied in a single transfer, either as a 96-bit vector or as a 128-bit vector with excess bits truncated.

//

*Internal State Variables*::
Besides the variables that the parameters name, the Machine may define variables for `process_block()`, `process_last_block()` and `finalize()`.
`max_len` keeps its value for the duration of the computation.

*State Machine Behavior*::

** When entering State _Current_State_:
 . If software sets `max_len`, it passes it to a Form B `kl.setst`, which sets `max_len` to the auxiliary argument `Xs`.
 . Otherwise, `max_len` is set by the Machine, and the `kl.setst` that enters the State is of Form A, or of Form B where
   the invoking Machine defines its `Xs`, which then does not set `max_len`.

** While in State _Current_State_, only ``kl.exec`` instructions of Form B are expected, whereby
 "`kl.exec Kn, INPUT`" processes the data block `INPUT` as follows.
+
--
. If `max_len` != 0, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ if `cumul_len` {ge} `max_len`, or if
  `last_block_state` is not `None` and `cumul_len` + `KLLEN` − 8 {interpunct} `klstart` {ge} `max_len`.
. `input_base <- 8` {interpunct} `klstart`: 0 when the instruction starts, the point of progress when it resumes.
  Nothing else distinguishes a start from a resumption.
. While `(input_base < KLLEN)` do
.. If `max_len` != 0 then `amount <- min(KLLEN - input_base, b - block_base, max_len - cumul_len)`,
   else `amount <- min(KLLEN - input_base, b - block_base)`.
   `amount` is always strictly positive, so the loop terminates.
.. if `mode` = `assign` then:
 ... ``block[block_base + amount - 1 : block_base] <- INPUT[input_base + amount - 1 : input_base]``.
.. else, that is if `mode` = `xor_accumulate`:
 ...  XOR ``INPUT[input_base + amount - 1 : input_base]`` to ``state[block_base + amount + state_offset - 1 : block_base + state_offset]``.
.. `input_base <- input_base + amount`.
.. `block_base <- block_base + amount`.
.. If `cumul_len` is not `None`, then `cumul_len <- cumul_len + amount`.
.. If (`block_base` = `b`) then:
 ... `process_block(block, state)`, if it is not `None`.
 ... `block_base <- 0`.
.. If `max_len` != 0 and `cumul_len = max_len` then:
 ... `finalize()`, if it is not `None`.
 ... The instruction is terminated, even if `input_base < KLLEN`.
.. *Here*, and only here, the instruction may be interrupted while `input_base < KLLEN`, with `klstart <- input_base / 8`.
--
+
The interruption points of `kl.exec` in _Current_State_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-interruption-point>>) are 0 and every byte offset `k`, with 8 {times} `k` a multiple of `granularity` where the invocation gives one, at which the `block_base` that the locker holds is 0; architected state alone determines them.
+
NOTE: Every iteration of a window but the last ends with a full block, so a halt leaves `block_base` = 0.
After the last iteration the instruction retires and keeps any partial block.
The interruption points thus do not depend on the `block_base` with which the halted instruction started.
`input_base` is then a whole number of bytes, so `klstart` never describes a position inside a byte, as is also the case on the output side of _Hash_Output_ (<<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>).

** Where `last_block_state` is not `None`:
 . If `max_len` = 0, the invoking Machine sets `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `b` on entering _Current_State_.
   Software then either enters `last_block_state` from _Current_State_ or transitions directly to another State.
 . If `max_len` != 0, _Current_State_ absorbs fewer than `max_len` bits (step 1), and software enters
   `last_block_state`, which absorbs the rest.

** When entering `last_block_state` from _Current_State_:
 . If `max_len` = 0:
 .. A Form A `kl.setst` keeps `last_block_len` = `b`.
 .. A Form B "`kl.setst Kn, #immed7, Xs`" sets `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `Xs`. Unless the Machine states otherwise,
    `Xs` must satisfy `0` < `Xs` {le} `b` and Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>; any other value transitions the locker
    to Error State _Invalid_.
 . If `max_len` != 0, the `kl.setst` is of Form A and sets `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `max_len` − `cumul_len`.
   If this exceeds `b`, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
 . `finalize()` is not performed.

** In `last_block_state`, a single Form B "`kl.exec Kn, INPUT`" absorbs the `last_block_len` least significant bits of
   `INPUT` and ignores the other bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
   A `KLLEN` smaller than `last_block_len` violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).
   The `kl.exec` performs:
+
--
. If `last_block_len` = 0, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
. `input_base` {leftarrow} 0.
. While (`input_base` < `last_block_len`) do
.. `amount` {leftarrow} `min(last_block_len - input_base, b - block_base)`.
.. Steps 3.b to 3.f of the `kl.exec` in _Current_State_.
.. If `block_base` = `b` and either `input_base` < `last_block_len` or `process_last_block()` is `None`, then:
... `process_block(block, state)`, if it is not `None`.
... `block_base` {leftarrow} 0.
. If `process_last_block()` is not `None`: `process_last_block(block, state)`, then `block_base` {leftarrow} 0.
. `last_block_len` {leftarrow} 0.
--
+
The only interruption point of this `kl.exec` is 0.

** A `kl.setst` that moves the locker from _Current_State_ or `last_block_state` to another State performs
   `finalize()` before the transition, if `finalize()` is not `None` and step 3.h has not performed it.
   This does not apply where the Machine states otherwise, or where the destination is:
 . _Ready_ or an Error State; or
 . `last_block_state`, from _Current_State_.

** The locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_, unless the Machine states otherwise, on:
 . leaving `last_block_state`, with `last_block_len` != 0, for a Valid State other than _Ready_;
 . where `max_len` != 0 and `last_block_state` is not `None`, leaving _Current_State_ for a Valid State other than
   _Ready_ or `last_block_state`.

[[KLE-GCM-mode]]
=== GCM Mode

The GCM mode was designed by David McGrew and John Viega cite:[DBLP-conf-indocrypt-McGrewV04] and became a NIST Recommendation in 2007 cite:[nist-SP-800-38D].
It is currently defined only for 128-bit block ciphers; the definition will be extended to other block sizes if needed.
(((KLE, Machine, GCM)))

Parameters::

* `k` = key size (64|`k`).
* `b` = 128 = block size in bits.
* `c` = 32 = counter size in bits.
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
+
--
** `key`: `k` bits.
** `J0`:  `128` bits. Big-endian as in the AES-GCM spec. `J0`[127:96] holds the counter of the last counter block used, `start_ctr` before the first block; the counter occupies bytes 12 to 15 of the counter block, big-endian, as in cite:[nist-SP-800-38D].
** `auth_key` (derived): `128` bits. Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>> applies.
** `tag`: `128` bits. This field stores the authentication tag.
** `start_ctr`: an integer in the range 0 to 2^32^-1, the counter of the pre-counter block `J0` of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1; the first block uses the initial counter block `inc32(J0)`, of counter `start_ctr` + 1. It is serialized as `bin(start_ctr,32)`.
** `last_block_len`: an integer in the range 0 to 127. The length of the last block of a plaintext or ciphertext.
--
+
In the internal state, variables `len`, `input_base`, `block_base`, `cumul_len`, `J0_padding` may overlap with `J0` or be separate fields.
The latter may be necessary if the implementation of macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>) works with integers of a different length.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[cols="^8%,<15%,<15%,<62%",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field          | Description
|  i    | `k` or 64     | `key`          | Key value or System Key Identifier
| ii    | 128           | `J0`           | Big-endian as in the AES-GCM spec. `J0`[127:96] is the current GCTR counter.
|  iii  | 128           | `tag`          | {empty}
|   iv  | 32            | `start_ctr`    | The initial counter: `start_ctr` = `int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`, the last four bytes of the *initial* `J0` read as a big-endian integer, serialized here as `bin(start_ctr,32)`.
|   v  | 16            | `last_block_len` | {empty}
|===

While in state _Set_Aux_Value_, `J0` is replaced by the following fields:

[cols="^8%,<15%,<15%,<62%",options="header"]
|===
| Pos.  | Size (Bits)     | Field       | Description
| ii.a  | 16              | `len`       | Total length of the IV, in bits. Maximum supported size is 1024 bytes (8192 bits).
| ii.b  | 16              | Padding     | `input_base` is not serialized: it is set to 8 {interpunct} `klstart` at the start of every `kl.exec` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>, step 2).
| ii.c  | 16              | `block_base`| Amount of data, in bits, absorbed so far into the current block. Each time this value reaches `b`, the block is processed.
| ii.d  | 48              | `cumul_len` | Cumulative length, in bits, of the data absorbed so far.
| ii.e  | 32              | `J0_padding`| Zero.
|===
--

NOTE: There are no fields for the lengths of the AD and of the plaintext, as they are maintained by the calling software.

Machine-Specific Functions::
+
--
* `enc_blk(key, p) -> c` encrypts block `p` using the key, and returns the
  result `c`.

* `absorb(data)` absorbs data into the hash function by performing:
  . `tag <- tag xor data`.
  . `tag <- Galoismul(tag, auth_key)`.

* `Galoismul(a, b)` is multiplication in the GHASH field of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}6.3, in the representation of that section.
+
The field is GF(2)[x] modulo `x^128^ + x^7^ + x^2^ + x + 1`.
A 128-bit value `V` represents the element whose coefficient of
`x^8i+j^`, for `0` {le} `i` < `16` and `0` {le} `j` {le} `7`, is `V[8i + (7-j)]`.
+
In other words, the byte order runs forward (“little endian”:
the byte at offset 0 contains the coefficients of `x^0^` through `x^7^`,
the byte at offset 1 contains the coefficients of `x^8^` through `x^15^`,
and so on) while the bit order is reversed within each byte:
in the byte at offset 0, bit 0, the least significant bit, is the coefficient of `x^7^`,
bit 1 that of `x^6^`, and so on.
--

State Machine::

* *States:*
+
--
Besides _Ready_, _Success_, and _Failure_, the GCM Mode Machines make use of the following states:

 ** _Set_Aux_Value_: Absorb the IV; the `kl.exec` that completes it computes `J0`.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Process Associated Data (AD) into the tag.
 ** _Encrypt_: Encrypt the plaintext and absorb the ciphertext into the tag.
 ** _Enc_Last_Block_: Encrypt the final, fractional block of the plaintext.
 ** _Enc_Tag_Finalize_: Finalize the encryption tag, prepare to return its value.
 ** _Decrypt_: Absorb the ciphertext into the tag and decrypt it.
 ** _Dec_Last_Block_: Decrypt the final, fractional block of the ciphertext.
 ** _Dec_Tag_Finalize_: Finalize the computation of the tag and keep it internally.
 ** _Hash_Verify_: Compare the tag against a user supplied value.

The Associated Data (AD) and the plaintext or ciphertext are absorbed into `GHASH`
differently, which places two obligations on the
caller:

* *For the AD*, the caller must zero every bit of the final block beyond the actual end
  of the AD. `GHASH` absorbs that block whole, following cite:[nist-SP-800-38D].
  No State processes a fractional last AD block separately.

* *For the plaintext and ciphertext*, the caller must not pass a final block shorter than `b`, zero-padded, to
  _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_: the padding would be encrypted, emitting keystream, and absorbed, producing a tag that does
  not conform to cite:[nist-SP-800-38D].
  Such a block goes through State _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_.
--

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Set_Aux_Value_ -> _Hash_Absorb_.
 ** _Set_Aux_Value_ -> _Set_Aux_Value_, which restarts the IV (see below).
 ** _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Encrypt_, if encryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Decrypt_, if decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Encrypt_ -> { _Enc_Last_Block_ -> } _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ -> _Success_.
 ** _Decrypt_ -> { _Dec_Last_Block_ -> } _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ -> _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** Gated States (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>): _MachinePolicy_[0], _Encrypt_, _Enc_Last_Block_ and _Enc_Tag_Finalize_; _MachinePolicy_[1], _Decrypt_, _Dec_Last_Block_, _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ and _Hash_Verify_.

* *Behavior:*
 ** When entering State _Ready_:
  . `auth_key <- enc_blk(key, zeros(b))`;
  . `tag <- zeros(b)`.

 ** When entering State _Set_Aux_Value_:
  . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value, Xs```" sets the IV length in bits: `len <- Xs`.
  . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `8192`.
  . `tag`, `input_base`, `block_base` and `cumul_len` are set to zero.
  . A same-State Form B `kl.setst` repeats these operations, restarting the IV and discarding the part absorbed so far
    (Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>> and <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).

 ** While in State _Set_Aux_Value_, the IV is loaded under Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with _W_ = `cumul_len`, and absorbed as it is
    loaded by the macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>), called as +
  `PROCESS_VLI(len, block=tag, b, state=tag, b, input_base, block_base, 0, cumul_len, process_block(), finalize(), mode=xor_accumulate, granularity=b)`, where:
   *** `process_block()` performs `tag <- Galoismul(tag, auth_key)` if `len != 96`, and nothing otherwise;
   *** `finalize()` is defined as follows:
    . If `len = 96`, then:
      .. `J0 <- bswap(bin(1,32)) @ tag[95:0]`.
+
The value of `J0` is `IV {vvert} 0^31^ {vvert} 1` of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1, where the IV occupies bytes 0 to 11, i.e.
`J0[95:0]`, and the counter occupies bytes 12 to 15 as a *big-endian* integer.

    . If `len != 96`, then let `s <- 128 ⎡len/128⎤ - len`, and the computation of `J0 := GHASH(IV {vvert} 0^s+64^ {vvert} bswap(bin(len,64)), auth_key)` is completed by performing:
      .. if `block_base != 0`, then `tag <- Galoismul(tag, auth_key)`
      .. `tag <- tag xor (bswap(bin(len,64)) @ zeros(64))`
      .. `tag <- Galoismul(tag, auth_key)`
      .. `J0 <- tag`
    . `start_ctr <- int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`,
    . `tag <- zeros(b)`.
    . Transitions to state _Hash_Absorb_.

 ** When leaving State _Set_Aux_Value_:
  . the `kl.exec` that completes the IV performs `finalize()` (step 3.h of <<KLE-process-VLI>>), which transitions the
    locker to _Hash_Absorb_;
  . a `kl.setst` naming _Ready_ performs no `finalize()`: the partial IV is discarded, and `tag`, `J0`, `len`,
    `block_base` and `cumul_len` are zeroed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).
+
Entering _Hash_Absorb_ requires a complete IV (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
Any other `Xs` on entering _Set_Aux_Value_, `0` included, or a `kl.setst` naming _Hash_Absorb_ from _Set_Aux_Value_,
transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

[NOTE]
====
The IV is absorbed into `tag`, not into `J0`, and only the finished value is copied to `J0`.
`tag` is free for this: entering _Set_Aux_Value_ zeroes it, which is GHASH's initial value.
The slot of `J0` in the Serialized Content instead carries `len`, `block_base` and `cumul_len` while this State is
active. Accumulating into `J0` would place the running hash and those counters in the same 128 bits, so a CC
exported part-way through a non-96-bit IV could not resume.
====

 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, (multi-block) Form B "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" instructions absorb each data block into `tag`:
    `absorb(INPUT)`.
 ** The `kl.setst` from _Hash_Absorb_ into _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_ is of Form A.
** In State _Encrypt_, (multi-block) Form A "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" instructions encrypt `INPUT` into
   `OUTPUT` and absorb `OUTPUT` into `tag`:
+
--
  * `local ctr : int` - the running counter, as an integer.
  * `local tmp : bits(b)`

[]
  . `ctr <- int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`   // counter as an integer
  . `ctr <- (ctr + 1) mod 2^32^`.
  . If `ctr` = `(start_ctr - 1) mod 2^32^`, then: (at most 2^32^ − 2 blocks)
   .. the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
   . else:
  .. `J0[127:96] <- bswap(bin(ctr,32))`.  // counter back to big-endian
  .. `tmp <- INPUT xor enc_blk(key, J0)`.
  .. `absorb(tmp)`.
  .. `OUTPUT <- tmp`.
--
** When entering State _Enc_Last_Block_:
 . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_enc_last_block, Xs```" sets the bit length of the last block:
   `last_block_len <- Xs`.
 . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
 . The following Form A `kl.exec` ignores any input bits beyond `last_block_len`.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
+
NOTE: cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] admits any bit length, but numbers the bits of a byte from the most significant.
A truncation to the least significant bits does not map onto that order; hence the multiples of 8.

** In State _Enc_Last_Block_, a single Form A "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" encrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT` and
   absorbs `OUTPUT` into `tag`:
+
--
  * local `ctr : int` – the running counter, as an integer.
  * `local tmp : bits(b)`

[]
  . If `last_block_len = 0`, the final block has already been processed: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
  . `ctr <- int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`
  . `ctr <- (ctr + 1) mod 2^32^`.
  . If `ctr` = `(start_ctr - 1) mod 2^32^`, then:
   .. the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
  . else:
   .. `J0[127:96] <- bswap(bin(ctr,32))`
   .. `tmp <- (zeros(128-last_block_len) @ INPUT[last_block_len-1:0])` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}xor (zeros(128-last_block_len) @ enc_blk(key, J0)[last_block_len-1:0])`.
   .. `absorb(tmp)`.
   .. `OUTPUT <- tmp`.
   .. `last_block_len <- 0`
--

** When entering State _Enc_Tag_Finalize_:
 . A Form C "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_enc_tag_finalize, INPUT```" supplies the length block, which software computes: +
   `INPUT <- bswap(bin(len_in_bits(plaintext), 64)) @ bswap(bin(len_in_bits(AD), 64))`. +
   `INPUT` is truncated to its 128 least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
 . The tag is computed and retained internally: `absorb(INPUT)`, then
   `tag <- tag xor enc_blk(key, bswap(bin(start_ctr,32)) @ J0[95:0])`.
+

** In State _Enc_Tag_Finalize_, a single Form C "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn```" writes `tag` to `OUTPUT`, and the locker
   transitions to _Success_.
   A `KLLEN` smaller than 128 must be 120, 112, 104, 96 or 64 (cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}5.2.1.2), and `OUTPUT` then
   receives `tag[KLLEN-1:0]`, the truncated tag `MSB~KLLEN~(T)` of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1; any other `KLLEN`
   below 128 violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

** In State _Decrypt_, (multi-block) Form A "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" instructions absorb the ciphertext
   `INPUT` into `tag`, then decrypt `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`:
+
--
 * `local ctr : int` - the running counter, as an integer.

[]
 . `ctr <- int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`.
 . `ctr <- (ctr + 1) mod 2^32^`.
 . If `ctr` = `(start_ctr - 1) mod 2^32^`, then:
  .. the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
 . else:
  .. `absorb(INPUT)`.
  .. `J0[127:96] <- bswap(bin(ctr,32))`.
  .. `OUTPUT <- INPUT xor enc_blk(key, J0)`.
--

** When entering State _Dec_Last_Block_:
 . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_dec_last_block, Xs```" sets the bit length of the last block:
   `last_block_len <- Xs`.
 . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Dec_Last_Block_, a single Form A "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" absorbs the zero-padded final block
   of the ciphertext into `tag` and decrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`:
+
--
  * local `ctr : int` – the running counter, as an integer.
  * `local tmp : bits(b)`

[]
  . If `last_block_len = 0`, the final block has already been processed: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
  . `ctr <- int(bswap(J0[127:96]))`
  . `ctr <- (ctr + 1) mod 2^32^`.
  . If `ctr` = `(start_ctr - 1) mod 2^32^`, then:
   .. the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
  . else:
  .. `tmp <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ INPUT[last_block_len-1:0]`.
  .. `absorb(tmp)`.
  .. `J0[127:96] <- bswap(bin(ctr,32))`.
  .. `OUTPUT <- tmp xor (zeros(128-last_block_len) @ enc_blk(key, J0)[last_block_len-1:0])`.
  .. `last_block_len <- 0`
--

** When entering State _Dec_Tag_Finalize_:
 . A Form C "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_dec_tag_finalize, INPUT```" supplies the length block, which software computes: +
   `INPUT <- bswap(bin(len_in_bits(plaintext), 64)) @ bswap(bin(len_in_bits(AD), 64))`. +
   `INPUT` is truncated to its 128 least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
 . The tag is computed: `absorb(INPUT)`, then `tag <- tag xor enc_blk(key, bswap(bin(start_ctr,32)) @ J0[95:0])`.
+

** When entering State _Hash_Verify_, a Form C "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_verify, INPUT```" supplies the value
   against which the internally computed tag is compared (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>):
 . The tag length is `t` = `min(KLLEN, 128)`, which must be one of 128, 120, 112, 104, 96 or 64
   (cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}5.2.1.2).
 . `INPUT[t-1:0]` is compared with `tag[t-1:0]`. If they match, the locker transitions to _Success_; otherwise, to
   _Failure_.
+
Any other `t` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
+
NOTE: Truncated tags are prefixes of one another, and each verification chooses its own `t`.
The floor of 64 bits excludes the 32 bits that cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] admits for some applications.
It keeps a search for the tag through a verification oracle at about 2^64^ trials. Keeping one `t` per key is the caller's (Rule <<KLE-MGR-caller-limits>>).
+
NOTE: _Decrypt_ and _Dec_Last_Block_ release the plaintext block by block, before _Hash_Verify_ checks the tag,
whereas cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] returns no plaintext when the check fails. The caller must not use the plaintext
before the locker reaches _Success_, and must discard it on _Failure_.
The same holds for the other AEAD Machines: <<KLE-GCM-with-IV-mode>>, <<KLE-GCM-SIV-mode>> (whose
_Dec_Tag_Finalize_ verifies the tag), <<KLE-OCB-mode>>, <<KLE-OCB-with-nonce-mode>>, <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>,
<<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128-wsn>> and <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128-N-masking>>.

[[KLE-GCM-with-IV-mode]]
=== GCM with Set IV

The mode of operation “GCM with a Set IV” is a variant of GCM in which the initialization vector (IV) is provisioned with the key, rather than supplied by software for each message.

This Machine is the same as GCM (<<KLE-GCM-mode>>), except that it does not set the IV.
A 96-bit IV is carried in the Provisioning Input, and provisioning forms `J0` from it as cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1
does for a 96-bit IV; `J0` itself is never provisioned.
State _Set_Aux_Value_ and everything that GCM specifies for it therefore do not apply, and _Hash_Absorb_ is entered
from _Ready_. The differences follow.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[cols="^8%,<15%,<15%,<62%",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field       | Description
|  ii   | `k` or 64     | `key`       | Key value or System Key Identifier
| iii   | 96            | `IV`        | The IV of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1. Only a 96-bit IV is supported.
|===
--

* *Internal State*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for GCM.

Upon provisioning, before the operations of entering _Ready_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-management-no-state-operations>>),
`J0 <- bswap(bin(1,32)) @ IV`, that is `IV {vvert} 0^31^ {vvert} 1`, and `start_ctr <- 1`. `start_ctr` is 1 in every
CC of this Machine: an import whose Serialized Content holds another value completes into Error State _Invalid_
(Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
--

* *Serialized Content*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for GCM.
--

* *States and Allowed Transitions:* {empty}
+
--
Same as for GCM, with the following exceptions:

** There is no state _Set_Aux_Value_. The transition is from _Ready_ to _Hash_Absorb_.
--

* *State Machine Behavior:* {empty}
+
--
The differences with respect to GCM are:

 ** The ``kl.setst`` into State _Hash_Absorb_ must be of Form A, i.e., _without any additional inputs_: the IV is already in the PI.
 ** Entering State _Ready_ also restores the provisioned `J0`, by `J0[127:96] <- bswap(bin(1,32))`: a later message repeats the provisioned GCM invocation. Software that needs another IV provisions another locker.
--

This Machine is not a `kl.derive` destination (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>): its `key` and IV are configured together, by provisioning or import.

NOTE: This Machine protects the confidentiality of the IV, not against its reuse (Rule <<KLE-nonce-reuse>>): encrypting a different message under the same `key` and `J0` violates the uniqueness requirement of cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}8.
A key that software knows, derived into `key` while `J0` is kept, would let it recover `J0` from the keystream.
A provisioned `J0` would let software choose the counter blocks that the Machine encrypts: with
`J0` = 0^96^ {vvert} `FFFFFFFF`, a decrypt-only locker would output `E(K, 0)`, the GHASH key, and with `J0` = `N` {vvert} 0^32^
the tag mask of IV `N`, so that software could forge tags under a key that it never sees.

[[KLE-GCM-SIV-mode]]
=== GCM-SIV Mode

The GCM-SIV mode is described in cite:[DBLP-journals-iacr-GueronLL17] and cite:[RFC8452].
It is currently defined only for 128-bit block ciphers; the definition will be extended to other block sizes if needed.
(((KLE, Machine, GCM-SIV)))

The decryption path of GCM-SIV resembles that of GCM; the encryption path differs:

* *Encryption:* _Hash_Absorb_ absorbs the whole associated data (AD) and plaintext. _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ then absorbs the lengths and produces the SIV, which is also the tag. _Encrypt_ then makes a second pass over the plaintext, with the SIV as its initialization vector.
* *Decryption:* _Hash_Absorb_ absorbs only the AD. _Decrypt_ decrypts the ciphertext and absorbs the resulting plaintext. _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ then absorbs the lengths, recomputes the SIV, and compares it internally with the SIV set in _Set_Aux_Value_2_.

//

Parameters::

* `k` = key size, either 128 or 256: cite:[RFC8452] defines AES-GCM-SIV only for these two.
* `b` = 128 = block size.
* `c` = 32 = counter size.
* Granularity: `b`.
* The authentication tag is `b` = 128 bits long.
* A message holds at most 2^32^ blocks, the final partial block included, which is the `P_MAX` of cite:[RFC8452] {sect}6.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
  ** `key`: `k` bits.
 ** `enc_key` (derived): `k` bits.
 ** `auth_key` (derived): 128 bits. Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>> applies to both.
 ** `nonce`: 96 bits.
 ** `ctr`: an integer in the range 0 to 2^32^, held and serialized on 33 bits: bits [31:0] in field iii, bit 32 in field vi.
    `ctr` = 2^32^ is reached only after the last block of a message of 2^32^ blocks, and any further block invalidates the locker,
    also after an export and an import.
 ** SIV: 128 bits.
 ** `tmp`: 128 bits.
 ** `last_block_len`: 7 bits (value between 0 and 127).

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)     | Field
|    i  |  `k` or 64      | `key` or System Key Identifier
|   ii  |  96             | `nonce`
|  iii  |  32             | `ctr[31:0]`
|   iv  |  128            | SIV
|    v  |  128            | `tmp`
|   vi  |  16             | `ctr[32]` in bit 15, `last_block_len` in bits [6:0], the other bits zero
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `enc_blk(key, p) -> c` encrypts block `p` using the key, and returns the result `c`.
* `RFC8452_KeyDeriv(k, key, nonce)` derives the per-nonce message-encryption and
  message-authentication keys of cite:[RFC8452] {sect}4:
+
[[KLE-GCM-SIV-KeyDeriv]]
.AES-GCM-SIV Key Derivation
[]
----
function RFC8452_KeyDeriv(k : int, key : bits(k), nonce : bits(96))
    → struct{enc_key : bits(k), auth_key : bits(128)} =
{
  local A : array [0..5] of bits(128);
  local E : bits(k);
  local i : int;

  foreach(i from 0 to k/64 + 1) do:
    A[i] ← enc_blk(key, nonce @ bin(i,32));
  foreach(i from 2 to k/64 + 1) do:
    E[64*i-65 : 64*i-128] ← A[i][63:0];

  return struct { enc_key = E, auth_key = A[1][63:0] @ A[0][63:0] };
}
----
+
The loop makes `k`/64 + 2 calls, four for `k` = 128 and six for `k` = 256, and keeps the low
64 bits of each: these are the “first eight bytes” of cite:[RFC8452] under <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>.
* `absorb(data)` absorbs data into the hash function by performing a step of POLYVAL:
  . `tmp <- tmp xor data`.
  . `tmp <- Montmul(tmp, auth_key)`

State Machine::
  * *States:*
+
--
Besides _Ready_, _Success_, and _Failure_, the GCM-SIV Machine makes use of the following states:

 ** _Set_Aux_Value_: Set the value of `nonce`.
 ** _Set_Aux_Value_2_: Set the SIV.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Absorb the AD and, on the encryption path, the plaintext into the partial tag `tmp`.
 ** _Encrypt_: Encrypt the plaintext.
 ** _Enc_Last_Block_: Encrypt the final, fractional block of the plaintext.
 ** _Enc_Tag_Finalize_: Finalize the SIV with the AD and PT lengths, prepare to return its value.
 ** _Decrypt_: Decrypt the ciphertext and absorb the resulting plaintext into the partial tag.
 ** _Dec_Last_Block_: Decrypt the final, fractional block of the ciphertext.
 ** _Dec_Tag_Finalize_: Finalize the tag with the AD and CT lengths, then compare it against the SIV.
--

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Set_Aux_Value_.
 ** _Set_Aux_Value_ -> _Set_Aux_Value_2_ or _Hash_Absorb_.
 ** _Set_Aux_Value_2_ -> _Hash_Absorb_.
 ** For encryption: _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ -> _Encrypt_ { -> _Enc_Last_Block_ }.
    The path ends in _Encrypt_ or _Enc_Last_Block_, not in _Success_, since the _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ `kl.exec` outputs the tag;
    the next message starts with a return to _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).
 ** For decryption: _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Decrypt_ -> { _Dec_Last_Block_ -> } _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** Gated States (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>): _MachinePolicy_[0], _Encrypt_, _Enc_Last_Block_ and _Enc_Tag_Finalize_; _MachinePolicy_[1], _Decrypt_, _Dec_Last_Block_ and _Dec_Tag_Finalize_.
 ** A final plaintext or ciphertext block shorter than `b` goes through _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_.

* *Behavior:*
** Upon entering State _Ready_:
  *** The following initialization operations are performed:
   . `nonce`, `ctr`, `tmp`, `SIV <- 0`.

** When entering State _Set_Aux_Value_:
   . A Form C "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value, INPUT```" is expected.
   . `nonce <- INPUT[95:0]` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
   . (`enc_key`, `auth_key`) `<-` `RFC8452_KeyDeriv(k, key, nonce)`.

** When entering State _Set_Aux_Value_2_:
   . A Form C "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value_2, INPUT```" is expected.
   . `SIV <- INPUT[127:0]` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
+
[NOTE]
====
This State serves decryption.
Entering it during encryption is harmless: the encryption path reaches _Encrypt_ only through the _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ `kl.exec`, which overwrites `SIV` with the synthetic value, so an externally set `SIV` never feeds the encryption keystream.
Skipping it during decryption leaves `SIV` at the zero that entering _Ready_ set, and repeating it only overwrites `SIV`, from which no other field is derived.
====

** The `kl.setst` into _Hash_Absorb_, _Enc_Tag_Finalize_, _Decrypt_ or _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ is of Form A.
** Only the _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ `kl.exec` enters _Encrypt_, after synthesizing `SIV`: a `kl.setst` naming _Encrypt_, in any State,
   _Encrypt_ included, transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>>).

** In State _Hash_Absorb_, (multi-block) Form B "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" instructions are expected, each performing `absorb(INPUT)`.
   The caller must zero-pad the AD and, on the encryption path, the plaintext, each separately to a multiple of 16 bytes,
   as cite:[RFC8452] {sect}4 specifies: every bit of the final partial block of each, beyond the end of its data, is zero.

** In State _Enc_Tag_Finalize_, a single Form A "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" is expected, whose `INPUT` the caller sets to
   `bin(len_in_bits(plaintext), 64) @ bin(len_in_bits(AD), 64)`, truncated to its 128 least significant bits
   (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>). It finalizes the SIV:
   . `absorb(INPUT)`.
   . `tmp <- enc_blk(enc_key, 0 @ tmp[126:96] @ (tmp[95:0] xor nonce))`.
   . `SIV <- tmp` and `OUTPUT <- SIV`.
   . The state transitions to _Encrypt_.

** In State _Encrypt_, (multi-block) Form A ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected.
      Issuing "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" encrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`
      as follows:
+
--
  . If `ctr` = 2^32^, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than a message may hold.
  . ``OUTPUT <- INPUT xor enc_blk(enc_key, 1 @ SIV[126:32] @ bin\((int(SIV[31:0]) + ctr) mod 2^32^, 32)),``
  . `ctr <- ctr + 1`.
--
** When entering State _Enc_Last_Block_:
   . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_enc_last_block, Xs```" is expected, where `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
   . `last_block_len <- Xs`, the bit length of the last block.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Enc_Last_Block_, a single Form A ``kl.exec`` instruction is expected.
      Issuing "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" encrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`
      as follows:
+
--
. If `last_block_len = 0`, the final block has already been processed: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
. If `ctr` = 2^32^, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than a message may hold.
    . ``OUTPUT <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ (INPUT[last_block_len-1:0] xor enc_blk(enc_key, 1 @ SIV[126:32] @ bin\((int(SIV[31:0]) + ctr) mod 2^32^, 32))[last_block_len-1:0]),``
    . `ctr <- ctr + 1`.
    . `last_block_len <- 0`
--

** In State _Decrypt_, (multi-block) Form A ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected.
   "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" decrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT` and then absorbs the plaintext `OUTPUT` into `tmp`:
+
--
. If `ctr` = 2^32^, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than a message may hold.
. ``OUTPUT <- INPUT xor enc_blk(enc_key, 1 @ SIV[126:32] @ bin\((int(SIV[31:0]) + ctr) mod 2^32^, 32))``,
. `absorb(OUTPUT)`.
. `ctr <- ctr + 1`.
--
** When entering State _Dec_Last_Block_:
   . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_dec_last_block, Xs```" is expected, where `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
   . `last_block_len <- Xs`.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Dec_Last_Block_, a single Form A ``kl.exec`` instruction is expected.
      Issuing “``kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT``” decrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT` and
      absorbs the zero-padded plaintext into the partial tag, as follows:
+
--
. If `last_block_len = 0`, the final block has already been processed: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
. If `ctr` = 2^32^, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than a message may hold.
    . ``OUTPUT <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ (INPUT[last_block_len-1:0] xor enc_blk(enc_key, 1 @ SIV[126:32] @ bin\((int(SIV[31:0]) + ctr) mod 2^32^, 32))[last_block_len-1:0]),``
    . `absorb(OUTPUT)`.
    . `ctr <- ctr + 1`.
    . `last_block_len <- 0`
--
+
A `kl.setst` into _Dec_Tag_Finalize_ before this `kl.exec` omits the final block from `tmp`.

** In State _Dec_Tag_Finalize_, a single Form B "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" is expected, whose `INPUT` the caller sets to
   `bin(len_in_bits(plaintext), 64) @ bin(len_in_bits(AD), 64)`, truncated to its 128 least significant bits
   (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
   Since the SIV was supplied first, this `kl.exec` performs the comparison itself (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>):
   . `absorb(INPUT)`.
   . `tmp <- enc_blk(enc_key, 0 @ tmp[126:96] @ (tmp[95:0] xor nonce))`.
   . If `tmp` = `SIV`, the state transitions to _Success_; otherwise, it transitions to _Failure_.

   Repeating the operations of entering the State would admit a second final block (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).

** `enc_key` and `auth_key` are derived fields (Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>>):
(`enc_key`, `auth_key`) <- `RFC8452_KeyDeriv(k, key, nonce)`.

[[KLE-OCB-mode]]
=== OCB Mode

Only the OCB3 variant of OCB is architected, following RFC{nbsp}7253 cite:[RFC7253] and cite:[DBLP-journals-joc-KrovetzR21].
The state machine follows RFC{nbsp}7253 closely, with one difference of notation: bit positions are numbered from 0 at the least significant bit (<<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>), whereas RFC{nbsp}7253 numbers string positions from 1 at the first, most significant, bit.
Block indices, `index` included, start from 1 as in RFC{nbsp}7253.
This specification defines OCB only for block ciphers with 128-bit blocks.
Generalization to other widths is straightforward, supported by the security proof of the original algorithm and a lower bound on nonce length from cite:[cryptoeprint-2023-326].

Parameters::
+
--
* `b` = 128 = block size, assumed to be a power of 2.
* `k` = key size. Currently, it can only be 128, 192, or 256.
* `MAX_BLOCKS` = 2^48^ = maximum number of blocks, a final partial block included, in the AD or in the plaintext.
  This bound is this specification's: cite:[RFC7253] places no limit on the length of a message.
  The caller keeps the uniqueness of the nonce (Rule <<KLE-nonce-reuse>>) and the recommendation of
  cite:[RFC7253] {sect}5 that a key process at most 2^48^ blocks in total, the associated data included
  (Rule <<KLE-MGR-caller-limits>>).
* Granularity: `b`.
--

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64      | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
  ** `key`: `k` bits.
 ** `N`: `120` bits.
 ** `N_len`: 7 bits. Bit length of the nonce. It cannot exceed 120.
 ** `HasN`: bit 0 of _StateExtension_, set once `N` holds a loaded nonce (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).
 ** `L*`, `offset`, `hash_A`, `checksum_P`: 128 bits each.
 ** `L$` and `L[i]` (derived): 128 bits each, for `0` {le} `i` {le} `floor(log_2(MAX_BLOCKS))` = 48; `L[i]` is `L~i~` of cite:[RFC7253]. Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>> applies: they may be cached or recomputed.
 ** `tag_len`: 8 bits. Bit length of the tag. Currently, the allowed values are 64, 96, and 128.
 ** `last_block_len`: `7` bits. Length of the last block of AD or plaintext/ciphertext in bits.
 ** `index`: 49 bits, corresponding to `i` in the loops in RFC{nbsp}7253. It ranges from 0 to `MAX_BLOCKS` + 1 = 2^48^ + 1.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits) | Field
|   i   | `k` or 64   | `key` or System Key Identifier
|   ii  | 120         | `N`
|  iii  |   7         | `N_len`, the bit length of the nonce
|   iv  |   1         | Padding
|    v  | 128         | `L*`; the derived `L$` and `L[i]` are not serialized (Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>>)
|   vi  | 128         | `offset` (current offset)
|  vii  | 128         | `hash_A`
| viii  | 128         | `checksum_P` (used also for the final tag)
|   ix  |  49         | `index`
|    x  |   2         | `tag_len`, encoded as `tag_len`/32 − 2. The value 3 is not produced; an import that carries it transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
|   xi  |   7         | `last_block_len`
|===
--

//

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `enc_blk(K,p) -> c` encrypts block `p` using the key `K` and returns the result `c`.
* `dec_blk(K,c) -> p` decrypts block `c` using the key `K` and returns the result `p`.
* `ntz(n)` = the number of trailing zero bits in the base-2 representation of the positive integer `n`.
Formally, `ntz(n)` is the largest integer `x` for which `2^x^` divides `n`.
* `ocb_pad(X, n)` = the `b`-bit value holding the `n`-bit partial block `X[n-1:0]`
  followed, in string order, by a single `1` bit and then zeros. This is
  `X {vvert} 1 {vvert} 0^127-n^` of cite:[RFC7253] {sect}4.2, and under
  <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>> it is
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `ocb_pad(X, n)` = `zeros(120-n) @ 0b10000000 @ X[n-1:0]`.
+
The terminating `1` is the *most significant* bit of byte `n/8`, not bit `n` of the
value: in string order it immediately follows the last data bit, and within a byte the
first bit of the string is that byte's most significant bit. `ocb_pad` is defined for
`n` a multiple of 8 with `0` {le} `n` {le} 120; for `n` = 0 it is
`zeros(120) @ 0b10000000`.
* `double(S)`: doubling in the OCB3 field.
Note that cite:[RFC7253] specifies this operation over a big-endian string, hence:
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `double(S) <- bswap(update_mask(bswap(S)))`.
* `nonce_be(N, n)` = the big-endian view of the `n`-bit nonce bit string held in `N`.
  cite:[RFC7253] treats the nonce as a bit string whose first bit is the most significant, so
  when it is serialized into bytes the final, possibly partial byte is *left-aligned*: its
  `r` = `n` mod 8 most significant bits carry nonce and its remaining bits are padding. With
  `q` = {lceil}``n``/8{rceil} the number of bytes so occupied,
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `nonce_be(N, n)` = `bswap(N[8q-1:0], q) >> ((8 - r) mod 8)`.
+
The first bit of the nonce string is `nonce_be(N, n)[n-1]` and the last is bit `0`.
For `r` = 0 this reduces to `bswap(N[n-1:0])`, so a nonce occupying a whole number of bytes is
unaffected. `bswap` alone cannot express the general case, since it is defined only over byte
strings.

//

State Machine::
* *States:*
+
--
Besides _Ready_, _Success_, and _Failure_, the Machines implementing OCB Mode make use of the following states:

 ** _Set_Aux_Value_: Set the length and the value of the nonce.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Set the tag length, and process the AD.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_: Set the length of the final block (mandatory, can be zero) of the AD and process it.
 ** _Encrypt_: Process the plaintext by absorbing it into the tag and encrypting it.
 ** _Enc_Last_Block_: Provide the last block of the plaintext, then process it.
 ** _Enc_Tag_Finalize_: Return the tag.
 ** _Decrypt_: Process the ciphertext by decrypting it and absorbing the plaintext into the tag.
 ** _Dec_Last_Block_: Provide the last block of the ciphertext, then process it.
 ** _Hash_Verify_: Compare a user-provided value with the internally computed tag.
--

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Set_Aux_Value_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Encrypt_, if encryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Decrypt_, if decryption is allowed (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Encrypt_ -> _Enc_Last_Block_ -> _Enc_Tag_Finalize_ -> _Success_.
 ** _Decrypt_ -> _Dec_Last_Block_ -> _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** The `kl.exec` of _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_, makes the transition to _Enc_Tag_Finalize_, resp.
    _Hash_Verify_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 ** Gated States (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>): _MachinePolicy_[0], _Encrypt_, _Enc_Last_Block_ and _Enc_Tag_Finalize_; _MachinePolicy_[1], _Decrypt_, _Dec_Last_Block_ and _Hash_Verify_.

* *Behavior:*
** `L*` is set on entering State _Hash_Absorb_ and serialized. `L$` and `L[i]` are derived from it (Rule <<KLE-MGR-recomputed-fields>>): they are recomputed from the imported `L*` when an import completes, and an implementation may also keep them in the ADS of an exported SCC.

** Upon entering State _Ready_:
  *** The following initialization operations are performed:
   . `N <- zeros(120)`.
   . `N_len <- 0`.
   . `HasN` is cleared.
   . `hash_A <- zeros(128)`
   . `checksum_P <- zeros(128)`
   . `index <- 0`
   . `tag_len <- 128`

** When entering State _Set_Aux_Value_:
   . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value, Xs```" is expected, where `Xs` must satisfy `6` {le} `Xs` {le} `120`.
     Every bit length in that range is admissible: the nonce is a bit string and need not fill whole bytes.
   . `N_len <- Xs` and `N <- zeros(120)`, and `HasN` is cleared.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
+
[NOTE]
====
cite:[RFC7253] itself places no lower bound on the nonce length. KLE requires at least 6 bits
because a shorter nonce leaves the `bottom` and `Stretch` derivation of cite:[RFC7253] {sect}4.2
without enough entropy to remain sound, as analyzed in cite:[cryptoeprint-2023-326]. An
implementation of this specification is therefore not a general RFC{nbsp}7253 implementation for
nonces shorter than 6 bits.
====

** While in State _Set_Aux_Value_, a Form B "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" loads `N` from the `q` = {lceil}``N_len``/8{rceil}
   least significant bytes of `INPUT`, which is truncated to its `8q` least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>):
   . `N <- zeros(120 - 8q) @ INPUT[8q-1:0]`, with the `(8 - N_len) mod 8` least significant bits of byte `q-1` cleared:
     they pad the final, partial byte of the nonce string, are not part of the nonce, and are ignored.
   . `HasN` is set.
+
A repeated `kl.exec` rewrites `N`.
// Not an admissible pair for now (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>):
// A `kl.derive` into `N` (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>) loads it as the `kl.exec` does and sets `HasN`; one that
// transfers fewer than `q` bytes transitions the destination to Error State _Invalid_
// (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> item 4).

** When entering State _Hash_Absorb_, which requires `HasN` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>):
   . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_absorb, Xs```" is expected, where `Xs` must be 64, 96 or 128.
   . `index <- 1` and `tag_len <- Xs`.
   . `offset <- zeros(b)` – this is `Offset~0~` in the RFC.
   . `L* <- enc_blk(key, zeros(b))` – this is `L~*~` in the RFC.
   . `L$ <- double(L*)`, `L[0] <- double(L$)`, and `L[i] <- double(L[i-1])` for all `i` with `0 < i` {le} 48, possibly lazily.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Hash_Absorb_, (multi-block) `kl.exec` instructions are expected to be of Form B, whereby "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" absorbs a data block into `hash_A`, i.e.:
+
--
 . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the AD may hold.
 . `offset <- offset xor L[ntz(index)]`.
 . `hash_A <- hash_A xor enc_blk(key, INPUT xor offset)`.
 . `index <- index + 1`.
--
** When entering State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_:
 . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_last_block, Xs```" is expected: `last_block_len <- Xs`, the bit length of the final block of the AD.
 . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `0` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, a single Form B `kl.exec`, "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```", absorbs the last block of the AD into `hash_A`:
+
--
  * `tmp : bits(b)`

[]
  . If `last_block_len = 0`, the final block is empty or has already been processed: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).
  . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the AD may hold.
  . `offset <- offset xor L*`
  . `tmp <- ocb_pad(INPUT, last_block_len) xor offset`
  . `hash_A <- hash_A xor enc_blk(key, tmp)`
  . `last_block_len <- 0`
--
+
An empty final block of the AD, set with `last_block_len` = 0, needs no `kl.exec`: the next instruction is the `kl.setst` into _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_.
A `kl.setst` that leaves _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ before its `kl.exec` omits the final block of the AD.

** On entering State _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_, by a Form A `kl.setst`, the Machine performs the “setup” and “init” stages of OCB:
+
--
 * `Nonce_be : bits(128)` – this is `Nonce` in the RFC, in the big-endian view.
 * `Ktop : bits(128)`, `Ktop_be : bits(128)`
 * `Stretch_be : bits(192)` – this is `Stretch` in the RFC, in the big-endian view.
 * `bottom : int`

[]
 . `L*` and `L$` keep the values set on entering State _Hash_Absorb_.
 . `Nonce_be <- bin(tag_len mod 128, 7) @ zeros(120 - N_len) @ 1 @ nonce_be(N, N_len)`
 . `bottom <- int(Nonce_be[5:0])`
 . `Ktop <- enc_blk(key, bswap(Nonce_be[127:6] @ zeros(6)))`
 . `Ktop_be <- bswap(Ktop)`
 . `Stretch_be <- Ktop_be @ (Ktop_be[127:64] xor Ktop_be[119:56])`
 . `index <- 1`
 . `offset <- bswap(Stretch_be[191-bottom : 64-bottom])`
--
+
[NOTE]
====
cite:[RFC7253] {sect}4.2 specifies these variables over a big-endian bit string, with
positions numbered from 1. They are therefore computed here in the `bswap` view of <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>.
====

** In State _Encrypt_, (multi-block) `kl.exec` instructions are expected to be of Form A:
"```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" absorbs the plaintext `INPUT` into `checksum_P` and encrypts it into `OUTPUT`, namely:
+
--
 . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the plaintext may hold.
 . `offset <- offset xor L[ntz(index)]`.
 . `checksum_P <- checksum_P xor INPUT`
 . `OUTPUT <- offset xor enc_blk(key, INPUT xor offset)`
 . `index <- index + 1`.
--
** When entering State _Enc_Last_Block_:
 . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_enc_last_block, Xs```" is expected: `last_block_len <- Xs`, the bit length of the final block of the plaintext.
 . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `0` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** {empty}
+
--
In State _Enc_Last_Block_, if `last_block_len` is nonzero, a single Form A
"```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" is expected, which performs:

 * `tmp : bits(b)`

[]
 . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the plaintext may hold.
 . `offset <- offset xor L*`
 . `tmp[last_block_len-1:0] <- enc_blk(key, offset)[last_block_len-1:0]` // PAD in RFC{nbsp}7253
 . `OUTPUT <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ (INPUT[last_block_len-1:0] xor tmp[last_block_len-1:0])`
 . `tmp <- offset xor ocb_pad(INPUT, last_block_len)`
 . `checksum_P <- enc_blk(key, checksum_P xor tmp xor L$) xor hash_A` // tag

If `last_block_len` is zero, a single Form D "```kl.exec Kn```" is expected instead,
which performs:

. `checksum_P <- enc_blk(key, checksum_P xor offset xor L$) xor hash_A`

In both cases the state then transitions to _Enc_Tag_Finalize_.
--

** In State _Enc_Tag_Finalize_, a single Form C "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn```" is expected.
   It performs `OUTPUT <- zeros(b-tag_len) @ checksum_P[tag_len-1:0]`, and the state transitions to _Success_.
   A `KLLEN` smaller than `tag_len` violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

** In State _Decrypt_, (multi-block) ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected to be of Form A.
   Such a ``kl.exec`` decrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`, and absorbs the
   plaintext `OUTPUT` into `checksum_P`, namely:
+
--
  * `tmp : bits(b)`

[]
  . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the ciphertext may hold.
  . `offset <- offset xor L[ntz(index)]`.
  . `tmp <- offset xor dec_blk(key, INPUT xor offset)`
  . `checksum_P <- checksum_P xor tmp`
  . `OUTPUT <- tmp`
  . `index <- index + 1`.
--
** When entering State _Dec_Last_Block_:
 . A Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_dec_last_block, Xs```" is expected: `last_block_len <- Xs`, the bit length of the final block of the ciphertext.
 . `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `0` {le} `Xs` {le} `120` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Dec_Last_Block_, if `last_block_len` is nonzero, a single Form A
"```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" is expected, which performs:
+
--
   * `tmp : bits(b)`

[]
   . If `index` > `MAX_BLOCKS`, then the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_: the block would be one more than the ciphertext may hold.
   . `offset <- offset xor L*`
   . `tmp[last_block_len-1:0] <- enc_blk(key, offset)[last_block_len-1:0]` // PAD in RFC{nbsp}7253
   . `OUTPUT <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ (INPUT[last_block_len-1:0] xor tmp[last_block_len-1:0])`
   . `tmp <- offset xor ocb_pad(OUTPUT, last_block_len)`
   . `checksum_P <- enc_blk(key, checksum_P xor tmp xor L$) xor hash_A` // tag

If `last_block_len` is zero, a single Form D "```kl.exec Kn```" is expected instead,
which performs:

. `checksum_P <- enc_blk(key, checksum_P xor offset xor L$) xor hash_A`

In both cases the state then transitions to _Hash_Verify_.
--

** In State _Hash_Verify_, which the decryption path enters by itself, a single Form B "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" with `KLLEN`
   {ge} `tag_len` completes decryption (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>): it compares the `tag_len` least significant bits of
   `INPUT` with those of `checksum_P` in constant time. If they match, the state transitions to _Success_; otherwise, it
   transitions to _Failure_. A `KLLEN` smaller than `tag_len` violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

[[KLE-OCB-with-nonce-mode]]
=== OCB with Set Nonce

The mode of operation “OCB with a Set Nonce” is a variant of OCB (<<KLE-OCB-mode>>) in which the nonce is
carried in the Provisioning Input, as the IV is in <<KLE-GCM-with-IV-mode>>.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[cols="^8%,<15%,<15%,<62%",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field       | Description
|  ii   | `k` or 64     | `key`       | Key value or System Key Identifier
| iii   | 120           | `N`         | The nonce, in the format of the `INPUT` of the _Set_Aux_Value_ `kl.exec` of <<KLE-OCB-mode>>.
|  iv   | 7             | `N_len`     | The bit length of the nonce.
|   v   | 1             | Padding     | {empty}
|===
--

* *Internal State*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for OCB.

Provisioning sets `N_len` from the PI, and sets `N` from the PI as the _Set_Aux_Value_ `kl.exec` of <<KLE-OCB-mode>> sets it from its `INPUT`.
An `N_len` outside [6, 120] causes the locker to transition to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
--

* *Serialized Content*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for OCB.
--

* *States and Allowed Transitions:* {empty}
+
--
Same as for OCB, with the following exceptions:

** There is no state _Set_Aux_Value_. The transition is from _Ready_ to _Hash_Absorb_.
--

* *State Machine Behavior:* {empty}
+
--
The differences with respect to OCB are:

 ** The Form B `kl.setst` that sets `tag_len` and transitions to State _Hash_Absorb_ is issued in State _Ready_.
 ** This Machine does not use `HasN`: bit 0 of _StateExtension_ stays 0, and entering State _Hash_Absorb_ requires no `HasN`, since provisioning and import check `N_len` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
 ** Entering State _Ready_ clears neither `N` nor `N_len`: a later message uses the same nonce. Software that needs another nonce provisions another locker.
--

This Machine is not a `kl.derive` destination (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>): its key and its nonce are configured together, by provisioning or import.

NOTE: This Machine protects the confidentiality of the nonce, not against its reuse (Rule <<KLE-nonce-reuse>>).
A key that software knows, derived into the key field while the nonce is kept, would let it recover the nonce.

[[KLE-CMAC-mode]]
=== CMAC Functions

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, CMAC)))
CMAC is a message authentication code built on a block cipher.
Tetsu Iwata and Kaoru Kurosawa designed it, and it became a NIST
Recommendation in May 2005 cite:[nist-SP-800-38B].

Parameters::

* `b` = 64 or 128, the block size of the underlying block cipher (cite:[nist-SP-800-38B] {sect}5.3).
* `k` = key size (8|`k`).
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key`: `k` bits.
 ** `hash`: `b` bits.
 ** `last_block_len`: an integer in the range 0 to `b`, serialized on 32 bits.
 ** `tag_len`: the number of bits that _Hash_Verify_ compares, an integer in the range 64 to `b`, or 0 before it is set; serialized on 32 bits.
 ** `absorbed_any`: a boolean, set once a block has been absorbed in _Hash_Absorb_.
 ** `absorbed_all`: a boolean, set once the last block has been absorbed.

* *MachinePolicy*: bit 0 permits generating a CMAC (_Hash_Output_), and bit 1 verifying one (_Hash_Verify_); at least one bit must be set (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
  A locker with only bit 1 set thus verifies CMACs but cannot produce them.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|    i  | `k` or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|   ii  | `b`           | `hash`
|  iii  | 32            | `last_block_len`
|   iv  | 32            | `tag_len`
|    v  | 1 (boolean)   | `absorbed_any`
|   vi  | 1 (boolean)   | `absorbed_all`
|===
--

//

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `enc_blk(key, p) -> c` encrypts block `p` using the key `key`, and returns the result `c`.
* `double(S)` = `bswap(dbl(bswap(S)))`, the doubling of cite:[nist-SP-800-38B] {sect}5.3, where `dbl(V)` = `V << 1` if
  `V[b-1]` = 0, and `(V << 1) xor (zeros(b-8) @ R~b~)` otherwise, with `R~128~` = `0b10000111`
  (`x^128^ + x^7^ + x^2^ + x + 1`) and `R~64~` = `0b00011011` (`x^64^ + x^4^ + x^3^ + x + 1`).
  For `b` = 128, `dbl` is the `update_mask` of <<KLE-XEX-XTS-modes>>, and `double` the OCB3 doubling of <<KLE-OCB-mode>>.
* `gen_subkeys(K) -> (K1,K2)` generates subkeys `K1` and `K2` from the master key `K` as follows:

 . `L <- enc_blk(K, zeros(b))`
 . `K1 <- double(L)`
 . `K2 <- double(K1)`
 . return `(K1, K2)`.


State Machine::

* *States:*
 ** _Ready_: The CC is ready.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Absorb data into the CMAC function.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_: Set the length of the last block to absorb, then absorb it.
 ** _Hash_Output_: Output the CMAC.
 ** _Hash_Verify_: Compare the CMAC with a value supplied by software, without disclosing it.
 ** _Success_: The CC has completed the CMAC operation.
 ** _Failure_: A CMAC verification has failed.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** Entering State _Ready_:
  *** `last_block_len <- 0`
  *** `tag_len <- 0`
  *** `hash <- zeros(b)`
  *** `absorbed_any <- false`
  *** `absorbed_all <- false`

 ** When entering State _Hash_Absorb_, the `kl.setst` is of Form A.

 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, a Form B "`kl.exec Kn, INPUT`", which may span several blocks, absorbs each `b`-bit block `P` of `INPUT` in turn (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>):
+
--
   . `hash <- enc_blk(key, hash xor P)`.
   . `absorbed_any <- true`.
--
 ** When entering State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_:
+
--
. a Form B "`kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_last_block, Xs`", where `Xs` is the bit length of the last block, sets `last_block_len <- Xs`;
. `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `Xs` {le} `b` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>); `Xs` = 0 denotes the empty message, and requires `absorbed_any` = `false`;
. if `Xs` = 0, the empty final block is absorbed at once, without a `kl.exec` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transfer-window-checks>>): `(K1, K2) <- gen_subkeys(key)`, `hash <- enc_blk(key, hash xor (zeros(b-8) @ 0b10000000) xor K2)`, and `absorbed_all <- true`.

Any other `Xs`, or `Xs` = 0 with `absorbed_any` = `true`, transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--

 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, a single Form B "`kl.exec Kn, INPUT`" absorbs the last block.
    It uses only the `last_block_len` least significant bits of `INPUT` and ignores the others.
+
--
  . `(K1, K2) <- gen_subkeys(key)`.
  . if `last_block_len` = `b`, then:
    .. `tmp <- hash xor INPUT[b-1:0] xor K1`
  . else, that is if `0` < `last_block_len` < `b`:
    .. `tmp <-` +
`hash xor (zeros(b - 8 - last_block_len) @ 0b10000000 @ INPUT[last_block_len-1:0])` +
`xor K2`
  . `hash <- enc_blk(key, tmp)`
  . `absorbed_all <- true`

A `kl.exec` with `absorbed_all` = `true`, or a `KLLEN` smaller than `last_block_len` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>), transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--

** From State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, software outputs or verifies the CMAC.

** To output the CMAC where _MachinePolicy_[0] is set (following Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>):
+
--
. a Form A `kl.setst` with `#immed7` = `#kl_state_hash_output` enters State _Hash_Output_;
. a single Form C ``kl.exec`` with `KLLEN` {ge} 64 writes `hash` into `OUTPUT` and clears the bits beyond the `b`-th (Rule <<KLE-MGR-clear-output>>).
  With `KLLEN` < `b`, it emits the first `KLLEN`/8 bytes: the truncated tag of cite:[nist-SP-800-38B] {sect}6.2;
  as a `kl.derive` source (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>), _Hash_Output_ supplies the `b`/8 bytes of `hash`;
. the CC transitions to State _Success_.

That `kl.setst` with `absorbed_all` = `false`, or a `KLLEN` smaller than 64 (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>), transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--

** To verify the CMAC where _MachinePolicy_[1] is set (following Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>):
+
--
. a Form B `kl.setst` with `#immed7` = `#kl_state_hash_verify` and auxiliary parameter `Xs`
  enters State _Hash_Verify_ and sets `tag_len <- min(Xs, b)`; `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `64` {le} `Xs`;
. in State _Hash_Verify_, a single Form B `kl.exec` with `KLLEN` {ge} `tag_len` compares `INPUT[tag_len-1:0]` with `hash[tag_len-1:0]` in constant time (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>).
  `hash[tag_len-1:0]` is the first `tag_len`/8 bytes of the CMAC, MSB~Tlen~ of cite:[nist-SP-800-38B] {sect}6.2;
. the CC transitions to State _Success_ if they match, and to _Failure_ otherwise.

That `kl.setst` with `absorbed_all` = `false` or with any other `Xs`, or a `KLLEN` smaller than `tag_len` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>) transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--

//

Pseudocode is given in <<Zkl-pseudocode.adoc#KLE-CMAC-mode-example>>.

[[KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs]]
=== Hash and MAC Functions, and XOFs

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, general hashing)))
(((KLE, Machine, MAC functions)))
(((KLE, Machine, XOF)))
This section defines an interface for hash functions, MAC functions and XOFs.
No interface fits every construction,
but this one covers the most common, such as SHA-2 cite:[nist-fips-180-4], SHA-3, and SHAKE cite:[nist-fips-202].

Parameters::

* `b` = block size (8|`b`): the size of an absorbed block, which may differ from the size of the internal state. For a sponge function, `b` is the _rate_.
* `t` = tag length (8|`t`): the size of the digest, or of each block that an XOF outputs. This description assumes `t` {le} `b`.
* `n` = size of the internal state kept by the function (8|`n`). For a sponge function, `n-b` is the _capacity_ of the function.
* `k` = key size (8|`k`). Present only if keyed, i.e., a MAC.
* `state_offset` = offset into the state at which the block is absorbed.
* `tag_offset` = offset of the tag into the state, 0 unless the Machine states otherwise.
  A Machine must satisfy `tag_offset` + `t` {le} `b` for a sponge, so that no output bit comes from the capacity,
  and `tag_offset` + `t` {le} `n` otherwise.
* `g` = granularity, set by each Machine.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)             | Field
.2+|  ii  | If keyed: `k` or 64  | `key` or System Key Identifier
<|          If unkeyed: 0          | none: the Provisioning Input is the MDH only
|===
--

* *MachinePolicy*: unless the instantiating Machine states otherwise, no State is gated by _MachinePolicy_
  (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and a non-zero value is unsupported Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).

* *Internal State*:
 ** `key`: `k` bits. Present only if keyed, i.e., a MAC.
 ** `state`: `n` bits.
 ** `last_block_len`: int. Present only if the Hash/MAC processes a final block specially, beyond the padding and length encoding that the caller supplies.
 ** `input_base`/`output_base` (same field): int. Offset within the input or output at which to read or write.
 ** `block_base`: int. Length of the data absorbed so far into the current block. Present only if the algorithm of the Machine requires it.
 ** `cumul_len`: int. Cumulative length of the data absorbed so far.
 ** `block`: `b` bits. Present only if a partial block is kept separately, for instance when inputs are not absorbed directly into `state`.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[cols="^6%,<21%,<18%,<55%",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)          | Field             | Notes
.2+| i  | If keyed: `k` or 64  | `key` or SKID     | --
<|        If unkeyed: 0        | none              | --
|   ii | `n`                  | `state`           | --
|  iii | 16                   | `last_block_len`    | Present only if the Hash/MAC processes a final block specially, beyond the padding and length encoding that the caller supplies.
|   iv | 16                    | `block_base`     | Amount of data, in bits, stored so far into `block` or accumulated (usually XORed) into `state`.  Only if used by the Machine, else zero padding.
|    v | 16                    | Padding          | --
|   vi |  16                   | Padding          |--
|   vii | 64                   | `cumul_len`       | Cumulative length of the data absorbed so far; its width limits the length of a message (Rule <<KLE-MGR-caller-limits>>). Present only if incorporated into the hash by the Machine, or under HMAC (<<KLE-HMAC>>), else absent.
|  viii | `b`                  | `block`           | Only if `block_base` is defined and the block is stored separately from the `state`.
|===

`input_base`/`output_base` is not serialized: it is set to 8 {interpunct} `klstart` at the start of every `kl.exec`.
--

//

NOTE: An unused `last_block_len` or `block_base` is zero padding; an unused `cumul_len` is absent.
If all three are unused, rows iii to vii are omitted, as is `block`.

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `process_block()`: Process `block` into the hash or MAC.
* `process_last_block()`: Process the final `block` into the hash or MAC, as the algorithm
  processes its last block.
* `finalize()`: Process `state`, in place, so that it holds the digest, or the first `t` bits of XOF output,
  at `tag_offset`.
* `update()`: Update `state` during the output stage.

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ (optionally), _Hash_Output_ and _Success_.

 ** _Ready_: The CC is ready.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Absorb data into the hash function.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_: Set the length of the last block to absorb, then absorb it.
    Not every Machine has this State.
 ** _Hash_Output_: Output the hash.
 ** _Success_: The CC has completed the operation (XOFs do not have this state).

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> { _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> } _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
 ** If the Machine is an XOF, there is no transition from _Hash_Output_ to _Success_.
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** Upon entering State _Ready_:
  *** `last_block_len`, `block_base`, `cumul_len` and `block` are set to zero.
  *** `state`: the initial value is set as specified by the Machine.

 ** State _Hash_Absorb_ invokes macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>) as: +
`{fournbsp}PROCESS_VLI(max_len, block, b, state, n, input_base, block_base,` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp} state_offset, cumul_len, process_block(), None, mode, g)`, +
where:
+
--
** `max_len` is the total length of the data to be absorbed, in bits, as the Machine defines it, or 0 where the Machine fixes none (<<KLE-process-VLI>>);
** `state_offset` is Machine-defined;
** `finalize()` is `None` in this invocation:
     it is performed once, on transitioning to State _Hash_Output_, either from this State or from _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_;
** `mode` is:
*** `assign` for a construction that fills a separate `block` and then processes it into `state` through `process_block()`, and
*** `xor_accumulate` for one that absorbs the input directly into `state`, as a sponge does; each instantiation states which it uses.
--
** When entering State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_:
+
--
 . a Form B "`kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_last_block, Xs`", where `Xs` is the bit length of the last block, sets `last_block_len <- Xs`;
 . `block_base` must be 0, and `Xs` a multiple of 8 (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>) with `8` {le} `Xs` {le} `b`.

A non-zero `block_base`, or any other `Xs`, transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--

** State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ follows _Hash_Absorb_, except that:
 *** `last_block_len` replaces `b` in steps 3.a and 3.g of <<KLE-process-VLI>>.
 *** `process_last_block()` replaces `process_block()` in step 3.g.
 *** The instruction ends after `process_last_block()`, having absorbed one block,
     and the state transitions to _Hash_Output_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 *** The whole last block comes in a single `kl.exec`: a `KLLEN` smaller than `last_block_len` violates the
     granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>), and the only interruption point is 0
     (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>).

** A Machine that invokes `PROCESS_VLI` with `last_block_state` = _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ follows
   <<KLE-process-VLI>> for that State in place of the two items above, and enters _Hash_Output_ from it by the
   `kl.setst` that enters _Hash_Output_ from _Hash_Absorb_.

** In a Machine without _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, or one that passes it as `last_block_state`, the `kl.setst` from
   _Hash_Absorb_ into _Hash_Output_ is of Form A, unless the Machine states otherwise.

** In State _Hash_Output_, hash functions and XOFs behave differently.
 *** Upon entering the state, the following operations are first performed:
 . `finalize()`, if it is not `None`
 . `block_base <- 0`
*** For each Form C `kl.exec` instruction "`kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn`", the
    following operations are performed:
+
--
    * `amount : int`, `{nbsp}` amount of data to copy from `state` to `OUTPUT`

[]
   . `output_base <- 8 {interpunct} klstart`: 0 when the instruction starts.
    . while (`output_base` < `KLLEN`) do:
     .. `amount <- min(KLLEN - output_base, t - block_base)`
     .. ``OUTPUT[output_base + amount - 1 : output_base] <- `` +
           ``{fournbsp}state[tag_offset + block_base + amount - 1 : tag_offset + block_base]``
     .. `output_base <- output_base + amount`
     .. `block_base <- block_base + amount`
     .. if (`block_base` = `t`) then:
      ... for a hash function, any bits of `OUTPUT` beyond `output_base` are cleared,
          the state transitions to _Success_, and the instruction returns.
      ... `update()`
      ... `block_base <- 0`
      ... *Here* the instruction can be interrupted, with `klstart <- output_base / 8`.
          A halt here leaves `block_base` = 0.
--
+
NOTE: The interruption points of `kl.exec` in _Hash_Output_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-interruption-point>>) are, for an XOF, 0 and every byte offset at which `block_base` is 0; a hash function, which never reaches `update()`, has 0 only.

[[KLE-SHA-2]]
=== SHA-2 Family Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, SHA-2)))
The primitives of the SHA-2 family cite:[nist-fips-180-4] — SHA-224, SHA-256, SHA-384,
SHA-512, SHA-512/224, and SHA-512/256 — are instantiations of the algorithm specified in
<<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>, and only the differences with respect to the behavior described
there are given here.

Parameters::

* The word size `w`, and `b`, `n`, and `t`, as given in <<KLE-SHA-2-parameters>>; the
  internal state consists of the eight `w`-bit chaining variables of
  cite:[nist-fips-180-4].
* Granularity: 32 bits, the smallest element width that may be used for the vector
  inputs of `kl.exec`.

[[KLE-SHA-2-parameters]]
.Parameters of the SHA-2 family
[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="<,^,^,^,^",options="header"]
|===
| Function    | `w` | `b`  | `n`  | `t`
| SHA-224     |  32 |  512 |  256 | 224
| SHA-256     |  32 |  512 |  256 | 256
| SHA-384     |  64 | 1024 |  512 | 384
| SHA-512     |  64 | 1024 |  512 | 512
| SHA-512/224 |  64 | 1024 |  512 | 224
| SHA-512/256 |  64 | 1024 |  512 | 256
|===


Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*: the MDH only (<<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>, unkeyed).

* *Internal State*:

   ** `state`: `n` bits, holding the eight chaining variables `H~i~` big-endian:
      `state[(i+1)w-1 : iw]` = `bswap(bin(H~i~, w))`, for `0` {le} `i` < 8.
   ** `block` (`b` bits).
   ** `block_base`.
   ** `cumul_len` only when operating under HMAC (<<KLE-HMAC>>).

* *Serialized Content*: as prescribed by <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>, with `last_block_len`
  unused (replaced by padding) and `block` present.

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `process_block()`: applies the compression function of cite:[nist-fips-180-4] {sect}6 for the
  selected function to absorb the current `block` into `state`, message word `j` being
  `int(bswap(block[(j+1)w-1 : jw]))`, for `0` {le} `j` < 16.
* `finalize()`: `None` for stand-alone hashing — padding and length encoding are the
  caller's responsibility. When operating under HMAC, `finalize()` applies the padding of
  cite:[nist-fips-180-4] {sect}5.1 internally; see <<KLE-HMAC>>.

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Output_, and _Success_. There is no
  _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ state: in stand-alone hashing, the caller supplies the padded message as whole blocks.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** Upon entering State _Ready_, `state` is set to the initial hash value of the selected function
    per cite:[nist-fips-180-4] {sect}5.3, and `block_base` and `block` are zeroed.
 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>) is
    entered as
    `PROCESS_VLI(max_len=0, block, b, state, n, input_base, block_base, 0, cumul_len, process_block(), None, mode=assign, 32)`,
    where `cumul_len` is `None` in stand-alone hashing, and tracked under HMAC.
+
NOTE: In stand-alone hashing the input length is neither tracked nor bounded; under HMAC,
`cumul_len` feeds the padding. `mode` is `assign` because the SHA-2 family fills a separate
`block` and compresses it into `state`.
 ** In stand-alone hashing, a `kl.setst` into _Hash_Output_ with `block_base` {ne} 0 transitions the locker
    to Error State _Invalid_: the padded message is a whole number of blocks.
    The check precedes the operations on entering _Hash_Output_, which zero `block_base`.
 ** In State _Hash_Output_, the bytes emitted are the chaining variables in the big-endian encoding,
    truncated to `t` bits; once `t` bits have been made available, the state transitions to _Success_.

[[KLE-SM3]]
=== SM3 Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, SM3)))
The ShangMi 3 hash function, standardized as GB/T 32905-2016 cite:[GBT-32905-2016], follows exactly the rules
of the SHA-2 family (<<KLE-SHA-2>>) with `w` = 32, `b` = 512, `n` = 256, and `t` = 256.
It has the big-endian representation, padding rule, word mapping, `state` layout and state machine of SHA-256.
Its initial hash value and compression function are those of the SM3 standard.
Under HMAC (<<KLE-HMAC>>, `SM3_HMAC`), SM3 behaves as SHA-256 does under HMAC:
`cumul_len` is tracked and serialized, and `K0` is `b` = 512 bits.

[[KLE-BLAKE2]]
=== BLAKE2 Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, BLAKE2)))
BLAKE2b and BLAKE2s cite:[RFC7693], unkeyed or keyed, instantiate <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>; only the
differences are given here.
`nn` and `kk` are the digest and key lengths in bytes.

Parameters::

* `w`, `r` (the rounds of `F`), `b`, `n`, `nn~max~`, `kk~max~` and the rotation constants of `G`, as in
  <<KLE-BLAKE2-parameters>>; `t` = 8 {interpunct} `nn`.
* Granularity: 8 bits.

[[KLE-BLAKE2-parameters]]
.Parameters of BLAKE2
[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="<,^,^,^,^,^,^,^",options="header"]
|===
| Function | `w` | `r` | `b`  | `n` | `nn~max~` | `kk~max~` | (`R1`, `R2`, `R3`, `R4`)
| BLAKE2b  |  64 |  12 | 1024 | 512 |    64     |    64     | (32, 24, 16, 63)
| BLAKE2s  |  32 |  10 |  512 | 256 |    32     |    32     | (16, 12, 8, 7)
|===

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*: the MDH only, or, with a system key (_KeyType_ = 1):
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits) | Field
|  ii  | 64          | System Key Identifier
| iii  | 64          | zero padding
|===

`kk` is the length of the key that the SKS returns; a length outside 1 to `kk~max~` transitions the locker to Error State
_Invalid_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-system-keys>>). When an import resolves the SKID again, a key whose length differs
from the restored `kk` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
_KeyType_ = 2 is invalid: the Machine holds no key by value.
A caller with its own key absorbs it (see below).
--

* *MachinePolicy*: without a system key, as in <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>.
  With a system key, the Machine is a MAC that only generates tags: bit 0 enables _Hash_Output_
  (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>); _MachinePolicy_ = 0 is invalid Metadata, and a set bit 1 is unsupported Metadata
  (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).

* *Internal State*:
 ** `nn`, `kk`.
 ** `state`: the words `h~i~` of cite:[RFC7693], little-endian: `state[(i+1)w-1 : iw]` = `bin(h~i~, w)`, for `0` {le} `i` < 8.
 ** `block`, `block_base`, `last_block_len`, and `cumul_len`, which also counts a key block, so that `cumul_len`/8 is the
    counter of cite:[RFC7693] {sect}3.2.
 ** `syskey`: with a system key, the key that the SKID resolves to, zero-padded to `b` bits. It is not serialized: the
    SKID in Position i of the Serialized Content stands for it (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>).

* *Serialized Content*: as in <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>, with the SKID, if any, in Position i, and `nn` and `kk`
  in Positions v and vi.

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `F(f)`: the compression function of cite:[RFC7693] {sect}3.2 on `state`, with message words
  `m~j~` = `int(block[(j+1)w-1 : jw])`, the counter `cumul_len`/8, and the final-block flag `f`.
* `process_block()`: `F(false)`.
* `process_last_block()`: zeroes `block[b-1 : last_block_len]`, then performs `F(true)`.
* `finalize()`: `None`.

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, _Hash_Output_, and _Success_.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> { _Hash_Absorb_ -> } _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
    The `kl.exec` of _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ makes the transition to _Hash_Output_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 ** _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Hash_Output_, for a message that ends in `block`: a `kl.setst` into _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ with
    `Xs` = 0 enters _Hash_Output_ directly.
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Output_, for the empty message: a `kl.setst` into _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ with `Xs` = 0 enters
    _Hash_Output_ directly; a `kl.setst` naming _Hash_Output_ transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior*, where it differs from <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>:
 ** When entering State _Ready_:
+
--
. `nn` {leftarrow} `nn~max~`, `kk` {leftarrow} 0 or the length of the system key, and `block` {leftarrow} `zeros(b)`;
. `state` is the IV of cite:[RFC7693] {sect}2.6, with `h~0~` XORed with
  `0x01010000` {oplus} `(kk << 8)` {oplus} `nn` ({sect}2.5, {sect}3.3).
--
 ** In State _Ready_, setting the parameters is an operation, not a State (Rule <<KLE-operation-immediates>>):
+
--
. a Form B `kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value, Xs` sets `nn` {leftarrow} `Xs[7:0]` and, without a system key,
  `kk` {leftarrow} `Xs[15:8]`, and recomputes `h~0~`;
. `Xs` must satisfy 1 {le} `Xs[7:0]` {le} `nn~max~` and `Xs[15:8]` {le} `kk~max~`, with `Xs[15:8]` = 0 with a
  system key, and its bits above bit 15 must be 0.

Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--
 ** When leaving State _Ready_ with a system key, for _Hash_Absorb_ or for _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ with `Xs` > 0,
    the `kl.setst` compresses the key block: `block` {leftarrow} `syskey`, `cumul_len` {leftarrow} `b`, `F(false)`, and
    `block` {leftarrow} `zeros(b)`.
+
NOTE: `syskey` enters `block` only within that `kl.setst`, so that no Serialized Content holds it
(Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>).
 ** A caller with its own key sets `kk` by the _Set_Aux_Value_ operation, and absorbs the key, zero-padded to `b` bits,
    as the first block of the message.
    For an empty message, that block is the final block.
 ** From _Ready_, a final-block length `Xs` = 0 denotes the empty message:
+
--
. with a system key, the `kl.setst` performs
  `block` {leftarrow} `syskey`, `cumul_len` {leftarrow} `b`, `F(true)` and `block` {leftarrow} `zeros(b)`;
. without a system key and with `kk` = 0, it applies `F(true)` to the zero `block`;
. it then transitions to _Hash_Output_.

Without a system key and with `kk` {ne} 0, it transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
--
 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, `PROCESS_VLI` is invoked with `max_len` = 0, `state_offset` = 0 and `mode` = `assign`,
    except that step 3.g is postponed: a `block` filled to `block_base` = `b` is kept, and compressed by `F(false)`,
    with `block_base` {leftarrow} 0, only when the next byte of the message is absorbed into it;
    _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ processes its block at once.
    The interruption points of `kl.exec` in _Hash_Absorb_ are 0 and every byte offset at which `block_base` is `b`.
 ** From _Hash_Absorb_, a Form B `kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_hash_last_block, Xs`:
+
--
. with `Xs` = 0 and `block_base` > 0, ends the message held in `block`: `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `block_base`,
  `process_last_block()`, `block_base` {leftarrow} 0 and `last_block_len` {leftarrow} 0, and the locker transitions to
  _Hash_Output_;
. with `Xs` > 0 and `block_base` = `b`, first performs `F(false)` and `block_base` {leftarrow} 0, and then proceeds as in
  <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>.

With `Xs` = 0 and `block_base` = 0, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
--

[[KLE-HMAC]]
=== HMAC

Hash function Machines suffice to compute HMACs, but an environment may want to hide the HMAC key.
This section extends <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>> accordingly.

Under HMAC, the Machine, not the caller, performs the padding and length encoding of the hash function, at finalization.
For SHA-2 (<<KLE-SHA-2>>) and SM3 (<<KLE-SM3>>), it applies the standard padding over the total absorbed length, computed from `cumul_len`, which is maintained under HMAC: `b` + `cumul_len` bits for the inner hash, and `b` + `t` bits for the outer.
For SHA-3, the padding of <<KLE-SHA-3>> applies.

Parameters::
+
--
* `H` = the underlying hash function.
* `b` = the input block size of `H`: its parameter `b`, which for SHA-3 is the rate (<<KLE-SHA-3-parameters>>).
* `t` = the digest size: the parameter `t` of `H` (<<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>).
* Granularity: same as that of the underlying hash function.

The two padding constants of cite:[nist-fips-198-1] are:

* `ipad` = the byte `0x36` repeated `b`/8 times, and
* `opad` = the byte `0x5c` repeated `b`/8 times,

both `b` bits long. All bytes of each constant are equal, so its
representation does not depend on the byte order of <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>>.
--

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
Each HMAC Machine has two variants, which the _Machine_ field distinguishes (<<KLE-exec-encodings>>): by _Type_ for the SHA-2 and SHA-3 HMACs, and by _Mode_ for `SM3_HMAC`.

The PI of the variant that starts with “No Initial Key” (NIK) contains only the MDH.

The PI of the variant with the “Key in PI” (KIP) has the following format:

[cols="^7%,<23%,<70%",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)               | Field
|  ii  | `b` or 64                 | `K0`, the key already reduced to `b` bits (see below), or a System Key Identifier.
|===

The key provisioned or held in a CC is the value `K0` of cite:[nist-fips-198-1] {sect}4, Steps 1 to 3: the key `K` padded with zeros to `b` bits, or, if `K` is longer than `b` bits, `H(K)` padded with zeros to `b` bits.
Whoever provisions the key derives `K0` from it.
With _KeyType_ = 1, the SKS returns `K`, and the KLE unit forms `K0` from it by the same Steps.
--

* *Internal State*:
  ** `hash_fn_state`: the entire state of `H`, with the following fields:
  *** `state`: `n` bits.
  *** `last_block_len`: int. Present only if the underlying hash function has State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_.
  *** `input_base`: int. Offset within input/output to read from/write to.
  *** `block_base`: int. Length of absorbed data so far for the current block. Only if needed by the Machine.
  *** `cumul_len`: int. Cumulative length of data absorbed so far, tracked by every HMAC Machine (for the `K0` load in _Set_Key_, and for the padding of SHA-2 and SM3).
  *** `block`: `b` bits.  Included only if a partial block is maintained—for instance when the inputs are not directly absorbed into `state`.
 ** `K0`: `b` bits, the value `K0` as per cite:[nist-fips-198-1] {sect}4, Steps 1 to 3.
 ** `tag_len`: the number of bits that _Hash_Verify_ compares, 64 to `t`, or 0 before it is set.
 ** `HasKey` (NIK variant only): Boolean, bit 0 of _StateExtension_, recording that `K0` holds a completely loaded key (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).
    The KIP variant does not use _StateExtension_.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[cols="^7%,<23%,<70%",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)              | Field
|  i   | variable                 | Serialized Content of the underlying hash function, in its entirety, including its own padding to a multiple of 128 bits.
| ii   | `b` or 64                | `K0`, or a System Key Identifier.
| iii  | 16                       | `tag_len`
|===
--

//

State Machine:: {empty}
+
--
The NIK variant inserts State _Set_Key_ between _Ready_ and _Hash_Absorb_; both variants add States _Hash_Verify_ and
_Failure_.

The two variants differ only in how the key first reaches the CC.

In the KIP variant, `K0` is carried in the Provisioning Input.
The key is given by value (_KeyType_ = 0), as an SKID (_KeyType_ = 1), or as `b` random bits (_KeyType_ = 2).
A KIP CC cannot be re-keyed.

In the NIK variant, the Provisioning Input carries no key. `K0` must be loaded in State _Set_Key_ before _Hash_Absorb_ can be entered:
entering _Hash_Absorb_ requires `HasKey` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and leaving _Set_Key_ before the load completes zeroes `K0` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).
A loaded key is kept across a return to _Ready_, so that one load of `K0` serves any number of messages.
--
+
* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** For the NIK variant: _Ready_ -> _Set_Key_ -> _Hash_Absorb_, and _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ once `K0` is loaded
    (`HasKey`); then as in the underlying hash function.
 ** For the KIP variant: _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_; then as in the underlying hash function.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_, or _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ where `H` has it, -> _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).
 ** _Hash_Output_ is enabled by _MachinePolicy_[0], and _Hash_Verify_ by
    _MachinePolicy_[1]; the other States by neither (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** When entering _Ready_: `tag_len` {leftarrow} 0.

 ** When entering State _Set_Key_:
  . `K0` is zeroed, `HasKey` is cleared, and `input_base`, `block_base` and `cumul_len` are set to zero.
  . Re-entering _Set_Key_, also by a same-State `kl.setst` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>), restarts
    the load and replaces the key.

 ** While in State _Set_Key_:
  . one or more Form B ``kl.exec`` instructions load the `b`-bit `K0` through macro `PROCESS_VLI`
    (<<KLE-process-VLI>>), entered as
+
`{fournbsp}` `PROCESS_VLI(max_len=b, block=K0, b, state=K0, n=b,` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `input_base, block_base, state_offset=0, cumul_len,` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `process_block=None, finalize=None, mode=assign, g)`,
+
where `g` is the granularity of `H`;
  . when `cumul_len` reaches `b`, the load is complete and `HasKey` is set;
  . a `kl.derive` into `K0` (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>) transfers a whole key `K` of `eff_length` {le} `b`/8 bytes
    (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> item 5): it requires `klstart` = 0 and a source field, or remaining `kl.exec` output,
    of exactly `eff_length` bytes, which excludes the output of an XOF, and otherwise transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_;
    it replaces all of `K0`, whatever a `kl.exec` loaded before it: `K0` {leftarrow} `K` zero-filled to `b` bits (cite:[nist-fips-198-1] {sect}4, Step 3), `block_base` {leftarrow} 0,
    `cumul_len` {leftarrow} `b`, and `HasKey` is set.

 ** When entering _Hash_Absorb_:
  . `state` is set to the initial value of the underlying hash function.
  . `block`, `block_base` and `cumul_len` are set to zero, and, where `H` has State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_,
    `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `b`.
  . `absorb(K0 xor ipad)`, not counted in `cumul_len`, where `absorb(x)` absorbs `x` as a `kl.exec` in
    _Hash_Absorb_ of `H` does.

 ** While in _Hash_Absorb_, the message is absorbed as in `H`.
** When entering State _Hash_Output_ or _Hash_Verify_, the following steps replace the operations of `H` on entering
   _Hash_Output_:
+
--
   * local `inner : bits(t)`

[]
  . `finalize()` of `H`, which leaves the inner hash `H((K0 xor ipad) {vvert} message)` in
    `state[t+tag_offset-1 : tag_offset]`.
  . `inner <- state[t+tag_offset-1 : tag_offset]`.
  . Reinitialize `state` to the initial value of the underlying hash function `H`, and set `block`, `block_base` and
    `cumul_len` to zero.
  . `absorb(K0 xor opad)` as per `H`, not counted in `cumul_len`.
  . `absorb(inner)` as per `H`, which leaves `cumul_len` = `t`, so that the outer hash digests
    `(K0 xor opad) {vvert} H((K0 xor ipad) {vvert} message)`, as
    cite:[nist-fips-198-1] {sect}4 requires.
  . `finalize()` of `H`, and `block_base` {leftarrow} 0. The `t`-bit HMAC value is then output in
    _Hash_Output_ as by `H`, or verified in _Hash_Verify_.
--

** To verify an HMAC where _MachinePolicy_[1] is set (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>):
   . a Form B `kl.setst` with `#immed7` = `#kl_state_hash_verify` enters State _Hash_Verify_ and sets
     `tag_len` {leftarrow} `min(Xs, t)`; `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `64` {le} `Xs`;
   . in State _Hash_Verify_, a single Form B `kl.exec` with `KLLEN` {ge} `tag_len` compares `INPUT[tag_len-1:0]` with
     `state[tag_offset+tag_len-1 : tag_offset]`, the first `tag_len`/8 bytes of the HMAC, in constant time
     (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>);
   . if they match, the state transitions to _Success_, otherwise to _Failure_.
+
Any other `Xs`, or a `KLLEN` smaller than `tag_len` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>), transitions the locker to Error
State _Invalid_.

[[KLE-SHA-3]]
=== SHA-3 Family Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, SHA-3)))
The SHA-3 family currently comprises SHA3-224, SHA3-256, SHA3-384 and SHA3-512, written SHA3-``N`` below, and the XOFs SHAKE128 and SHAKE256.
All are parameter choices of KECCAK cite:[DBLP-conf-eurocrypt-BertoniDPA13], standardized by NIST cite:[nist-fips-202].
They are instantiations of the Machine of <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>; this section gives only the differences.

//

Parameters::

* `n` = 1600.
* `c` = capacity, `b` = rate = 1600 - `c`, and `t` = the number of output bits taken from
  `state` per squeeze, all as given in <<KLE-SHA-3-parameters>>.
* Granularity: 8 bits.

[[KLE-SHA-3-parameters]]
.Parameters of the SHA-3 family
[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="<,^,^,^,^,<",options="header"]
|===
| Function |  `c` | `b` = 1600 - `c` | `t` | XOF? | Suffix `D`
| SHA3-224 |  448 |       1152       |  224 |   no |   `01`
| SHA3-256 |  512 |       1088       |  256 |   no |   `01`
| SHA3-384 |  768 |        832       |  384 |   no |   `01`
| SHA3-512 | 1024 |        576       |  512 |   no |   `01`
| SHAKE128 |  256 |       1344       | 1344 |  yes | `1111`
| SHAKE256 |  512 |       1088       | 1088 |  yes | `1111`
|===

`D` is written in the order of cite:[nist-fips-202], first bit leftmost: for SHA3-``N``, `D[0]` = 0 and `D[1]` = 1.

For the four fixed-output functions, `t` is the digest length, which is `c`/2, and a
single squeeze suffices. For the two extendable-output functions, `t` is the full rate
`b`, since a sponge makes exactly `b` bits available between successive applications of
the permutation.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*: the MDH only.

* *Internal State*:
 ** `state`: `n` bits.
 ** `input_base`: int.
 ** `block_base`: int.
 ** `last_block_len`: int.
 ** `cumul_len`: int, under HMAC (<<KLE-HMAC>>) only.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^,<,<",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|   i  | 1600            | `state`
|  ii  | 16              | `block_base`, in bits
| iii  | 16              | `last_block_len`, in bits
|  iv  | 64 or 0         | `cumul_len`, under HMAC (<<KLE-HMAC>>) only
|===

`input_base` is not serialized: it is set to 8 {interpunct} `klstart` at the start of every `kl.exec`
(<<KLE-process-VLI>>, step 2), so it carries no state across a context switch.
This layout replaces that of <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>: the padding rows are absent, and
`cumul_len` immediately follows `last_block_len`. The fields are zero-padded to a multiple of 128 bits.
--

//

Machine-Specific Functions:: {empty}
 * `P()`: computes `KECCAK-p[1600, 24]` on `state`, with result in place.
 * `process_block()`: there is no explicit operation to copy data into
   `state`, as the inputs are directly XORed into it; instead, `process_block`
   applies `P()` to the state.
 * `update()`: updates the `state` during the digest output stage.  It applies `P()` to the state.

//

State Machine:: {empty}
* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_,
  _Hash_Output_ and, for SHA3-``N`` only, _Success_.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> { _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> } _Hash_Output_, and, for SHA3-``N``, _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
 ** From any valid state to State _Ready_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** Upon entering State _Ready_, `state` is zeroed.
 ** Upon entering State _Hash_Absorb_, `last_block_len` {leftarrow} `b`.
 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, the input data is XORed directly into `state`,
 through macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>), invoked as: +
`{fournbsp}PROCESS_VLI(max_len=0, state, b, state, n, input_base, block_base, 0,` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}cumul_len, process_block(), None, mode=xor_accumulate, 8,` +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}Hash_Absorb_Last_Block, None, last_block_len)`, +
 where `cumul_len` is `None` in stand-alone hashing, and tracked under HMAC.
 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, a final piece of 1 to `b` bits is absorbed (<<KLE-process-VLI>>), so that the
    message may have any bit length. cite:[nist-fips-202] orders the bits within a byte as
    <<Zkl-notation.adoc#KLE-Notation>> does, so `last_block_len` need not be a multiple of 8
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
 ** Upon transitioning to State _Hash_Output_, `finalize()`:
 . forms `S` = `D` {vvert} `pad10*1` (cite:[nist-fips-202] {sect}5.1, {sect}B.2), with `D` the suffix of
   <<KLE-SHA-3-parameters>>, `S[0]` its first bit, and `|S|` the smallest length of at least `|D|` + 2 that makes
   `block_base` + `|S|` a multiple of `b`;
 . XORs `S[b-block_base-1 : 0]` into `state[b-1 : block_base]`, and applies `P()`;
 . if `|S|` > `b` - `block_base`, XORs `S[|S|-1 : b-block_base]` into `state[b-1 : 0]`, and applies `P()`;
 . leaves the output in the first `t` bits of the rate.
+
NOTE: `block_base` is less than `b`, since it returns to 0 when it reaches `b`. When `block_base` is a multiple of 8,
`S` fits in the current block: it is the byte `d` at `state[block_base+7 : block_base]`, with `d` = `0x06` for
SHA3-``N`` and `0x1F` for SHAKE128 and SHAKE256, XORed with the byte `0x80` at `state[b-1 : b-8]`.
Otherwise, after a final piece whose length is not a multiple of 8, `S` starts within a byte, and a second block is
needed only when `b` - `block_base` < `|D|` + 2.

 ** In State _Hash_Output_, output is extracted as in <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>:
 . SHA3-``N``: a single `t`-bit digest, after which the locker transitions to _Success_;
 . SHAKE128 and SHAKE256: output of unbounded length; the locker never transitions to
   _Success_.

The rest of the Machine follows <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>.

[[KLE-KMAC]]
=== KMAC128, KMAC256, KMACXOF128, and KMACXOF256

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, KMAC)))
KMAC is the keyed MAC built on cSHAKE, standardized by NIST in cite:[nist-SP-800-185].
It is defined only over the KECCAK permutation of SHA-3, and this section gives only its differences from <<KLE-SHA-3>>.

Parameters::

* `n` = 1600.
* KMAC128 and KMACXOF128 use `c` = 256 and rate `b` = 1344 bits (168 bytes);
  KMAC256 and KMACXOF256 use `c` = 512 and rate `b` = 1088 bits (136 bytes).
* `t` = `b`, as for the extendable-output functions of <<KLE-SHA-3-parameters>>.
* `L` = the output length in bits (_Hash_Output_ of `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`; 0 for `KMACXOF128` and `KMACXOF256`, cite:[nist-SP-800-185] {sect}4.3.1) or the tag length in bits (_Hash_Verify_), set by the `kl.setst` that enters that State.
* Granularity: 8 bits, as for <<KLE-SHA-3>>.

Data Structures::
+
--
A CC does not hold the key `K` and the customization string `S` in their raw form. It
holds the two rate-sized blocks that cite:[nist-SP-800-185] derives from them,
prepared by whoever provisions the CC:

`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `cshake_block` = `bytepad(encode_string("KMAC") {vvert} encode_string(S), b/8)`, and +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `key_block{nbsp}{nbsp}{nbsp}` = `bytepad(encode_string(K), b/8)`,

where `bytepad`, `encode_string` and `left_encode` are as defined in cite:[nist-SP-800-185] {sect}2.3:

[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="<,^,^,^",options="header"]
|===
| Function           | rate `b`/8 | maximum \|`K`\| | maximum \|`S`\|
| KMAC128/KMACXOF128 | 168 bytes | 163 bytes | 157 bytes
| KMAC256/KMACXOF256 | 136 bytes | 131 bytes | 125 bytes
|===

The Machine supports only a `K` and an `S` within these maximums.

NOTE: cite:[nist-SP-800-185] admits a longer `K` or `S`, which `bytepad` spreads over more than one block,
and unlike the key of HMAC, no shorter value computes the same function.

With _KeyType_ = 1, the PI and the SCC carry a System Key Identifier in place of `key_block` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>).
The SKS returns the key `K`, and the KLE unit forms `key_block` from it as above.
A `K` longer than the maximum in the table above transitions the locker to
Error State _Invalid_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-system-keys>>).
With _KeyType_ = 2 (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KeyType-field>>), `K` is a random value of `c`/2 bits,
from which `key_block` is formed in the same way.
`cshake_block` is always carried by value.
--
+
* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | `b`           | `cshake_block`
| iii  | `b` or 64     | `key_block` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*: as for <<KLE-SHA-3>>, with the addition of
 ** `cshake_block`, `key_block`: `b` bits each; and
 ** `L`: 32 bits: the value set by the `kl.setst` into _Hash_Output_ or _Hash_Verify_ (0 for KMACXOF); in _Hash_Output_ of KMAC128 and KMAC256, the number of output bits still to be made available.

* *Serialized Content*: as for <<KLE-SHA-3>>, with `cshake_block`, `key_block` and `L`
  appended, and padding to a multiple of 128 bits. Explicitly:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^,<,<",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits) | Field
|   i  | 1600        | `state`
|  ii  | 16          | `block_base`
| iii  | 16          | `last_block_len`
|  iv  | 32          | Padding
|   v  | `b`         | `cshake_block`
|  vi  | `b` or 64   | `key_block` or System Key Identifier
| vii  | 32          | `L`
| viii | variable    | Padding to a multiple of 128 bits
|===
--

//

Machine-Specific Functions::

 * `P()`, `process_block()` and `update()` are as in <<KLE-SHA-3>>.
 * `right_encode(x)`, as in cite:[nist-SP-800-185] {sect}2.3, is the value `x`
   written as an unsigned big-endian integer on the smallest possible positive number `m`
   of bytes, followed by a single byte holding `m`.

//

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ and _Hash_Output_, and, for `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`
  only, _Success_, _Hash_Verify_ and _Failure_.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** As in <<KLE-SHA-3>>.
 ** For `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`: _Hash_Absorb_ or _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_ -> _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or
    _Failure_.
 ** For `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`, _Hash_Output_ is enabled by _MachinePolicy_[0], and
    _Hash_Verify_ by _MachinePolicy_[1]; the other States by neither. `KMACXOF128` and `KMACXOF256`, which serve as key
    derivation functions, gate no State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).

* *Behavior:* as in <<KLE-SHA-3>>, with the following differences.

 ** When entering _Ready_: `L` {leftarrow} 0.

 ** When leaving _Ready_ for _Hash_Absorb_, the `kl.setst` first:
  . XORs `cshake_block` into the rate and applies `P()`;
  . XORs `key_block` into the rate and applies `P()`.
+
NOTE: The key enters `state` only within that `kl.setst`, so that the Serialized Content of a CC in _Ready_ is the same
on every implementation and holds no key-dependent `state`.

 ** When entering _Hash_Output_ or _Hash_Verify_ from _Hash_Absorb_ or _Hash_Absorb_Last_Block_, the `kl.setst`
    first checks and sets `L`, then performs `finalize()`:
  . for `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`, the `kl.setst` into _Hash_Output_ is of Form B, and sets `L` {leftarrow} `Xs`, the
    output length in bits, with 0 < `Xs` < 2^32^;
  . for `KMACXOF128` and `KMACXOF256`, the `kl.setst` into _Hash_Output_ is of Form A, and sets `L` {leftarrow} 0;
  . for `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`, where _MachinePolicy_[1] is set, the `kl.setst` into _Hash_Verify_ is of Form B,
    and sets `L` {leftarrow} `Xs`, the tag length in bits; `Xs` must be a multiple of 8 with `64` {le} `Xs` {le} `b`;
  . `finalize()` absorbs `right_encode(L)` as a `kl.exec` in _Hash_Absorb_ would, continuing from the current `block_base`
    and applying `P()` whenever `block_base` reaches `b`, then applies the padding of
    <<KLE-SHA-3>>, with the cSHAKE domain-separation suffix `D` = `00` (`d` = `0x04`) in place of the `1111` of the
    raw SHAKE functions.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

 ** In State _Hash_Output_, for `KMAC128` and `KMAC256`, `L` counts the bits still to be made
    available. The output loop of <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>> takes
    `amount` {leftarrow} `min(KLLEN - output_base, t - block_base, L)`, copies and advances as there, and replaces its step
    `if (block_base = t)` with:
+
--
. `L` {leftarrow} `L - amount`.
. If `L` = 0, the bits of `OUTPUT` beyond `output_base` are cleared, the state transitions to _Success_, and the instruction returns.
. If `block_base` = `t`: `update()`, `block_base` {leftarrow} 0, and the instruction can be interrupted here, with `klstart` {leftarrow} `output_base / 8`.
--
+
The output thus consists of {lceil}``L``/8{rceil} bytes for the value of `L` set on entering _Hash_Output_;
the last of them holds the final `L` mod 8 bits, if any, in its least significant bits.
+
For `KMACXOF128` and `KMACXOF256`, arbitrarily many bits can be squeezed,
following the semantics of <<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>, producing `KLLEN` bits for each `kl.exec`,
and the Machine never transitions to _Success_.

 ** In State _Hash_Verify_, a single Form B `kl.exec` with `KLLEN` {ge} `L`:
  . compares `INPUT[L-1:0]` with `state[L-1:0]` in constant time (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>);
  . if they match, the state transitions to _Success_, otherwise to _Failure_.
+
A `KLLEN` smaller than `L` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

In every variant, the interruption points of `kl.exec` in _Hash_Output_ are those of an XOF (<<KLE-hash-functions-MACs-XOFs>>): 0 and every byte offset at which `block_base` is 0.
The output of KMAC128 and KMAC256 may also span several blocks.

[[KLE-Ascon-AEAD128]]
=== Ascon-AEAD128

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, Ascon)))
Ascon is a public permutation cite:[DBLP-journals-joc-DobraunigEMS21] standardized by NIST cite:[nist-SP-800-232] together with various cryptographic algorithms designed around it.

In Ascon-AEAD128, the caller applies ``pad(``{nbsp}{interpunct}``, 128)`` to non-empty associated data, so that the Machine in State _Hash_Absorb_ absorbs a whole number of 128-bit blocks; empty associated data is not padded.
The caller must *not* pad the final block of the plaintext or ciphertext.
It supplies that block in State _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_, together with its bit length `last_block_len`, and the Machine applies ``pad(``{nbsp}{interpunct}``, 128)`` to the final plaintext block internally.
Padding it in software as well corrupts both the ciphertext and the tag.

Parameters::

* `b` = 128.
* `k` = 128.
* Granularity: 128 bits.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | 128 or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
|===
--

* *Internal State*:
  ** `key`: 128 bits.
 ** `state[0 .. 4]`: five 64-bit words, i.e., 320 bits.
 ** `tag_len`: 8 bits as int.
 ** `last_block_len`: 7 bits as int.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)     | Field
|    i | 128 or 64       | `key` or System Key Identifier
|   ii | 64              | `state`[0]
|  iii | 64              | `state`[1]
|   iv | 64              | `state`[2]
|    v | 64              | `state`[3]
|   vi | 64              | `state`[4]
|  vii | 16              | `last_block_len`
| viii | 16              | `tag_len`
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::
* `ASCON(p)` applies the permutation `Ascon-p[p]` of cite:[nist-SP-800-232], of `p` rounds, to the `state`.
* `pad(x,r)` appends the bit 1 to the bit string `x`, followed by the bit string `0^j^` where `j` = `(- |x| - 1) mod r`: `pad(x,r) = 0^j^ @ 1 @ x`.

State Machine::

* *States:*
 ** _Ready_: The CC is ready.
 ** _Hash_Absorb_: Set the nonce, and process the AD.

 ** _Encrypt_: Encrypt the plaintext.
 ** _Enc_Last_Block_: Encrypt the last block of the plaintext.
 ** _Hash_Output_: Return the tag.

 ** _Decrypt_: Decrypt the ciphertext.
 ** _Dec_Last_Block_: Decrypt the last block of the ciphertext.
 ** _Hash_Verify_: Compare a user-provided value with the internally computed tag.

 ** _Success_: The Machine has completed its operation without errors.
 ** _Failure_: The Machine has completed its operation with an authentication failure.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_.
 ** _Encrypt_ -> { _Enc_Last_Block_ -> } _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_.
 ** _Decrypt_ -> { _Dec_Last_Block_ -> } _Hash_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
 ** The Machine makes these transitions itself (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>):
  . a `kl.setst` into _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_, with `Xs` = 0 pads the empty final block and enters
    _Hash_Output_, resp. _Hash_Verify_, directly; no `kl.setst` naming _Hash_Output_ is admitted from _Encrypt_, nor
    one naming _Hash_Verify_ from _Decrypt_;
  . the `kl.exec` of _Enc_Last_Block_, resp. _Dec_Last_Block_, enters _Hash_Output_, resp. _Hash_Verify_;
  . the `kl.exec` of _Hash_Output_ enters _Success_.
 ** Gated States (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>): _MachinePolicy_[0], _Encrypt_, _Enc_Last_Block_ and _Hash_Output_; _MachinePolicy_[1], _Decrypt_, _Dec_Last_Block_ and _Hash_Verify_.

* *Behavior:*
** Upon entering State _Ready_:
   . `state <- zeros(320)`
   . `tag_len <- 128`
   . `last_block_len <- 0`

** In State _Ready_, a Form B `kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_set_aux_value, Xs` is an operation, not a State
   (Rule <<KLE-operation-immediates>>): it sets `tag_len` {leftarrow} `Xs`, where 64 {le} `Xs` {le} 128.
   Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
+
NOTE: A truncated tag is a prefix of the 128-bit tag (cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}4.2.1).
Entering _Ready_ restores `tag_len` to 128, so the caller sets it again for each message. Re-running a decryption with
`tag_len` raised one bit at a time would find the full tag in about 2^64^ verifications from the floor of 64 bits.
Keeping one `tag_len` per key, and the bound on failed verifications, is the caller's (cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}4.3
R4-R5, Rule <<KLE-MGR-caller-limits>>).

** [[KLE-Ascon-AEAD128-nonce-setup,nonce setup of Ascon-AEAD128]]The `kl.setst` into _Hash_Absorb_ is of Form C. It takes the nonce from `INPUT` and performs:
+
--
  . `state[0] <- 0x00001000808c0001`
  . `state[1] <- key[63:0]`
  . `state[2] <- key[127:64]`
  . `state[3] <- INPUT[63:0]`
  . `state[4] <- INPUT[127:64]`
  . `ASCON(12)`
  . `state[3] <- state[3] xor key[63:0]`
  . `state[4] <- state[4] xor key[127:64]`
--
+
`INPUT` is truncated to its 128 least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>).
+
NOTE: The key enters `state` only within this `kl.setst` and within the tag computation of _Hash_Output_ and
_Hash_Verify_. Each of these instructions completes with the key either permuted or masked by permutation output.
No Serialized Content thus holds the key in `state`, as Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>
requires for a key configured by a SKID.

** In State _Hash_Absorb_, only (multi-block) ``kl.exec`` instructions of Form B are expected.
"```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" absorbs a data block into the state, i.e.:
+
--
 . `state[0] <- state[0] xor INPUT[63:0]`
 . `state[1] <- state[1] xor INPUT[127:64]`
 . `ASCON(8)`
--
+
If the associated data is empty, the caller absorbs no block, not even a padding block, and no permutation is applied.
No ``kl.exec`` is then needed in this State (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transfer-window-checks>>).

** Upon entering State _Encrypt_ or _Decrypt_, by a Form A `kl.setst`, the following domain separation
   operation is performed:
 . `state[4] <- state[4] xor (1 << 63)`

** In State _Encrypt_, (multi-block) ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected to be of Form A.
"```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" encrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`:
+
--
 . `state[0] <- state[0] xor INPUT[63:0]`
 . `state[1] <- state[1] xor INPUT[127:64]`
 . `OUTPUT <- state[1] @ state[0]`
 . `ASCON(8)`
--
** When entering State _Enc_Last_Block_:
. a Form B ``kl.setst`` gives the bit length of the final block as the auxiliary argument `Xs`, where
  0 {le} `Xs` {le} 127.
  `Xs` need not be a multiple of 8 (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>): cite:[nist-SP-800-232] is written in little-endian
  bit order, so `pad` places the terminating `1` at bit position `Xs` for any `Xs`;
. if `Xs` = 0, the `kl.setst` pads the empty final block, `state[0] <- state[0] xor 1` (this is `pad(∅,128)` =
  `zeros(127) @ 1`), and transitions the locker to _Hash_Output_ instead;
. otherwise, it sets `last_block_len <- Xs`, and _Enc_Last_Block_ waits for a Form A `kl.exec`, as described next.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Enc_Last_Block_, exactly one Form A `kl.exec` is expected, to encrypt the last block.
It performs:
+
--
* `tmp : bits(b)`

[]
. `tmp <- pad(INPUT[last_block_len-1:0], 128)`
. `state[0] <- state[0] xor tmp[63:0]`
. `state[1] <- state[1] xor tmp[127:64]`
. `tmp <- state[1] @ state[0]`
. `OUTPUT <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ tmp[last_block_len-1:0]`
.  The state machine then transitions to State _Hash_Output_.
--
+
State _Enc_Last_Block_ processes a single block (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).

** In State _Hash_Output_, only one ``kl.exec`` instruction of Form C is expected to be issued, to compute the tag.
"`kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn`"  performs:
+
--
 . `state[2] <- state[2] xor key[63:0]`
 . `state[3] <- state[3] xor key[127:64]`
 . `ASCON(12)`
 . `state[3] <- state[3] xor key[63:0]`
 . `state[4] <- state[4] xor key[127:64]`
 . `OUTPUT <- zeros(128 - tag_len) @ (state[4] @ state[3])[tag_len-1:0]`
 . The state machine then transitions to State _Success_.
--
+
State _Hash_Output_ processes a single block (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>). `KLLEN` must be a multiple of 128,
whatever `tag_len` is (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).
// Not a source for now (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>):
// As a `kl.derive` source (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>), _Hash_Output_ supplies {lceil}``tag_len``/8{rceil} bytes, the least
// significant bytes that the Form C `kl.exec` would write, and then transitions to _Success_.

** In State _Decrypt_, (multi-block) ``kl.exec`` instructions are expected to be of Form A.
"```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn, INPUT```" decrypts `INPUT` into `OUTPUT`:
+
--
* `tmp : bits(128)`

[]
. `tmp <- (state[1] xor INPUT[127:64]) @ (state[0] xor INPUT[63:0])`
. `state[0] <- INPUT[63:0]`
. `state[1] <- INPUT[127:64]`
. `ASCON(8)`
. `OUTPUT <- tmp`
--

** When entering State _Dec_Last_Block_:
. a Form B "```kl.setst Kn, #kl_state_dec_last_block, Xs```" gives the bit length `Xs` of the final block, where
  0 {le} `Xs` {le} 127; `Xs` need not be a multiple of 8, as for _Enc_Last_Block_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>);
. if `Xs` = 0, the `kl.setst` pads the empty final block, `state[0] <- state[0] xor 1` (this is `pad(∅,128)` =
  `zeros(127) @ 1`), and transitions the locker to _Hash_Verify_ instead;
. otherwise, it sets `last_block_len <- Xs`, and _Dec_Last_Block_ waits for a Form A `kl.exec`, as described next.
+
Any other `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Dec_Last_Block_, exactly one Form A `kl.exec` is expected, to decrypt the last block.
It performs:
+
--
* `S_r : bits(128)` – the rate part of the state
* `P : bits(128)`

[]
. `S_r <- state[1] @ state[0]`
. `P <- zeros(128-last_block_len) @ (S_r[last_block_len-1:0] xor INPUT[last_block_len-1:0])`
. `OUTPUT <- P`
. `S_r <- S_r xor pad(P[last_block_len-1:0], 128)`
. `state[0] <- S_r[63:0]`
. `state[1] <- S_r[127:64]`
. The state machine then transitions to State _Hash_Verify_.
--
+
State _Dec_Last_Block_ processes a single block (Rule <<KLE-MGR-single-last-block>>).

** In State _Hash_Verify_, which the decryption path enters by itself, the tag is verified by a single Form B
"```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" with `KLLEN` {ge} `tag_len` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-tag-verify>>), which performs:
+
--
. `state[2] <- state[2] xor key[63:0]`
. `state[3] <- state[3] xor key[127:64]`
. `ASCON(12)`
. `state[3] <- state[3] xor key[63:0]`
. `state[4] <- state[4] xor key[127:64]`
. `INPUT[tag_len-1:0]` is compared to `(state[4] @ state[3])[tag_len-1:0]`.
. If the values match, the state machine transitions to _Success_;
  otherwise, it transitions to _Failure_.
--
+
`INPUT` is truncated to its 128 least significant bits (Rule <<KLE-MGR-fixed-width-input>>), and a `KLLEN` smaller than `tag_len`
violates the granularity of the State (Rule <<KLE-MGR-granularity>>).

[[KLE-Ascon-AEAD128-wsn]]
=== Ascon-AEAD128 with set Nonce

Like GCM with Set IV, Ascon-AEAD128 has a variant with a set nonce.
The PI carries the nonce, so the transition into State _Hash_Absorb_ uses a Form A ``kl.setst``
instead of the Form C of <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>.

Data Structures::

* *Provisioning Input*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|  ii  | 128 or 64     | `key` or System Key Identifier
| iii  | 128           | `nonce`
|===
--

* *Internal State*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for Ascon-AEAD128, with in addition:

 ** `nonce`: 128 bits.
--

* *Serialized Content*: {empty}
+
--
Same as for Ascon-AEAD128, followed by:

[%autowidth,cols="^1,<2,<3",options="header"]
|===
| Pos. | Size (Bits)     | Field
|   ix | 128             | `nonce`
|===

`nonce` is serialized because every transition into _Hash_Absorb_, also after an import, loads it into `state`.
--

* *States and Allowed Transitions:* {empty}
+
--
Same as for Ascon-AEAD128.
--

* *State Machine Behavior:* {empty}
+
--
As for Ascon-AEAD128, except that:

 ** The ``kl.setst`` into _Hash_Absorb_ is of Form A, without auxiliary input.
    It performs the operations of the Form C <<KLE-Ascon-AEAD128-nonce-setup>>, with `nonce` in place of `INPUT`.
 ** The Machine is not a `kl.derive` destination (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>): its key and its nonce are configured
    together, by provisioning or import.
--

NOTE: This Machine protects the confidentiality of the nonce, not against its reuse (Rule <<KLE-nonce-reuse>>).
A key derived into the key field while the nonce is kept would pair the provisioned nonce, which is secret when it
holds a masked nonce `N xor K2` (cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}4.3, R2), with a key it was not provisioned with:
a related-key use that cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}4.2.2 excludes for nonce masking.

[[KLE-Ascon-Hash256]]
=== Ascon-Hash256

The caller applies `pad(M, 64)` of cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}5.1 to the message `M` and absorbs the resulting 64-bit blocks.
An empty `M` yields the single block `bin(1, 64)`.

Parameters::

* `b` = 64.
* Granularity: `b`.

Data Structures::

The *Provisioning Input* contains only the MDH.

* *Internal State*:
  ** `state[0 .. 4]`: five 64-bit words, i.e., 320 bits.
 ** A `countdown`, an integer going from 3 down to 0, is stored in bits [1:0] of _MachineUse_.

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<1,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)   | Field
|    i  | 64              | `state`[0]
|   ii  | 64              | `state`[1]
|  iii  | 64              | `state`[2]
|   iv  | 64              | `state`[3]
|    v  | 64              | `state`[4]
|===
--

Machine-Specific Functions::

* `ASCON(p)` applies the permutation `Ascon-p[p]` of cite:[nist-SP-800-232], of `p` rounds, to the `state`.

State Machine::

* *States:* _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_, _Hash_Output_, and _Success_.

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** _Ready_ -> _Hash_Absorb_ -> _Hash_Output_ -> _Success_. The `kl.exec` that emits the last digest word makes the
    transition to _Success_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 ** The Machine does not use _MachinePolicy_: no State is gated (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and a `kl.restrictl`
    that requests a change of _MachinePolicy_ transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_
    (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>>).

* *Behavior:*
 ** Upon entering State _Ready_:
   . `state[0] <- 0x0000080100cc0002`
   . `state[1] <- zeros(64)`
   . `state[2] <- zeros(64)`
   . `state[3] <- zeros(64)`
   . `state[4] <- zeros(64)`
   . `ASCON(12)`
   . `countdown <- 0`

 ** The `kl.setst` into _Hash_Absorb_ is of Form A.

 ** In State _Hash_Absorb_, only (multi-block) ``kl.exec`` instructions of Form B are expected to be issued.
    "```kl.exec Kn, INPUT```" absorbs a data block into the state, i.e.:
+
--
 . `state[0] <- state[0] xor INPUT`
 . `ASCON(12)`
--

 ** When entering State _Hash_Output_, by a Form A `kl.setst`: `countdown` {leftarrow} 3.

** In State _Hash_Output_, at most four ``kl.exec`` instructions of Form C
   are expected to be issued. To compute the digest, each "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn```" performs, for each 64-bit block
   `i` = 0, 64, {ellipsis}, `KLLEN` − 64 of `OUTPUT` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>), until it transitions to _Success_:
+
--
. If `countdown` != 3, then:
.. `ASCON(12)`
. `OUTPUT[i+63 : i] <- state[0]`
. If `countdown` = 0 then:
.. Transition to State _Success_.
. else:
.. `countdown <- countdown - 1`
--
+
Each ``kl.exec`` writes from `OUTPUT`[63:0] upwards.
If it transitions to _Success_ before writing all of `OUTPUT`, the unwritten bits are cleared (Rule <<KLE-MGR-clear-output>>), as in the second ``kl.exec`` when `KLLEN` = `192`.
+
NOTE: The clearing rarely matters: output vectors usually hold 64, 128 or 256 bits, which need four, two or one ``kl.exec`` instructions, respectively.

//

NOTE: A keyed version of this algorithm is not needed.
A trusted entity can provide a CC that has already absorbed secret content, and reuse it for multiple invocations by keeping a copy of the original SCC.

[[KLE-Ascon-XOF128]]
=== Ascon-XOF128

The Machine is the same as Ascon-Hash256 (<<KLE-Ascon-Hash256>>) with the following differences:

. The IV written to `state[0]` in State _Ready_ is `0x0000080000cc0003`.
. The `countdown` is used in a different way:

** Upon entering _Hash_Output_, `countdown` is set to 1.

** In State _Hash_Output_, each Form C "```kl.exec OUTPUT, Kn```" performs, for each 64-bit block
   `i` = 0, 64, {ellipsis}, `KLLEN` − 64 of `OUTPUT` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-one-klexec-per-block>>):
+
--
. If `countdown` = 0, then `ASCON(12)`.
. `OUTPUT[i+63 : i] <- state[0]`.
. `countdown <- 0`.
--
+
`ASCON(12)` is thus applied before every output word except the first, as in
cite:[nist-SP-800-232] Algorithm 6.

. As much output as desired can be produced in State _Hash_Output_. The States are _Ready_, _Hash_Absorb_ and _Hash_Output_, and `countdown` takes only the values 1 and 0.

[[KLE-Ascon-CXOF128]]
=== Ascon-CXOF128

Ascon-CXOF128 differs from Ascon-XOF128 in two ways:
the IV is `0x0000080000cc0004`, and the customization string `Z` precedes the message. The States and their semantics are otherwise those of Ascon-XOF128.
In _Hash_Absorb_, the caller absorbs, before the message, the blocks `Z~0~` {ellipsis} `Z~m~` of cite:[nist-SP-800-232] {sect}5.3: the block `bin(len_in_bits(Z), 64)`, then `Z` padded as the message is.
The caller also ensures that `len_in_bits(Z)` {le} 2048; the Machine does not check it.

[[KLE-ECC]]
== Elliptic Curve Cryptography

The Elliptic Curve Machines provide:

. Scalar multiplication with known and unknown base point.
. ECDSA/EdDSA signature generation and verification.

The x25519 and x448 Machines (<<KLE-X25519-X448>>) provide only the former.

NOTE: KDFs, hash functions and formats vary too much for general KEM or PKE support; among the elliptic curve standards, only SM2 fully specifies an integrated encryption scheme cite:[GMT-0003-4-2012].
Software builds such schemes on scalar multiplication, and `kl.derive` moves the shared secret into the hash function of the KDF without exposing it.
A key that is exposed anyway should be provisioned into a locker and erased at once.

Provisioning sets the `Generator` field to the default base point (“generator”) that the standards listed below define for the curve.
Software may overwrite the field; a Form A `kl.setst` to _Set_Generator_, or one of Form B with zero in the GPR, restores
the default, as does a Form B `kl.setst` to _Ready_ with bit 0 of `Xs` set.

All values (integers, coordinates, etc.) are in little-endian format, except the shared secret of the Weierstrass curves that _Output_ emits, which follows the byte order of its standard (see _Output_ below).

Parameters::
+
--
The parameters used in this section are:

 . The bit length `b` of the main fields, such as coordinates, signature R and S fields, scalars;
 . The bit length `h` of the hash used in the signature scheme. For the ECDSA curves,
   `Hash` holds `bin(e, h)`, where `e` is the leftmost `min(N, outlen)` bits of the message digest,
   read as a big-endian integer, as cite:[nist-fips-186-5] {sect}6.4 and {sect}6.4.1, step 3, prescribe.
   `N` is the bit length of the group order `n`, and `outlen` that of the digest.
   The digest is truncated, not reduced modulo `n`.
   The caller performs this truncation and chooses an approved hash function.
   For SM2 the value placed in `Hash` is `bin(e, 256)`, with `e` the big-endian integer of `SM3(Z~A~ {vvert} M)` of
   cite:[GMT-0003-2-2012], where `Z~A~` = `SM3(ENTL~A~ {vvert} ID~A~ {vvert} a {vvert} b {vvert} x~G~ {vvert} y~G~
   {vvert} x~A~ {vvert} y~A~)`; here `a` and `b` are the curve coefficients, not the parameter `b`,
   and (`x~A~`, `y~A~`) is the public key.
   The caller computes `Z~A~` and `e`, since every input to `Z~A~` is public; the KLE unit computes neither.
   For the Edwards curves the field is used differently, and only in pre-hash mode; see
   <<KLE-EdDSA>>;
 . The bit length `j` of the random number used as nonce in signature generation;
 . The number `u` of coordinates used to represent a point (`u` can be 1 or 2); and
 . The number `v` of elements used to represent a signature (`v` can be 0, 1 or 2, where `v` = 0
   denotes a curve for which no signature scheme is provided).

The granularity of every Elliptic Curve Machine, X25519, X448 and EdDSA included, is 8 bits: a field can be transferred with any `SEW` from 8 to 64 bits that divides its size, or through the KLIOBUF in any number of bytes.

The parameters for the defined curves are:

* NIST curves cite:[NIST-SP-800-186,nist-fips-186-5]:
  ** secp256r1 (NIST P-256): `b` = `h` = `j` = 256, `u` = `v` = 2.
  ** secp384r1 (NIST P-384): `b` = `h` = `j` = 384, `u` = `v` = 2.
  ** secp521r1 (NIST P-521): `b` = 576 (55 msbs set to zero), `h` = `j` = 576 (55 msbs set to zero), `u` = `v` = 2.
* Edwards curves for signatures cite:[RFC8032,nist-fips-186-5]:
  ** ed25519:  `b` = 256, `h` = 512; `u` = 1, `v` = 2.
  ** ed448: `b` = 456, `h` = 512; `u` = 1, `v` = 2.
* Montgomery curves, for key exchange only cite:[RFC7748]:
  ** x25519 (curve25519): `b` = 256; `u` = 1, `v` = 0.
  ** x448 (curve448): `b` = 448; `u` = 1, `v` = 0.
* Brainpool cite:[RFC5639,RFC8734]:
  ** brainpoolP256r1: `b` = `h` = `j` = 256; `u` = `v` = 2.
  ** brainpoolP384r1: `b` = `h` = `j` = 384; `u` = `v` = 2.
  ** brainpoolP512r1: `b` = `h` = `j` = 512; `u` = `v` = 2.
* SM2 cite:[GMT-0003-1-2012,GMT-0003-2-2012,GMT-0003-3-2012,GMT-0003-4-2012]: `b` = `h` = `j` = 256; `u` = `v` = 2.

For the Edwards curves, a point is the compressed `b`-bit string of cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5 (`u` = 1), and a signature is the pair (`R`, `S`) of such strings (`v` = 2).
Hence `b` = 456 for ed448, whose strings are 57 bytes.
`h` = 512 because, in pre-hash mode, `Hash` holds the 64-byte `PH(M)`.
There is no `j`, since EdDSA uses no `RndNum` (<<KLE-EdDSA>>).

For the Montgomery curves, a point is its _u_-coordinate alone (cite:[RFC7748] {sect}5, `u` = 1).
Their Machines provide key exchange only (<<KLE-X25519-X448>>), hence `v` = 0, and `h` and `j` are not defined.
They are listed apart from the Edwards curves because their points differ, and so does `b` for x448.

All curves here are defined over prime fields.
For secp521r1, the 55 most significant bits of `Scalar`, `Hash` and `RndNum`, of each coordinate of `Generator` and `SecondPt`, and of each component of `Signature` must be zero.
The locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ if a write sets one of them (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>), or if _Point_Mul_, _Sign_Generate_ or _Sign_Verify_ finds one set in a value it uses (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-read>>), as a resumed `RndNum` may carry.

A point is _valid_ if:

* each of its coordinates is less than `p`;
* it satisfies the curve equation;
* it is not the neutral element: the point at infinity of a Weierstrass curve, resp. (0, 1) of an Edwards curve;
* where the cofactor is not 1, it lies in the prime-order subgroup; and
* on an Edwards curve, it decodes as cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.3, resp. {sect}5.2.3, prescribes.

_Point_Mul_, _Sign_Generate_ and _Sign_Verify_ check the validity of `Generator` and `SecondPt` when they read them (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-read>>).
For the Edwards curves, only _Point_Mul_ uses `Generator`, and _Sign_Verify_ checks `SecondPt` only as <<KLE-EdDSA>> states.
An invalid point whose encoding satisfies the representation of the curve, whatever its role, a coordinate not less than `p` included, is a data error and leads to _Failure_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>); a violated representation, such as a set msb of secp521r1, is a configuration error, as stated above.
For the Montgomery curves, the check of <<KLE-X25519-X448>> replaces this one.
--

* *Behavior:*

 ** Provisioning sets `Generator` to the default of the curve, and `Scalar` to zero.

 ** The _Set_ states and _Output_ follow Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the progress recorded in `block_base`, in
    bytes.

 ** Entering _Set_Generator_:
  . by a Form A `kl.setst`, or a Form B one whose GPR holds zero, sets the default generator of the curve, and
    `block_base` {leftarrow} `ub`/8, which completes the load;
  . by a Form B `kl.setst` whose GPR is non-zero, erases `Generator` and starts a load: Form B `kl.exec` instructions then
    load the `ub`/8 bytes of `Generator`.

 ** Entering _Set_Scalar_, _Set_Hash_, _Set_SecondPt_ or _Sign_Input_ by a Form A `kl.setst`:
  . erases the field _X_ that the State sets, `Scalar` becoming zero, and clears ``Has``_X_ where that flag exists;
  . Form B `kl.exec` instructions then load _X_, and ``Has``_X_ is set when the load completes.

 ** A Form B `kl.setst` into _Set_Scalar_:
  . whose GPR holds zero acts as a Form A `kl.setst`;
  . whose GPR is non-zero sets `Scalar` to a random private key, and `block_base` {leftarrow} `b`/8, which completes the load;
    no `kl.exec` follows. Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>> applies if the RBG fails. The key is:
  .. for the NIST and Brainpool curves, an integer _d_ with 1 {le} _d_ < `n`, generated as cite:[nist-fips-186-5]
     Appendix A.2.1 or A.2.2 prescribes;
  .. for SM2, one with 1 {le} _d_ {le} `n` − 2 (cite:[GMT-0003-1-2012]);
  .. for the Montgomery and Edwards curves, a uniformly random `b`-bit string.
+
The bits of `Scalar` beyond the key are zero.

 ** *Retry rules* of signature generation:
  . for the NIST and Brainpool curves, the signature is recomputed with a fresh `k` whenever `r` = 0 or `s` = 0
    (cite:[nist-fips-186-5] {sect}6.4.1);
  . for SM2, whenever `r` = 0, `r` + `k` = `n`, or `s` = 0 (cite:[GMT-0003-2-2012]);
  . the Edwards curves use no random secret, and have no retry rules.

 ** In State _Point_Mul_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` performs a scalar multiplication:
  . The base point `P` is `SecondPt` if `HasSecondPt` is set, and `Generator` otherwise.
  . If `P` is not a valid point, the state machine transitions to _Failure_.
  . `SecondPt` {leftarrow} `int(Scalar)` {interpunct} `P`, encoded as in <<KLE-ECC>>, and `HasSecondPt` is set.
  . `OutputType` {leftarrow} _shared secret_ if `P` is `SecondPt`, and _point_ otherwise, and the state transitions to
    _Output_.

 ** In State _Sign_Generate_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` signs, over the base point `Generator`, with private key
    `d` = `int(Scalar)` and message value `e` = `int(Hash)`:
  . If `Generator` is not a valid point, the state transitions to _Failure_.
  . If `Progress` is zero, the per-signature secret `k`, uniformly distributed in [1, `n` − 1], is generated from the RBG
    (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Prerequisites>>, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>) as
    cite:[nist-fips-186-5] Appendix A.3.1 or A.3.2 prescribes, also for SM2; `RndNum` {leftarrow} `bin(k, j)`, and
    `HasRndNum` is set.
  . If `Progress` is non-zero, the interrupted operation is resumed with the `RndNum` held, or restarted
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>).
  . The signature (`r`, `s`) is computed as cite:[nist-fips-186-5] {sect}6.4.1 specifies for the NIST and Brainpool curves,
    and as cite:[GMT-0003-2-2012] specifies for SM2, with the retry rules above.
  . `Signature` {leftarrow} (`r`, `s`), and `HasSignature` is set; `RndNum` is destroyed and `HasRndNum` cleared.
  . `OutputType` {leftarrow} _signature_, and the state transitions to _Output_.

 ** In State _Sign_Verify_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` verifies `Signature` against the public key held in `SecondPt`
    and the message value `e` = `int(Hash)`, as cite:[nist-fips-186-5] {sect}6.4.2 specifies for the NIST and Brainpool
    curves, and as cite:[GMT-0003-2-2012] specifies for SM2:
  . it performs every check those specifications require, the ranges of `r` and `s` included, and checks that
    `SecondPt` and `Generator` are valid points;
  . if a check fails, or the signature equation does not hold, the state machine transitions to _Failure_, else to
    _Success_.

 ** In State _Output_, `OutputType` determines the output and its length:
     *** _point_: `SecondPt`, `ub` bits;
     *** _signature_: `Signature`, `vb` bits;
     *** _shared secret_: the byte string that the standard of the curve defines as the shared secret, in the byte order of
         that standard; for the Weierstrass curves, this is the one exception to the little-endian encoding:
      **** for the NIST and Brainpool curves, `Z` of NIST SP 800-56A Rev. 3 {sect}5.7.1.2: the X coordinate `x` as a
           big-endian string of `m` = {lceil}log~2~(`p`)/8{rceil} bytes (its Appendix C.2), that is
           `bswap(bin(x, 8m))`, 8``m`` bits (66 bytes for secp521r1);
      **** for SM2, `x~2~ {vvert} y~2~` of cite:[GMT-0003-4-2012], each coordinate a big-endian string of 32 bytes,
           that is `bswap(bin(x, 256)) @ bswap(bin(y, 256))` with `x~2~` first, 512 bits;
      **** for the Montgomery curves, the whole `b`-bit string `K` of cite:[RFC7748] {sect}6, little-endian as that
           standard defines it;
      **** for the Edwards curves, whose standard defines no shared secret, the encoded point (cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.2, resp. {sect}5.2.2).

 ** In State _Output_, Form C `kl.exec` instructions emit the output (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>). Once the output is
    complete, the state machine transitions to _Success_, in which no `kl.derive` is admitted.
  *** This State allows the export of the shared secret through `kl.exec`. The shared secret is therefore also an
      unrestricted `kl.derive` source, as a field (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>), any number of
      times while the locker is in _Output_.
      Each transfer reads it from its first byte (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>): a key
      field receives its first `eff_length` bytes, and a message input `length` bytes, at most the length of the field
      rounded up to the granularity of the destination, zero beyond the end of the field
      (Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> item 5 and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>).
// Not a source for now (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>):
//  *** A point or a signature is instead a `kl.exec` output source (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-exec-implies-unrestricted>>).

 ** Entering _Ready_ by a Form B `kl.setst`: bits 0 to 6 of `Xs` select the fields `Generator`, `Scalar`, `SecondPt`,
    `Hash` and `Signature` to reset, erase or keep; a non-zero bit 7 or above transitions the locker to Error State
    _Invalid_.
  . First the copies are made, both from the values held before the instruction, so that bits 4 and 5 together exchange
    the two points:
  .. bit 4: `Generator` is copied onto `SecondPt`, and `HasSecondPt` is set;
  .. bit 5: if `HasSecondPt` was set, `SecondPt` is copied onto `Generator`; otherwise bit 5 has no effect.
  . Then the fields are reset or erased:
  .. bit 0: `Generator` is reset to the default value;
  .. bit 1: `SecondPt` is erased and `HasSecondPt` is cleared;
  .. bit 2: `Scalar` is erased;
  .. bit 3: `Hash` is erased and `HasHash` is cleared;
  .. bit 6: `Signature` is erased and `HasSignature` is cleared.
  . Every field whose bit is clear is kept. A Form A `kl.setst` sets no bit, and so keeps all five fields.

 ** `Progress`, bits [13:2] of _MachineUse_, is the field _P_ of Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>> for _Point_Mul_,
    _Sign_Generate_ and _Sign_Verify_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>>), and `RndNum` is its
    material: each event of that Rule that zeroes `Progress` also destroys `RndNum` and clears `HasRndNum`.

[[KLE-X25519-X448]]
=== X25519- and X448-Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, X25519)))
(((KLE, Machine, X448)))
The x25519 and x448 Machines implement the functions `X25519` and `X448` of cite:[RFC7748] {sect}5, and with them
the Diffie-Hellman key exchange of cite:[RFC7748] {sect}6. Of the operations of <<KLE-ECC>> they provide only
scalar multiplication. They differ from the other curves in three respects: a point is a single coordinate,
the scalar is an arbitrary `b`-bit string, and the input point is not checked for validity, only the result. This subsection states the
consequences; everything not restated here follows <<KLE-ECC>>.

Parameters::

* The curves are the Montgomery curves `v^2^` = `u^3^` + `A` {interpunct} `u^2^` + `u` of cite:[RFC7748] {sect}4:
  curve25519 for x25519, with `p` = 2^255^ - 19 and `A` = 486662, and curve448 for x448, with
  `p` = 2^448^ - 2^224^ - 1 and `A` = 156326.
  curve25519 is birationally equivalent to the Edwards curve of ed25519; curve448 is related to that of ed448
  by an isogeny of degree 4. The Machines are nevertheless distinct, and points and private keys are not
  interchangeable between them.
* A point is represented in compressed form, by its _u_-coordinate alone, so the parameter `u` = 1.
  The _u_-coordinate is held as the X coordinate of `Generator` and `SecondPt` of <<KLE-ECC>>.
  A public key and a shared secret are thus 32 bytes for x25519 and 56 bytes for x448.
* There is no signature scheme: `v` = 0, and `h` and `j` are not defined.
* `X25519(k, u)` and `X448(k, u)` map a scalar and a _u_-coordinate, both `b`-bit strings, to a `b`-bit
  string, decoding their inputs as cite:[RFC7748] {sect}5 prescribes:
 ** the scalar is _clamped_: for x25519, bits 0, 1, 2 and 255 are cleared and bit 254 is set; for x448,
    bits 0 and 1 are cleared and bit 447 is set;
 ** for x25519, bit 255 of the _u_-coordinate is ignored; and
 ** a _u_-coordinate not smaller than `p` is reduced modulo `p`.
* The private key is the unclamped `b`-bit string held in `Scalar`, which holds a
  *configured private key* when it is non-zero. It is not compared with the order of the group.

Data Structures::
+
--
Those of <<KLE-ECC>>, with the following changes:

* *MDH*:
 ** _MachinePolicy_ must be zero; a non-zero value is unsupported Metadata
    (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
 ** Of the _StateExtension_ bits (<<KLE-ECC-StateExtension>>) only `HasSecondPt` is used: an SCC
    with any of the bits 1 to 3 set is invalid Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
 ** `OutputType` (<<KLE-ECC-MachineUse>>) is never _signature_: a multiplication of `Generator` outputs a point, the public key, and one of `SecondPt` a shared secret, `K`; both are a single `b`-bit string.

* *Internal State*: `Generator` and `SecondPt` are each a single `b`-bit string, a _u_-coordinate.
  `SecondPt` holds the peer's public key and, after a scalar multiplication, the public key of the locker
  or the shared secret.
  `Signature`, `Hash` and `RndNum` do not exist.

* *Serialized Content*: Positions i to iv of <<KLE-ECC>>, with `u` = 1; Positions v to vii are always empty.
  Hence
+
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `varlen` <- ``b``/8.
--

State Machine::

* *States:* besides _Ready_ (1), _Success_ (46) and _Failure_ (47), only the following States of <<KLE-ECC>>:
 ** _Set_Generator_ (2)
 ** _Set_Scalar_ (3)
 ** _Point_Mul_ (4)
 ** _Set_SecondPt_ (8)
 ** _Output_ (10)

+
_Sign_Generate_, _Sign_Verify_, _Set_Hash_ and _Sign_Input_ are not defined: a `kl.setst` that names one of them
transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>>).

* *Allowed State Transitions:* the _Set_ states are _Set_Generator_, _Set_Scalar_ and _Set_SecondPt_.
 ** _Ready_ or any _Set_ state -> any _Set_ state, itself included, or -> _Point_Mul_, which requires `Scalar` to hold a
    configured private key (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
 ** _Point_Mul_ -> _Output_ -> _Success_, as in <<KLE-ECC>>, or -> _Failure_ when the result is the all-zero string
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>).

* *Behavior:*

** Provisioning sets `Generator` to the base point of cite:[RFC7748] {sect}4.1, resp. {sect}4.2, that is to the
   _u_-coordinate 9 for x25519 and 5 for x448, and `Scalar` to zero.

** `Generator`, `SecondPt` and `Scalar` accept every `b`-bit string: no check is made when they are written
   (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).

** In State _Point_Mul_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` performs a scalar
   multiplication, in the following steps, which replace those of <<KLE-ECC>>:

  . The base point `P` is `SecondPt` if `HasSecondPt` is set, and `Generator` otherwise. `P` is not checked for
    validity: `X25519` and `X448` are defined for every `b`-bit string.
  . `R` {leftarrow} `X25519(Scalar, P)`, resp. `X448(Scalar, P)`.
  . If `R` is the all-zero string, the state transitions to _Failure_, and `SecondPt` and `HasSecondPt` are left
    unchanged. The check is made in constant time; cite:[RFC7748] {sect}6 makes it optional.
  . Otherwise `SecondPt` {leftarrow} `R`, `HasSecondPt` is set, `OutputType` {leftarrow} _shared secret_ if `P` is
    `SecondPt`, and _point_ otherwise, and the state transitions to _Output_.


** Entering _Ready_ by a Form B `kl.setst`, bits 3 and 6 of `Xs` have no effect: `Hash` and `Signature` do not exist.

** Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>> applies to _Point_Mul_, with no `RndNum` to destroy.

[NOTE]
====
A key exchange is two scalar multiplications with the same `Scalar`: with `HasSecondPt` clear and the default
`Generator`, it yields the public key; after the peer's public key is loaded in _Set_SecondPt_, it yields the shared
secret `K`. cite:[RFC7748] {sect}6 uses `K` as the input of a key derivation function, together with the two public
keys; as a `kl.derive` source in _Output_ (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>), `K` reaches that function without leaving the unit.
A small-order `P` yields the all-zero `R`.
====

[[KLE-EdDSA]]
=== EdDSA-Specific Rules

{empty}
(((KLE, Machine, EdDSA)))
EdDSA, standardized in cite:[RFC8032] and cite:[nist-fips-186-5], does not fit the
division of labor used by the other curves of <<KLE-ECC>>, under which the caller hashes
the message and the KLE unit performs only the group arithmetic. Both of its hash
invocations take material the caller does not hold:

`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `r{nbsp}`  = `int(H(dom {vvert} prefix {vvert} M)) mod L`, where `prefix` is secret, and +
`{fournbsp}{fournbsp}` `k'` = `int(H(dom {vvert} R {vvert} A {vvert} M)) mod L`, where `R` = `r` {interpunct} `B` is computed inside.

The Machine therefore needs to perform the hashing itself. This subsection states the
consequences; everything not restated here follows <<KLE-ECC>>.

Parameters::

* `H` is `SHA-512` for ed25519 and `SHAKE256`, with a 114-byte output, for ed448.
* The private key, or *seed*, is the `b`-bit string held in `Scalar`; `Scalar` holds a
  *configured seed* when it is non-zero.
* `dom` is `dom2(x, ctx)` for ed25519 and `dom4(x, ctx)` for ed448, as defined in
  cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1 and {sect}5.2, with `x` = 0 in pure mode and 1 in pre-hash mode.
  For ed25519 in pure mode with an empty context, `dom` is the empty string.

Pure and pre-hash modes::
+
--
In *pure* mode the unit hashes the message itself; in *pre-hash* mode the caller supplies
`PH(M)`, 64 bytes for both curves, in the `Hash` field. Which modes an implementation
offers depends on the hash extensions it provides:

[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="<,<,<",options="header"]
|===
| Curve extension | Also implements | Modes available
| `Zkled25519c` | --           | Ed25519ph only
| `Zkled25519c` | `Zklesha2h`  | Ed25519, Ed25519ctx and Ed25519ph
| `Zkled448c`   | --           | Ed448ph only
| `Zkled448c`   | `Zklesha3h`  | Ed448 and Ed448ph
|===

The curve extension alone must provide enough of `H` to hash the fixed-size inputs above,
which are a few hundred bytes at most. Absorbing a message of arbitrary length is what
requires the general hashing machinery, and hence `Zklesha2h` or `Zklesha3h`, respectively.

The _AuxInfo_ field of the MDH (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-MachineUse>>) is used as follows:
bit 0 records the modes a CC may use, only pre-hash mode with bit 0 = 0, and pure mode as well with bit 0 = 1;
bits [2:1] hold `msg_pass` (see the State Machine below); and bits [15:3] are reserved.
At provisioning and at import, bit 0 = 1 on an implementation that does not offer pure mode for the curve,
or a non-zero reserved bit, is unsupported Metadata, and raises a KLE unsupported-Metadata exception (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
This allows an M-mode handler to emulate pure mode where the hardware does not offer it
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>); a handler that cannot resolve the exception, including for a reserved value,
transitions the locker to Error State _Unsupported_.
Provisioning keeps bit 0 of the PI, and a transition to _Ready_ leaves it unchanged.
Provisioning ignores and zeroes bits [2:1] of the PI, and entering State _Ready_ zeroes them.
--

Data Structures::
+
--
In addition to the fields of <<KLE-ECC>>:

* `ctx`: 255 bytes, and `ctxlen`: 8 bits, the context string of cite:[RFC8032]. It is
  optional for ed25519 and mandatory, though possibly empty, for ed448. Both are zero
  upon provisioning and upon entering State _Ready_, and are set in State _Set_Ctx_.
* `msg_pass`: 2 bits, held in bits [2:1] of _AuxInfo_. In State _Msg_Absorb_ it identifies the open pass: 0 for signing
  pass 1, 1 for signing pass 2, and 3 for the verification pass. In any other State it records the last completed pass:
  0 for none, 1 for signing pass 1, 2 for signing pass 2, and 3 for the verification pass.
* `k'`: `b` bits, `bin(int(H(dom {vvert} R {vvert} A {vvert} M)) mod L, b)`, the whole hash read as a little-endian integer and
  reduced modulo `L`, computed by signing pass 2 or by the verification pass, and zero otherwise; it is always below `L`. It is not secret: anyone holding `R`, `A` and `M` can compute it.
* `r`: `b` bits, the secret scalar produced by the first signing pass, reduced modulo the
  group order `L`. Like `RndNum` for the ECDSA curves, it never leaves the locker except in
  the encrypted part of an SCC while a signing operation is in progress. It also binds the two
  signing passes: pass 2 recomputes it from its own message and requires the value to match
  (see the State Machine).

There is no stored pre-hash mode selector: the mode is inferred from the path taken, as
described below.

`RndNum` is *not* used: the EdDSA nonce is deterministic by construction, being derived from `prefix` and the message.
Its absence is deliberate; `j` = 0, so Position vii of <<KLE-ECC>> is always empty.
Bit 3 of _StateExtension_, `HasRndNum` in the other ECC Machines, is instead `HasHashState` (<<KLE-ECC-StateExtension>>):
it is set on entering State _Msg_Absorb_ and cleared on leaving it, so that it is set exactly while a pass
is open, including across the same-State re-entry that ends one pass and opens the next.
A _StateExtension_ @ _State_ with `HasHashState` set in a State other than _Msg_Absorb_, or clear in _Msg_Absorb_, is not
supported by the Machine: an SCC that carries it is invalid Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>). It does not follow the pattern of
Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, since it records no loaded field.

The clamped scalar `s` and the 32-byte or 57-byte `prefix`, respectively, are derived from the seed
by hashing it with `H`, as cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.5 and {sect}5.2.5 prescribe. An
implementation may recompute them on each use or cache them in the ADS.
The public key `A` is likewise derived from the seed, or cached in the ADS.

* *Serialized Content*: the serialized content of <<KLE-ECC>>, with the following fields
  appended.
+
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<3,<5",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits)  | Field
| viii  | 8            | `ctxlen`
|   ix  | 2040         | `ctx`
|    x  | `b`          | `r`
|   xi  | `b`          | `k'`
|  xii  | `hs`                                | The state of `H`, zero while no pass is open
| xiii  | If _MachinePolicy_[0] = 1: `hs`, else: 0 | The state of `H'`, zero except in signing pass 2
|===

`hs` is the length of the Serialized Content of the hash Machine of `H` as it is serialized under HMAC
(<<KLE-HMAC>>), that is with `cumul_len` and without a key or MDH: for ed25519 that of SHA-512 (<<KLE-SHA-2>>), 1664 bits,
and for ed448 that of SHAKE256 (<<KLE-SHA-3>>), 1792 bits with its padding to a multiple of 128 bits. The state of `H` is always
present, _MachinePolicy_ being never 0 for EdDSA, and that of `H'` whenever _MachinePolicy_[0] is set, so that the length of
`Content1` depends only on _MachinePolicy_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-length-rule>>).

`r` and `k'` are present unconditionally, and hold zero whenever no signing operation, resp. no signing or verification
operation, is in progress. Making their presence conditional on `msg_pass` would only increase the complexity of the Machine.

The state of an open pass is thus architected: `msg_pass` identifies the pass, and `Content1` carries `r`, `k'` and the
hash states. An export and import, also to a different implementation, resumes the pass where it stopped.
`r` is `b` bits (256 for ed25519 and 456 for ed448), a small addition to an SCC
of this size, and it remains confidential, being carried in the encrypted `Content`.
--

State Machine::

* *States:* those of <<KLE-ECC>>, and in addition
 ** _Msg_Absorb_ (11), in which message data is absorbed.
 ** _Set_Ctx_ (12), in which the context string is set.

* *Allowed State Transitions:* those of <<KLE-ECC>>, with the following changes:
 ** _Set_Ctx_ has the transitions of the _Set_ states of <<KLE-ECC>>.
 ** _Msg_Absorb_:
  *** is entered, by the Form B `kl.setst` described below, from _Ready_, any _Set_ state, _Set_Ctx_, or _Msg_Absorb_
      itself; the Machine allows this same-State `kl.setst` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>), which marks a pass boundary;
  *** is left only for _Msg_Absorb_, _Sign_Generate_, _Sign_Verify_, _Ready_ or an Error State; a `kl.setst` naming any
      other State transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.
 ** In _Msg_Absorb_, after a `kl.restrictl` or `kl.restrictv` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>>) that clears
    _MachinePolicy_[0] during signing pass 1 or 2, resp. _MachinePolicy_[1] during the verification pass, the next
    instruction of the pass transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_
    (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>).
 ** _MachinePolicy_ = 0 is invalid for EdDSA (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>), at provisioning and as the
    result of a `kl.restrict*`.
 ** These entry conditions (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>) replace those of <<KLE-ECC>>, in addition to the gating by
    _MachinePolicy_:
  *** _Point_Mul_: `Scalar` holds a configured seed.
  *** _Sign_Generate_: `Scalar` holds a configured seed, and either `msg_pass` = 2 (pure mode) or `msg_pass` = 0 and
      `HasHash` (pre-hash mode).
  *** _Sign_Verify_: `HasSecondPt` and `HasSignature`, and either `msg_pass` = 3 (pure mode) or `msg_pass` = 0 and
      `HasHash` (pre-hash mode).

* *Behavior:*

** In State _Point_Mul_, the Behavior of <<KLE-ECC>> applies, except that the multiplier is the secret scalar `s`
   derived from the seed (cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.5, resp. {sect}5.2.5, steps 1 to 3):
   `SecondPt` {leftarrow} `s` {interpunct} `P`.

** Signature generation and verification use the base point `B` of cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1, resp. {sect}5.2, whatever
   `Generator` holds: `R` {leftarrow} `r` {interpunct} `B`, the public key `A` derived from the seed is
   `s` {interpunct} `B`, and verification checks its equation over `B`. The validity check of `Generator`
   (<<KLE-ECC>>) applies only to _Point_Mul_.

** Signature generation takes two passes over the message, and verification one. Entering _Ready_ clears `msg_pass`,
   `r` and `k'`.
   `r`, `k'` and `Signature`, which the passes compute, are not material of _Sign_Generate_ or _Sign_Verify_ under
   Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>: a restarted operation uses them as held.

** Entering _Set_Ctx_:
  . A Form B `kl.setst` is used, whose `Xs` sets `ctxlen`; a value greater than 255 transitions the locker to Error State
    _Invalid_.
  . If `ctxlen` {ne} 0, Form B `kl.exec` instructions then load `ctx`, following Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>> as the
    _Set_ states of <<KLE-ECC>> do, with the progress recorded in `block_base`.
  . Leaving _Set_Ctx_ before `ctx` is complete zeroes `ctx` and `ctxlen` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).

** Entering _Msg_Absorb_ (pure mode only), by a Form B `kl.setst` whose `Xs` selects the pass, requires bit 0 of
   _AuxInfo_ to be 1 (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>); `Xs` is not the `max_len` of <<KLE-process-VLI>>, which is 0.
   Each pass has its own entry conditions (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>) and entry operations:
  . `Xs` = 0, *signing pass 1*: requires that signature generation be allowed and that `Scalar` hold a configured seed;
    `msg_pass` {leftarrow} 0, `k'` {leftarrow} 0, and `H` is initialized with `dom {vvert} prefix`.
  . `Xs` = 1, *signing pass 2*: requires `msg_pass` = 1; `H` is initialized with `dom {vvert} R {vvert} A`, with `R` from
    `Signature` and `A` derived from the seed. A second instance `H'`, initialized with `dom {vvert} prefix` as in pass 1,
    absorbs the same message bytes and recomputes `r`; finalization compares it with the `r` of pass 1.
  . `Xs` = 2, *verification pass*: requires that signature verification be allowed and that `HasSignature` and
    `HasSecondPt` hold; `msg_pass` {leftarrow} 3, and `H` is initialized with `dom {vvert} R {vvert} A`, with `R` from
    `Signature` and `A` = `SecondPt`.
  . Any other value of `Xs` transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_.

** In State _Msg_Absorb_, Form B ``kl.exec`` instructions absorb message bytes into `H` — and, in signing pass 2, into
   `H'` — through macro `PROCESS_VLI` (<<KLE-process-VLI>>), invoked as +
   `{fournbsp}` `PROCESS_VLI(max_len=0, H.block, H.b, H.state, H.n, input_base, H.block_base, 0, H.cumul_len, H.process_block(), None, mode)`, +
   and, in signing pass 2, also with `H'` in place of `H`, where:
  *** `H.block`, `H.b`, `H.state`, `H.n`, `H.block_base` and `H.cumul_len` are the buffer, block size, state, state size,
      and partial-block and cumulative-length counters, in bits, of the hash instance `H`, and likewise for `H'`; for
      `SHAKE256`, `H.block` is `H.state` (<<KLE-SHA-3>>);
  *** `H.block_base` is distinct from the byte counter `block_base` of <<KLE-ECC>>, which _Msg_Absorb_ does not use;
  *** `H.process_block()` applies the compression function, resp. the permutation, of `H`;
  *** `H.cumul_len` is tracked, since the unit appends the padding, and for SHA-512 the length, at pass finalization;
  *** `mode` is `assign` for ed25519, whose `H` is `SHA-512` (<<KLE-SHA-2>>), and `xor_accumulate` for ed448, whose `H`
      is `SHAKE256` (<<KLE-SHA-3>>).
+
The interruption points of _Msg_Absorb_ are those of `H`: 0, and every byte offset at which `H.block_base` is 0. In
signing pass 2, `H'` may then hold a partial block, which is architected (Position xiii).

** Leaving _Msg_Absorb_ for _Msg_Absorb_ (the same-State re-entry above), _Sign_Generate_ or _Sign_Verify_ finalizes the
   current pass:
+
--
   . after pass `Xs` = 0:
   .. `r` {leftarrow} `bin(int(H(dom {vvert} prefix {vvert} M)) mod L, b)`,
   .. `Signature.R` {leftarrow} `r` {interpunct} `B`, and `Signature.S` {leftarrow} 0,
   .. `msg_pass` {leftarrow} 1, and
   .. `HasSignature` is cleared: `Signature.R` holds an intermediate value, and a signature loaded before is discarded;
   . after pass `Xs` = 1:
   .. if `int(H'(dom {vvert} prefix {vvert} M)) mod L` {ne} `int(r)`, the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_;
   .. otherwise `k'` {leftarrow} `bin(int(H(dom {vvert} R {vvert} A {vvert} M)) mod L, b)` and `msg_pass` {leftarrow} 2;
   . after pass `Xs` = 2: `k'` as above, and `msg_pass` remains 3.
--
+
Leaving it for _Ready_ abandons the pass without finalizing it: no hash is completed and no binding check is made.
A `kl.setst` that enters or leaves _Msg_Absorb_ is not resumable: if it is preempted, it is restarted
(Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>).

** In State _Sign_Generate_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` completes the signature per cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.6, resp.
   {sect}5.2.6:
  . In *pure* mode (`msg_pass` = 2): `S` {leftarrow} `(r + k'` {interpunct} `s) mod L`.
  . In *pre-hash* mode (`msg_pass` = 0): `r` {leftarrow} `int(H(dom {vvert} prefix {vvert} Hash)) mod L`,
    `R` {leftarrow} `r` {interpunct} `B`, `k'` {leftarrow} `int(H(dom {vvert} R {vvert} A {vvert} Hash)) mod L`, and
    `S` {leftarrow} `(r + k'` {interpunct} `s) mod L`, with `Hash` holding `PH(M)`.
  . `Signature` {leftarrow} (`R`, `S`), `HasSignature` is set, `r` and `k'` are destroyed, `msg_pass` {leftarrow} 0,
    `OutputType` {leftarrow} _signature_, and the state transitions to _Output_.

** In State _Sign_Verify_, a single Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` verifies `Signature` per cite:[RFC8032] {sect}5.1.7, resp.
   {sect}5.2.7:
  . in *pure* mode if `msg_pass` = 3, with the `k'` of the verification pass; in *pre-hash* mode if `msg_pass` = 0, with
    `k'` computed internally, `Hash` (holding `PH(M)`) standing in for the message;
  . the checks of step 1 of those sections are performed: `S` < `L`, and `R` and `A` decode; `A` need not lie in the
    subgroup of order `L`;
  . the equation checked is the cofactored one, [2^c^][S]B = [2^c^]R + [2^c^][k']A, with `c` = 3 for ed25519 and 2 for
    ed448 (cite:[nist-fips-186-5] {sect}7.8.2);
  . if a check fails, or the equation does not hold, the state transitions to _Failure_, else to _Success_; in either
    case `k'` is zeroed.

[NOTE]
====
* Pass 2 recomputes `r` from its own message, and a mismatch with pass 1 invalidates the locker: two signatures that share
  `R` but commit to different messages would reveal the private scalar `s`.
* The restriction on leaving _Msg_Absorb_ keeps `Scalar`, `ctx`, `Signature` and `SecondPt` fixed between the passes,
  and between the verification pass and _Sign_Verify_. It prevents a different `R` with the same `r`, since two signatures
  with the same `r` and different `k'` reveal `s`, and a substitution of `Signature` that would forge against the `k'`
  already computed.
* The verification pass records 3, open or completed, so that signing pass 1 followed by _Sign_Verify_ is rejected: it
  would verify against a `k'` that was never computed.
* Pre-hash mode is unaffected: `PH(M)` in the `Hash` field feeds both hashes.
====

[[KLE-PQC]]
== Post Quantum Public-Key Cryptography

[[KLE-PQC-ML-KEM]]
=== ML-KEM

The _Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism Standard_ was announced by NIST in August 2024 cite:[nist-fips-203].
The standard defines several procedures, including:

* `ML-KEM.KeyGen`: Generates an encapsulation key `encapsk` and a corresponding decapsulation key `decapsk`.
* `ML-KEM.Encaps`: Uses the encapsulation key `encapsk` to generate a shared secret key `sharedkey` and an associated ciphertext.
* `ML-KEM.Decaps`: Uses the decapsulation key `decapsk` to produce a shared secret key `sharedkey` from a ciphertext.

ML-KEM comes in three versions, ML-KEM-512, ML-KEM-768, and ML-KEM-1024, with increasing cryptographic strength.
They require RBGs of corresponding strength: 128, 192, and 256 bits, respectively.
The sizes of keys and ciphertexts of the three subtypes of ML-KEM are summarized in <<KLE-ML-KEM-sizes>>.

[[KLE-ML-KEM-sizes]]
.Sizes (in bytes) of keys and ciphertexts of ML-KEM
[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="^1,^1,^1,^1,^1",options="header"]
|===
| Subtype     | `encapsk` | `decapsk` | `ciphertext` | `sharedkey`
| ML-KEM-512  |       800 |      1632 |          768 |        32
| ML-KEM-768  |      1184 |      2400 |         1088 |        32
| ML-KEM-1024 |      1568 |      3168 |         1568 |        32
|===

The granularity of the ML-KEM Machines is 8 bits: a field can be transferred with any `SEW` from 8 to 64 bits that divides its size, or through the KLIOBUF in any number of bytes.

In State _sharedkey_Output_, `kl.derive` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-derive>>) moves the `sharedkey` that _Encapsulate_ or _Decapsulate_ produced into a second, already provisioned locker.
The destination is a key field of a symmetric Machine, or the `kl.exec` input of a hash function, MAC or XOF, that <<KLE-derive-endpoints>> lists as a destination (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>).

[NOTE]
====
In KLE, the ML-KEM shared key is not a `kl.exec` output.
ML-KEM produces key material for a symmetric Machine or a key derivation function, and `kl.derive` moves it directly into a second locker.
The shared key thus stays inside the KLE unit and never appears in the memory of the process using it.
====

Data Structures::

* The *Provisioning Input* contains only the MDH.

* _MachinePolicy_ is not used and must be zero; a non-zero value is unsupported Metadata
  (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>). No State is gated by it (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and a
  `kl.restrictl` or `kl.restrictv` of it transitions the locker to _Invalid_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>>).

* *Internal State*:
+
--
 ** `decapsk`: 13056 / 19200 / 25344 bits (1632 / 2400 / 3168 bytes), the string `dk_PKE {vvert} ek {vvert} H(ek) {vvert} z` of cite:[nist-fips-203] Algorithm 16,
 where `dk_PKE` has 384·`k` bytes, `ek` has 384·`k` + 32 bytes, and `H(ek)` and `z` have 32 bytes each (`k` = 2, 3, 4).
 ** `encapsk`: 6400 / 9472 / 12544 bits (800 / 1184 / 1568 bytes). It is not stored separately: it is the `ek` sub-field of `decapsk`,
 at byte offset 384·`k` (768 / 1152 / 1536). When only an encapsulation key is held, the other bytes of `decapsk` are zero.
 ** `HasEncapsk`, `HasDecapsk`: Booleans, recording whether `encapsk`, resp. a complete `decapsk`, holds a configured value.
 They are bits 0 and 1 of _StateExtension_, resp., and follow the pattern of Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the departures stated below.
 ** `HasCiphertext`: Boolean, bit 2 of _StateExtension_, recording whether `ciphertext` holds a complete value, loaded and
 checked, or computed by _Encapsulate_. It follows the pattern of Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>.
 ** `HasSharedKey`: Boolean, bit 3 of _StateExtension_, recording whether `sharedkey` holds the key that _Encapsulate_ or
 _Decapsulate_ produced, whatever its value, zero included. It is set when that operation completes, and cleared whenever
 `sharedkey` is zeroed. A _StateExtension_ @ _State_ with _sharedkey_Output_ and `HasSharedKey` clear is not supported
 by the Machine: an SCC that carries it is invalid Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
 ** `ciphertext`: 6144 / 8704 / 12544 bits (768 / 1088 / 1568 bytes).
 ** `sharedkey`: 256 bits.

`encapsk`, `decapsk`, resp. `sharedkey` correspond to `ek`, `dk`, resp. `K` in cite:[nist-fips-203].

In the States that load a long field (_encapsk_Input_, _decapsk_Input_ and _ciphertext_Input_) or emit one (_encapsk_Output_ and _ciphertext_Output_), _MachineUse_ records the number of bytes transferred so far.
It is the transfer counter _W_ of Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, which the ECC Machines keep in `block_base` instead (<<KLE-ECC>>).
--

* *Serialized Content*:
+
--
[%autowidth,cols="^1,<3,<3",options="header"]
|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits) +
for ML-KEM-512/768/1024 | Field
|    i  | 13056 / 19200 / 25344 | `decapsk`, which holds either a complete decapsulation key or only `encapsk` in its `ek` sub-field, according to `HasDecapsk`
|   ii  | 6144 / 8704 / 12544   | `ciphertext`
|  iii  | 256                   | `sharedkey`
|===

These contexts are large: for ML-KEM-512, ML-KEM-768 and ML-KEM-1024 the serialized content is
2432, 3520 and 4768 bytes, that is 152, 220 and 298 blocks of 128 bits; `encapsk` takes no
space of its own, since it is held in the `ek` sub-field of `decapsk`.
--

Machine-Specific Functions::
+
--
The procedures implemented by ML-KEM are taken directly from cite:[nist-fips-203]:

* `ML-KEM.KeyGen()` (cite:[nist-fips-203] Algorithm 19) draws the random seeds `d`, `z` and computes the encapsulation key `encapsk` and the decapsulation key `decapsk` as (`encapsk`, `decapsk`) = `ML-KEM.KeyGen_internal(d, z)` (Algorithm 16).
* `ML-KEM.Encaps(encapsk)` (cite:[nist-fips-203] Algorithm 20) draws the randomness `m` and computes the shared key `sharedkey` and the ciphertext `ciphertext` as (`sharedkey`, `ciphertext`) = `ML-KEM.Encaps_internal(encapsk, m)` (Algorithm 17).
* `ML-KEM.Decaps(decapsk, ciphertext)` computes shared key `sharedkey` from decapsulation key `decapsk` and ciphertext `ciphertext` (cite:[nist-fips-203] Algorithm 21).

The loop of `SampleNTT` (cite:[nist-fips-203] Algorithm 7) iterates an indeterminate number of times (cite:[nist-fips-203] Appendix B).
Like cite:[nist-fips-203], KLE requires neither a bound on its iterations nor an execution time independent of their number (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCProtection-field>>).
An implementation that bounds it uses at least the limit of cite:[nist-fips-203] Appendix B.
When the limit is reached, the operation ends without output: it destroys its intermediate values and any value drawn for it, and zeroes _MachineUse_.
The state machine then transitions to _Failure_.
--

State Machine::
+
--
* *States:*
Besides
_Ready_ (1) and
_Failure_ (47),
ML-KEM Machines define the following States:
 ** _GenerateKeyPair_ (2)
 ** _Encapsulate_ (3)
 ** _Decapsulate_ (4)
 ** _encapsk_Input_ (5)
 ** _decapsk_Input_ (6)
 ** _encapsk_Output_ (7)
 ** _ciphertext_Input_ (8)
 ** _ciphertext_Output_ (9)
 ** _sharedkey_Output_ (10)
--

* *Allowed State Transitions:*
 ** By `kl.setst`, under the entry conditions given below, from _Ready_ or any State listed above to another of _Ready_,
    _GenerateKeyPair_, _Encapsulate_, _Decapsulate_, _encapsk_Input_, _decapsk_Input_, _ciphertext_Input_,
    _encapsk_Output_ or _ciphertext_Output_; from _Failure_, only as Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>> allows.
 ** On completion of its `kl.exec`: _GenerateKeyPair_ -> _encapsk_Output_; _Encapsulate_ -> _ciphertext_Output_; _Decapsulate_ -> _sharedkey_Output_.
 ** When a `SampleNTT` limit is reached (see above): _GenerateKeyPair_, _Encapsulate_ or _Decapsulate_ -> _Failure_.
 ** Once its field has been completely emitted: _encapsk_Output_ -> _Ready_; _ciphertext_Output_ -> _sharedkey_Output_ if `HasSharedKey` is set, as after _Encapsulate_, and -> _Ready_ otherwise.
 ** Once its field has been completely loaded: _ciphertext_Input_ -> _Ready_; _encapsk_Input_ -> _Failure_ if the encapsulation key check fails.
 ** _sharedkey_Output_ is entered only by these transitions (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
 ** ML-KEM Machines never enter _Success_.

* *Behavior:*
+
--
** Provisioning clears `decapsk` (and with it `encapsk`), `ciphertext` and `sharedkey`, clears `HasEncapsk`, `HasDecapsk`,
   `HasCiphertext` and `HasSharedKey`, and zeroes _MachineUse_.

** Entering _Ready_ zeroes `sharedkey` and _MachineUse_, and keeps `decapsk` (and with it `encapsk`), `ciphertext`,
   `HasEncapsk`, `HasDecapsk` and `HasCiphertext`.

** Leaving _sharedkey_Output_ zeroes `sharedkey`.
Leaving _ciphertext_Output_ for a State other than _sharedkey_Output_ and _ciphertext_Output_ also zeroes `sharedkey`.

** Entering:
  . _encapsk_Input_ zeroes the whole `decapsk` field and clears `HasEncapsk` and `HasDecapsk`; the load writes the `encapsk`
    sub-field, so that the locker then holds an encapsulation key only;
  . _decapsk_Input_ clears `HasEncapsk` and `HasDecapsk`;
  . _ciphertext_Input_ zeroes `ciphertext` and `sharedkey`, and clears `HasCiphertext`;
  . _Encapsulate_ or _Decapsulate_ zeroes `sharedkey`.

** No `kl.setst` of this Machine takes an auxiliary parameter.

** The input checks of cite:[nist-fips-203] {sect}7.2 and {sect}7.3 are performed when a field is written
   (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>): when its load completes, and when an import restores a key that `HasEncapsk` or
   `HasDecapsk` marks as configured.
   A `kl.setst` that leaves _encapsk_Input_, _decapsk_Input_ or _ciphertext_Input_ before the field is complete zeroes
   the field (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>): `HasEncapsk` and `HasDecapsk`, resp. `HasCiphertext`, stay clear.
   Departing from Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, excess data in the final loading transfer of `encapsk`, `decapsk` or
   `ciphertext` fails the type check.
   The checks and their outcomes are, for each field:
  . `encapsk`: the *encapsulation key check* of cite:[nist-fips-203] {sect}7.2, that is, the type check (`encapsk` has
    the length required by the parameter set) and the modulus check (`encapsk` re-encodes to itself, i.e. every
    coefficient is reduced modulo `q`).
    A completed load that passes it sets `HasEncapsk`.
    A failure, of a loaded `encapsk` or of an imported one that `HasEncapsk` marks, is a *data* error
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>): the whole `decapsk` field is zeroed, `HasEncapsk` and `HasDecapsk` stay
    clear, and the state machine transitions to _Failure_.
  . `decapsk`: the *decapsulation key check* of cite:[nist-fips-203] {sect}7.3, that is, the type check and the hash
    check (the copy of `H(encapsk)` embedded in `decapsk` matches the embedded `encapsk`).
    A failure is a *configuration* error: the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_.
    When the load completes, the encapsulation key check is also performed on the embedded `encapsk`.
    A completed load that passes the decapsulation key check sets `HasDecapsk`, and also `HasEncapsk` if the embedded
    `encapsk` passes the encapsulation key check; a failure of that check only leaves `HasEncapsk` clear.
  . `ciphertext`: when the load completes, the *ciphertext type check* of cite:[nist-fips-203] {sect}7.3, which a
    `ciphertext` loaded without excess data passes: `HasCiphertext` is set, and the state machine transitions to _Ready_.
    A failure is a *data* error: `ciphertext` is zeroed, `HasCiphertext` stays clear, and the state machine transitions
    to _Failure_.
+
NOTE: These checks do not affect the implicit rejection of `ML-KEM.Decaps`: a ciphertext that passes the type check but
does not decapsulate correctly still yields the `z`-derived shared key, and the state machine still transitions to
_sharedkey_Output_.

** In States _GenerateKeyPair_, _Encapsulate_ and _Decapsulate_ the operation is long-running: _MachineUse_ is the field
   _P_ of Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>, and, when it is non-zero, the interrupted operation is resumed or restarted as
   per that Rule. Its random values and intermediate values are kept in the ADS
   (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>>).
   On completion, the operation destroys the values drawn for it and its intermediate values, except those that its
   result holds, such as `z` in `decapsk`; values computed from `encapsk` or `decapsk` alone, in any representation,
   masked shares included, may be kept in the ADS until that field is replaced or erased
   (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Auxiliary-Data-Section>>, cite:[nist-fips-203] {sect}3.3).

** In State _GenerateKeyPair_, a Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` generates a new key pair with `ML-KEM.KeyGen` of
   cite:[nist-fips-203] (Algorithm 19):
  . Besides a `SampleNTT` limit (see above), it fails only if the RBG fails; the locker then transitions to Error State
    _Invalid_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>).
  . Otherwise `decapsk` (and with it `encapsk`) receives the new key pair, `HasEncapsk` and `HasDecapsk` are set, and
    the state machine transitions to _encapsk_Output_, to emit the public key.
  . Leaving _encapsk_Output_ keeps the key pair, whatever State follows; only entering _encapsk_Input_ or _decapsk_Input_
    discards it (see below).

** Entering _Encapsulate_ requires `HasEncapsk` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>). In it, a Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` applies
   `ML-KEM.Encaps` of cite:[nist-fips-203] (Algorithm 20) to `encapsk`:
  . it draws one random value and derives from it both the shared key and the ciphertext, which it stores in
    `sharedkey` and `ciphertext`; `HasCiphertext` is set;
  . besides a `SampleNTT` limit, it fails only if the RBG fails; the locker then transitions to Error State _Invalid_
    (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>);
  . otherwise the state machine transitions to _ciphertext_Output_.

** Entering _Decapsulate_ requires `HasDecapsk` and `HasCiphertext` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>). In it, a Form D
   ``kl.exec Kn`` applies `ML-KEM.Decaps` of cite:[nist-fips-203] (Algorithm 21):
  . it runs whatever the ciphertext, and fails only on a `SampleNTT` limit;
  . it places in `sharedkey` the shared key derived from the ciphertext or, by implicit rejection, the one derived from
    `z`, which the caller cannot tell apart, and the state machine transitions to _sharedkey_Output_.

** In States _encapsk_Input_, _decapsk_Input_ and _ciphertext_Input_, Form B `kl.exec` instructions load `encapsk`,
   `decapsk` and `ciphertext`, following Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the progress recorded in _MachineUse_.
   The parameter set and the State fix the size of each field (<<KLE-ML-KEM-sizes>>).

** In States _encapsk_Output_ and _ciphertext_Output_, Form C `kl.exec` instructions emit `encapsk` and `ciphertext`,
   following Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the progress recorded in _MachineUse_. Entering _encapsk_Output_
   requires `HasEncapsk`, and entering _ciphertext_Output_ requires `HasCiphertext` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).

** No `kl.exec` emits `sharedkey`. Besides the instructions that move or copy the whole CC, an export and import,
   `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` and `kl.swap`, only `kl.derive`
   (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-derive>>) moves it, as a field source (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>),
   and only in State _sharedkey_Output_ (<<KLE-derive-endpoints>>):
  . that State admits no `kl.exec`, and any number of `kl.derive` until the caller leaves it;
  . each `kl.derive` reads the field from its first byte (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>):
    a key field receives its first `eff_length` bytes, and a message input `length` bytes, at least the 32 of the field
    and at most 32 rounded up to the granularity of the destination, zero beyond the field (Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>,
    <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> item 5 and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>).
--

[[KLE-PQC-ML-DSA]]
=== ML-DSA

The _Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Standard_ was announced by NIST in August 2024 cite:[nist-fips-204] in three variants: ML-DSA-44, ML-DSA-65, and ML-DSA-87.

[[KLE-ML-DSA-sizes]]
.Sizes (in bytes) of keys and signatures of ML-DSA
[%autowidth,float="center",align="center",cols="^1,^1,^1,^1",options="header"]
|===
| Subtype   | Private Key | Public Key | Signature Size
| ML-DSA-44 |     2560    |    1312    |     2420
| ML-DSA-65 |     4032    |    1952    |     3309
| ML-DSA-87 |     4896    |    2592    |     4627
|===

They require RBGs of security strength at least 128 (192 recommended), 192, and 256 bits, respectively, for `xi`
(cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}3.6.1); an ML-DSA-44 CC with a 128-bit RBG has security category 1.

The granularity of the ML-DSA Machines is 8 bits: a field can be transferred with any `SEW` from 8 to 64 bits that divides its size, or through the KLIOBUF in any number of bytes.

The principal functions offered by ML-DSA are:

* `ML-DSA.KeyGen`: Generates a public-private key pair.
* `ML-DSA.Sign`:   Generates a digital signature for a given message.
* `ML-DSA.Verify`: Verifies a digital signature for a given message.
* `HashML-DSA.Sign`:   Generates a digital signature for a given pre-hashed message.
* `HashML-DSA.Verify`: Verifies a digital signature for a given pre-hashed message.

The KLE ML-DSA Machines execute the core lattice operations of signing and verification, specifically `ML-DSA.Sign_internal` and `ML-DSA.Verify_internal` as defined in cite:[nist-fips-204] (Algorithms 7 and 8), with an _external_ message representative μ:
the caller supplies μ in State _mu_Input_, and the steps of these algorithms that compute μ from `tr` and the formatted message `M'`
are not performed. Consequently the Machine never receives the message or the context string `ctx`, and holds `tr` only within `privkey`.

Message hashing and domain formatting (cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}5) are structured as follows:

* *Pure ML-DSA:* The caller formats the domain-separated message string `M' = 0x00 {vvert} bin(|ctx|, 8) {vvert} ctx {vvert} M` and computes the 64-byte message representative `mu = SHAKE256(tr {vvert} M', 64)`, which is supplied to the KLE unit via State _mu_Input_.
* *HashML-DSA (Pre-Hash):* The caller pre-hashes message `M` with the hash function `PH` specified in the _AuxInfo_ field of the MDH, constructs `M' = 0x01 {vvert} bin(|ctx|, 8) {vvert} ctx {vvert} OID(PH) {vvert} PH(M)`, and computes `mu = SHAKE256(tr {vvert} M', 64)` to be loaded into State _mu_Input_.
For FIPS conformance, cite:[nist-fips-204] ({sect}5.4 and {sect}5.4.1) requires `PH(M)` to be computed within a FIPS-140-validated cryptographic module.
That module may be the KLE unit itself, if the unit is FIPS-140-validated.
* *Public Key Hash (`tr`):* `tr = SHAKE256(pubkey, 64)`. It is embedded in `privkey` (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 24), where `ML-DSA.KeyGen` places it; the caller computes it from the public key.

[NOTE]
====
*The caller's duties in forming μ.*
The message representative μ and the string `M'` are formed as stated above, following cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}5.2 to 5.4 and Algorithms 2 to 5, 7 and 8,
with `PH` the function that _AuxInfo_ records, and `ctx` a context string of at most 255 bytes, chosen by the protocol, `|ctx|` being its length in bytes.
The caller is expected to:

. obtain the public key `pk` of the CC: the key it loaded in State _pubkey_Input_ or, for a CC that holds a private key, the key
  emitted in State _pubkey_Output_ after _GenerateKeyPair_ or _compute_pubKey_, or a copy of it from an authenticated source,
  such as a certificate;
. compute `tr` from `pk` once, and keep it with the CC;
. for each message, choose the mode that _AuxInfo_ selects (pure if _AuxInfo_ = 0), form `M'` with the context string that the
  protocol prescribes, compute μ, and load it in State _mu_Input_, before entering _Sign_Generate_ or _Sign_Verify_;
. use, when verifying, the same `ctx` and mode as the signer; and
. compute `PH(M)` for HashML-DSA within a cryptographic module of the required validation level (see above).

The Machine cannot check any of these steps: a μ formed with a wrong `tr`, `ctx` or mode yields a signature that the
intended verifier rejects, or a verification that fails, but it never discloses or weakens the private key.
====

Data Structures::
+
--
* The *Provisioning Input* consists only of the MDH.
**  The _MachinePolicy_ field in the MDH has the following meaning:

. _MachinePolicy_[0] gates _Sign_Generate_ and _Sign_Output_;
. _MachinePolicy_[1] gates _Sign_Input_ and _Sign_Verify_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
. A _MachinePolicy_ of 0 is admissible, but makes no sense, since the locker can then neither generate nor verify signatures (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Machine-field>>).

** The _AuxInfo_ field is zero for pure `ML-DSA`. For `HashML-DSA` it records the pre-hash function
`PH`, in the format of bits [15:0] of the MDH (_Machine_, _MachinePolicy_ and _MachineExtension_),
which the caller chooses among the hash functions and XOFs that cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}5.4 admits for the parameter set.
The Machine never reads _AuxInfo_: it neither computes `PH` nor checks or enforces its use.
It keeps the field unchanged, also through provisioning and transitions to _Ready_, so that the MDH, in the clear, informs the recipients of the CC,
which need not implement `PH`. The caller always computes μ with SHAKE256, whatever `PH` is (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 7).

* *Internal State*:

** _MachineUse_, in the MDH, is the transfer counter _W_ of Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, in bytes, in the States that load or emit a long field.
  In States _GenerateKeyPair_, _compute_pubKey_, _Sign_Generate_ and _Sign_Verify_, it is the field _P_ of
  Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>> for their long-running operations, and encodes their progress in an implementation-defined way.
** `HasPrivKey`, `HasPubKey`: Booleans, recording whether `privkey`, resp. `pubkey`, holds
  a configured value. They are bits 0 and 1 of _StateExtension_, resp., and follow the pattern of Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the departures stated below.
** `Hedged`: Boolean, bit 2 of _StateExtension_, recording the signing mode selected on entering _Sign_Generate_.
** `HasMu`: Boolean, bit 3 of _StateExtension_, recording that `mu` holds a completely loaded μ. It follows the pattern of
  Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>.
** `privkey`: 2560/4032/4896 bytes (20480/32256/39168 bits) for ML-DSA-44/65/87, resp.
** `pubkey` : 1312/1952/2592 bytes (10496/15616/20736 bits) for ML-DSA-44/65/87, resp.
** `signature`: 2420/3309/4627  bytes (19360/26472/37016 bits) for ML-DSA-44/65/87, resp.
** `mu`: 64 bytes (512 bits), the external message representative μ
** `rnd`: 32 bytes (256 bits), the random value of hedged signing; it is non-zero only while a hedged signing operation is interrupted (Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>), and zero otherwise, including for deterministic signing.

[none]
* The ML-DSA Machine does not hash the message: the caller computes μ as described above.

* Besides `rnd`, the internal state may contain several additional variables, for instance intermediate values of the computation of the NTT.
Their definition and their size are implementation-dependent.
An SCC must save them in the ADS, so that an interrupted operation can resume on the same implementation, as Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>> prescribes.
When an operation completes, it destroys the values drawn for it, `xi` and `rnd` included, and its intermediate values; values computed from `privkey` or `pubkey` alone, in any representation, masked shares included, may be kept in the ADS until that field is replaced or erased (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Auxiliary-Data-Section>>, cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}3.6.3).

* *Serialized Content*:
+

|===
|  Pos. | Size (Bits) +
for ML-DSA-44/65/87 | Field
|   i   | 20480 / 32256 / 39168            | `privkey` (contains `tr`)
|  ii   | 10496 / 15616 / 20736            | `pubkey`

These contexts are large: for ML-DSA-44, ML-DSA-65 and ML-DSA-87 the serialized content is
6400, 9392 and 12224 bytes, that is 400, 587 and 764 blocks of 128 bits, padding included.

Only `HasPrivKey`, `HasPubKey`, `Hedged` and `HasMu`, in _StateExtension_, record state: nothing records whether `signature` holds a value.
Departing from Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, _Sign_Verify_ has no entry condition on `signature`: one not loaded,
or left incomplete, is zero and is verified as loaded.
A `privkey` with a coefficient of `s1` or `s2` outside [−η, η] (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 25) is a configuration error:
the locker transitions to Error State _Invalid_ when `privkey` is written, by a completed load or by an import with `HasPrivKey` set
(Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-write>>).
Every `pubkey` of the correct length is well formed (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 23).
A `signature` is not checked when it is loaded: as in cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 8, `ML-DSA.Verify_internal` decodes it
(Algorithm 27), and a malformed `signature`, for which the decoding returns ⊥, is, like one that does not verify
against `mu`, a data error on which _Sign_Verify_ transitions to _Failure_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-config-data-error>>).

Machine-Specific Functions::
+
--
The procedures implemented by ML-DSA are taken directly from cite:[nist-fips-204]:

* `ML-DSA.KeyGen(xi)` generates public key `pubkey` and private key `privkey` from random seed `xi` (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 1).
* `ML-DSA.Sign_internal(privkey, mu, rnd)` generates digital signature `signature` from private key `privkey`, message representative `mu`, and randomness `rnd` (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 7).
* `ML-DSA.Verify_internal(pubkey, mu, signature)` verifies signature `signature` against public key `pubkey` and message representative `mu` (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 8).

Four loops of these procedures, in `ML-DSA.Sign_internal`, `RejBoundedPoly`, `RejNTTPoly` and `SampleInBall`, iterate an indeterminate number of times (cite:[nist-fips-204] Appendix C).
An implementation bounds the iterations of each of them by an implementation-defined limit of at least the value of
cite:[nist-fips-204] Appendix C, Table 3: 814 for `ML-DSA.Sign_internal`, 481 for `RejBoundedPoly`, 298 for `RejNTTPoly`
and 121 for `SampleInBall`, which a correct execution reaches with probability at most 2^-256^.
Like cite:[nist-fips-204], KLE requires no execution time independent of the number of iterations
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCProtection-field>>).
When a limit is reached, the operation ends without output: it destroys its intermediate values and any value drawn for it, and zeroes _MachineUse_.
The state machine then transitions to _Failure_.

NOTE: With deterministic signing, the execution time may reveal information about μ, but not about the private key (cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}3.6.1, footnote 3); hedged signing does not.

NOTE: An implementation must not let the values of a rejected iteration of `ML-DSA.Sign_internal`, the challenge `c` and
the response `z`, or which check rejected it, become observable, through _MachineUse_, side channels or otherwise:
from the challenges and responses of rejected iterations, the private key can be recovered
(cite:[DBLP-journals-tches-ZhouWSY25]; see also cite:[DBLP-conf-host-KarabulutAA21] and
cite:[DBLP-conf-sacrypt-UlitzschMTS22]).
This is why the progress of _Sign_Generate_ reveals at most the number of completed iterations, which the execution time
reveals anyway.
--

State Machine:: {empty}
+
--
* *States:*
Besides
_Ready_ (1),
_Success_ (46),
_Failure_ (47),
ML-DSA Machines define:
 ** _GenerateKeyPair_ (2)
 ** _pubkey_Output_ (3)
 ** _pubkey_Input_ (4)
 ** _mu_Input_ (6)
 ** _Sign_Generate_ (8)
 ** _Sign_Output_ (9)
 ** _Sign_Verify_ (10)
 ** _Sign_Input_ (11)
 ** _privkey_Input_ (12)
 ** _compute_pubKey_ (13)

* *Allowed State Transitions:*

** By `kl.setst`, between any two different States listed above, subject to Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>> and to the entry conditions below; _Sign_Output_ is entered only on completion of _Sign_Generate_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).
** On completion of its `kl.exec`: _GenerateKeyPair_ -> _pubkey_Output_; _compute_pubKey_ -> _Ready_; _Sign_Generate_ -> _Sign_Output_; _Sign_Verify_ -> _Success_ or _Failure_.
** Once its field has been completely emitted: _pubkey_Output_ -> _Success_; _Sign_Output_ -> _Success_.
** When an implementation-defined loop limit is reached (see above): -> _Failure_.

* *Behavior:*

** Provisioning clears `privkey` and `pubkey`, clears `HasPrivKey` and `HasPubKey`, zeroes _MachineUse_, and leaves the
   locker in _Ready_.

** A Form A `kl.setst` enters _Ready_, _GenerateKeyPair_, _privkey_Input_, _pubkey_Input_, _mu_Input_, _Sign_Input_,
   _pubkey_Output_, _compute_pubKey_ and _Sign_Verify_.

** Entering _Ready_ clears `signature`, `mu`, `rnd` and `HasMu`, zeroes _MachineUse_, and keeps `privkey`, `pubkey`,
   `HasPrivKey` and `HasPubKey`.

** In State _GenerateKeyPair_, a Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` generates a new key pair with `ML-DSA.KeyGen` of
   cite:[nist-fips-204] (Algorithm 1), from a seed `xi` drawn from the RBG (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>):
  . the key pair replaces `privkey` (which embeds `tr`) and `pubkey`, and `HasPrivKey` and `HasPubKey` are set;
  . the state machine transitions to _pubkey_Output_, with _MachineUse_ zeroed; leaving it keeps the key pair.

** Entering _privkey_Input_ clears `HasPrivKey` and `HasPubKey` and erases `pubkey`. When the load completes and
   `privkey` passes its check (see Serialized Content), `HasPrivKey` is set.

** Entering _pubkey_Input_ requires `HasPrivKey` to be clear (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and clears `HasPubKey`; when
   the load completes, `HasPubKey` is set.

** In States _privkey_Input_, _pubkey_Input_, _mu_Input_ and _Sign_Input_, Form B `kl.exec` instructions load `privkey`,
   `pubkey`, `mu` and `signature`, respectively, following Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>, with the progress recorded in
   _MachineUse_, in bytes.

** Entering _compute_pubKey_ requires `HasPrivKey` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>). In it, a Form D `kl.exec`:
  . derives `pubkey` from `privkey`: it decodes it with skDecode (cite:[nist-fips-204] Algorithm 25) and applies steps 3, 5,
    6 and 8 of Algorithm 6;
  . checks that the public key hashes to `tr`, embedded in `privkey`; if not, the locker transitions to Error State
    _Invalid_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-check-on-read>>);
  . writes the derived public key into `pubkey` and sets `HasPubKey`;
  . transitions to _Ready_, which keeps the key pair.

** When entering _Sign_Generate_, by a Form B `kl.setst` whose `Xs` selects the signing mode of
   cite:[nist-fips-204] {sect}3.4:
  . the State requires `HasPrivKey` and `HasMu` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>);
  . `Xs` = 0, for instance with `Xs` = `X0`, selects _hedged_ signing and sets `Hedged`;
  . any other `Xs` selects *deterministic* signing and clears `Hedged`.

** In State _Sign_Generate_, a Form D ``kl.exec Kn`` signs with `ML-DSA.Sign_internal` of cite:[nist-fips-204]
   (Algorithm 7). The operation is long-running, and _MachineUse_ is its field _P_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>),
   which reveals at most the number of completed iterations of the signing loop:
  . if _MachineUse_ is zero, the operation starts, with `rnd` drawn from an approved RBG
    (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>) if `Hedged` is set, and `rnd` = `zeros(256)` otherwise;
  . if _MachineUse_ is non-zero, the interrupted operation is resumed with the `rnd` held, or restarted
    (Rule <<KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>);
  . on completion, the output of `ML-DSA.Sign_internal` is written in `signature`, and the locker
    transitions to _Sign_Output_.

** Entering _Sign_Verify_ requires `HasPubKey` and `HasMu` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>). In it, a Form D ``kl.exec Kn``
   verifies `signature` with `ML-DSA.Verify_internal` of cite:[nist-fips-204] (Algorithm 8): if it returns `true`, the
   state machine transitions to _Success_, otherwise to _Failure_. Nothing is written to `signature`.

** In _pubkey_Output_ and _Sign_Output_, Form C `kl.exec` instructions emit `pubkey`, resp. `signature`, recording the
   progress in _MachineUse_, in bytes; once the field is complete, the locker transitions to _Success_
   (Rule <<KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>).
  . Entering _pubkey_Output_ requires `HasPubKey` (Rule <<KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>).
  . _Sign_Output_ is entered only on completion of the `kl.exec` of _Sign_Generate_ (Rule <<KLE-MGR-machine-transitions>>).

** _MachinePolicy_ can be narrowed using `kl.restrictl` or `kl.restrictv` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>).
