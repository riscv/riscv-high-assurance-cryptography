The KLE Privileged Architecture is _implemented_ on a hart whose `mstatus` implements the `KLS` field. It is mandatory on every hart that is not M-mode-only, and optional on an M-mode-only hart.
The KLE exceptions of <<KLE-exceptions>> exist on every hart, whether or not it implements the KLE Privileged Architecture.
Where the KLE Privileged Architecture is not implemented:

* `KLS` is not present, and is treated as Initial;
* `Smklstatus` and `Ssklvbootscrt` are not implemented; and
* the `mklcsk`, `mklphysbootscrt` and `mkllocality` groups may be implemented or not, each where its extension is implemented, with the accessibility that <<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>> and <<KLE-CSR-locality-groups>> prescribe.

On an M-mode-only hart, none of the extensions in the table above is mandatory.
`Smklcsk` and `Smklpbootscrt` are allowed in every configuration; with `Smklpbootscrt`, the `mklphysbootscrt`
CSR group sets the Physical Boot Secret Locality.
The requirements for the other extensions are:

[[KLE-priv-config-requirements]]
.Privileged requirements, per system configuration
[float="center",align="center",cols="<19%,<27%,<27%,<27%",options="header"]
|===
| Extension {rightarrow} +
Configuration {downarrow}  | `Smklstatus` | `Smkllclt` | `Ssklvbootscrt`

| M-mode only
| Optional: If implemented, `mllockerstatus` is implemented; `sllockerstatus` is not.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality` configures MLocality
| {empty}

| M+U
| Mandatory: `mllockerstatus` is implemented; `sllockerstatus` is not.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality` configures MLocality
| {empty}

| M+S+U
| Mandatory: `mllockerstatus` and `sllockerstatus` (`Ssklstatus`) are implemented and are distinct.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality` and `skllocality` (`Sskllclt`) configure MLocality and SLocality
| {empty}

| M+S+U with H
| Mandatory: `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` (the latter two `Ssklstatus`) are implemented and are distinct.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality`, `hkllocality` and `skllocality` (the latter two `Sskllclt`) configure MLocality, HLocality and SLocality
| Optional: If implemented, `hklvirtbootscrt` configures the Virtual Boot Secret Locality
|===

The last two columns give the CSR groups implemented with the extension,
and the Localities they set (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>); an empty cell means the extension has no effect in
that configuration.

[[KLE-exceptions]]
== Exceptions Raised by KLE

This section describes the error conditions that raise exceptions, not those that set an Error State (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-State-field>>).

When an exception is raised, `*cause` holds the cause, as <<KLE-exception-codes>> describes; the table lists the causes in decreasing priority order.

The KLE fatal-error exception precedes every exception that depends on the state of the KLE unit, including the address-misaligned, access-fault and page-fault exceptions of KLE memory instructions, whose transfer bounds are computed from that state (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-mv>>); it is preceded only by the exceptions of the instruction fetch and by the illegal-instruction or virtual-instruction exception of a `KLS` field in effect that is Off or of a `*stateen0` denial, and precedes the other illegal-instruction grounds.

[[KLE-exception-codes]]
.Exception causes in decreasing priority order
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^10%,<24%,<66%",options="header"]
|===
| Exception Code(s)    | Mnemonic/RISC-V type           | Examples in KLE (((KLE, error codes)))
|  3                   | Instruction address breakpoint | As per the Privileged Specification.
| 12, 20, 1            | Instruction page, guest-page, instruction access faults | As per the Privileged Specification.
|  2, 22               | Illegal-instruction, virtual-instruction | One of the conditions in the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-first-group-ILL-grounds>> is satisfied.
| TBD                  | *KLE fatal-error*              | Fatal system failure in the KLE unit.
|  2                   | Illegal-instruction            | One of the conditions in the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-second-group-ILL-grounds>> is satisfied.
| TBD                  | *KLE missing-CSK*              | Use of the KLE unit while no CSK is active: either none has been established, or the `mklcsk` group is only partially written (see <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>, <<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>>). Only M-mode can write the `mklcsk` group or control implementation-specific components that set the CSK.
| TBD                  | *KLE locker-Off*               | Any access, as defined in <<KLE-CSR-llockerstatus>>, to a locker whose `*llockerstatus` field is _Off_; it reaches the mode that owns the _Off_ field by delegation or forwarding.  This exception enables on-demand (lazy) locker loading and trap-and-emulate.
|  2                   | Illegal-instruction            | One of the conditions in the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-third-group-ILL-grounds>> is satisfied.
|  3                   | Load/store address breakpoint  | On the address of the first component access of `kl.load` or `kl.store`, before the address-misaligned check (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>, item 8).
| 4, 6                 | Load address misaligned, Store/AMO address misaligned | On the effective address of `kl.load`, resp. `kl.store`, with `klnamedlocker` < 32, before any component access (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>); never raised by a KLE memory instruction with `klnamedlocker` = 32.
| TBD                  | *KLE unsupported-Metadata*     | Unsupported Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>) when opening a provisioning or import.
| TBD                  | *KLE privilege-violation*      | A usage instruction that the _UsagePolicy_ denies (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-policy>>), or any use, clone or restriction of a locker in a Configuration State, which is forbidden at all privilege levels (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-config-state>>).
| TBD                  | *KLE clock-unavailable*        | With `Zklexpire`, a _usage_ instruction on a locker with a non-zero _ExpirationDate_ while the secure clock cannot be read (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-expiration-date>>).
| TBD                  | *KLE out-of-memory*            | Insufficient KLF capacity when opening a provisioning or import, or cloning.
| TBD                  | *KLE RBG-unavailable*          | Random bits that an operation needs remain unavailable past the implementation-defined bound of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>.
|  3                   | Load/store address breakpoint, later components | On the address of a later component access of `kl.load` or `kl.store`, and of any component access of `kl.input` or `kl.output`, when that access is about to be performed.
| 13, 15, 21, 23, 5, 7 | Load/store page fault, load/store guest-page fault, load/store access fault | Per component access, in offset order, as ordered by the Privileged Specification.
|===

`kl.load` and `kl.input` raise the load causes (4, 13, 21, 5), and `kl.store` and `kl.output` the store/AMO causes (6, 15, 23, 7).
The relative order of the standard exceptions is that of the Privileged Specification, reproduced for reference only; the load/store address breakpoint and the load/store faults are evaluated per component access, because the component addresses of a KLE memory instruction are determined from the state of the KLE unit.

NOTE: The `medeleg` and `hedeleg` bits of all KLE exceptions follow the standard ISA definition and so may be read-only zero or writable, but never read-only one.

Each of the 64-bit CSRs `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` that is implemented
is accompanied, when MXLEN, resp. SXLEN, is 32, by a 32-bit CSR, `mllockerstatush`, resp. `sllockerstatush`, `vsllockerstatush`,
which accesses bits [63:32]; the CSR itself then accesses bits [31:0].
`vsllockerstatush` also exists when VSXLEN is 32: while V=1, an access to `sllockerstatush` then accesses it, as one to `sllockerstatus` accesses `vsllockerstatus`.
In this Book, RV32 and RV64 denote an XLEN of 32, resp. 64, in the privilege mode that accesses the CSR.

None of these CSRs, including the `mklcsk` group, is available without the KLE extension, or while a `KLS` field in effect is Off
(<<KLE-KL-field-in-misa>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>>):
an access then raises an illegal-instruction exception, a condition evaluated after the `Smstateen` check below, where implemented, and before the privilege check.
An access from a mode below the privilege of the CSR also raises an illegal-instruction exception, or, while V=1, the virtual-instruction exception that the H extension prescribes where HS-mode would allow the access.
Where `Smstateen` is implemented, the KLE bit (to be allocated) of `mstateen0`, `hstateen0` and `sstateen0` gates every KLE instruction and every KLE CSR in the modes below the register's own; a clear bit makes the instruction or access raise the exception that extension prescribes: an illegal-instruction exception, or a virtual-instruction exception for an `hstateen0` denial while V=1.
These exceptions are decided without accessing the KLE unit, are grounds of the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-first-group-ILL-grounds>>, and precede every other KLE condition.

[[KLE-KL-field-in-misa]]
=== KLE field in misa

{empty}
(((CSR, KLE field in `misa`)))
Bit `L` (11) of `misa` indicates that the KLE extension is present; if KLE is implemented, it must not be read-only zero.

M-mode firmware that provides an emulated KLE unit may set this bit to 1.
It must not clear `misa.L` without first zeroizing all KLE state, emulated or in hardware (`kl.clearall` and the CSRs of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>); while `misa.L` is clear, the KLE state is inaccessible, and need not be retained.

[[KLE-CSR-impl-ids]]
=== mklvendorid, mklarchid, and mklimpid

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklvendorid`)))(((CSR, `mklarchid`)))(((CSR, `mklimpid`)))`mklvendorid`, `mklarchid` and `mklimpid` identify the version of the KLE unit, as `mvendorid`, `marchid` and `mimpid` do for the hart.
They are part of `Zkl` common.
Their values out of KLE unit reset identify the KLE unit.
Where M-mode software emulates the unit, in whole or in part, it keeps the `IMPQUAL` it uses in its own memory, or takes it from these CSRs of the hardware part; no other mode reads it.
// For the reviewer: if the unit is emulated entirely, M-mode software keeps the `IMPQUAL` it uses in secure memory of its own, and the instructions that export an SCC and compare `IMPQUAL` are emulated by the same software, so these CSRs are never read. If it is emulated in part, M-mode takes the values of the CSRs as set by the hardware part.
They are read-only and M-mode-only: a write in any mode, and any access from a lower mode, raises an illegal-instruction exception, or the virtual-instruction exception of the H extension where it prescribes one.

. `mklvendorid` is a 32-bit register holding the JEDEC manufacturer ID of the KLE unit provider, or 0 if non-commercial, in the JEDEC bank/offset encoding of `mvendorid` in the Privileged ISA.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:mvendorid_enc>>).

. `mklarchid` is a 32-bit register encoding the base microarchitecture of the KLE unit, as `marchid` does in the Privileged ISA.
Bit 31 is 0 for an open-source architecture ID, which RVI allocates globally, and 1 for a commercial one, which is specific to the `mklvendorid`.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:marchid_sz_acc_op>>).

. `mklimpid` is a 32-bit register uniquely encoding the version of the KLE unit implementation, as `mimpid` does for the hart in the Privileged ISA; it reads 0 if not implemented; an implementation that produces an ADS must not do this.
Its value reflects the design of the KLE unit itself, not of the surrounding system.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:mimpid_op>>).

On RV64, a read of any of these three CSRs returns its 32-bit value zero-extended to XLEN.

Together, the values of these three CSRs form the _Implementation Qualifier_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Auxiliary-Data-Section>>).

[[KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus]]
=== KLE Context Status in mstatus and sstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,KLS field in mstatus)))
(((CSR,KLS field in sstatus)))
`mstatus`[26:25] holds a KLE context status field, `KLS`, shadowed in `sstatus`[26:25].
Defined like the floating-point and vector context status fields `FS` and `VS`,
it tracks the dirtiness of the lockers, the KLIOBUF and every writable KLE-specific CSR other than the `mklcsk` group.
`mstatus.KLS` is always in effect; when V=1, the `vsstatus.KLS` field of <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>> is
also in effect.

[[KLE-aces-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `KLS` field
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<8%,<84%",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Name |Meaning
|   0   | Off     | KLE disabled. The KLE state is inaccessible: KLE instructions and every KLE CSR access raise an illegal-instruction exception. This holds also for a CSR that M-mode emulates under `kleemulation` (<<KLE-kleemulation>>): M-mode keeps `medeleg` bit 2 zero, so it takes the exception and decides whether to complete the access.
|   1   | Initial | KLE enabled. Every locker is _Unconfigured_, and every other state tracked by the field holds its reset value. The hart may issue any KLE instruction.
|   2   | Clean   | KLE enabled. The state tracked by the field is potentially different from its reset value, but matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty   | KLE enabled. At least one locker, KLIOBUF, or KLE-specific CSR may have changed since the last context swap.
A locker that was configured and is now _Unconfigured_ also makes `KLS` Dirty, since context-switch code must erase its stale saved state.
|===


The following holds for every `KLS` field, in the privilege modes in which it is in effect:

* If a field in effect is Off, every KLE instruction and every access to a KLE CSR raises an illegal-instruction exception.
* Where none is Off, an instruction that modifies KLE state sets every field in effect to Dirty.
  So does a software write to a `*llockerstatus` CSR or to a Locality CSR; context-restoring software must write `KLS` after those CSRs.
* An implementation may set a `KLS` field, or a `*llockerstatus` field, to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, even without a state change.
  Setting a field to Dirty is not a change of KLE state for Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>.
* The `*llockerstatus` and `KLS` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are set before any trap is taken on or after that instruction, and before any later instruction executes, including when the instruction continues in the background (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-preemption>>) or is discarded and restarted (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>).
* While a field is Dirty, the `SD` bit of the register holding it is 1; otherwise that `SD` is set in accordance with the existing specifications.

A hart that implements the KLE Privileged Architecture implements the `KLS` field in every `*status` register it implements.
The field is present and writable while `misa.L` is clear, like `FS` and `VS`; an emulated KLE unit relies on this.

NOTE: Precise management of `mstatus.KLS` is an optimization that software may use to reduce context-switch overhead.

A write to a `KLS` field modifies no KLE state:
setting the field to Off, Initial or Clean does not clear the lockers, the KLIOBUF or the KLE CSRs,
so any such state change must be explicit, and must precede the write.

[[KLE-kleemulation]]
=== KLE Emulation Control in mstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,kleemulation field in mstatus)))
`mstatus`[27] holds `kleemulation`, a bit M-mode sets when it emulates Machines the KLE unit does not implement natively.
It is not visible in `sstatus` or `vsstatus`, and it resets to 0.
M-mode that sets `kleemulation` keeps `medeleg` bit 2 zero, so the exception reaches it, as for trap-and-emulate (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-extensions-overview-unpriv>>), and forwards any other illegal-instruction exception in software.
It controls the Forms B and C of `kl.avail` and `kl.size`, which take an MDH supplied in registers rather than a locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-size>>), once they reach the lookup of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-size-avail-lookup,item 5 of `kl.size`>>; the earlier items, among them the 16 that `kl.size` returns for an MDH in an Error State, do not depend on which Machines are implemented:

* while `kleemulation` is 1, such an instruction raises an illegal-instruction exception if the KLE unit does not natively implement the combination of _Machine_, _MachinePolicy_, _MachineExtension_, _KeyType_ and _SCProtection_ that the MDH carries, so M-mode can supply the value for an emulated Machine;
* while `kleemulation` is 0, it returns 0 for such an MDH, as for any combination that is not implemented.

NOTE: The position of `kleemulation` is provisional and will be assigned by the ARC (<<Zkl-introduction.adoc#KLE-open-points>>).

[[KLE-KLS-field-VS]]
=== KLE Context Status in vsstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,KLS field in vsstatus)))
With the H extension, `vsstatus`[26:25] also holds a `KLS` field. When V=1, it is in
effect together with `mstatus.KLS`, and <<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>> governs both.

NOTE: `mstatus.KLS` and `vsstatus.KLS` are not KLE context state
for state tracking, so a write to `vsstatus.KLS` does not set `mstatus.KLS` to Dirty.

[[KLE-CSR-llockerstatus]]
=== mllockerstatus, sllockerstatus, and vsllockerstatus for Dirtiness Tracking

{empty}
(((CSR, `mllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `sllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `vsllockerstatus`)))
The 64-bit registers `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, and `vsllockerstatus` track per-locker status, including dirtiness, at all levels below M-mode, S-mode, and VS-mode, respectively.

For each of these three CSRs::
`*llockerstatus`(__i__) = `*llockerstatus`[2{interpunct}__i__+1:2{interpunct}__i__] is a two-bit field that holds the status of ``K(``__i__``)``, encoded as follows.
+
--
[[KLE-llockerstatus-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `*llockerstatus` bits
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<12%,<80%",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Name           | Behavior
|   0   | Off            | The locker is inaccessible.
|   1   | Initial        | The locker is accessible and in an initial state as defined by software.
|   2   | Clean          | The locker is accessible and its state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty          | The locker is accessible and its state may not match the last context swap.
|===
--
+
`*llockerstatus` is evaluated after the illegal-instruction grounds that do not require KLE unit state (the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-second-group-ILL-grounds>>), and the KLE fatal-error and missing-CSK exceptions, but before all other conditions, including the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-third-group-ILL-grounds>> and the conditions of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-valid-state>>. The hardware state of a locker with an _Off_ field in effect is never consulted (but see `kl.getmd*` below): conditions that depend on its contents are evaluated only after lazy loading or by the M-mode code that emulates the locker.
+
A KLE instruction _accesses_ ``K(``__i__``)`` if it names the locker as an operand, directly or as `KLNL` while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-override>>); if it is a `kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin` or `kl.mvout` issued while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>); or if it is `kl.clearall`. An access to a locker with an _Off_ field in effect raises a KLE locker-Off exception, except by a <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,_destructive configuration instruction_>> other than `kl.swap`, which reads the locker it overwrites: a provisioning- or import-opening `kl.mgmt`, `kl.clear`, `kl.clearall`, and `kl.clone` or `kl.rename` into it; nor does any access of a `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` or `kl.swap` whose source and destination are the same locker, which performs no operation (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-clone>>). A destructive configuration instruction, on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect, sets every field in effect for the locker to _Dirty_, even if the locker was already _Unconfigured_. However, a `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` or `kl.swap` with identical source and destination performs no operation, raises no KLE locker-Off exception, and sets no field to _Dirty_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-clone>>). An exempted instruction that raises an exception before its zeroizing step likewise sets no field: an instruction replaces _Off_ with _Dirty_ only if it has zeroized or overwritten the locker.
+
A mode whose _Off_ mark has been replaced by _Dirty_ treats the hardware locker as authoritative: a mode that held the authoritative image of the locker for lazy loading discards it, and M-mode, which alone emulates a locker, never again uses the emulated copy it held (<<KLE-unsupported-handling>>).
+
`kl.getmd*` on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect traps like any other access. An implementation may return the MDH without trapping only if the result matches what the trap and subsequent restore would have produced.
+
A mode marks a locker _Off_ only while it holds the authoritative image or emulates the locker; the hardware contents of an _Off_ locker are never the sole copy of a CC. A handler that has not yet saved a locker leaves its field _Clean_ or _Dirty_.

For `mllockerstatus`::
+
--
When the privilege mode is (H)S, VS or U/VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while `mllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `mllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `sllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `vsllockerstatus`(__i__), where these are distinct registers (<<KLE-priv-config-requirements>>).
`mstatus.KLS` is also in effect.
--


For `sllockerstatus`::
+
--
When the privilege mode is VS, U or VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while `mllockerstatus`(__i__) or `sllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `sllockerstatus`(__i__) and `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `sllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `mllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) (if defined).
`mstatus.KLS`, `vsstatus.KLS` when V=1, and `mllockerstatus` are also in effect.
--


For `vsllockerstatus`::
+
--
`vsllockerstatus` exists only with the H extension.
When V=1, it substitutes for `sllockerstatus`: CSR accesses that would target `sllockerstatus` instead access `vsllockerstatus`, as for `vsstatus` and `sstatus`.

When the privilege mode is VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while any of `mllockerstatus`(__i__), `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) or `sllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `vsllockerstatus`(__i__), `sllockerstatus`(__i__) and `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `mllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `sllockerstatus`(__i__) (if defined).
`mstatus.KLS`, `vsstatus.KLS`, `sllockerstatus` and `mllockerstatus` are also in effect.
--

The `*llockerstatus` fields that an instruction sets to _Dirty_ are those in effect, that is, those owned by the privilege modes above the one in which it executes; an instruction never writes the field owned by its own mode.

The KLE locker-Off exception is a single exception cause, whichever `*llockerstatus` field is _Off_, and `medeleg` and `hedeleg` delegate it as any other cause.
While any of its own fields is _Off_, a mode keeps the cause undelegated, in `medeleg` if it is M-mode or in `hedeleg` if it is HS-mode.
The handler that takes the exception examines the fields in effect, in the order `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, `vsllockerstatus`, starting from the field owned by its own mode. If its own field (__i__) is _Off_, it resolves the condition itself, and otherwise forwards the exception to the most privileged mode whose field is _Off_, which owns that field and can write it.
A handler never services a KLE locker-Off exception for a field it cannot write, and an instruction it issues to resolve the condition is not gated by a higher level's field.

[NOTE]
====
The _Off_ value and the KLE locker-Off exception let software load lockers on demand. At context restore, a handler may leave a locker unrestored, set its `*llockerstatus` field to _Off_, and keep the authoritative SCC in memory. A later access from a lower-privileged mode traps with a KLE locker-Off exception to the mode that owns the _Off_ field, directly by delegation or forwarded by a more privileged handler. That handler imports the SCC before resuming execution. The architecture defines no separate "`lazy`" state: software that marks a locker _Off_ must ensure that the authoritative SCC is restored.
====

[[KLE-unsupported-handling]]
==== Emulation of Unsupported Machines

A KLE unsupported-Metadata exception (<<KLE-exception-codes>>) can be resolved only by M-mode emulating the Machine; a handler below M-mode treats it as unresolvable.
An M-mode handler that emulates the Machine instead marks the locker _Off_, holds it in its own protected memory, and services every operation on it, including SCC import and export.
For these, an implementation that supports the emulation of Machines must provide M-mode-only, implementation-specific primitives that compute `SCC_KeyDeriv`, `SCC_Encrypt` and `SCC_Decrypt` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>>) under the active CSK and the Locality Secrets in effect, with the substitutions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>, exactly as the KLE unit computes them, and with the protection of the _SCProtection_ level of each emulated locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>).
The primitives signal when a _Locality_ names an unconfigured entry without a configured substitute, and return neither the CSK, nor the Locality Secrets of the HW Binding Group, nor the keys derived from them.
An implementation without these primitives does not support the emulation of Machines: `kleemulation` is read-only zero, and M-mode treats a KLE unsupported-Metadata exception as unresolvable.

An emulator keeps one copy per locker slot and replaces it:

. at the next KLE unsupported-Metadata exception on that slot;
. at an emulated `kl.clone` naming the slot as destination, which also clears the slot's hardware locker and marks it _Off_, since the clone of an emulated locker is emulated; or
. at an emulated `kl.rename` naming the slot as destination, or an emulated `kl.swap` naming it, either of which moves a copy into the slot.

A copy orphaned by a <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,destructive configuration instruction>> is never used again but lingers until replaced.
As a robustness recommendation, as for a cleared locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config>>), an emulator should zeroize a copy whose _Off_ mark is gone before M-mode next returns control to a less privileged mode; checking `mllockerstatus` whenever it runs finds such copies.

[[KLE-CSR-mklcsk]]
=== mklcsk CSR Group

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklcsk`)))
The optional `mklcsk` group consists of the four MXLEN-bit M-mode RW CSRs `mklcsk0` to `mklcsk3` and, when MXLEN = 32, of their shadows `mklcsk0h` to `mklcsk3h`.

A write to `mklcsk`__i__ supplies bits [64·_i_+MXLEN−1 : 64·_i_], and a write to `mklcsk`__i__``h`` bits [64·_i_+63 : 64·_i_+32], of the value that becomes the CSK once the group has been written in full.

This CSR group is accessible in M-mode whenever `KLS` is not Off and `misa.L` is set; any other access raises an illegal-instruction exception.
Out of reset `KLS` is Off (<<KLE-out-of-reset-priv>>); M-mode firmware enables `KLS` before it establishes the CSK.

A new CSK becomes active only once every CSR of the group has been written since the previous activation; from the first such write until then, the CSK is _partially written_, and the KLE unit is unavailable (KLE missing-CSK exception, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).

Reading a register of the group returns the corresponding part of the active CSK; while no CSK is active (none established, or partially written), the read raises the KLE missing-CSK exception.

A write to the group is not checked against the state of the lockers, configured or under management included: M-mode must write it only after saving all KLE state that it must preserve.

A CSK whose value is `zeros(256)` is unconfigured under every model of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>: writing that value in full through the `mklcsk` group discards any established CSK and leaves the unit without a CSK; and whenever a CSK is randomly generated, as under the fourth model, an all-zero output of the RBG is discarded and regenerated.

The KLE unit is unavailable until a CSK is established (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>): at once for a hardwired CSK, and as soon as it has been generated for a boot-generated one.

[[KLE-CSR-mklmaxiobuflen]]
=== mklmaxiobuflen

(((CSR, `mklmaxiobuflen`)))`mklmaxiobuflen` is an MXLEN-bit M-mode CSR, present with `Zklio` only, that holds the maximum configurable length of the KLIOBUF, in bytes; its hardwired value out of KLE unit reset is its maximum.
Software in the other modes has no access to it: as with `vsetvl`, it learns the maximum by writing a larger value to `kliobuflen` and reading it back.
M-mode should write it only while the KLE unit is in its state out of reset (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>), before any write to `kliobuflen`, and should never reduce it below the current `kliobuflen`: after a write that does, `kliobuflen` and `kliobuftop` become 0 and the whole KLIOBUF is zeroized; no other state is affected.

A write to `mklmaxiobuflen` is WARL: a value above its value out of KLE unit reset sets that value, and a value below the lower bound sets that lower bound.
The lower bound is the larger of 16 bytes and the largest basic processing unit (granularity) of any implemented Machine.
Without `Zklio`, the KLIOBUF does not exist, and `mklmaxiobuflen`, `kliobuflen` and `kliobuftop` are not present.

[[KLE-CSR-locality-groups]]
=== Locality Secret CSR Groups

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklphysbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `hklvirtbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `mkllocality`)))
(((CSR, `hkllocality`)))(((CSR, `skllocality`)))
Each group listed in <<KLE-locality-CSR-groups>> holds one 128-bit Locality Secret
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-locality-indexes>>) in two RW CSRs, numbered “0” and “1”, of _W_ bits, where _W_ is MXLEN for an M-mode group and SXLEN for an HS-mode or S-mode group, and, when _W_ = 32, in their shadows, numbered “0h” and “1h”.
A write to CSR __i__ of a group sets bits [64·_i_+_W_−1 : 64·_i_] of its secret, and a write to CSR __i__``h`` bits [64·_i_+63 : 64·_i_+32].
An access below the privilege of the group raises the exception that <<KLE-privileged-CSRs>> prescribes; an access in
Debug mode that the External Debug Security extension does not authorize for the privilege of the group raises an illegal-instruction exception.
The groups are accessible whether or not a CSK has been established (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>): no ordering between CSK and Locality configuration is enforced.

Software that exports lockers on behalf of a lower privilege level configures every SW Filter Group register it owns before doing so: the export of a locker whose _Locality_ names an unconfigured entry is refused (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-export>>). A hypervisor uses its own `skllocality` value for its host processes, swaps it with the guest's value on entering and leaving the guest, and sets its own value before saving guest KLE state (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>).
`hkllocality` is the hypervisor's tag on its virtual machines: it enters the bindings of every export and import a guest performs, and the hypervisor carries it with the virtual machine.

[[KLE-out-of-reset-priv]]
== KLE Privileged State out of Reset

This section and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> are the authoritative statement of the KLE state out of
reset:

. bit `L` of `misa` is 1 if the KLE unit and the KLE ISA are at least partially implemented in hardware, and 0 for a purely emulated KLE unit;
 if this bit is writable, M-mode firmware may set it to 1;
. the `KLS` field of `mstatus` (visible in `sstatus`) and, where implemented, of `vsstatus` is Off;
. the CSK is unconfigured under the first and second ways of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>; a hardwired CSK is kept, and so is a boot-generated one, which the reset of one hart neither clears nor regenerates;
. the entire KLF memory is zeroed;
. `mklphysbootscrt` and `hklvirtbootscrt` are zero;
. `mkllocality`, `hkllocality` and `skllocality` are zero;
. all fields of `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` are _Initial_;
. `kleemulation` in `mstatus` is 0; and
. with `Zklio`, `mklmaxiobuflen` holds its hardwired maximum (<<KLE-CSR-mklmaxiobuflen>>).

A KLE unit reset caused by a fatal condition (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>) differs in items 1, 2 and 7: `misa.L` is unchanged, a `KLS` field that is Off stays Off and every other `KLS` field becomes Dirty, and every `*llockerstatus` field is set to _Dirty_.
The values of `klstart` and `klnamedlocker` after such a reset are not defined; until the KLE fatal-error exception is raised, any access to them raises it instead (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>).
