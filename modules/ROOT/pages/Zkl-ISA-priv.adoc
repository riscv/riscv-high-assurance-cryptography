//

The KLE Privileged Architecture is _implemented_ on a hart whose `mstatus` implements the `KLS` field.
It is mandatory on every hart that is not M-mode-only, and optional on an M-mode-only hart.
The exceptions of <<KLE-exceptions>> also exist without the KLE Privileged Architecture.
Where the KLE Privileged Architecture is not implemented:

* `KLS` is not present and is treated as Initial;
* `kleemulation` is read-only zero (<<KLE-kleemulation>>);
* `Smklstatus` and `Ssklvbootscrt` are not implemented;
* `mklcfg` is implemented under the first and second ways (<<KLE-CSR-mklcfg>>), `mklmaxiobuflen` with `Zklio` (<<KLE-CSR-mklmaxiobuflen>>), and `mklvendorid`, `mklarchid` and `mklimpid` always (<<KLE-CSR-impl-ids>>); and
* the `mklcsk`, `mklphysbootscrt` and `mkllocality` groups are optional, each where its extension is implemented, with the accessibility that <<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>> and <<KLE-CSR-locality-groups>> prescribe.

On an M-mode-only hart, none of the extensions in the table above is mandatory, except that a hart with more than one _SCProtection_ level whose CSKs are hardwired or boot-generated implements `Smklmultcsk` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-selection>>), and a hart whose CSKs are established under the first way implements `Smklcsk` (<<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>>).
`Smklpbootscrt` is allowed in every configuration, and `Smklcsk` only in those whose CSKs are established under the first way.
The requirements for the other extensions are:

[[KLE-exceptions]]
== Exceptions Raised by KLE

<<KLE-exception-codes>> lists exception causes, held in `*cause`, and their conditions; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-states>> covers Error States.
Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>> orders the locker-state-independent checks.
Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-state-dependent-checks>> and each instruction's stated steps order the subsequent checks.

The KLE fatal-error exception precedes every exception that depends on the state of the KLE unit, including the address-misaligned, access-fault and page-fault exceptions of KLE memory instructions, whose transfer bounds are computed from that state (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-mv>>).
Only the exceptions of the instruction fetch, an instruction-address breakpoint included, and the illegal-instruction or virtual-instruction exceptions of the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-first-group-ILL-grounds>> precede it; it precedes the other illegal-instruction grounds.

`kl.load` raises the load causes (4, 13, 21, 5) and `kl.store` the store/AMO causes (6, 15, 23, 7); `kl.input` raises 13, 21 and 5, and `kl.output` 15, 23 and 7.
The relative order of the standard exceptions is that of the Privileged Specification, with one exception: for `kl.load` and `kl.store`, the load/store address breakpoint and the address-misaligned exception of the first component access (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>, item 8) precede the illegal-instruction exceptions of the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-fourth-group-ILL-grounds>> and the other locker-state-dependent exceptions. The Privileged Specification gives the illegal-instruction exception priority over the address-misaligned exception, and puts it in the same priority class as the load/store address breakpoint.
The load/store address breakpoint and the load/store faults are evaluated per component access, because the state of the KLE unit determines the component addresses of a KLE memory instruction.

The `medeleg` and `hedeleg` bits of the KLE fatal-error cause are read-only zero, so the exception always traps to M-mode: the KLE unit reset that accompanies it clears M-mode state that only M-mode can restore (<<KLE-out-of-reset-priv>>).
M-mode may forward the exception to a less privileged mode in software.

WARNING: An `mtval` value is needed to distinguish a persisting fatal condition (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>) from one that the KLE unit reset ends.
None is defined yet.

NOTE: The `medeleg` and `hedeleg` bits of the other KLE exceptions follow the standard ISA definition and so may be read-only zero or writable, but never read-only one.

When MXLEN is 32, the implemented 64-bit CSR `mllockerstatus` is accompanied by a 32-bit CSR `mllockerstatush`; when SXLEN is 32, each of the implemented `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` is accompanied by `sllockerstatush`, resp. `vsllockerstatush`. Each such 32-bit CSR accesses bits [63:32]. An access to the 64-bit CSR made at an XLEN of 32 accesses bits [31:0]; one made at an XLEN of 64 accesses all 64 bits.
`vsllockerstatush` also exists when VSXLEN is 32: while V=1, an access to `sllockerstatush` then accesses it, as one to `sllockerstatus` accesses `vsllockerstatus`.
Likewise, `skllocality{0..1}h` exist when VSXLEN is 32, and are accessible while V=1 (<<KLE-locality-CSR-groups>>).
An access to an `h` shadow made at an XLEN of 64 raises an illegal-instruction exception, or the virtual-instruction exception that the H extension prescribes.
In this Book, RV32 and RV64 denote an XLEN of 32, resp. 64, in the privilege mode that accesses the CSR.

None of these CSRs, including the `mklcsk` group, is available without the KLE extension or while a `KLS` field in effect is Off (<<KLE-KL-field-in-misa>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>>).
An access then raises an illegal-instruction exception; this condition is evaluated after the `Smstateen` check below, where implemented, and before the privilege check.
An access from a mode below the privilege of the CSR also raises an illegal-instruction exception, or, while V=1, the virtual-instruction exception that the H extension prescribes where HS-mode would allow the access.
Where `Smstateen` is implemented, the KLE bit (to be allocated) of `mstateen0`, `hstateen0` and `sstateen0` gates every KLE instruction and every KLE CSR: `mstateen0` in every mode below M-mode, `hstateen0` in VS-mode and VU-mode, and `sstateen0` in U-mode and VU-mode.
A clear bit makes the instruction or access raise the exception that `Smstateen` prescribes: an illegal-instruction exception, or, for an `hstateen0` denial while V=1 that `mstateen0` does not also deny, a virtual-instruction exception.
These exceptions are decided without accessing the KLE unit, are grounds of the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-first-group-ILL-grounds>>, and precede every other KLE condition.

[[KLE-KL-field-in-misa]]
=== KLE field in misa

{empty}
(((CSR, KLE field in `misa`)))
Bit `L` (11) of `misa` indicates that the KLE extension is present; if KLE is implemented, it must not be read-only zero.

M-mode firmware that provides an emulated KLE unit may set this bit to 1.
Before it clears `misa.L`, it must zeroize all KLE state, emulated or in hardware (`kl.clearall` and the CSRs of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>).
While `misa.L` is clear, the KLE state is inaccessible and need not be retained.

[[KLE-CSR-impl-ids]]
=== mklvendorid, mklarchid, and mklimpid

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklvendorid`)))(((CSR, `mklarchid`)))(((CSR, `mklimpid`)))`mklvendorid`, `mklarchid` and `mklimpid` identify the version of the KLE unit, as `mvendorid`, `marchid` and `mimpid` do for the hart.
They are part of `Zkl` common.
Their values out of KLE unit reset identify the KLE unit.
Where M-mode software emulates the unit, in whole or in part, it keeps the `IMPQUAL` it uses in its own memory, or takes it from these CSRs of the hardware part; no other mode reads it.
// For the reviewer: if the unit is emulated entirely, M-mode software keeps the `IMPQUAL` it uses in secure memory of its own, and the instructions that export an SCC and compare `IMPQUAL` are emulated by the same software, so these CSRs are never read. If it is emulated in part, M-mode takes the values of the CSRs as set by the hardware part.
They are read-only and M-mode-only: a write in any mode, and any access from a lower mode, raises an illegal-instruction exception, or the virtual-instruction exception of the H extension where it prescribes one.

. `mklvendorid` is a 32-bit register holding the JEDEC manufacturer ID of the KLE unit provider, or 0 if non-commercial, in the JEDEC bank/offset encoding of `mvendorid` in the Privileged ISA.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:mvendorid_enc>>).

. `mklarchid` is a 32-bit register encoding the base microarchitecture of the KLE unit, as `marchid` does in the Privileged ISA.
Bit 31 is 0 for an open-source architecture ID, which RVI allocates globally, and 1 for a commercial one, which is specific to the `mklvendorid`.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:marchid_sz_acc_op>>).

. `mklimpid` is a 32-bit register uniquely encoding the version of the KLE unit implementation, as `mimpid` does for the hart in the Privileged ISA.
It reads 0 if not implemented, and an implementation that produces an ADS must implement it.
Its value reflects the design of the KLE unit itself, not of the surrounding system.
//(<<machine.adoc#norm:mimpid_op>>).

On RV64, a read of any of these three CSRs returns its 32-bit value zero-extended to XLEN.

Together, the values of these three CSRs form the _Implementation Qualifier_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Auxiliary-Data-Section>>).

[[KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus]]
=== KLE Context Status in mstatus and sstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,KLS field in mstatus)))
(((CSR,KLS field in sstatus)))
`mstatus`[26:25] holds a KLE context status field, `KLS`, shadowed in `sstatus`[26:25].
Defined like the floating-point and vector context status fields `FS` and `VS`, it tracks the dirtiness of the lockers, the KLIOBUF, the authentication registers and every writable KLE-specific CSR other than `mklcfg` and the `mklcsk` group.
`mstatus.KLS` is always in effect; when V=1, the `vsstatus.KLS` field of <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>> is also in effect.

[[KLE-aces-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `KLS` field
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<8%,<84%",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Name |Meaning
|   0   | Off     | KLE disabled. The KLE state is inaccessible: KLE instructions and every KLE CSR access raise an illegal-instruction exception. This also holds for a KLE CSR that M-mode emulates: M-mode keeps `medeleg` bit 2 zero, so it takes the exception and decides whether to complete the access.
|   1   | Initial | KLE enabled. Every locker is _Unconfigured_, and every other state that the field tracks and that the mode writing the field can write holds its initial value, as defined by software. The hart may issue any KLE instruction.
|   2   | Clean   | KLE enabled. The state tracked by the field may differ from its reset value, but matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty   | KLE enabled. At least one locker, the KLIOBUF, an authentication register or a KLE-specific CSR may have changed since the last context swap.
A locker that was configured and is now _Unconfigured_ also makes `KLS` Dirty, since context-switch code must erase its stale saved state.
|===


For every `KLS` field in effect:

* Off makes every KLE instruction and KLE CSR access raise an illegal-instruction exception, as the table states.
* If none is Off, an instruction that modifies KLE state sets them all to Dirty.
  This includes a write, by a CSR instruction or a setter instruction, to `klstart`, `klnamedlocker`, `kliobuflen`, `kliobuftop`, `mklmaxiobuflen`, a `*llockerstatus` CSR or a Locality CSR; context-restoring software must write `KLS` after those CSRs.
  A write to `mklcfg` or to the `mklcsk` group does not set them.
* An implementation may set a `KLS` field, or a `*llockerstatus` field, to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, even without a state change.
  Setting a field to Dirty is not a change of KLE state for Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>.
* The `*llockerstatus` and `KLS` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are set before any trap is taken on or after that instruction, and before any later instruction executes, including when the instruction continues in the background or is discarded and restarted (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-resumability-options>>, options 1 and 5).

While the `KLS` field of `mstatus` or `vsstatus` is Dirty, the `SD` bit of that register is 1, whether or not the field is in effect; otherwise that `SD` bit follows the existing specifications.

A hart that implements the KLE Privileged Architecture implements the `KLS` field in every `*status` register it implements.
The field is present and writable while `misa.L` is clear, like `FS` and `VS`; an emulated KLE unit relies on this.

NOTE: Precise management of `mstatus.KLS` is an optimization that software may use to reduce context-switch overhead.

A write to `KLS` modifies no KLE state.
Before writing Off, Initial or Clean, software must explicitly make any changes to lockers, the KLIOBUF or KLE CSRs that the value is meant to describe.

[[KLE-kleemulation]]
=== KLE Emulation Control in mstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,kleemulation field in mstatus)))
`mstatus`[27] holds `kleemulation`, a bit M-mode sets when it emulates Machines the KLE unit does not implement natively.
It is not visible in `sstatus` or `vsstatus`, and it resets to 0.
It controls Forms B and C of `kl.avail` and `kl.size`, which take an MDH supplied in registers rather than a locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-size>>), once they reach the lookup of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-size-avail-lookup,item 5 of `kl.size`>>:

* while `kleemulation` is 1, such an instruction raises an illegal-instruction exception if the KLE unit does not natively implement the combination of _Machine_, _MachinePolicy_, _MachineExtension_, _KeyType_ and _SCProtection_ that the MDH carries, so M-mode can supply the value for an emulated Machine;
* while `kleemulation` is 0, it returns 0 for such an MDH, as for any combination that is not implemented.

The items of `kl.size` before that lookup, among them the 16 it returns for an MDH in an Error State, do not depend on which Machines are implemented.
M-mode that sets `kleemulation` keeps `medeleg` bit 2 zero, so that exception reaches it, as for trap-and-emulate (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-unpriv-exts-overview>>), and forwards any other illegal-instruction exception in software.

An implementation may provide implementation-defined means to disable, selectively, Machines or combinations of MDH fields that the KLE unit implements natively.
These means belong to the KLE unit, not to a hart: harts that share a KLE unit share them.
No mode below M-mode can operate these means.
A disabled combination is not natively implemented: its Metadata is unsupported (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>), and M-mode may emulate it in place of the native one.
These means can be changed only after a reset that holds every hart that the KLE unit serves in reset at the same time (a _full reset_ of the KLE unit, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-reset-and-m-mode-only>>), until the first KLE instruction, KLE CSR access or invocation of an emulation primitive (<<KLE-unsupported-handling>>) is issued in any mode on any of these harts, whether or not it raises an exception or succeeds.
An access to the means themselves is not such an access, and `KLS` does not gate it.
From then on they are locked until the next full reset: neither a reset of only some of these harts, nor a KLE unit reset caused by a fatal condition, unlocks them.
Their state out of reset is implementation-defined and documented.
M-mode that emulates Machines sets these means while it sets up its environment and does not change them afterwards: it ensures that they do not change while any software on the platform may be using KLE.
The KLE units of all harts among which software can migrate must have these means set alike: the harts of a cluster that shares a KLE unit share them, and a system with several KLE units is responsible for setting them in the same way in each.

NOTE: Disabling a native Machine lets M-mode replace a defective implementation with a corrected emulated one, and import the CCs of the native Machine into it.
The lock keeps the set of native Machines of a KLE unit fixed between two full resets, so no CC is native and emulated in the same KLE unit in the same boot.
It does not protect SCCs in memory: after a full reset, or in another KLE unit whose harts hold the same CSK and that has the Machine disabled, the primitives of <<KLE-unsupported-handling>> unseal the SCCs of that Machine.
An implementation can keep a hart that is reset on its own from changing these means, for instance by accepting a change only while a single hart of the cluster is running.
M-mode is trusted with them, as with every CC that it emulates (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).

NOTE: The position of `kleemulation` is provisional and will be assigned by the ARC (<<Zkl-introduction.adoc#KLE-open-points>>).

[[KLE-CSR-llockerstatus]]
=== mllockerstatus, sllockerstatus, and vsllockerstatus for Dirtiness Tracking

{empty}
(((CSR, `mllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `sllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `vsllockerstatus`)))
The 64-bit registers `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, and `vsllockerstatus` track per-locker status, including dirtiness, at all levels below M-mode, S-mode, and VS-mode, respectively.

For each of these three CSRs::
`*llockerstatus`(__i__) = `*llockerstatus`[2{interpunct}__i__+1:2{interpunct}__i__] is a two-bit field that holds the status of ``K(``__i__``)``, encoded as follows.
+
--
[[KLE-llockerstatus-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `*llockerstatus` bits
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<12%,<80%",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Name           | Behavior
|   0   | Off            | The locker is inaccessible.
|   1   | Initial        | The locker is accessible and in an initial state as defined by software.
|   2   | Clean          | The locker is accessible and its state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty          | The locker is accessible and its state may not match the last context swap.
|===
--
+
Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>, item 6, orders the `*llockerstatus` check: after fatal-error and global missing-CSK (`mtval` = 0), before locker-state-dependent checks, including per-level missing-CSK (`mtval` = 8 + L).
The hardware state of a locker with an _Off_ field in effect is never consulted: conditions that depend on its contents are evaluated only after lazy loading or by the M-mode code that emulates the locker.
+
A KLE instruction _accesses_ ``K(``__i__``)`` if it names the locker as an operand, directly or as `KLNL` while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-override>>); if it is a `kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin` or `kl.mvout` issued while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>); or if it is `kl.clearall`.
An access to a locker with an _Off_ field in effect raises a KLE locker-Off exception, except the accesses that item 6 of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>> exempts.
An exempted <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,_destructive configuration instruction_>> on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect sets every field in effect for the locker to _Dirty_, even if the locker was already _Unconfigured_, except a `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` or `kl.swap` with identical source and destination, which performs no operation (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-clone>>).
An exempted instruction that raises an exception before its zeroizing step likewise sets no field: an instruction replaces _Off_ with _Dirty_ only if it has zeroized or overwritten the locker.
+
A mode whose _Off_ mark has been replaced by _Dirty_ treats the hardware locker as authoritative: a mode that held the authoritative image of the locker for lazy loading discards it, and M-mode, which alone emulates a locker, never again uses the emulated copy it held (<<KLE-unsupported-handling>>).
+
`kl.getmd*` on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect traps like any other access.
+
A mode marks a locker _Off_ only while it holds the authoritative image or emulates the locker; the hardware contents of an _Off_ locker are never the sole copy of a CC.
A handler that has not yet saved a locker leaves its field _Clean_ or _Dirty_.

The fields in effect are those owned by modes above the executing mode:

[[KLE-llockerstatus-in-effect]]
[cols="<2,<5",options="header"]
|===
| Executing mode | `*llockerstatus` fields in effect, where implemented
| M | None
| (H)S | `mllockerstatus`
| VS or U | `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`
| VU | `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, `vsllockerstatus`
| Debug | None in authenticated Debug mode (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-architectural-model>>); otherwise those of the debug access privilege of the External Debug Security extension
|===

`vsstatus.KLS` is in effect in Debug mode only when the debug access privilege is VS or VU.

An access to ``K(``__i__``)`` raises KLE locker-Off if any field in effect for it is _Off_, subject to the exemptions above.
An instruction that modifies the locker sets every field in effect for it to _Dirty_; it never writes its own mode's field.
A direct write to a `*llockerstatus` CSR changes no other distinct register in that group (<<KLE-priv-config-requirements>>).
`mstatus.KLS`, and `vsstatus.KLS` when V=1, are also in effect (<<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>>).

`vsllockerstatus` exists only with the H extension.
When V=1, CSR accesses to `sllockerstatus` instead access `vsllockerstatus`, as for `sstatus` and `vsstatus`.

The KLE locker-Off exception is a single exception cause, whichever `*llockerstatus` field is _Off_; `medeleg` and `hedeleg` delegate it like any other cause.
While any of its own fields is _Off_, a mode keeps the cause undelegated, in `medeleg` if it is M-mode or in `hedeleg` if it is HS-mode.
The handler that takes the exception examines the fields in effect, in the order `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, `vsllockerstatus`, starting from the field owned by its own mode.
If its own field (__i__) is _Off_, it resolves the condition itself; otherwise it forwards the exception to the most privileged mode whose field is _Off_, which owns that field and can write it.
If M-mode's own field is _Off_ because it emulates the locker (<<KLE-unsupported-handling>>), and a field in effect of a less privileged mode is also _Off_, M-mode instead forwards the exception to the most privileged of the modes that own such a field: that mode holds the authoritative image of the locker, and M-mode's copy may belong to another context.
A handler never services a KLE locker-Off exception for a field it cannot write.
The instructions that it issues to resolve the condition remain subject to the fields of more privileged modes; a KLE locker-Off exception that one of them raises, as for a locker that M-mode emulates, is taken by the mode that owns that field.

[NOTE]
====
For on-demand loading, a handler may defer a locker restore, mark its field _Off_, and keep the authoritative SCC in memory.
A later access from a lower mode traps to the owner of the _Off_ field, by delegation or forwarding as above.
That handler must restore the authoritative SCC before resuming execution.
There is no separate "`lazy`" state.
====

[[KLE-unsupported-handling]]
==== Emulation of Unsupported Machines

Only M-mode, by emulating the Machine, can resolve a KLE unsupported-Metadata exception (<<KLE-exception-codes>>); a handler below M-mode treats it as unresolvable.
M-mode that emulates Machines keeps the KLE unsupported-Metadata cause undelegated in `medeleg`.
An M-mode handler that emulates the Machine marks the locker _Off_, holds it in its own protected memory, and services every operation on it, including SCC import and export.
For these, an implementation that supports the emulation of Machines must provide M-mode-only, implementation-specific primitives that compute `HW_KDF`, `SCC_KeyDeriv`, `SCC_Encrypt` and `SCC_Decrypt` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>>) under the `eff_CSK` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-eff-CSK,`eff_CSK`>>) of the CSK of the _SCProtection_ level of each emulated locker and the Locality Secrets in effect, with the substitutions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>, exactly as the KLE unit computes them.
The primitives must compute them with the protection of the _SCProtection_ level of each emulated locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>).
The primitives signal when that CSK is not in use (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-selection>>), where the KLE unit would raise a KLE missing-CSK exception, and when a _Locality_ names an unconfigured entry without a configured substitute.
They return neither the CSK, nor the Locality Secrets of the HW Binding Group, nor the keys derived from them.
The primitives refuse a locker at a level whose protection they lack; M-mode then treats the KLE unsupported-Metadata exception for that locker as unresolvable.
They seal or unseal only a whole SCC.
The MDH that they test is the one that enters `AD[0]`, and it must be unsupported Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>).
They compute the `sep` = 1 segment only for an SCC whose `sep` = 0 segment they have sealed, or have unsealed with a successful authentication, in the same invocation.
M-mode therefore cannot seal or unseal through them any part of a CC that the same KLE unit would import before the next full reset of that KLE unit (<<KLE-kleemulation>>).
An implementation without these primitives does not support the emulation of Machines: `kleemulation` is read-only zero, and M-mode treats a KLE unsupported-Metadata exception as unresolvable.

An emulator keeps one copy per locker slot and replaces it:

. at the next KLE unsupported-Metadata exception on that slot; or
. at an emulated `kl.clone` or `kl.rename` whose source is emulated and that names the slot as destination, or an emulated `kl.swap` that brings an emulated locker into the slot; the emulator clears the slot's hardware locker and marks it _Off_.

The emulator discards the copy of a slot that an emulated `kl.rename` or `kl.swap` moves away, and of a destination slot that receives a native locker; for the latter it executes the instruction on the hardware lockers.
In every case it leaves the `*llockerstatus` and `KLS` fields as the instruction would have set them.

A copy orphaned by a <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,destructive configuration instruction>> is never used again but lingers until replaced.
For robustness, as for a cleared locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config>>), an emulator should zeroize a copy whose _Off_ mark is gone before M-mode next returns control to a less privileged mode.
Checking `mllockerstatus` whenever the emulator runs finds such copies.

[[KLE-CSR-mklcsk]]
=== mklcsk CSR Group

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklcsk`)))
The `mklcsk` group, implemented exactly when the CSKs are established under the first way of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>, consists of the four MXLEN-bit M-mode RW CSRs `mklcsk0` to `mklcsk3` and, when MXLEN = 32, of their shadows `mklcsk0h` to `mklcsk3h`.

A write to `mklcsk`__i__ supplies bits [64·_i_+MXLEN−1 : 64·_i_], and a write to `mklcsk`__i__``h`` bits [64·_i_+63 : 64·_i_+32], of the value that becomes CSK~L~, for the level L that `mklcfg` selects (<<KLE-CSR-mklcfg>>), once the group has been written in full.

This CSR group is accessible in M-mode whenever `KLS` is not Off and `misa.L` is set; any other access raises an illegal-instruction exception.
An access in Debug mode that the External Debug Security extension does not authorize for M-mode raises an illegal-instruction exception.
Out of reset, `KLS` is Off (<<KLE-out-of-reset-priv>>).
M-mode firmware enables `KLS` before it establishes a CSK.

The _current write_ of the group begins at the latest of: a KLE unit reset; the completion of the previous full write of the group, whether it configured CSK~L~ or, with the value `zeros(256)`, unconfigured it; a write to `mklcfg`, or a KLE missing-CSK exception with the same effect, that abandoned a partial write (<<KLE-CSR-mklcfg>>); and a write to `mklcsk0`, which discards the parts written before it and is itself part of the write that it begins.
A new CSK~L~ becomes configured when, and only when, every CSR of the group has been written in the current write.
From the first write to the group in the current write until then, CSK~L~ is _partially written_ and not configured, so with trapped selection the KLE unit is unavailable (KLE missing-CSK exception, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).
The first write of a current write unconfigures CSK~L~, and abandoning the partial write does not restore it: CSK~L~ stays unconfigured until a full write completes.

A read of a register of the group returns the corresponding part of CSK~L~.
While CSK~L~ is not configured (none established, or partially written), the read returns zero, the value of an unconfigured CSK (below).

A write to the group or to `mklcfg` is not checked against the state of any locker, including a configured locker or one under management: M-mode must write it only after saving all KLE state that it must preserve.

A CSK whose value is `zeros(256)` is unconfigured in each of the four ways of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>.
Writing that value in full through the `mklcsk` group unconfigures CSK~L~, as erasing it through `mklcfg` does, but more slowly.
Whenever a CSK is randomly generated, as under the fourth way, an all-zero output of the RBG is discarded and regenerated.

The KLE unit is unavailable until a CSK is established (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>); a hardwired CSK is established at once, and a boot-generated one as soon as it has been generated.

[[KLE-CSR-mklcfg]]
=== mklcfg CSR

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklcfg`)))
`mklcfg` is an MXLEN-bit M-mode RW CSR that selects and erases the CSKs of the hart (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).
It is implemented when the CSKs are established under the first or second way, and not under the third or fourth.
It has the accessibility of the `mklcsk` group (<<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>>), and is accessible while no CSK is configured.

[[KLE-mklcfg-fields]]
.Fields of `mklcfg`
[float="center",align="center",cols="^12%,^14%,<74%",options="header"]
|===
| Bits       | Field       | Description
| [2:0]      | _CSKSel_    | WARL. The side-channel protection level L of the selected CSK, CSK~L~. A value is legal only if the implementation defines that level (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCProtection-field>>).
| 3          | _CSKErase_  | Writing 1 erases a CSK, as described below. Reads as 0.
| [MXLEN−1:4] | —          | WPRI; read-only zero in this version.
|===

A write of a value `v` to bits [3:0] of `mklcfg` leaves `mklcfg` and the CSKs unchanged if `v`[2:0] is not a level that the hart implements, except that under trapped selection a write with `v`[3] = 1 still unconfigures the CSK held, abandons a partially written `mklcsk` group, and leaves _CSKSel_ unchanged.
Any other write sets _CSKSel_ to `v`[2:0], abandons a partially written `mklcsk` group, and has the effect of <<KLE-mklcfg-writes>>.

[[KLE-mklcfg-writes]]
.Effect of a write to `mklcfg`
[float="center",align="center",cols="^14%,<43%,<43%",options="header"]
|===
| _CSKErase_ (`v`[3]) | Trapped selection (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-selection>>) | `Smklmultcsk`
| 0 | Selects level `v`[2:0] for the next full write of the `mklcsk` group; unconfigures the CSK held if its level differs, since a CSK never changes level. | Selects the CSK of level `v`[2:0], which the `mklcsk` group then writes and reads.
| 1 | Unconfigures the CSK held, whatever its level. | Unconfigures the CSK of level `v`[2:0].
|===

Under the second way, M-mode writes _CSKSel_ before it asks the secure hardware block to establish a CSK, which the block establishes for that level.

A KLE missing-CSK exception writes `mtval`:

* 0, if no CSK is configured;
* 8 + L, if an export or import at level L finds CSK~L~ not in use (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).
  With `Smklmultcsk`, the exception also sets _CSKSel_ to L, as a write of L to `mklcfg` does, so that M-mode establishes CSK~L~ without writing `mklcfg`.
  With trapped selection, _CSKSel_ is unchanged, since setting it would unconfigure the CSK held: M-mode reads L from `mtval` and writes it to `mklcfg` before it establishes the CSK.

M-mode keeps the KLE missing-CSK cause undelegated in `medeleg`, since only M-mode can establish a CSK.

NOTE: After `mtval` = 0, the re-executed instruction may trap again with 8 + L.
The second trap is harmless, and M-mode makes it rare by first establishing the CSK of the level that its platform uses most.

[[KLE-CSR-mklmaxiobuflen]]
=== mklmaxiobuflen

(((CSR, `mklmaxiobuflen`)))`mklmaxiobuflen` is an MXLEN-bit M-mode CSR, present with `Zklio` only, that holds the maximum configurable length of the KLIOBUF, in bytes.
Its hardwired value out of KLE unit reset is its maximum.
Software in the other modes has no access to it: as with `vsetvl`, it learns the maximum by writing a larger value to `kliobuflen` and reading it back.
M-mode should write it only while the KLE unit is in its state out of reset (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>), before any write to `kliobuflen`, and should never reduce it below the current `kliobuflen`.
After a write that reduces it below `kliobuflen`, `kliobuflen` and `kliobuftop` become 0 and the whole KLIOBUF is zeroized; no other state is affected.

A write to `mklmaxiobuflen` is WARL: a value above its value out of KLE unit reset sets that value, and a value below the lower bound sets that lower bound.
The lower bound is the larger of 16 bytes and the largest basic processing unit (granularity) of any implemented Machine.

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklphysbootscrt`)))

Each group listed in <<KLE-locality-CSR-groups>> holds one 128-bit Locality Secret (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-locality-indexes>>) in two _W_-bit RW CSRs, numbered “0” and “1”, and, when _W_ = 32, in their shadows, numbered “0h” and “1h”.

`hkllocality` is the hypervisor's tag on its virtual machines: it enters the bindings of every export and import a guest performs, and the hypervisor carries it with the virtual machine.

[[KLE-out-of-reset-priv]]
== KLE Privileged State out of Reset

This section and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> are the authoritative statement of the KLE state out of reset:

. bit `L` of `misa` is 1 if the KLE unit and the KLE ISA are at least partially implemented in hardware, and 0 for a purely emulated KLE unit; if this bit is writable, M-mode firmware may set it to 1;
. the `KLS` field of `mstatus` (visible in `sstatus`) and, where implemented, of `vsstatus` is Off;
. every CSK is unconfigured under the first and second ways of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>, and `mklcfg` holds the least legal value of _CSKSel_ in bits [2:0] and 0 in its other bits; hardwired CSKs are kept, and so are boot-generated ones, which the reset of one hart neither clears nor regenerates;
. the entire KLF memory is zeroed;
. `mklphysbootscrt` and `hklvirtbootscrt` are zero;
. `mkllocality`, `hkllocality` and `skllocality` are zero;
. all fields of `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` are _Initial_;
. `kleemulation` in `mstatus` is 0;
. with `Zklio`, `mklmaxiobuflen` holds its hardwired maximum (<<KLE-CSR-mklmaxiobuflen>>); and
. the means of <<KLE-kleemulation>> that disable native Machines are unlocked, in their implementation-defined reset state, at a full reset of the KLE unit (<<KLE-kleemulation>>); at any other reset they are unchanged.

A KLE unit reset caused by a fatal condition (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>) differs in items 1, 2, 7 and 10: `misa.L` is unchanged; a `KLS` field that is Off stays Off, and every other `KLS` field becomes Dirty; every `*llockerstatus` field becomes _Dirty_; and the means that disable native Machines keep their setting and stay locked.
After such a reset, `klstart` is 0 and `klnamedlocker` is 32, as out of reset (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>).
Until the KLE fatal-error exception is raised, any KLE instruction or KLE CSR access raises it instead, unless an exception that precedes it is raised (<<KLE-exceptions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>).
Under a persisting fatal condition (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>), every later one raises it as well.
