The KLE Privileged Architecture is _implemented_ on a hart that implements the `KLS` field. It is mandatory on every hart that is not M-mode-only, and optional on an M-mode-only hart.
The KLE exceptions of <<KLE-exceptions>> exist on every hart, whether or not it implements the KLE Privileged Architecture.
Where the KLE Privileged Architecture is not implemented:

* `KLS` is not present, and is treated as Initial;
* `Smklstatus` and `Ssklvbootscrt` are not implemented; and
* the `mklcsk`, `mklphysbootscrt` and `mkllocality` groups can be implemented or not, each where its extension is implemented, with the accessibility that <<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>> and <<KLE-CSR-locality-groups>> prescribe.

On an M-mode-only hart, none of the extensions of the table above is mandatory.
`Smklcsk` and `Smklpbootscrt` are allowed in every configuration; with `Smklpbootscrt`, the `mklphysbootscrt`
CSR group sets the Physical Boot Secret Locality.
The requirements for the other extensions are:

|===

| M-mode only
| Optional: If implemented, `mllockerstatus` is implemented and shadowed in `sllockerstatus`.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality` configures MLocality
| {empty}

| M+U
| Mandatory: `mllockerstatus` is implemented and shadowed in `sllockerstatus`.

| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality` and `skllocality` (`Sskllclt`) configure MLocality and SLocality
| {empty}

| M+S+U with H
| Mandatory: `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` (the latter two `Ssklstatus`) are implemented and are distinct.
| Optional: If implemented, `mkllocality`, `hkllocality` and `skllocality` (the latter two `Sskllclt`) configure MLocality, HLocality and SLocality

The last two columns give the CSR groups implemented with the extension,
and the Localities they set (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>); an empty cell means that the extension has no effect in

[[KLE-exceptions]]
== Exceptions Raised by KLE

This section describes the error conditions that raise exceptions, as opposed to those that set an Error State (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-State-field>>).

When an exception is raised, `*cause` holds the cause, as <<KLE-exception-codes>> describes; the table lists the causes in decreasing priority order.

The KLE fatal-error exception precedes every exception that depends on the state of the KLE unit, including the address-misaligned, access-fault and page-fault exceptions of KLE memory instructions, whose transfer bounds are computed from that state (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-mv>>); it is preceded only by the illegal-instruction or virtual-instruction exception of a `KLS` field in effect that is Off or of a `*stateen0` denial, and precedes the other illegal-instruction grounds.

[[KLE-exception-codes]]
.Exception causes in decreasing priority order
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^10%,<24%,<66%",options="header"]
|===
|Exception Code(s) | Mnemonic/RISC-V type           | Examples in KLE (((KLE, error codes)))
|  3   | Instruction address breakpoint | As per the Privileged Specification.
| 12, 20, 1 | Instruction page fault, instruction guest-page fault, instruction access fault | As per the Privileged Specification.
|  2   | Illegal-instruction            | A `KLS` field in effect that is Off, or a `*stateen0` denial (part of item 6 of the first group of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>); at the same point, an `hstateen0` denial while V=1 raises a virtual-instruction exception (code 22) instead (<<KLE-privileged-CSRs>>).
| TBD  | *KLE fatal-error*             | Fatal system failure in the KLE unit.
|  2   | Illegal-instruction            | The other KLE grounds decided without accessing the state of the KLE unit, such as a reserved encoding, listed first in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>.
| TBD  | *KLE missing-CSK*             | Use of the KLE unit while no CSK is active: either none has been established, or the `mklcsk` group is only partially written (see <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>, <<KLE-CSR-mklcsk>>). Only M-mode can establish a CSK.
| TBD  | *KLE locker-Off*              | Any access, as defined in <<KLE-CSR-llockerstatus>>, to a locker whose `*llockerstatus` field is _Off_; it reaches the mode that owns the _Off_ field by delegation or forwarding.  This exception enables on-demand (lazy) locker loading and trap-and-emulate.
|  2   | Illegal-instruction            | Raised if the KLE grounds for an illegal-instruction exception that require the state of the KLE unit are fulfilled, listed second in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>.
For a usage instruction, an unconfigured KLIOBUF is evaluated in the order of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-valid-state>>, after the KLE privilege-violation exception for a locker in a Configuration State.
| TBD  | *KLE unsupported-Metadata*    | Unsupported Metadata (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-validity>>) when opening a provisioning or import.
| TBD  | *KLE privilege-violation*     | A usage instruction that the _UsagePolicy_ denies (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata>>), or any use, clone or restriction of a locker in a Configuration State, which is forbidden at all privilege levels (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-config-state>>).
| TBD  | *KLE clock-unavailable*         | With `Zklexpire`, a _usage_ instruction on a locker with a non-zero _ExpirationDate_ while the secure clock cannot be read (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-expiration-date>>).
| TBD  | *KLE out-of-memory*           | Insufficient KLF capacity when opening a provisioning or import, or cloning.
| TBD  | *KLE RBG-unavailable*         | Random bits that an operation needs remain unavailable past the implementation-defined bound of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>>.
|  3   | Load/store address breakpoint  | On the address of a component access of `kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.input` or `kl.output`, when that access is about to be performed; for the first component access, before the address-misaligned check.
| 4, 6 | Load address misaligned, Store/AMO address misaligned | On the effective address of `kl.load`, resp. `kl.store`, before any component access (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instructions-memory>>); for `kl.input` and `kl.output`, as for an ordinary access on the implementation.
| 13, 15, 21, 23, 5, 7 | Load/store page fault, load/store guest-page fault, load/store access fault | Per component access, in offset order, as ordered by the Privileged Specification.
|===

`kl.load` and `kl.input` raise the load causes (4, 13, 21, 5), and `kl.store` and `kl.output` the store/AMO causes (6, 15, 23, 7).
The relative order of the standard exceptions is that of the Privileged Specification, reproduced for reference only; the load/store address breakpoint and the load/store faults are evaluated per component access, because the component addresses of a KLE memory instruction are determined from the state of the KLE unit.

The `medeleg` bits of all KLE exception causes are read-only zero: KLE exceptions are never delegated below M-mode.

[[KLE-privileged-CSRs]]
== CSRs

KLE extends `*status` and `misa`, and provides up to 17 direct CSRs (34 on RV32, counting the
high shadows).

[[KLE-CSRs-priv]]
.KLE Privileged CSRs.
[cols="^12%,^12%,<26%,<50%"]
[float="center",align="center",options="header"]
|===
| Address | Privilege | Field                    | Description
|   TBD   |    MRW    | `mllockerstatus`         | Per-locker dirtiness and accessibility tracking below M-mode; supports on-demand locker loading
|   TBD   |    MRW    | `mllockerstatush`        | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `mllockerstatus`
|   TBD   |    SRW    | `sllockerstatus`         | Per-locker dirtiness and accessibility tracking below S-mode; supports on-demand locker loading
|   TBD   |    SRW    | `sllockerstatush`        | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `sllockerstatus`
|   TBD   |    HRW    | `vsllockerstatus`        | Per-locker dirtiness and accessibility tracking below VS-mode; supports on-demand locker loading
|   TBD   |    HRW    | `vsllockerstatush`       | RV32 only: Shadow of the top 32 bits of `vsllockerstatus`
|   TBD   |    MRW    | `mklcsk{0..7}`           | KLE Context Sealing Key.  CSRs “4” to “7” are RV32 only.
|   TBD   |    MRW    | `mklphysbootscrt{0..3}`  | Locality: Physical (host) Boot Session Secret. CSRs “2” and “3” are RV32 only.
|   TBD   |    HRW    | `hklvirtbootscrt{0..3}`  | Locality: Virtual (guest) Boot Session Secret. CSRs “2” and “3” are RV32 only.
|   TBD   |    MRW    | `mkllocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate processes below M-mode. CSRs “2” and “3” are RV32 only.
|   TBD   |    HRW    | `hkllocality{0..3}`      | Locality: the hypervisor's tag on its virtual machines, separating them from each other. CSRs “2” and “3” are RV32 only.
|   TBD   |    SRW    | `skllocality{0..3}`      | Locality: Mutually isolate the OS's processes. CSRs “2” and “3” are RV32 only.
|===

On RV32, each of the 64-bit CSRs `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` that is implemented
is accompanied by a 32-bit CSR, `mllockerstatush`, resp. `sllockerstatush`, `vsllockerstatush`,
which accesses bits [63:32]; the CSR itself then accesses bits [31:0].
In this Book, RV32 and RV64 denote an XLEN of 32, resp. 64, in the privilege mode that accesses the CSR; a CSR stated to exist on RV32 only exists for accesses made with an XLEN of 32.

None of these CSRs, the `mklcsk` group included, is available without the KLE extension, or while a `KLS` field in effect is Off
(<<KLE-KL-field-in-misa>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>>, <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>>):
an access then raises an illegal-instruction exception, a condition evaluated after the `Smstateen` check below, where implemented, and before the privilege check.
An access from a mode below the privilege of the CSR also raises an illegal-instruction exception, or, while V=1, the virtual-instruction exception that the H extension prescribes where HS-mode would allow the access.
Where `Smstateen` is implemented, the KLE bit (to be allocated) of `mstateen0`, `hstateen0` and `sstateen0` gates every KLE instruction and every KLE CSR, including the identification CSRs, in the modes below the register's own: a clear bit makes the instruction or access raise the exception that extension prescribes: an illegal-instruction exception, or a virtual-instruction exception for an `hstateen0` denial while V=1.
These exceptions are decided without accessing the KLE unit, are evaluated with the first group of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>, and precede every other KLE condition.

[[KLE-KL-field-in-misa]]
=== KLE field in misa

{empty}
(((CSR, KLE field in `misa`)))
Bit `L` (11) of `misa` indicates that the KLE extension is present; if KLE is implemented, it must not be read-only zero.

M-mode firmware that provides an emulated KLE unit can set this bit to 1.
It must not clear `misa.L` without first zeroizing all KLE state, emulated or in hardware (`kl.clearall` and the CSRs of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>>); while `misa.L` is clear, the KLE state is inaccessible, and need not be retained.

[[KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus]]
=== KLE Context Status in mstatus and sstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,KLS field in mstatus)))
(((CSR,KLS field in sstatus)))
`mstatus`[26:25] holds a KLE context status field, `KLS`, shadowed in `sstatus`[26:25].
Defined like the floating-point and vector context status fields `FS` and `VS`,
it tracks the dirtiness of the lockers, of the KLIOBUF and of every writable KLE-specific CSR other than the `mklcsk` group.
`mstatus.KLS` is always in effect; when V=1, the `vsstatus.KLS` field of <<KLE-KLS-field-VS>> is
in effect as well.

[[KLE-aces-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `KLS` field
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<8%,<84%",options="header"]
|===
|Value |Name |Meaning
|   0   | Off     | KLE disabled. The KLE state is inaccessible: KLE instructions, and KLE CSR accesses other than reads of hardwired identification CSRs (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSRs-unpriv>>), raise an illegal-instruction exception; a read of an emulated identification CSR also raises it, so that M-mode can supply the authoritative value.
|   1   | Initial | KLE enabled. Every locker is _Unconfigured_, and every other state tracked by the field holds its reset value. The hart may issue any KLE instruction.
|   2   | Clean   | KLE enabled. The state tracked by the field is potentially different from its reset value, but matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty   | KLE enabled. At least one locker, KLIOBUF, or KLE-specific CSR may have changed since the last context swap.
A locker that was configured and is now _Unconfigured_ also makes `KLS` Dirty, since context-switch code must erase its stale saved state.
|===


The following holds for every `KLS` field, in the privilege modes in which it is in effect:

* If a field in effect is Off, every KLE instruction, and every access to a KLE CSR other than a read of a hardwired identification CSR (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSRs-unpriv>>), raises an illegal-instruction exception.
* Where none is Off, an instruction that modifies KLE state sets every field in effect to Dirty.
  So does a software write to a `*llockerstatus` CSR or to a Locality CSR; context-restoring software therefore writes `KLS` after those CSRs.
* An implementation may set a `KLS` field, or a `*llockerstatus` field, to Dirty from Initial or Clean at any time, even without a state change.
  Setting a field to Dirty is not a change of KLE state for Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>.
* The `*llockerstatus` and `KLS` fields that an instruction sets to Dirty are set before any trap is taken on or after that instruction, and before any later instruction executes, including when the instruction continues in the background (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-preemption>>) or is discarded and restarted (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>).
* While a field is Dirty, the `SD` bit of the register holding it is 1; otherwise that `SD` is set in accordance with the existing specifications.

A hart that implements the KLE Privileged Architecture, and whose `misa.L` is writable, implements the `KLS` field in every `*status` register it implements.
The field is present and writable while `misa.L` is clear, like `FS` and `VS`; an emulated KLE unit relies on this.

NOTE: Precise management of `mstatus.KLS` is an optimization that software may use to reduce context-switch overhead.

A write to a `KLS` field modifies no KLE state:
setting the field to Off, Initial or Clean does not clear the lockers, the KLIOBUF or the KLE CSRs,
so any such state change must be explicit, and must precede the write.

[[KLE-kleemulation]]
=== KLE Emulation Control in mstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,kleemulation field in mstatus)))
`mstatus`[27] holds `kleemulation`, a bit that M-mode sets when it emulates Machines that the KLE unit does not implement natively.
It is not visible in `sstatus` or `vsstatus`, and it resets to 0.
It controls the Forms B and C of `kl.avail` and `kl.size`, which take an MDH supplied in registers rather than a locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-size>>):

* while `kleemulation` is 1, such an instruction whose MDH names a Machine, or a variant of it, that the KLE unit does not implement natively raises an illegal-instruction exception, so that M-mode can supply the value for an emulated Machine;
* while `kleemulation` is 0, it returns 0 for such an MDH, as for any Machine that is not implemented.

NOTE: The position of `kleemulation` is provisional and is to be assigned by the ARC (<<Zkl-introduction.adoc#KLE-open-points>>).

[[KLE-KLS-field-VS]]
=== KLE Context Status in vsstatus

{empty}
(((CSR,KLS field in vsstatus)))
With the H extension, `vsstatus`[26:25] also holds a `KLS` field. When V=1, it is in
effect together with `mstatus.KLS`, and <<KLE-KLS-field-in-mstatus>> governs both.

NOTE: `mstatus.KLS` and `vsstatus.KLS` are not KLE context state

[[KLE-CSR-llockerstatus]]
=== mllockerstatus, sllockerstatus, and vsllockerstatus for Dirtiness Tracking

{empty}
(((CSR, `mllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `sllockerstatus`)))
(((CSR, `vsllockerstatus`)))
The 64-bit registers `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, and `vsllockerstatus` track per-locker status, including dirtiness, at all levels below M-mode, S-mode, and VS-mode, respectively.

For each of these three CSRs::
`*llockerstatus`(__i__) = `*llockerstatus`[2{interpunct}__i__+1:2{interpunct}__i__] is a two-bit field that holds the status of ``K(``__i__``)``, encoded as follows.
+
--
[[KLE-llockerstatus-encoding]]
.Encoding of the `*llockerstatus` bits
[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="^8%,<12%,<80%",options="header"]
|===
| Value | Name           | Behavior
|   0   | Off            | The locker is inaccessible.
|   1   | Initial        | The locker is accessible and in an initial state as defined by software.
|   2   | Clean          | The locker is accessible and its state matches the last context swap.
|   3   | Dirty          | The locker is accessible and its state may not match the last context swap.
|===
--
+
`*llockerstatus` is evaluated after the illegal-instruction grounds that do not require KLE unit state (the first group of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>), and the KLE fatal-error and missing-CSK exceptions, but before all other conditions, including the second group of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>> and the conditions of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-valid-state>>. The hardware state of a locker with an _Off_ field in effect is never consulted: conditions that depend on its contents are evaluated only after lazy loading or by the M-mode code that emulates the locker.
+
A KLE instruction _accesses_ ``K(``__i__``)`` if it names the locker as an operand, directly or as `KLNL` while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-override>>); if it is a `kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin` or `kl.mvout` issued while `klnamedlocker` = _i_ (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>); or if it is `kl.clearall`. An access to a locker with an _Off_ field in effect raises a KLE locker-Off exception, except by a <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,_destructive configuration instruction_>> other than `kl.swap`, which reads the locker it overwrites: a provisioning- or import-opening `kl.mgmt`, `kl.clear`, `kl.clearall`, and `kl.clone` or `kl.rename` into it. Such an instruction, on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect, sets every field in effect for the locker to _Dirty_, even if the locker was already _Unconfigured_. However, a `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` or `kl.swap` with identical source and destination performs no operation, raises no KLE locker-Off exception, and sets no field to _Dirty_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-clone>>). An exempted instruction that raises an exception before its zeroizing step likewise sets no field: an instruction replaces _Off_ with _Dirty_ only if it has zeroized or overwritten the locker.
+
A mode whose _Off_ mark has been replaced by _Dirty_ treats the hardware locker as authoritative: a mode that held the authoritative image of the locker for lazy loading discards it, and M-mode, which alone emulates a locker, never again uses the emulated copy it held for it (<<KLE-unsupported-handling>>).
+
`kl.getmd*` on a locker with an _Off_ field in effect traps like any other access. An implementation may return the MDH without trapping only if the result matches what the trap and subsequent restore would have produced.
+
A mode marks a locker _Off_ only while it holds the authoritative image or emulates the locker; the hardware contents of an _Off_ locker are never the sole copy of a CC. A handler that has not yet saved a locker leaves its field _Clean_ or _Dirty_.

For `mllockerstatus`::
+
--
When the privilege mode is (H)S, VS or U/VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while `mllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `mllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `sllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `vsllockerstatus`(__i__), where these are distinct registers (<<KLE-priv-config-requirements>>).
`mstatus.KLS` is also in effect.
--


For `sllockerstatus`::
+
--
When the privilege mode is VS, U or VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while `mllockerstatus`(__i__) or `sllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `sllockerstatus`(__i__) and `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `sllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `mllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) (if defined).
`mstatus.KLS`, `vsstatus.KLS` when V=1, and `mllockerstatus` are also in effect.
--


For `vsllockerstatus`::
+
--
`vsllockerstatus` exists only with the H extension.
When V=1, it substitutes for `sllockerstatus`: CSR accesses that would target `sllockerstatus` instead access `vsllockerstatus`, as for `vsstatus` and `sstatus`.

When the privilege mode is VU:

* an access to ``K(``__i__``)`` while any of `mllockerstatus`(__i__), `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) or `sllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_ raises a KLE locker-Off exception; and
* an instruction that modifies ``K(``__i__``)`` sets `vsllockerstatus`(__i__), `sllockerstatus`(__i__) and `mllockerstatus`(__i__) to _Dirty_.

A direct write to `vsllockerstatus`(__i__) changes neither `mllockerstatus`(__i__) nor `sllockerstatus`(__i__) (if defined).
`mstatus.KLS`, `vsstatus.KLS`, `sllockerstatus` and `mllockerstatus` are also in effect.
--

The `*llockerstatus` fields that an instruction sets to _Dirty_ are those in effect, that is those owned by the privilege modes above the one in which it executes; an instruction never writes the field owned by its own mode.

The KLE locker-Off exception is a single exception cause and is not delegable below M-mode, whichever `*llockerstatus` field is _Off_: when raising it, the hardware does not know which mode holds the authoritative copy of the locker. The M-mode handler examines the fields in effect in the order `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus`, `vsllockerstatus`. If `mllockerstatus`(__i__) is _Off_, it resolves the condition itself, and otherwise forwards the exception to the most privileged mode whose field is _Off_, which owns that field and can write it.
A handler therefore never services a KLE locker-Off exception for a field it cannot write, and an instruction it issues to resolve the condition is not gated by a higher level's field.

[NOTE]
====
The _Off_ value and the KLE locker-Off exception provide an architectural hook for on-demand (lazy) locker loading. At context restore, a handler may leave a locker unrestored, set its `*llockerstatus` field to _Off_, and retain the authoritative SCC in memory. A subsequent access from a lower-privileged mode traps with a KLE locker-Off exception to M-mode, which forwards it to the mode that owns the _Off_ field; that handler then imports the SCC before resuming execution. The architecture defines no separate "`lazy`" state: software that marks a locker _Off_ is responsible for ensuring that the authoritative SCC is restored.
====

[[KLE-unsupported-handling]]
==== Emulation of Unsupported Machines

A KLE unsupported-Metadata exception (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exceptions-list>>) can be resolved only by M-mode, by emulating the Machine; a handler below M-mode treats it as unresolvable.
An M-mode handler that emulates the Machine instead marks the locker _Off_, holds it in its own protected memory, and services every operation on it, including SCC import and export.
For these, it needs either (a) the CSK and the Locality Secrets of the HW Binding Group, inputs of `SCC_Encrypt` and `SCC_Decrypt` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>>) that it cannot otherwise obtain, or (b) implementation-specific primitives that compute these functions for it.

An emulator keeps one copy per locker slot and replaces it:

. at the next KLE unsupported-Metadata exception on that slot;
. at an emulated `kl.clone` naming the slot as destination, which also clears the slot's hardware locker and marks it _Off_, since the clone of an emulated locker is emulated too; or
. at an emulated `kl.rename` naming the slot as destination, or an emulated `kl.swap` naming it, either of which moves a copy into the slot.

A copy orphaned by a <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config,destructive configuration instruction>> is never used again but lingers until replaced.
As a robustness recommendation, as for a cleared locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config>>), an emulator should zeroize a copy whose _Off_ mark is gone before M-mode next returns control to a less privileged mode; checking `mllockerstatus` whenever it runs finds such copies.

[[KLE-CSR-mklcsk]]
=== mklcsk CSR Group

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklcsk`)))
The optional `mklcsk` group consists of XLEN-bit M-mode RW CSRs: eight on RV32, four on RV64.

A write to the __i__-th CSR supplies bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of the value that becomes the CSK once the group has been written in full.

This CSR group is accessible in M-mode whenever `KLS` is not Off and `misa.L` is set; any other access raises an illegal-instruction exception.
Out of reset `KLS` is Off (<<KLE-out-of-reset-priv>>), so M-mode firmware enables `KLS` before it establishes the CSK, and until then the KLE unit is not usable.

A new CSK becomes active only once every CSR of the group has been written since the previous activation; from the first such write until then, the CSK is _partially written_, and the KLE unit is unavailable (KLE missing-CSK exception, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>).

Reading a register of the group returns the corresponding part of the active CSK.

A CSK whose value is `zeros(256)` is unconfigured under every model of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>: writing that value in full through the `mklcsk` group discards any established CSK and leaves the unit without a CSK; and whenever a CSK is randomly generated, as under the fourth model, an all-zero output of the RBG is discarded and regenerated.

The KLE unit is unavailable until a CSK is established (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>): at once for a hardwired CSK, and as soon as it has been generated for a boot-generated one.

[[KLE-CSR-locality-groups]]
=== Locality Secret CSR Groups

{empty}
(((CSR, `mklphysbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `hklvirtbootscrt`)))
(((CSR, `mkllocality`)))
(((CSR, `hkllocality`)))(((CSR, `skllocality`)))
Each group listed in <<KLE-locality-CSR-groups>> holds one 128-bit Locality Secret
(<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-locality-indexes>>) in XLEN-bit RW CSRs: four on RV32 and
two on RV64, the CSRs numbered “2” and “3” existing only on RV32.
A write to the __i__-th CSR of a group sets bits [(_i_+1)·XLEN−1 : _i_·XLEN] of its secret.
An access below the privilege of the group raises the exception that <<KLE-privileged-CSRs>> prescribes, and one in
unauthenticated Debug mode an illegal-instruction exception.
The groups are accessible whether or not a CSK has been established (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>): no ordering between CSK and Locality configuration is enforced.

[[KLE-locality-CSR-groups]]
.Locality Secret CSR groups
[width="100%",cols="<25%,<18%,^11%,<46%",options="header"]
|===
| Group | Locality Secret | Privilege | Present when

| [[KLE-CSR-mklphysbootscrt,mklphysbootscrt]]`mklphysbootscrt{0..3}` | _PhysBootScrt_ |  M   | `Smklpbootscrt` is implemented.
| [[KLE-CSR-hklvirtbootscrt,hklvirtbootscrt]]`hklvirtbootscrt{0..3}` | _VirtBootScrt_ |  HS  | `Ssklvbootscrt` and the H extension are both implemented.
| [[KLE-CSR-mkllocality,mkllocality]]`mkllocality{0..3}`             | _MLocality_    |  M   | `Smkllclt` is implemented.

|===

Software that exports lockers on behalf of a lower privilege level configures every SW Filter Group register it owns before doing so: an export whose CC names an unconfigured entry yields an SCC that no import accepts (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>). Before running software not entrusted with the CCs of the virtual machine loaded, a hypervisor writes `zeros(128)`, or a value that no virtual machine uses, to `hkllocality` and `hklvirtbootscrt`.

The Locality CSR groups are readable at their respective privilege levels, and
context-switch software saves and restores them like any other CSR.

[NOTE]
====
`skllocality` is the OS's Locality, separating its processes whether the OS runs at S-mode or at VS-mode; a hypervisor uses its own `skllocality` value for its host processes, swaps it with the guest's value when it enters and leaves the guest, and configures its own value before it saves guest KLE state (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>).
`hkllocality` is the hypervisor's tag on its virtual machines: it enters the bindings of every export and import a guest performs, and the hypervisor carries it with the virtual machine.
====

[[KLE-out-of-reset-priv]]
== KLE Privileged State out of Reset

This section and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-out-of-reset-unpriv>> are the authoritative statement of the KLE state out of
reset:

. bit `L` of `misa` is 1 if the KLE unit and the KLE ISA are at least partially implemented in hardware, and 0 for a purely emulated KLE unit;
 if this bit is writable, M-mode firmware can set it to 1;
. the `KLS` field of `mstatus` (visible in `sstatus`) and, where implemented, of `vsstatus` is Off;
. the CSK is unconfigured under the first and second ways of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSK-requirements>>; a hardwired CSK is kept, and so is a boot-generated one, which the reset of one hart neither clears nor regenerates;
. the entire KLF memory is zeroed;
. `mklphysbootscrt` and `hklvirtbootscrt` are zero;
. `mkllocality`, `hkllocality` and `skllocality` are zero; and
. all fields of `mllockerstatus`, `sllockerstatus` and `vsllockerstatus` are _Initial_.

A KLE unit reset caused by a fatal condition (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>) differs in items 1, 2 and 7: `misa.L` is unchanged, a `KLS` field that is Off stays Off and every other `KLS` field becomes Dirty, and every `*llockerstatus` field is set to _Dirty_.
