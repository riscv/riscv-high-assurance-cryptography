_This section is normative.
It fixes the notation, the representation of values, the concatenation operator and the finite-field operations used throughout this specification. Every formula is read under these conventions, and no Machine description may introduce a different one._

// ////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

== Values, Bits, and Byte Strings

A value is a bit string whose length is fixed by its context.
For a value `V` of `n` bits, `V[i]` denotes its ``i``-th bit and `V[j:i]`, with
`0` {le} `i` {le} `j` < `n`, the bit slice from bit `i` to bit `j`, both inclusive.
Bit `0` is the least significant bit.

A referenced standard may specify an input, output, key, state field or constant as a *byte string* `M` = `M~0~`, `M~1~`, {ellipsis}, `M~n-1~`, `M~0~` being the first (leftmost, lowest-addressed) byte.
This specification represents such a string by the `8n`-bit value `V` with `V[8i+7 : 8i]` = `M~i~` for `0` {le} `i` < `n`, the only string-to-value mapping it uses.
The mapping matches the little-endian ordering of RISC-V and its vector interfaces: for a KLE memory operation with effective address `A`, the byte at `A + i` is `V[8i+7 : 8i]`; a vector register (group) that supplies `INPUT` to a KLE operation, or receives its `OUTPUT`, holds the value that a unit-stride byte load (`vle8.v`) of that memory would produce, and a unit-stride byte store (`vse8.v`) of it reproduces the memory content.
The mapping is independent of `SEW` and `VL`, of which KLE uses only the product (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-algo-notation>>).

[[KLE-Notation-concatenation]]
== Concatenation

For values `A` of `p` bits and `B` of `q` bits, `A @ B` is the `(p+q)`-bit value `C` with `C[q-1:0]` = `B` and `C[p+q-1:q]` = `A`: *the left operand of `@` occupies the more significant bits*.

[[KLE-Notation-operators]]
== Operators and Control Constructs

* `x {leftarrow} y`, also in listings (`x <- y` in inline code): assignment of `y` to `x`.
* `x = y`, `x {ne} y` (`x != y` in listings), `<`, `{le}`, `>`, `{ge}`: equality, inequality and the usual integer comparisons.
* `+`, `-`, `{times}`: integer addition, subtraction and multiplication; `a mod b`, for `b` > 0, the remainder of `a` divided by `b`, in the range `0` to `b-1`.
* `V xor W`, `V and W`, `V or W`: bitwise operations on values of equal length.
* `V << k`, `V >> k`: logical shifts of `V` by `k` bits within its declared width, bits shifted out being discarded and zeros shifted in; a left shift moves data towards the *later* bytes of the string.
* `A @ B`: concatenation (<<KLE-Notation-concatenation>>); `A[] || B[]`: concatenation of two arrays of blocks, the elements of `A[]` first, in index order.
* `X {vvert} Y`, for byte strings or arrays of blocks: `X` followed by `Y`, `X` at the lower offsets or indices; this specification uses `{vvert}` only in this little-endian sense (<<KLE-Notation-transcription>>).
* `foreach(i from a to b) { {ellipsis} }`: executes its body once for each integer `i` from `a` to `b`, both included, in increasing order, and not at all if `a` > `b`; `foreach(i from a to b by s)` advances `i` in steps of `s`, with `s` > 0.
* `x^k^`: `x` raised to the power `k`; `x**k` in listing blocks.
* `local x : T`: an internal variable of the pseudocode, not part of any architectural state.
* `function f(a : T, {ellipsis}) → T' = { {ellipsis} }`, `struct { {ellipsis} }`, `array [i..j] of T`, `return`: function definition, record, array type, and result.
* `if (c) then:` and `foreach({ellipsis}) do:` followed by indented lines: the same constructs, with the indented lines as their body.
* `(a, b)` after `return`, and `x, y {leftarrow} f({ellipsis})`: a result with several components and its component-wise assignment, in declaration order; a result type written `{x : T, {ellipsis}}` is such a tuple.
* `bool`, `true`, `false`: the Boolean type and its values.
* A variable first assigned in a listing without `local` is local to that function.

[[KLE-Notation-functions]]
== Functions

* `0`, `1`: a single bit equal to zero, resp. one.
* `bin(n,m)`: the `m`-bit binary representation of the unsigned integer `n`, zero-extended or truncated to its `m` least significant bits; bit `i` is the coefficient of 2^i^, so `bin(int(V), m)` = `V` for an `m`-bit `V`, and `int(bin(n, m))` = `n` mod 2^m^.
* `bswap(m)`: the byte string `m` with its byte order reversed; for a value `V` of `8n` bits, the value whose byte `i` is byte `n-1-i` of `V`.
  An optional second argument `n` takes the `n` least significant bytes of `m` and reverses them, discarding the others: `bswap(m, n)` = `bswap(m[8n-1:0])`, an `8n`-bit value.
* `ceil(x)`, also `{lceil}x{rceil}`: the smallest integer not smaller than `x`; `floor(x)`, also `{lfloor}x{rfloor}`: the largest integer not larger than `x`.
* `double(S)`: doubling in the OCB3 field (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-OCB-mode>>).
* `Galoismul(a, b)`: multiplication of `a` and `b` in the GHASH field (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-GCM-mode>>).
* `int(V)`: the unsigned integer with bit `V[i]` as the coefficient of 2^i^, that is `V` read as a little-endian unsigned binary number.
* `len_in_bits(M)`: the length of the value or string `M` in bits; `len_in_bytes(M)` = `len_in_bits(M) / 8`, defined only when `len_in_bits(M)` is a multiple of 8.
* `lsb(x)`, `msb(x)`: the least, resp. most, significant bit of `x`; `lsb~c~(x)`, `msb~c~(x)`: its `c` least, resp. most, significant bits.
* `m[i .. j]`: the bytes from the ``i``-th to the ``j``-th of byte array `m`, both inclusive; `m[j:i]`: the bits from the ``i``-th to the ``j``-th of `m`, both inclusive.
* `min(x, y, {ellipsis})`, `max(x, y, {ellipsis})`: the smallest, resp. largest, of the arguments.
* `Montmul(a, b)`: the POLYVAL field operation.
* `ones(i)`, `zeros(i)`: a sequence of `i` ones, resp. zeros.
* `update_mask(M)`: the XEX/XTS mask update (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-XEX-XTS-modes>>); outside that section, that of `AES128_XEX`.

[[KLE-Notation-transcription]]
== Transcribing a Formula from a Standard

Referenced standards often use the big-endian notation natural to communication protocols; this specification uses little-endian notation.
A formula taken from a standard is transcribed in two steps, in this order:

. *Reverse the concatenation:* `A~0~ {vvert} A~1~ {vvert} {ellipsis} {vvert} A~m-1~` becomes `A~m-1~ @ {ellipsis} @ A~1~ @ A~0~`.
  A wrong ordering is the single most common error in transcribing a standard.
  Long messages formed by concatenation keep the “{vvert}” notation, in the little-endian sense of <<KLE-Notation-operators>>, although the standard's own “{vvert}” may be ambiguous.
. *Encode each integer explicitly:* a field that the standard specifies as a big-endian integer becomes `bswap(bin(n, m))`, and one specified as little-endian `bin(n, m)`; neither is written as a bare numeral.

Two examples:

* cite:[RFC8452] {sect}4 builds each key-derivation input as the four-byte little-endian counter followed by the twelve-byte nonce, `LE32(i) {vvert} N`: step 1 gives `N @ LE32(i)`, and step 2 `N @ bin(i,32)`.
* cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] {sect}7.1 builds, for a 96-bit IV, `J0` = `IV {vvert} 0^31^ {vvert} 1`, whose trailing four bytes are the big-endian encoding of the integer 1: step 1 gives `1 @ 0^31^ @ IV`, and step 2 encodes the counter, giving `J0` {leftarrow} `bswap(bin(1,32)) @ IV`.
  The left-to-right transcription `IV @ zeros(31) @ 1` would place the IV in `J0[127:32]` and the counter in `J0[31:0]`, the reverse of what cite:[nist-SP-800-38D] requires.

[[KLE-Notation-standards]]
== Conventions of the Referenced Standards

In a standard, `A~0~ {vvert} A~1~ {vvert} {ellipsis} {vvert} A~m-1~` may make `A~0~` the most significant element, or the least.
The table records, for each standard whose algorithms this specification reproduces, its convention and the resulting obligation here.
It is an aid to review, and does not relieve an algorithm description of the obligation to be explicit.

[float="center",align="center",width="100%",cols="<26%,<37%,<37%",options="header"]
|===
| Standard | Convention used by the standard | Obligation in this specification
| SP 800-38A/B/E (ECB, CTR, CMAC, XTS)
| byte strings; SP 800-38A, Appendix B, permits different counter constructions that ensure uniqueness;
  the CMAC subkey shift is over the big-endian string; the XTS tweak and mask are
  little-endian.
| Direct mapping for ECB and for the XTS data path, with `update_mask` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-XEX-XTS-modes>>.
  KLE selects a 32-bit big-endian counter in the trailing bytes of the CTR counter block, assembled via `bswap` (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-keystream-modes>>), and
  CMAC's subkeys via the big-endian `double` (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-CMAC-mode>>): three orderings in one row.
| GB/T 17964-2021 (XTS with SM4)
| byte strings; the multiplication that updates the mask takes the bits of each byte in the reverse order of SP 800-38E.
| `update_mask` of `SM4_XEX` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-XEX-XTS-modes>>.
| HCTR2 (XCTR)
| byte strings; the block counter is a little-endian integer, XORed into the IV.
| Direct mapping: the input block is `IV xor (zeros(b - j) @ ctr)`, with no `bswap` (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-keystream-modes>>).
| SP 800-38D (GCM)
| byte strings; GHASH field bit-reflected within bytes; counters big-endian.
| `Galoismul` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-GCM-mode>>; every counter and length field via `bswap`.
| RFC 8452 (AES-GCM-SIV)
| byte strings; POLYVAL field fully little-endian; block counter little-endian.
| `Montmul` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>>; counters and length blocks via `bin`.
| RFC 7253 (OCB3)
| bit strings, first bit most significant; `double` and the `Stretch` window are
  big-endian.
| `double` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-OCB-mode>>; `bswap` written explicitly wherever the algorithm operates
  on the big-endian view.
| FIPS 202 (SHA-3, SHAKE)
| bit strings mapped to the state by {sect}3.1; lanes little-endian.
| Direct mapping of the absorbed string; the lane indexing of FIPS 202 {sect}3.1 applies
  unchanged; a final piece of any bit length maps directly, its last bits in the least significant positions (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-SHA-3>>).
| SP 800-185 (cSHAKE, KMAC)
| bit strings as in FIPS 202; `left_encode` and `right_encode` write an integer big-endian, most significant byte first.
| As FIPS 202; `right_encode` as defined in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-KMAC>>. The `cshake_block` and `key_block` use the same order, whether the provisioner forms them or, for a system key or a random key (_KeyType_ = 1 or 2), the KLE unit.
| RFC 7693 (BLAKE2)
| byte strings; words, counter and parameter block little-endian.
| Direct mapping: words and counter via `bin`, no `bswap`; a final piece is a whole number of bytes (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-BLAKE2>>).
| FIPS 180-4 (SHA-2), GB/T 32905 (SM3)
| Bit strings, first bit most significant within a byte; big-endian words and length encoding.
| Message schedule words, chaining variables (hence the digest) and the appended length via `bswap`; a final piece whose length is not a multiple of 8 carries its last bits in the most significant positions of its last byte (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-SHA-2>>; Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>, second bullet).
| SP 800-232 (Ascon)
| Little-endian throughout, including `{vvert}`.
| Direct mapping, also within the last byte of a final block of any bit length (_Enc_Last_Block_ and _Dec_Last_Block_ in <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-Ascon-AEAD128>>, the final piece of <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-Ascon-Hash256>> and the `Z` of <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-Ascon-CXOF128>>; Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-last-blk-len>>).
| FIPS 186-5, SP 800-56A, SP 800-186, RFC 5639, GM/T 0003 (ECDSA, ECDH, SM2)
| integers and coordinates as big-endian octet strings.
| Every value little-endian via `bin`, converted by the caller (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-ECC>>); `Hash` holds `bin(e, h)` of the big-endian digest integer `e`; the shared secret that _Output_ emits keeps the big-endian order of its standard, via `bswap`.
| RFC 7748, RFC 8032 (X25519, X448, EdDSA)
| little-endian integers, _u_-coordinates and point encodings.
| Direct mapping via `bin`, the shared secret `K` and the encoded points and signatures included (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-X25519-X448>>, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-EdDSA>>).
| FIPS 198-1 (HMAC)
| bit strings, as in its hash function; `ipad` and `opad` repeat a single byte.
| Direct mapping; a final piece in the bit order of `H` (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-HMAC>>).
| FIPS 203 / FIPS 204 (ML-KEM, ML-DSA)
| byte strings produced by the standard's own encoding routines.
| Direct mapping of the encoded byte strings; no re-encoding.
|===
