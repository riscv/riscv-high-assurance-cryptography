_This chapter is informative and non-normative._

This chapter states thirteen Design Principles, and six Design Decisions taken on the basis of the Principles, from which all Rules of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-unpriv>> follow, from Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>> onward.
The Principles name no individual instruction: each rule should read as one Principle applied to one instruction of State class.
Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-multiple-GPRs>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, which state operand conventions, and the SR rules are also Design Decisions and are not covered.

== Vocabulary

The Principles use only the following categories, all defined in the specification.

*State groups.*

* _Unconfigured_: the locker holds no CC.
* _Valid_ States: the locker holds a usable CC. _Ready_, _Success_ and _Failure_ are the architected ones.
* _Error_ States: the locker holds only an MDH, which records why its CC became unusable.
* _Configuration_ States: the CC is in transit, being provisioned, imported or exported.
* _Complete_ = Valid ∪ Error. _Inert_ = Unconfigured ∪ Error, plus the case of no locker at all (`klmanagedlocker` = 32).

*Instruction classes* (from <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-instruction-properties>> in Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>).

* _Usage_ instructions operate on the CC through the Machine; _UsagePolicy_ gates them.
** _Derivations_ are usage instructions with two lockers, gated on each, and with a policy consequence (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>).
* _Configuration_ instructions operate on the locker as a container; _UsagePolicy_ does not gate them. They are of three kinds:
** _inspection_: read the MDH or a size, change nothing;
** _destructive_: zeroize or overwrite the locker without reading it;
** _transfer_: move a CC, or part of it, unchanged, between lockers or between a locker and memory or registers: the management transfers (`kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin`, `kl.mvout`), `kl.rename` and `kl.swap`, and the closing of a management operation.
* _Memory_ instructions have a memory operand. The class cuts across the others: `kl.load` and `kl.store` are both transfer and memory instructions.

*The managed locker* is the locker whose CC is in transit. A pointer names it, not a locker number.

== The Design Principles

The thirteen Principles fall into five groups.

* _Transparency_: the architecture describes all the state there is (<<KLEE-Principle-1>>). It precedes the definition of a locker.
* _Security_: what software may always do, what it keeps, what it may never gain, and how the system defends itself (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>–<<KLEE-Principle-6>>): the availability of the lockers, the persistence, confidentiality and integrity of the CCs, the confidentiality of the keys they hold, and the invalidation of a misused key. <<KLEE-Principle-5>> gives the other Principles their purpose.
* _Structure_: how a Machine is driven (<<KLEE-Principle-7>>) and how an operation executes (<<KLEE-Principle-8>>).
* _Economy_: the least encoding space, the least architectural state, the least code (<<KLEE-Principle-9>>, <<KLEE-Principle-10>>, <<KLEE-Principle-11>>). The mechanism that the first two lead to, the Metadata Header, is the Design Decision <<KLEE-decision-1>>; the one that the last two lead to, the managed locker, is <<KLEE-decision-5>>.
* _Outcomes_: what happens when an instruction cannot do what it is asked, for the State groups that <<KLEE-Principle-6>> does not cover (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>, <<KLEE-Principle-13>>). <<KLEE-Principle-12>> is also one way to implement <<KLEE-Principle-11>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-1,P1]]
=== P1 — Resource management is architectural

The architecture, not the implementation, manages every resource that software relies on: a resource exists in an architected place, with an architected state that software can manage, and the architecture alone decides when it is available.

Hence there is no hidden state necessary to make the feature meet specific use cases.

*Rationale.*  Whereas other architectures mention may rely on caching to make more than one key available to software, KLEE makes locker handling entirely architectural.  KLEE exposes its state machines and resources instead (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CC-management>>), letting software manage them, and as result the implementation is simpler.

*Consequences.* There are 32 lockers, each with its MDH always present. The KLF capacity for Content is implementation-defined, but its exhaustion is an architected exception at the instruction that needs it, never a silent eviction. Context switching saves and restores the architected state. Whatever an implementation keeps beyond the MDH and the Content, the ADS included, can be discarded at any time without changing an architected outcome: the architected behavior of a locker never depends on state outside its MDH and Content. Such state is identified by architected registers, so that an import can tell whether it is usable (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CSR-impl-ids>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-KLF>>, the out-of-memory item of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-architecture>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; among the General Rules, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (progress outside the Content) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>> (implementation-bound Metadata).

[[KLEE-Principle-2,P2]]
=== P2 — It must always be possible to configure new Cryptographic Contexts

Configuration instructions are admitted in every State group, and neither a Machine nor a _UsagePolicy_ may restrict them: even when all lockers are in use, a caller can reconfigure any of them. In particular, the return to _Ready_ and the exits from an Error State are always open, and a locker in a Configuration State can be cleared, re-provisioned or re-imported like any other.

*Rationale.* Avoiding any denial of service on the KLEE resources.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-opening-locker-when>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-triggers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-to-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>> (first paragraph), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clearads>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>> (last sentence), `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-3,P3]]
=== P3 — Cryptographic Contexts persist: they can be stored and reloaded to survive context switches, power cycles, process and VM migrations,

A CC is not bound to the locker that holds it, nor to the hart, the power state or the machine: it can be saved from a locker and restored into any one, so that it survives a context switch, sleep and hibernation, and the migration of a virtual machine. What is saved is the whole CC, its MDH and Content, and restoring it yields a locker that behaves as the saved one did, including an operation interrupted at an architected interruption point; an interruption an an implementation-defined point need not survive, and the operation can always restart (<<KLEE-decision-6>>).

*Rationale.* Persistence is the counterpart to <<KLEE-Principle-2>> that makes the KLEE architecture so powerful: a CC evicted from a locker using the architecturally provided means to make room is not destroyed, only saved. Only the consumer of a CC (or a higher privileged piece of software) can decide when a CC is destroyed.

*Consequences.* The SCCs, and the mechanisms to export them from lockers and import them into lockers.
The format is defined independently of the implementation and of the ISA, so that it can be restored elsewhere (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC>>, <<Zkl-books.adoc#KLEE-separation>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-export>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-import>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-isolation>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-4,P4]]
=== P4 — Cryptographic Contexts keep their confidentiality and integrity outside a locker

A CC outside a locker reveals nothing of its secret material and cannot be altered undetected: it is sealed under a key that only the KLEE unit, or the set of units it was bound to, holds, and it is authenticated together with its MDH, so that neither the key nor the policies that govern it can be read or changed in transit. A CC can be bound to a locality, a chip, a chip family or a system, and is then restorable only there.

*Rationale.* Persistence (<<KLEE-Principle-3>>) moves a CC through memory, storage and networks that the KLEE unit does not control; the guarantees of <<KLEE-Principle-5>> hold outside a locker only if the saved form carries them.

*Consequences.* Export seals the CC with an AEAD under a key derived from a locality secret and binds the MDH as associated data; import verifies the seal before anything is restored, and a failed verification leaves an Error State, not a usable locker. Metadata that only the issuing implementation can interpret is bound to that implementation. A locality secret must not be withdrawn while a CC bound to it may still be exported.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-AEAD>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-Localities>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>> (the _Authentication Failed_ outcome); outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-locality-unconfigure>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-5,P5]]
=== P5 — Privileges are never elevated

No instruction yields a key, a locker or an output with more rights than its inputs and its issuer already hold. Three consequences cover the rules.

_No extraction._ Once a key is in a CC, it cannot be extracted in clear: it is used only through its Machine, no instruction reads it out, and an exported CC is sealed. Whoever obtains the CC obtains the right to use the key under its policies, never the key.

_Usage is gated._ An instruction exercises only the rights that the _UsagePolicy_ grants to the issuing mode; exercising any other is a privilege violation.

_Secrets flow only downhill._ Material derived from a secret inherits every constraint of the secret: it moves only into a locker whose policies, after narrowing, are at least as strict as the source's. Narrowing never widens a policy, raises a protection level or changes a Machine policy. A transfer is exempt from narrowing exactly when the caller could obtain its result with admitted instructions anyway, so the exemption adds no attack surface. A secret bound to the system never leaves it, and a bound key field is never replaced from outside.

Secrets do not linger. When a CC is destroyed, invalidated or restarted, the material that depended on it is released or zeroized; a destructive configuration instruction zeroizes before any check that could trap, so no stale copy survives in the KLF.

*Rationale.* This invariant is what makes least privilege enforceable: policies granted at provisioning and narrowed later can be relied upon only if no operation can widen or bypass them. For derived material, NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 §6.2.1 and SP 800-108r1 §8 require exactly this inheritance. The exemption from narrowing is a shortcut, not a privilege.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-concepts>> (no extraction), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>> (gating), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-narrowing>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>>, the paragraphs after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-unrestricted>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>>; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> (zeroizing step) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (restart) for the last paragraph.

[[KLEE-Principle-6,P6]]
=== P6 — Any misuse of a Valid locker makes it unavailable to software

A usage instruction that the Machine, the current State or the current policies do not admit on a Valid locker performs no operation and moves the locker to _Invalid_; so does an unrecoverable failure of a resource the operation needs. The architecture keeps no history: once in an Error State, the locker is inert (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) until reconfigured (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>).

*Rationale.* Fail closed. A sequencing error on a live key may be an attack; the only safe outcome is to make the key unusable until software, which can always reconfigure, decides.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>> (second paragraph), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-same-state>> (last sentence), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> (permanent failure), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-state-after-setst>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-7,P7]]
=== P7 — All Machines follow one state-machine protocol

Software drives every Machine the same way: a state-setting usage instruction names the next State, or an operation on the current one; an execute usage instruction performs the operation of the current State. The Machine specifies which transitions exist and which usage instructions each Valid State admits; the architecture supplies the transitions and admissions common to all Machines (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>, <<KLEE-Principle-2>>).

Two conventions complete the protocol: re-entering the current State is admitted unless the implemented standard gives it no meaning, and the completion of a management operation leaves the locker in the State its MDH names, a fresh provisioning in _Ready_.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-triggers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clearads>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-8,P8]]
=== P8 — Every operation is atomic or prefix-complete with atomic component operations and bounded progress, and asynchrony is invisible

An instruction is of one of two kinds. An _uninterruptible_ instruction completes, has no effect, or traps with only the effects that <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> lists. A _resumable_ instruction processes its input serially and halts only at a prefix-complete point, recorded in `klstart`, where it has committed exactly what a shorter instruction performing the completed prefix would commit. Its units, blocks or granules, are atomic: each is performed fully or not at all, although the memory accesses that implement it need not be (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>). A precise halt does not retire the instruction; a transition to an Error State after partial progress does.

The KLEE unit may execute asynchronously, but the issuing hart never observes it: a conflicting access waits for the instruction to complete or halt precisely. Memory instructions are RISC-V memory instructions in every respect, and touch only idempotent memory, so that a prefix can be replayed.

A management operation spans several instructions, and the same holds for it: between its instructions the locker is in a Configuration State, the prefix-complete state of the operation, which nothing consumes partially (<<KLEE-decision-4>>).

Progress is bounded. No instruction processes a potentially unbounded input, as some software libraries and some CISC architectures do. Every wait, retry and restart has a documented bound, past which the implementation either progresses or reports the condition with the _State_ unchanged. Key material is all-or-nothing: it enters a field in one uninterruptible step of exactly the field's length, or not at all, so no locker is ever usable with a partly written key.

*Rationale.* Context save and restore need a state that the locker, `klstart`, `vstart` and the ADS describe completely; for a management operation, that state is the locker in its Configuration State. The memory model must not acquire a new kind of access. Halting and resuming is meaningful only if resumption eventually completes.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-program-order>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> (interruption points, transfer size), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (halt points), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>> (key item), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> (temporary failure); for management operations, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-9,P9]]
=== P9 — Minimize the pressure on the encoding space

Instead of defining instructions for each cryptographic primitive, variant and mode of operation, KLEE defines a few generic instructions and encodes the algorithm, its mode and its parameters in the data, the MDH: one `kl.exec` and one `kl.setst` serve every Machine, present and future, and a new Machine needs a new _Machine_ value, never a new opcode.

*Rationale.* Opcode space is the scarcest resource of an ISA, and cryptographic algorithms are many and short-lived compared with an ISA. Binding them to the Metadata keeps the instruction set fixed while the set of Machines grows, and it is what lets every Machine follow one protocol (<<KLEE-Principle-7>>).

*Consequences.* The _Machine_ field of the MDH names the algorithm, and the immediate of `kl.setst` names the State to enter or the operation to perform on the current one, so that the same two instructions drive every state machine. The remaining instructions manage lockers, not algorithms: they clear, inspect, move, restrict and transfer, and none of them depends on the Machine.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-Machine-field>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-management-immeds>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-instruction-properties>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-10,P10]]
=== P10 — Additional architectural state beyond the lockers is kept to a minimum

KLEE adds to the hart only what its operations cannot do without: the lockers, one progress counter (`klstart`), one pointer to the managed locker (`klmanagedlocker`), one set of authentication and implementation-identification registers (`SIV`, `IMPQUAL`, `SIV2`) and, with `Zklio`, one buffer. Each exists once per hart, not per locker or per operation. Any other state an operation needs lives in its locker.

*Rationale.* Every added register is state that a context switch must save and restore, that interposed software must preserve, and that may leak between contexts. One instance of each, shared by all lockers, keeps the save-and-restore sequence short and fixed, and the cost of a hart's KLEE state independent of the number of lockers.

*Consequences.* One set of authentication registers means at most one management operation per hart in flight, the premise of <<KLEE-decision-5>>: one pointer names its locker, a second operation on another locker must first save the first by a nested export, and management operations are not interleaved with each other or with cryptographic operations. One `klstart` means that every instruction honoring it starts from the value it finds, that an opening `kl.mgmt` clears it, and that interposed software saves and restores it. The authentication registers and the pointer are shared, so touching them while an operation is in flight is a conflicting access. A long-running operation records its progress in its locker, in _MachineUse_ and the ADS, not in a register of its own.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>> (through <<KLEE-decision-5>>), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> (`klstart` cleared by `kl.mgmt`), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-save-shared-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-restore>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klmanagedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-11,P11]]
=== P11 — Make instructions as simple as possible, while still requiring short code sequences

Instructions are as simple as the RISC-V philosophy asks, and the code that uses them is short too: whatever a routine would otherwise repeat in every instruction, or check after every instruction, becomes architectural state or a single check. Two cases matter most. The locker a management routine works on is held in an architectural register, so the routine carries no locker number and the same code serves every locker. The outcomes of asynchronous events are shaped so that one check at the end of a sequence suffices.

*Rationale.* Management and context-switch code runs in handlers and libraries: written once, audited once, it must not grow with the number of lockers or of steps. Moving a repeated operand or check into the architecture costs one CSR and one read-out, and removes a class of software errors. Hardware complexity is added only where it brings the largest saving: a management routine that works on any locker, instead of one instance per locker, is 32 times less code, whereas cryptographic code can address fixed lockers and needs no such help.

*Consequences.* <<KLEE-decision-5>>, with <<KLEE-Principle-10>>: the managed-locker pointer, its implicit and `KLML` operands, and the instructions that read and write it. The "check once" property of <<KLEE-Principle-12>>: an instruction that falls through never traps and never acts elsewhere, so a routine checks the _State_ only after its last step, through the pointer, without knowing the locker number. One instruction clears every locker and the buffer. The KLIOBUF substitutes for vector operands, so one code path serves harts with and without vectors.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-override>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, the NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and the NOTE after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>; outside the GR series, the substitution rules of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-usage-input-output>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-12,P12]]
=== P12 — Inert lockers let instructions fall through

On an inert locker, every instruction other than a destructive one performs no operation and raises no exception: the _State_ is unchanged and any output is zero. A transfer instruction leaves `klstart` as it would after a transfer with an empty window. Inspection instructions read what is there: zeros, or the MDH.

*Rationale.* A locker becomes inert asynchronously, when a handler clears or invalidates it. The instructions that follow must neither trap nor act on something else, and the original error must stay readable, so that one check at the end of the sequence suffices: fall-through is one way to implement <<KLEE-Principle-11>>. Since `klstart` does not advance on an inert locker, the length of a transfer sequence is the caller's own count, taken from the image size before the transfer, never `klstart` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-no-klstart-loop>>).

*Boundary.* <<KLEE-Principle-12>> governs lockers that hold nothing to act on. A misused Valid locker falls under <<KLEE-Principle-6>>, a locker in a Configuration State under <<KLEE-Principle-8>>.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-openings>>, bullet 2 of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, inspection part of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-13,P13]]
=== P13 — Exceptions are raised only for error conditions that fall-through would misreport

An instruction traps only for an error condition that falling through would misreport. There are four: a malformed or reserved encoding; a sequencing error that would silently lose a CC; a use of unconfigured infrastructure, as opposed to an inert locker; and a policy violation, which the policy owner must see, not the caller. These checks precede every check that depends on the locker's _State_, so that fall-through (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) is never mistaken for an error.

*Rules.* First half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-openings>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-mgmt-klmanagedlocker-check>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-iobuf-configured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, check order of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>.

== Design Decisions

A _Design Decision_ is a mechanism that two or more Principles select together, and from which a group of rules follows. It is not a Principle: another mechanism could satisfy the same Principles. It is stated separately because the rules derive more easily from it than from the Principles directly.

[[KLEE-decision-1,D1]]
=== D1 — The Metadata Header

A locker is described by a 128-bit header carried with its CC, the MDH, which alone determines what the locker is, what it admits and how large it is. It is the architected description that <<KLEE-Principle-1>> requires, it names the algorithm as <<KLEE-Principle-9>> asks, and it is one fixed-size header rather than a table or a set of registers (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>). Its layout minimizes the lookahead to identify a locker (<<KLEE-Principle-11>>): _Machine_, its policies, _State_ and _StateExtension_ lie in the first 32 bits, and the fields that fix every length in the first 64, so that one GPR on RV32 identifies the locker and two size it (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-length-rule>>).

Hence the _State_ field alone identifies the points software must recognize; an Error-State locker _is_ its MDH, and is exported and imported as such; an _Unconfigured_ locker reads as the one MDH value that is not an MDH; a management operation restores exactly the MDH it was given; and implementation-defined progress lives outside the Content, so that every operation can restart from the Content.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-state-identifies-cc>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-locker-is-only-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>.

[[KLEE-decision-2,D2]]
=== D2 — The outcome of an instruction on a locker

<<KLEE-Principle-6>>, <<KLEE-Principle-12>> and <<KLEE-Principle-13>> assign one outcome to each State group; <<KLEE-Principle-8>> adds the Configuration States and <<KLEE-Principle-2>> the configuration instructions. Together they give a single rule, which the General Rules it replaces would then only refine:

--
After the checks that do not depend on the locker's _State_ (encoding, fatal error, missing CSK, locker _Off_, `klmanagedlocker`), the outcome of a KLEE instruction is determined by the locker's State group and the instruction's class:

. _Inert locker_ (_Unconfigured_, Error State, or no managed locker): every instruction except a destructive configuration instruction performs no operation and raises no exception; the _State_ is unchanged, an output operand is zeroed, and an inspection instruction returns zeros or the MDH.
. _Configuration State_: configuration instructions are admitted; a usage instruction raises a KLEE privilege-violation exception and leaves the locker unchanged.
. _Valid State_: a configuration instruction is admitted; a usage instruction that _UsagePolicy_ forbids raises a KLEE privilege-violation exception and leaves the locker unchanged; a usage instruction that the Machine, the State or _MachinePolicy_ does not admit performs no operation and transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_; any other usage instruction performs its operation.

A malformed or reserved encoding, a `kl.mgmt` that would interleave two management operations, and a KLIOBUF instruction without a configured KLIOBUF raise an illegal-instruction exception, resp. an unconfigured-KLIOBUF exception, in every State group.
--

Leg 1 is <<KLEE-Principle-12>>, leg 2 is <<KLEE-Principle-13>>, leg 3 combines <<KLEE-Principle-13>> and <<KLEE-Principle-6>>, and the last paragraph is the part of <<KLEE-Principle-13>> that does not depend on the locker. The order of the checks within each leg stays in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-gate-order>>.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, and the summary table <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-insns-on-unconf-or-error-locker>>.

[[KLEE-decision-3,D3]]
=== D3 — The Error-State architecture

An Error State is a value of the _State_ field, so an Error-State locker is described by its MDH alone (<<KLEE-decision-1>>): it holds nothing else, and its Content and ADS are released when it enters the State (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>). It got there by misuse or by an unrecoverable failure (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>), and it stays there, inert (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>, leg 1 of <<KLEE-decision-2>>), so that the original error remains readable until software reconfigures it, which it always can (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>). Since it is only an MDH, it is exported and imported as one, without a management operation, and a handler that cannot resolve the condition records it there by setting the Error State itself.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-state-transfer>>, the error list of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-architecture>>, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-handler-duty>>.

[[KLEE-decision-4,D4]]
=== D4 — The management operation protocol

A management operation is one operation spanning several instructions (<<KLEE-Principle-8>>): a `kl.mgmt` opens it, transfer instructions move the image, and a `kl.mgmt` closes it. Between them the locker is in a Configuration State, the prefix-complete state of the operation, which only configuration instructions may touch (<<KLEE-Principle-8>>). The opening for provisioning or import is a destructive configuration instruction: it is admitted in every State (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>) and zeroizes the locker before anything else (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>), so that no earlier content can leak into the new one even if the operation then traps. At most one operation per hart is in flight, named by the managed locker (<<KLEE-decision-5>>); a second one nests by exporting the first.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-opening-locker-when>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-destructive-config>>, the `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-locker-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-nested-mgmt>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klmanagedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.

[[KLEE-decision-5,D5]]
=== D5 — The managed locker architecture

At most one CC per hart is in transit at a time, together with its authentication and identification information `SIV`, `IMPQUAL` and `SIV2`: these registers exist once per hart (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>), so a second management operation must first save the first by a nested export. The pointer `klmanagedlocker` names the locker of the CC in transit, so that a management routine needs no locker number (<<KLEE-Principle-11>>). The pointer follows the CC when it moves, becomes "none" when the CC ceases to exist, is kept when the operation completes or fails so that the outcome stays readable, and is otherwise written only by software.

The instructions that take the pointer are the minimum that management routines need, since hardware complexity is added only where it brings the largest saving in code size (<<KLEE-Principle-11>>): the management and inspection instructions take it, because one routine written for the pointer replaces 32 written for fixed lockers; the usage instructions do not, because cryptographic code can address fixed lockers.

Two consequences follow with <<KLEE-Principle-12>> and <<KLEE-Principle-13>>. Opening a second operation on another locker while the pointer is in use is a sequencing error, and traps (<<KLEE-Principle-13>>). Naming the managed locker when there is none falls through (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) for every instruction but an opening, which has nothing to open and traps (<<KLEE-Principle-13>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>, pointer bullet of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>>. <<KLEE-decision-5>> follows from <<KLEE-Principle-10>> and <<KLEE-Principle-11>>.

[[KLEE-decision-6,D6]]
=== D6 — The resumption architecture

Since every operation is atomic or prefix-complete (<<KLEE-Principle-8>>) and the hart keeps a single progress register (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>), `klstart` records the prefix-complete point of whichever resumable instruction is in flight, in bytes, and `vstart` the same point in elements where a vector operand is involved. Interruption points are of two kinds. Those that the architecture defines, per instruction and per Machine State, are part of the CC: `klstart` and the State record them, and a restored CC resumes from them (<<KLEE-Principle-3>>). Those that an implementation defines, inside a long-running operation, are not: the operation records its progress in _MachineUse_ and the ADS, so that it is architected and not hidden (<<KLEE-Principle-1>>), but an import, a clone or another implementation may discard it and restart the operation from the Content. The architecture promises resumption only at its own points, and leaves the finer ones to the implementation. The issuing hart never observes the asynchrony: a conflicting access waits for completion or a precise halt, and interposed software saves and restores `klstart` around its own use.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CSR-klstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLEE-MGR-progress-discard>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-no-reset>>.

[[KLEE-Principles-residue]]
== What the Principles do not determine

The Principles constrain the following choices without fixing them; they remain rules or encodings in their own right.

* *The klmanagedlocker is kept on completion* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>). <<KLEE-decision-5>> would also allow resetting it; keeping it makes the outcome readable and lets the next opening reuse it.
* *Provisioning ends in _Ready_, with _StateExtension_ and _MachineUse_ zero* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>). A protocol convention.
* *The size arithmetic of derivations* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>: `min`, zero-fill, divisibility of granularities). <<KLEE-Principle-8>> requires only prefix-completeness and all-or-nothing keys.
* *Freedom within one memory instruction* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>: any granularity, any order, no atomicity). <<KLEE-Principle-8>> requires only prefix-completeness at halts.
* *Which block transfers are uninterruptible* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>: scalar `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout` are, vector ones are resumable). Both move 128-bit blocks; the split is a choice.
