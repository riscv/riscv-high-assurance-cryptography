_This chapter is informative and non-normative._

This chapter states thirteen Design Principles, and eight Design Decisions taken on their basis, from which the Rules of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-unpriv>> follow, from Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>> onward.
The Decisions could be revisited without changing the Principles, and where a summary below and a Rule differ, the Rule prevails.
No Principle is stated in terms of an individual instruction: each rule should read as one Principle applied to one instruction or State class.
Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-multiple-GPRs>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, which state operand conventions, and the SR rules are Design Decisions too, and are not covered.

== Vocabulary

The Principles use only the following categories, all defined in the specification.

*State groups.*

* _Unconfigured_: the locker holds no CC.
* _Valid_ States: the locker holds a usable CC. _Ready_, _Success_ and _Failure_ are the architected ones.
* _Error_ States: the locker holds only an MDH, which records why its CC became unusable.
* _Configuration_ States: the CC is in transit, i.e., being provisioned, imported or exported.
// * _Complete_ = Valid ∪ Error. _Inert_ = Unconfigured ∪ Error, plus the case of no locker at all (`klnamedlocker` = 32).

*Instruction classes* (from <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-properties>> in Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-policy>>).

* _Usage_ instructions operate on the CC through the Machine; _UsagePolicy_ gates them.
** _Derivations_ are usage instructions with two lockers, gated on each.
* _Configuration_ instructions operate on the locker as a container; _UsagePolicy_ does not gate them. They are of three kinds, which may overlap:
** _inspection_: read the MDH or a size, change nothing;
** _destructive_: zeroize or overwrite the locker without reading it; `kl.swap`, a special case, overwrites two lockers, each with the other's CC;
** _transfer_: move a CC, or part of it, unchanged, between lockers or between a locker and memory or registers: the management transfers (`kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin`, `kl.mvout`), `kl.rename` and `kl.swap`, and the closing of a management operation.
* _Memory_ instructions have a memory operand. The class cuts across the others: `kl.load` and `kl.store` are both transfer and memory instructions.

*The named locker* is the one whose number `klnamedlocker` holds, 32 meaning none: the locker of the CC in transit, which stays selected after the operation ends, so that software can read its outcome.

== Design Principles

The thirteen Principles fall into seven groups.

* _Composability_: several algorithms in use at once, and combined (<<KLE-Principle-multiple-algorithms>>). It requires the lockers and the derivation between them (<<KLE-Decision-derivation>>).
* _Transparency_: the architecture describes all the state there is (<<KLE-Principle-management-architectural>>). It precedes the definition of a locker.
* _Statefulness_: a CC keeps the state of its algorithm, not only its key (<<KLE-Principle-contexts-stateful>>).
* _Scalability_: KLE works on every class of RISC-V core, from microcontrollers without vectors to vector cores, and on SoCs whose cores share one KLE unit (<<KLE-Principle-scalable>>). The Economy Principles limit the cost of what this requires.
* _Economy_: simple instructions and short code, the least encoding space, the least architectural state (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>–<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>). The Metadata Header (<<KLE-Decision-metadata>>) follows from <<KLE-Principle-management-architectural>>, <<KLE-Principle-encoding-pressure>> and <<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>; the named locker (<<KLE-Decision-named-locker>>) from <<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>> and <<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>.
* _Security_: what software may always do, what it keeps, what it may never gain, even from a CC outside a locker, and what it cannot make an algorithm do (<<KLE-Principle-availability>>–<<KLE-Principle-no-misuse>>): the availability of the lockers, the persistence of the CCs, the confidentiality of the keys, the confidentiality and integrity of the CCs outside a locker, and the integrity of the algorithms. <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> gives the other Principles their purpose.
* _Structure_: how an operation executes (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>).

[[KLE-Principle-multiple-algorithms,P1]]
P1: _Multiple algorithms can be in use at the same time, and combined_::
+
--
Applications run several cryptographic operations at once and chain them: a protocol agrees on a key, derives session keys from it, and uses them to encrypt and authenticate while it hashes its transcript.
KLE therefore holds several CCs at once, each with its own Machine, policies and State, so that operations on different lockers interleave, and combines them inside the unit (<<KLE-Decision-derivation>>).

*Rules.* The lockers of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-clone>>; through <<KLE-Decision-derivation>>, the <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-derivation-rules>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-management-architectural,P2]]
P2: _The entire resource management is architectural_::
+
--
Whereas other architectures may rely on caching to make more than one key available to software, KLE instead exposes resource management to the caller (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CC-management>>) through an architectural interface.  The lockers that <<KLE-Principle-multiple-algorithms>> requires share the capacity of the KLF.  Capacity exhaustion is always reported directly to software, which handles it (see <<KLE-Principle-availability>> below).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; among the General Rules, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>> (progress outside the Content), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-clearads>> (the ADS can be dropped), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-drawn-values>> (drawn values are architected) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-custom-locality>> (implementation-bound Metadata).
--

[[KLE-Principle-contexts-stateful,P3]]
P3: _Cryptographic Contexts are stateful and active, not passive_::
+
--
Complex algorithms run in steps: a hash or MAC absorbs its input in pieces, an AEAD takes associated data, then the message, then the tag, and a key agreement or encapsulation emits and receives values between steps.
Their intermediate state is often secret, so it stays in the CC (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>): a CC holds the state of an algorithm, not only its key, each instruction of the caller advances it, and the _State_ records where the algorithm stands, so that it can be resumed, saved and restored (<<KLE-Principle-persistence>>) and checked against the sequences its standard allows (<<KLE-Principle-no-misuse>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-State-field>>; through <<KLE-Decision-machine-protocol>>, the Rules it lists; in the Machines, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-Machines>> and Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-scalable,P4]]
P4: _KLE must work on RISC-V cores with or without vector registers_::
+
--
Keys need protection on microcontrollers, many of which have no vector unit, and one KLE unit may serve cores with and without vectors on a SoC.

This Principle drives `Zklio`: the KLIOBUF, `kl.input`, `kl.output` and the substitutions, which turn vector code directly into code without vectors. Such code runs on any core with `Zklio`; where `Zklv` exists, vector code is faster.

Principles <<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>–<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>, which could be used to argue against the buffer, are used to shape it instead: `kl.input` and `kl.output` only move data, the substitutions reuse existing Forms, and there is only one buffer per hart, which a context switch need not save once software sets its length to zero. Every write to `kliobuflen` zeroes the buffer up to the new length, and the bytes beyond it are inaccessible, so no contents leak.

*Rules.* The conformance requirements of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-extensions-overview-unpriv>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-usage-input-output>>, the KLIOBUF variants of `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout` (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-mv>>), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-iobuf-configured>>; outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-kliobuflen-zero>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code,P5]]
P5: _Make instructions as simple as possible, while still requiring short code sequences_::
+
--
Various architectural decisions follow from this principle:

. <<KLE-Decision-named-locker>>, with <<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>: the named-locker index, its implicit and `KLNL` operands, and the instructions that read and write it.
. The "check once" property of <<KLE-Decision-fall-through>>: a routine checks the _State_ only after its last step.
. One instruction clears every locker and the KLIOBUF.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-override>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnl-no-named-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>>, the NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and the NOTE after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-memory-instructions>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-encoding-pressure,P6]]
P6: _Minimize the pressure on the encoding space_::
+
--
Opcode space is the scarcest resource of an ISA, and cryptographic algorithms are many and some even short-lived by comparison.
Ongoing standardization processes keep defining new ones.
For this reason, KLE encodes the algorithm, its mode and its parameters in the Metadata, not in opcodes:
the instruction set stays fixed as Machines are added, and every Machine follows one protocol (<<KLE-Decision-machine-protocol>>).
`kl.exec` performs every cryptographic operation and `kl.setst` drives every transition between States, with `kl.derive` moving material between lockers: KLE is the interface to an attached unit, whatever the implementation, and these instructions are messages to it.
The conceptual separation of the hart from the KLE unit also allows updatable implementations of the latter.

The remaining instructions manage lockers, not algorithms, and none depends on the Machine.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Machine-field>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-management-immeds>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-properties>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state,P7]]
P7: _Additional architectural state beyond the lockers is kept to a minimum_::
+
--
Every added register must be saved and restored at a context switch and preserved by interposed software, and may leak between contexts.
State that an operation needs only while under way therefore exists once per hart, not per locker.
One set of authentication and identification registers, `SIV`, `IMPQUAL` and `SIV2`, 128 or 384 bits depending on the features, serves every locker, so that software keeps at most one management operation in flight per hart, saving it before starting another (<<KLE-Decision-named-locker>>).
`klstart` and, with `Zklio`, the KLIOBUF (<<KLE-Principle-scalable>>) follow the same rule.

With one `klstart`, an instruction honoring it starts from the value it finds, so an opening `kl.mgmt` clears it and interposed software saves and restores it.
Touching the shared registers or the locker index while an operation is in flight is a conflicting access, and a long-running operation keeps its progress in its own locker (<<KLE-Decision-resumption>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>> (`klstart` cleared by `kl.mgmt`), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>>; through <<KLE-Decision-named-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-save-shared-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klstart-restore>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klnamedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-availability,P8]]
P8: _It must always be possible to configure new Cryptographic Contexts_::
+
--
No caller may be denied the KLE resources.
Neither a Machine nor a _UsagePolicy_ may therefore restrict a configuration instruction, and those that destroy, replace or move a CC are admitted in every State group: even when all lockers are in use, a caller can reconfigure any of them, in an Error State or a Configuration State included.
Only a configuration instruction wrong for a CC in transit traps (<<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>): a transfer of the other direction, a clone or a restriction (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>, <<KLE-Decision-management-protocol>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-opening-locker-when>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transition-triggers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>> (configuration instructions), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>> (last sentence), `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-persistence,P9]]
P9: _Cryptographic Contexts support persistence_::
+
--
CCs can be stored and reloaded to survive context switches, power cycles, process and VM migrations.

Persistence is the counterpart of <<KLE-Principle-availability>>: a CC evicted to make room is saved, not destroyed, and only its consumer, or more privileged software, decides when it is destroyed.
An export therefore completes, and its image is saved, before the locker is cleared (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>).
A CC is bound neither to its locker, nor to the hart, the power state or the machine: the whole CC, MDH and Content, is saved in a format independent of the implementation and of the ISA (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC>>, <<Zkl-books.adoc#KLE-separation>>), and restoring it into any locker yields one that behaves as the saved one did, an interrupted operation included (<<KLE-Decision-resumption>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-restart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-export>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-import>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-isolation>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation,P10]]
P10: _Privileges are never elevated_::
+
--
Least privilege is enforceable only if no operation can widen or bypass the policies granted at provisioning and narrowed later.
No instruction therefore yields a key, a locker or an output with more rights than its inputs and its issuer already hold, unless that is the purpose of the Machine, as for public output encrypted under a secret key. The consequences are:

. _No extraction._ A key in a CC is used only through its Machine and never read out, and an exported CC is sealed: whoever obtains the CC may use the key under its policies, but never obtains the key.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-no-extraction>>.

. _Usage is gated._ A usage instruction can change a Valid locker only if _UsagePolicy_ grants the issuing mode access; otherwise it raises a privilege-violation exception, the locker unchanged.
Configuration instructions, which preserve, transfer or discard a CC without using its secrets, are not gated, even when an import or a clone restarts implementation-defined progress; `kl.clearads` is a usage instruction.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-policy>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>>.

. _Secrets flow only downhill._
Material derived from a secret inherits all its constraints, except when it is an output meant to be public, a KLE choice in line with the NIST key-management principles cited in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-derivation-rules>>.
No operation widens a policy, raises a protection level or enables a Machine to perform operations beyond those defined at its provisioning.
A secret bound to the system never leaves it, and a bound key field is never replaced from outside.
Secrets do not linger: the material that depended on a destroyed, invalidated or restarted CC is released or zeroized, and a destructive configuration instruction destroys the CC it replaces right after the state-independent checks.
A `kl.setst` that replaces an Error State overwrites the only information the locker holds, and `kl.swap` moves each CC into the other locker, so neither has anything to erase.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-narrowing>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>> and the paragraphs after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-unrestricted>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-custom-locality>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>> (zeroizing step) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-restart>>.

. _Nothing stale is returned._ An instruction that performs no operation, or stops in an Error State, zeroes the unwritten part of its output window, and an inert locker reads as zeros: neither an input nor an earlier output can pass for a result.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zero-unwritten-output>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity,P11]]
P11: _Cryptographic Contexts keep their confidentiality and integrity also outside a locker_::
+
--
Persistence (<<KLE-Principle-persistence>>) moves a CC through memory, storage and networks that the KLE unit does not control, and the guarantees of <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> hold there only if the saved form carries them.
A CC outside a locker therefore reveals nothing of its secret material and cannot be altered undetected: export seals it with an AEAD under keys derived from the CSK, with the MDH and the Localities as associated data, and import verifies the seal before the CC becomes usable, a failure leaving an Error State, or, for the ADS alone, dropping it.
The seal can be bound to a locality, a chip, a chip family or a system, and the CC is then restorable only there; implementation-defined Metadata is interpreted only by the implementation that wrote it, and zeroed elsewhere (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-MachineUse>>).

// A locality secret must not be withdrawn while a CC bound to it may still be exported.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-no-extraction>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-custom-locality>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>> (restricting a CC in transit), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-skid-field>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-single-skid>>; outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-locality-unconfigure>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-no-misuse,P12]]
P12: _Machines prevent all abuse of their algorithms that is not otherwise unavoidable_::
+
--
A hidden key is safe only if the algorithm that uses it cannot be driven to leak it.
A Machine therefore admits only the sequences of operations that its referenced standard defines, so that no sequence of KLE instructions can manipulate its internal state by repeating, skipping or reordering steps; the final States _Success_ and _Failure_ admit none.
A step outside these sequences may be an attack, so the locker fails closed (<<KLE-Decision-misuse>>).
A same-State `kl.setst` repeats the entry operations instead of doing nothing, since otherwise later `kl.exec` instructions could act on internal state that the re-entry was expected to reset; where the repetition would itself yield a state that the standard does not define, the Machine forbids it.
Abuse that software can commit anyway is not prevented, since a constraint that software can bypass protects nothing and costs complexity: an earlier SCC can always be re-imported (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>>), so the return to _Ready_ stays open, and nonce discipline remains the caller's (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-nonce-reuse>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>> and the endpoint definitions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-derivation-rules>>; in the Machines, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-not-allowed-instructions>> and <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-no-hash-output-repeat>>.
--

[[KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony,P13]]
P13: _Every operation is atomic or prefix-complete, progress is bounded, and asynchrony is invisible_::
+
--
A context switch must find a state that the locker, `klstart`, `vstart` and the ADS describe completely, and a resumed operation must eventually complete.
An _uninterruptible_ instruction therefore completes, has no effect, or traps with only the effects of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>.
A _resumable_ instruction processes its input serially in atomic units, blocks or granules, whose memory accesses need not be atomic (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>), and halts only at a prefix-complete point recorded in `klstart`, having committed exactly what a shorter instruction would.
A precise halt does not retire the instruction; a transition to an Error State after partial progress does.
A committed prefix is never recomputed, which protects the one-time use of keystream and nonces.
Nor does a resource fail in mid-operation: random bits are drawn only by instructions that transfer no data (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-atomic-randomness>>), and the clock is read only when an instruction starts or resumes (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-expiration-date>>).
A management operation is prefix-complete too: between its instructions the locker is in a Configuration State, in which the CC in transit can only be transferred, saved by a nested export, completed or discarded, and never used, cloned or restricted (<<KLE-Decision-management-protocol>>, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>).
Key material is all-or-nothing: it enters a field in one uninterruptible step of exactly the field's length, and a shared secret enters a message input whole, in one such step.

The KLE unit may run asynchronously, but the hart never observes it: a conflicting access waits for completion or a precise halt.
Memory instructions are RISC-V memory instructions, allowed only on idempotent memory; the memory model gains no new kind of access. A visible prefix is never replayed: an instruction restarts from zero only before any of its accesses has become visible.
No instruction takes a potentially unbounded input, and every wait, retry and restart has a documented bound, past which the implementation progresses or reports the condition with the _State_ unchanged.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>> (leg 2), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-memory-program-order>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-no-data>> (interruptibility), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> (interruption points, transfer size), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>> (halt points), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> (key item), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>> (temporary failure); for management operations, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>.

== Design Decisions

A _Design Decision_ is a mechanism that two or more Principles select together, and from which a group of rules follows. It is not a Principle: another mechanism could satisfy the same Principles. It is stated separately because the rules derive more easily from it.

[[KLE-Decision-metadata,D1]]
D1: _The Metadata Header_::
+
--
A locker is described by a 128-bit header carried with its CC, the MDH, which alone determines what the locker is, what it admits and how large it is. It is the architected description that <<KLE-Principle-management-architectural>> requires, it names the algorithm as <<KLE-Principle-encoding-pressure>> asks, and it is one fixed-size header rather than a table or a set of registers (<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>). Its layout minimizes the lookahead to identify a locker (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>): _Machine_, its policies, _State_ and _StateExtension_ lie in the first 32 bits, and the fields that fix every length in the first 64, so that one GPR on RV32 identifies the locker and two size it (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-length-rule>>).

Hence the _State_ field alone identifies the points software must recognize; an Error-State locker _is_ its MDH (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>); an _Unconfigured_ locker reads as the one MDH value that is not an MDH; a management operation restores the MDH it was given, up to prescribed normalizations; and implementation-defined progress lives outside the Content (<<KLE-Decision-resumption>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-state-identifies-cc>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-locker-is-only-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-drawn-values>>.
--

[[KLE-Decision-machine-protocol,D2]]
D2: _The Machine protocol_::
+
--
With active CCs (<<KLE-Principle-contexts-stateful>>) and two generic instructions (<<KLE-Principle-encoding-pressure>>), every Machine follows one protocol, so that one routine shape serves all (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>).
`kl.setst` names the next State, or an operation on the current one, and `kl.exec` performs the operation of the current State; the architecture and each Machine's specification say which transitions exist and which usage instructions each Valid State admits (<<KLE-Principle-no-misuse>>).

// Two conventions complete the protocol: re-entering the current State is admitted unless the implemented standard gives it no meaning, and the completion of a management operation leaves the locker in the State its MDH names, a fresh provisioning in _Ready_.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transition-triggers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-state-after-setst>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-to-ready>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>>.
--

[[KLE-Decision-instruction-outcome,D3]]
D3: _The outcome of an instruction on a locker_::
+
--
When an instruction cannot do what it is asked, its outcome depends only on the State group of the locker and the class of the instruction.
Unless it raises an exception (<<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>), it falls through: it performs no operation (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-no-operation>>) and zeroes its output, and only the _State_ differs, which moves to _Invalid_ on a misused Valid locker (<<KLE-Decision-misuse>>) and is unchanged on an inert one (<<KLE-Decision-fall-through>>).
With <<KLE-Decision-exceptions>> for the traps, <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> for the Configuration States and <<KLE-Principle-availability>> for the configuration instructions, the two parts below select one rule, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>>: its leg 1 is <<KLE-Decision-fall-through>> and its leg 3 <<KLE-Decision-misuse>>; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-gate-order>> orders the checks within each leg.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnl-no-named-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, and the summary table <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-insns-on-unconf-or-error-locker>>.

[[KLE-Decision-misuse,D3(a)]]
D3(a): _Algorithm misuse invalidates a Valid locker_:::
The locker fails closed (<<KLE-Principle-no-misuse>>) and records the cause (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>): an instruction forbidden by the Machine's sequencing or _MachinePolicy_ rules, a narrowing that disables the current operation, or a permanent RBG failure moves it to _Invalid_, whereas a _UsagePolicy_ denial traps, the locker unchanged (<<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>), and a failed narrowing of a restricted transfer gives _Privilege Violation_.
The locker then stays inert (<<KLE-Decision-fall-through>>), with no history kept, until reconfigured (<<KLE-Principle-availability>>).
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure>>, the endpoint definitions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-derivation-rules>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>> (last sentence), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>> (permanent failure), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-state-after-setst>>.

[[KLE-Decision-fall-through,D3(b)]]
D3(b): _Inert lockers let instructions fall through_:::
A locker becomes inert asynchronously, when a handler clears or invalidates it (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>): the instructions that follow must neither trap nor act on something else, and the original error must stay readable (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>), so that one check at the end of a sequence suffices (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>).
Every instruction other than a destructive one therefore falls through with the _State_ unchanged: a transfer leaves `klstart` as an empty window would, and an inspection reads what is there, zeros or the MDH.
A destructive instruction is issued to destroy the error, and `kl.swap` keeps it readable in the other locker; a `kl.setst` that replaces an Error State counts as destructive, since it overwrites essentially all the locker holds.
As `klstart` does not advance on an inert locker, a transfer sequence is bounded by the caller's own count (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-no-klstart-loop>>).
A locker in a Configuration State falls under <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> and <<KLE-Decision-exceptions>> instead.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zero-unwritten-output>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnl-no-named-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnl-openings>>, bullet 2 of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, inspection part of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, the States paragraph of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>>.

[[KLE-Decision-error-architecture,D4]]
D4: _The error architecture_::
+
--
Algorithms in use at the same time fail independently (<<KLE-Principle-multiple-algorithms>>), an error must travel with its CC through context switches, exports and imports (<<KLE-Principle-persistence>>), and one check per locker must suffice (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>).
Each CC therefore records its own errors, with no global error register (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>), and only what falling through would misreport traps (<<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>); either way, a handler or the locker's owner acts on the cause without decoding the instruction or reconstructing the sequence.
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>.

[[KLE-Decision-error-states,D4(a)]]
D4(a): _An error is recorded as an Error State_:::
A failure is recorded in the _State_ of the locker whose precondition failed, as an Error State naming the class of the cause: _Invalid_ for sequencing or a _MachinePolicy_ gate, _Privilege Violation_ for a narrowing or a binding, _Unsupported_ for Metadata, _Expired_ for the clock, _Authentication Failed_ for a seal.
A negative result of the algorithm, such as a rejected signature, is not an error (_Failure_).
An Error State is a value of the _State_ field, so an Error-State locker is its MDH alone (<<KLE-Decision-metadata>>), and the choice fixes the format of the MDH and of the exported image: the locker holds nothing else, and its Content and ADS are released on entry (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>).
Being only an MDH, it is exported and imported as one, without a management operation; a handler that cannot resolve a condition records it by setting the Error State itself.
+
*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-state-transfer>>, the error list of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-architecture>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>> (which locker transitions), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-narrowing>> (_Privilege Violation_), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-rbg-failure>> (_Invalid_, not _Failure_).

[[KLE-Decision-exceptions,D4(b)]]
D4(b): _Exceptions are raised only for error conditions that the fall-through mechanism would misreport_:::
There are four: a malformed or reserved encoding, a programming error that an illegal-instruction exception signals; a sequencing error that would silently lose a CC (<<KLE-Principle-persistence>>); a use of unconfigured infrastructure, as opposed to an inert locker; and a policy violation, which the policy owner must see, not the caller (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>).
Their state-independent checks precede every condition on the locker, and the others, _UsagePolicy_ included, come in the order of Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-gate-order>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>: an inert locker falls through unchecked, while a Configuration State traps.
The other exceptions name a condition that a more privileged handler may resolve, by emulating a Machine, freeing memory, granting a right, or supplying a clock or entropy; Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-handler-duty>> says what a handler must do with one it cannot.
+
*Rules.* First half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnl-openings>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-mgmt-klnamedlocker-check>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-outcome-by-state>> (the trap legs), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>> (the address-misaligned exception), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-iobuf-configured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>>, <<Zkl-ISA-priv.adoc#KLE-exception-codes>>; outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-handler-duty>>.
--

[[KLE-Decision-management-protocol,D5]]
D5: _The management operation protocol_::
+
--
A management operation is one operation spanning several instructions (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>): a `kl.mgmt` opens it, transfer instructions move the image, a `kl.mgmt` closes it, and between them the locker is in a Configuration State. The opening for provisioning or import is a destructive configuration instruction: it is admitted in every State (<<KLE-Principle-availability>>) and zeroizes the locker and frees its resources right after the state-independent checks (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>), which also lets the capacity check count the locker's own allocation as free, so that a CC can be reconfigured in place. The named locker names the operation in flight (<<KLE-Decision-named-locker>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>> (transfers on a cleared locker), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-opening-locker-when>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config>>, the `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-locker-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-nested-mgmt>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klnamedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.
--

[[KLE-Decision-named-locker,D6]]
D6: _The named locker architecture_::
+
--
The authentication and identification registers `SIV`, `IMPQUAL` and `SIV2` exist once per hart (<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>), so software, not hardware, keeps one management operation in flight at a time: it completes or discards one before managing another locker or performing cryptographic operations, or saves it by a nested export (Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-save-shared-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-no-interleaving>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-nested-export-first>>).
`klnamedlocker` holds the number of the locker of the CC in transit, so that a management routine needs no locker number (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>). The number follows the CC when it moves, is kept when the operation completes or fails so that the outcome stays readable, and otherwise changes as Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>> lists, or as software writes it.

The management and inspection instructions take the locker index, because one routine written for it replaces 32 written for fixed lockers (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>); so do `kl.exec` and `kl.setst`, and `kl.clone`, `kl.rename`, `kl.swap` and `kl.derive` for one of their two lockers, so that cryptographic code, too, can work on a locker chosen at run time.

With <<KLE-Decision-fall-through>> and <<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>: a `kl.mgmt` naming a locker other than the named one traps, even after completion; naming the named locker when there is none falls through for every instruction but an opening, which has nothing to open and traps.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>> (no usage while in transit), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>> (because `klnamedlocker` is KLE state).
--

[[KLE-Decision-resumption,D7]]
D7: _The resumption architecture_::
+
--
Since every operation is atomic or prefix-complete (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>) and the hart keeps one progress register (<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>), `klstart` records the prefix-complete point of whichever resumable instruction is in flight, in bytes, and `vstart` the same point in elements where a vector operand is involved (for `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout`, `klstart` is the offset into the locker image instead). Interruption points are of two kinds. Those the architecture defines, per instruction and per Machine State, are part of the CC: `klstart` and the State record them, and a restored CC resumes from them (<<KLE-Principle-persistence>>). Those an implementation defines, inside a long-running operation, are not: the operation records its progress in _MachineUse_ and the ADS, so that it is architected and not hidden (<<KLE-Principle-management-architectural>>), but an import, a clone or another implementation may discard it and restart from the Content.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-klstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>> (memory exceptions), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-long-running-drawn-values>>, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-progress-discard>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klstart-no-reset>>.
--

[[KLE-Decision-derivation,D8]]
D8: _The derivation architecture_::
+
--
Combining algorithms (<<KLE-Principle-multiple-algorithms>>) while no key leaves the unit in clear (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>) requires a transfer between lockers inside KLE.
`kl.derive` moves material from a source endpoint to a destination endpoint, a field or the `kl.exec` output or input that a Machine lists for a State; one instruction serves every pair, the endpoints being Machine data rather than opcodes (<<KLE-Principle-encoding-pressure>>).
A transfer is restricted unless <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> exempts it, as it exempts a transfer of `kl.exec` output, a shortcut for instructions that software could issue anyway.
A shared secret may feed a key field, or, whole and in one step, the message input of a hash function, HMAC, KMAC or XOF, but not that of CMAC (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>>, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-derive-endpoints>>).
A failed check is recorded on the locker whose condition failed, on both for a pair that is not admitted (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>).
A transfer proceeds in units that commit at both endpoints at once, the narrowing with the first; a key and its initialization form one uninterruptible unit (Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-narrowing>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-derivation-rules>>: the endpoint definitions and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-checks>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-derive>>; in the Machines, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-derive-endpoints>>.

[[KLE-Principles-residue]]
== What the Principles do not determine

The choices below are constrained by the Principles and Design Decisions but not fixed by them, and remain rules in their own right: <<KLE-residue-GR>> lists those of the General Rules, <<KLE-residue-other>> the most important of the other normative statements of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-unpriv>>.

[[KLE-residue-GR]]
.Choices of the General Rules
[cols="<1,<2,<2",options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Choice | Why, and what the Principles leave open
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-mgmt-klnamedlocker-check>> | A `kl.mgmt` that names a locker explicitly traps whenever `klnamedlocker` names another locker, even one whose operation has completed. | <<KLE-Decision-named-locker>> leaves the exact check open. Testing `klnamedlocker` alone keeps it independent of the _State_ (<<KLE-Decision-exceptions>>), at the cost of Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-klnamedlocker-finish>>: software writes 32, or a saved value, once it has read the outcome.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-klnamedlocker-updates>> | `klnamedlocker` is kept on completion. | <<KLE-Decision-named-locker>> would also allow resetting it to 32. Keeping it lets the closing `kl.mgmt` be followed by `kl.getst Xd, KLNL`, which a reset would make read 0; software then writes 32, or a saved value.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-custom-locality>> | _ChipFamScrt_ is the weakest binding admitted for custom Metadata. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> asks only for a binding.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-same-state>> | A same-State `kl.setst` repeats the entry operations, unless the Machine forbids it. | <<KLE-Principle-no-misuse>> rules out a no-op, which would let later `kl.exec` instructions act on state that the re-entry was expected to reset, but forbidding every re-entry would satisfy it too. In a State that loads a field, repeating restarts the load (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-load-long-field>>); a Machine forbids it where its standard does not define the result.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>> | A `kl.restrictl` or `kl.restrictv` that would disable the operation of the current State invalidates the locker at once. | Invalidating it at the first instruction that relies on the operation would also satisfy <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> and <<KLE-Decision-misuse>>. Invalidating at once keeps every Valid State consistent with its policy, so that Machines check the policy only on entering a State (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-MGR-policy-gate>>), and records the error where it was caused (<<KLE-Decision-error-states>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>> | An Error-State image is the MDH alone, neither encrypted nor authenticated, and its import checks only the _State_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-error-state-transfer>>). | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> protects secret material and the policies bound to it, and an Error-State locker has neither. Anyone can create one with any MDH, by provisioning a locker and setting an Error State, so authentication would prove nothing.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-management-check-order>> | The address-misaligned exception is raised on an empty window, but not when `klnamedlocker` = 32; the state-independent checks are made in a fixed order. | Memory instructions follow the RISC-V conventions (<<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>>), under which alignment depends on the address alone. <<KLE-Decision-exceptions>> requires only that the state-independent checks precede the conditions on the locker, not their order.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-completion-state>> | Provisioning ends in _Ready_, with _StateExtension_ and _MachineUse_ zero. | A convention of the protocol (<<KLE-Decision-machine-protocol>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-shared-secret>> | A shared secret enters a key field, or, whole and in one uninterruptible step, the message input of a hash function, HMAC, KMAC or XOF; no other message input, CMAC's included. | <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> keeps the secret confidential, and the caller may know the key of the destination and absorb data of its choice before and after the secret. Recovering the secret from the output of a hash function, HMAC, KMAC or XOF then needs a preimage, provided that the whole secret enters at once: a prefix, a suffix or an interrupted transfer would let the caller finalize the hash and search the secret a few bytes at a time. CMAC, which is not one-way under a known key, is excluded (<<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLE-derive-endpoints>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> | The size arithmetic of derivations: `min`, zero-fill, divisibility of granularities, no truncated output units. | <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> requires only prefix-completeness and all-or-nothing keys.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-idempotent-memory>> | Any granularity, order and atomicity of component accesses; an access fault with `xtval` at the offending byte, re-execution faulting before any access. | RISC-V conventions for memory instructions; <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> requires only prefix-completeness at halts.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-exception-state-change>> | Every `kl.mgmt`, not only an opening one, clears `klstart` once it passes its `klnamedlocker` check, even if it then traps. | <<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>> requires only that an opening `kl.mgmt` clear `klstart`. Clearing it before the State-dependent checks gives every later outcome the same `klstart`, as zeroizing first does for the locker; keeping it when the `klnamedlocker` check fails protects the operation in flight from an interleaved `kl.mgmt`.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>> | The KLIOBUF variants of `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout` are uninterruptible although they transfer several blocks. | <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> requires halts only at block boundaries, which the GPR variants, transferring one block, and the vector variants, halting between blocks, satisfy. The KLIOBUF variants (<<KLE-Principle-scalable>>) could have been resumable, and are not because `klmaxiobuflen` bounds their length.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-single-skid>> | One SKID per Machine, not one per key. | A choice of granularity for the reference that <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> requires in place of the key.
|===

[[KLE-residue-other]]
.Choices of other normative statements
[cols="<1,<2,<2",options="header"]
|===
| Statement | Choice | Why, and what the Principles leave open
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-architectural-model>> | Locker state is accessible in clear only to M-mode and authenticated Debug mode, and only M-mode may emulate KLE. | The Principles say what software may never gain (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>), not which software is trusted: the architecture places M-mode, which may program the CSK, and authenticated Debug mode in the trusted base.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-cryptographic-registers>>, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-clear-before-lower-mode>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-save-shared-state>> | The lockers are shared by all privilege modes of a hart; isolating them between modes is the duty of software. | <<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>> argues against a set of lockers per mode. The _UsagePolicy_ gates usage (<<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>>), but configuration instructions are ungated (<<KLE-Principle-availability>>), so a less privileged mode could clear, replace or export any resident locker; protecting it is left to the more privileged software.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-KLF>> | Neither a locker nor its SCC resists rollback: an earlier SCC can always be re-imported. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> asks for confidentiality and integrity, not freshness, which would need per-CC state outside the locker (<<KLE-Principle-small-architectural-state>>). Re-import has legitimate uses, such as restarting an operation whose payload was corrupted, and exploiting it needs control of the target software.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-UsagePolicy>> | Bits 0–3 deny a mode and bit 4 grants Debug mode, so a zero field admits every mode but Debug; `mstatus.MPRV` is ignored. | <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> requires that the policy gate usage and only narrow; it fixes neither the default nor which mode counts as the issuer. With this polarity, narrowing ORs the deny bits and ANDs the Debug grant.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Localities>> | A Locality binds only the sealed CC: a CC in a locker stays usable after a Locality Secret it names changes, and its next export binds to the new value. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> requires the seal to carry the binding, and leaves open whether a resident locker is bound too. Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-clear-before-lower-mode>> closes the gap: software clears its lockers before a less privileged mode runs.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-expiration-date>> | _ExpirationDate_ is evaluated only when a usage instruction issues or resumes, and only in a Valid State. | The Principles say nothing about time.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Auxiliary-Data-Section>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-import>> | _ADSDropped_ is an unauthenticated bit of the MDH, and an ADS that fails its check is dropped, with the implementation-defined subfields of the MDH, without rejecting the SCC. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> asks that the MDH be authenticated, but this bit can only drop the implementation-specific part, which <<KLE-Principle-management-architectural>> lets an implementation discard at any time at the cost of a restart, so authenticating it would protect nothing. Unauthenticated, it lets a nested export preserve an import after `Content2` was dropped.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Machine-version>> | System-specific formats exist for import only, need not be encrypted or authenticated, and a CC imported from one is exported only as an SCC. | <<KLE-Principle-persistence>> requires one implementation-independent sealed format; importing other formats lets existing key stores feed the lockers. The result only narrows its declared policies and is bound to the chip (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-custom-locality>>); the protection of the input is left to the format.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-klstart>> | A `klstart` at or past the end of the transfer window makes the window empty, without error; one inside the window that is not an interruption point invalidates the locker. | <<KLE-Principle-atomicity-asynchrony>> requires resumption only from points the instruction can produce. An out-of-window value is treated as a completed instruction, as `vstart` {ge} `vl` is in RVV; an in-window value that is not an interruption point is misuse (<<KLE-Decision-misuse>>), which could, for instance, release plaintext in place of ciphertext.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-CSR-klstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-mv>> | `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout` keep `klstart` across instructions as an offset into the image, instead of clearing it on retirement. | <<KLE-Decision-resumption>> makes `klstart` the progress of one instruction. These transfers use it as the cursor of the whole transfer, so that a sequence needs no offset operand (<<KLE-Principle-small-instructions-code>>); hence `klstart` and `vstart` denote different points for them, and a sequence cannot loop on `klstart` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-no-klstart-loop>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-destructive-config>> | `kl.clear` and `kl.clearall` need only release the Content; zeroizing it at once is a recommendation. | <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> forbids returning stale secrets, and the KLF protects what it stores. Since the opening `kl.mgmt` zeroizes a locker before reusing it, zeroizing at once only shortens the time the secrets remain in the KLF.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-instruction-restrict>> | A request weaker than the current policy invalidates the locker for _MachinePolicy_, sets _Privilege Violation_ for _Locality_, and is ignored for _ExpirationDate_. | <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> requires only that no request widen a policy, not what a request that tries does. _ExpirationDate_ keeps the earlier date, as narrowing does (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-Metadata-narrowing>>); _Locality_, which narrowing would also keep, and _MachinePolicy_ report the attempt instead.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-GR-gate-order>> | On a Valid locker, a forbidden KLIOBUF substitution is checked before the _UsagePolicy_, and an unconfigured KLIOBUF after the _ExpirationDate_. | <<KLE-Decision-exceptions>> orders only the state-independent checks before the others, and <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> puts the _UsagePolicy_ before the _ExpirationDate_, so that an excluded mode cannot expire the locker. The place of the two KLIOBUF grounds (<<KLE-Principle-scalable>>), which depend on the _State_, is the Rule's choice.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-data-formats>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-locker-management>> | A PCCC is protected only as its base image is: a PI-shaped one is the clear PI, and a nested completion checks only the base type. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> protects a CC, and a partial provisioning is not yet one: it holds only what software supplied. Sealing it would add a third sealed format; the ciphertext of an SCC-shaped PCCC is authenticated when its base operation completes, and Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SR-clear-before-lower-mode>> keeps a clear one from a less privileged mode.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-system-keys>> | The SKS resolves a SKID whatever the issuing mode; only the policies it associates with the key restrict its use. | <<KLE-Principle-no-privilege-elevation>> requires that a system key never leave and that its policies be inherited. Making its resolution depend on the mode would restrict provisioning, which <<KLE-Principle-availability>> forbids; protecting a system key is thus the duty of whoever configures the SKS.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLE-SCC-AEAD>> | Sealing is deterministic: with no nonce, the same CC sealed twice under the same CSK and Localities gives the same SCC. | <<KLE-Principle-confidentiality-integrity>> asks that the SCC reveal nothing of the secret material; deterministic encryption reveals only whether two SCCs seal the same CC. In exchange the SCC is shorter and the derived keys can be cached; GCM-SIV keeps the construction secure up to about 2^64^ observed blocks.
|===
