= Appendix: Foundational Principles of KLEE

_This chapter is informative and non-normative._

This chapter states fourteen Design Principles, and six Design Decisions taken on the basis of the Principles, from which all Rules of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-unpriv>> follow, from Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>> onward.
The Principles name no individual instruction: each rule should read as one Principle applied to one instruction or State class.
Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-multiple-GPRs>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, which state operand conventions, and the SR rules are also Design Decisions and are not covered.

== Vocabulary

The Principles use only the following categories, all defined in the specification.

*State groups.*

* _Unconfigured_: the locker holds no CC.
* _Valid_ States: the locker holds a usable CC. _Ready_, _Success_ and _Failure_ are the architected ones.
* _Error_ States: the locker holds only an MDH, which records why its CC became unusable.
* _Configuration_ States: the CC is in transit, being provisioned, imported or exported.
* _Complete_ = Valid ∪ Error. _Inert_ = Unconfigured ∪ Error, plus the case of no locker at all (`klmanagedlocker` = 32).

*Instruction classes* (from <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-instruction-properties>> in Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>).

* _Usage_ instructions operate on the CC through the Machine; _UsagePolicy_ gates them.
** _Derivations_ are usage instructions with two lockers, gated on each.
* _Configuration_ instructions operate on the locker as a container; _UsagePolicy_ does not gate them. They are of three kinds:
** _inspection_: read the MDH or a size, change nothing;
** _destructive_: zeroize or overwrite the locker without reading it;
** _transfer_: move a CC, or part of it, unchanged, between lockers or between a locker and memory or registers: the management transfers (`kl.load`, `kl.store`, `kl.mvin`, `kl.mvout`), `kl.rename` and `kl.swap`, and the closing of a management operation.
* _Memory_ instructions have a memory operand. The class cuts across the others: `kl.load` and `kl.store` are both transfer and memory instructions.

*The managed locker* is the locker whose CC is in transit. A pointer names it, not a locker number.

== Design Principles

The fourteen Principles fall into five groups.

* _Transparency_: the architecture describes all the state there is (<<KLEE-Principle-1>>). It precedes the definition of a locker.
* _Economy_: simple instructions and short code, then the least encoding space and the least architectural state (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>, <<KLEE-Principle-3>>, <<KLEE-Principle-4>>). The mechanism that <<KLEE-Principle-1>>, <<KLEE-Principle-3>> and <<KLEE-Principle-4>> lead to, the Metadata Header, is the Design Decision <<KLEE-decision-1>>; the one that <<KLEE-Principle-2>> and <<KLEE-Principle-4>> lead to, the managed locker, is <<KLEE-decision-5>>.
* _Security_: what software may never gain, what it may always do, and what it keeps (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>–<<KLEE-Principle-8>>): the confidentiality of the keys, the availability of the lockers, and the persistence, confidentiality and integrity of the CCs. <<KLEE-Principle-5>> gives the other Principles their purpose.
* _Structure_: how a Machine is driven (<<KLEE-Principle-9>>) and how an operation executes (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>).
* _Outcomes_: what happens when an instruction cannot do what it is asked, one Principle per State group, and how a failure is reported (<<KLEE-Principle-11>>–<<KLEE-Principle-14>>); <<KLEE-Principle-11>> is also how the system defends itself, <<KLEE-Principle-12>> one way to implement <<KLEE-Principle-2>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-1,P1]]
P1: _The entire resource management is architectural_::
+
--
The architecture, not the implementation, manages every resource that software relies on: a resource exists in an architected place, with an architected state that software can manage, and the architecture alone decides when it is available.

Hence there is no hidden state necessary to make the feature meet specific use cases.

*Rationale.*  Whereas other architectures may rely on caching to make more than one key available to software, KLEE makes locker handling entirely architectural.  KLEE exposes its state machines and resources instead (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CC-management>>), letting software manage them, and as result the implementation is simpler.

*Consequences.* There are 32 lockers, each with its MDH always present. The KLF capacity for Content is implementation-defined, but its exhaustion is an architected exception at the instruction that needs it, never a silent eviction; lack of memory is the only reason for which the KLF refuses a request, and otherwise it complies. Context switching saves and restores the architected state. Whatever an implementation keeps beyond the MDH and the Content, the ADS included, can be discarded at any time without changing an architected outcome: the architected behavior of a locker never depends on state outside its MDH and Content. Such state is identified by architected registers, so that an import can tell whether it is usable (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CSR-impl-ids>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-KLF>>, the out-of-memory item of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-architecture>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; among the General Rules, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (progress outside the Content) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>> (implementation-bound Metadata).
--

[[KLEE-Principle-2,P2]]
P2: _Make instructions as simple as possible, while still requiring short code sequences_::
+
--
Instructions are as simple as the RISC-V philosophy asks, and the code that uses them is short too: whatever a routine would otherwise repeat in every instruction, or check after every instruction, becomes architectural state or a single check. Two cases matter most. The number of the locker a management routine works on is held in an architectural register, so the routine carries no locker number and the same code serves every locker. The outcomes of asynchronous events are shaped so that one check at the end of a sequence suffices.

*Rationale.* Management and context-switch code runs in handlers and libraries: written once, audited once, it must not grow with the number of lockers or of steps. Moving a repeated operand or check into the architecture costs one CSR and one read-out, and removes a class of software errors. Hardware complexity is added only where it brings the largest saving: a management routine that works on any locker, instead of one instance per locker, is 32 times less code, whereas cryptographic code can address fixed lockers and needs no such help.

*Consequences.* <<KLEE-decision-5>>, with <<KLEE-Principle-4>>: the managed-locker pointer, its implicit and `KLML` operands, and the instructions that read and write it. The "check once" property of <<KLEE-Principle-12>>: an instruction that falls through never traps and never acts elsewhere, so a routine checks the _State_ only after its last step, through the pointer, without knowing the locker number. One instruction clears every locker and the buffer. The KLIOBUF substitutes for vector operands, so one code path serves harts with and without vectors.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-override>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, the NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and the NOTE after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>; outside the GR series, the substitution rules of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-usage-input-output>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-3,P3]]
P3: _Minimize the pressure on the encoding space_::
+
--
Instead of defining instructions for each cryptographic primitive, variant and mode of operation, KLEE defines a few generic instructions and encodes the algorithm, its mode and its parameters in the data, the MDH: one `kl.exec` and one `kl.setst` serve every Machine, present and future, and a new Machine needs a new _Machine_ value, never a new opcode.

*Rationale.* Opcode space is the scarcest resource of an ISA, and cryptographic algorithms are many and short-lived compared with an ISA. Binding them to the Metadata keeps the instruction set fixed while the set of Machines grows, and it is what lets every Machine follow one protocol (<<KLEE-Principle-9>>).

*Consequences.* The _Machine_ and ancillary fields of the MDH name the algorithm, and the immediate of `kl.setst` names the State to enter or the operation to perform on the current one, so that `kl.setst` and `kl.exec` alone essentially drive every state machine. The remaining instructions manage lockers, not algorithms: they clear, inspect, move, restrict and transfer lockers, and none of them depends on the Machine.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-Machine-field>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-management-immeds>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-instruction-properties>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-4,P4]]
P4: _Additional architectural state beyond the lockers is kept to a minimum_::
+
--
State that an operation needs only while it is under way exists once per hart, not once per locker. The authentication and implementation-identification registers `SIV`, `IMPQUAL` and `SIV2` are the main case: one set per hart serves every locker, at the price of one small CSR, `klmanagedlocker`, that names the locker they belong to (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>). The progress counter `klstart` and, with `Zklio`, the KLIOBUF follow the same rule. The remaining CSRs, unprivileged and privileged, configure or identify the KLEE unit and are not per-operation state.

*Rationale.* Every added register is state that a context switch must save and restore, that interposed software must preserve, and that may leak between contexts. Authentication and implementation-identification registers require, depending on which features are implemented, 128 or 384 bits. Adding them to each locker is expensive. One instance of each, shared by all lockers, reduces the hardware cost.
Suitable save-and-restore instruction sequences can be tied to `klmanagedlocker`, reducing also the cost of software.

*Consequences.* One set of authentication registers means at most one management operation per hart in flight, the premise of <<KLEE-decision-5>>: one pointer names its locker, a second operation on another locker must first save the first by a nested export, and management operations are not interleaved with each other or with cryptographic operations. One `klstart` means that every instruction honoring it starts from the value it finds, that an opening `kl.mgmt` clears it, and that interposed software saves and restores it. The authentication registers and the pointer are shared, so touching them while an operation is in flight is a conflicting access. A long-running operation records its progress in its locker, in _MachineUse_ and the ADS, not in a register of its own.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>> (through <<KLEE-decision-5>>), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> (`klstart` cleared by `kl.mgmt`), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-save-shared-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-restore>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klmanagedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-5,P5]]
P5: _Privileges are never elevated_::
+
--
No instruction yields a key, a locker or an output with more rights than its inputs and its issuer already hold, except where it is the purpose of the Machine (for instance, generating public encrypted output using a secret key). Three consequences cover the rules.

_No extraction._ Once a key is in a CC, it cannot be extracted in clear: it is used only through its Machine, no instruction reads it out, and an exported CC is sealed. Whoever obtains the CC obtains the right to use the key under its policies, never the key.

_Usage is gated._ An instruction exercises only the rights that the _UsagePolicy_ grants to the issuing mode; exercising any other is a privilege violation. A mode may use or alter only the lockers that its policies admit, which is why an instruction that can change the cryptographic state of a locker, the progress of its operation included, is gated; it may always narrow, copy or destroy them (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>), which changes no operation and elevates nothing.

_Secrets flow only downhill._ Material derived from a secret inherits every constraint of the secret: it moves only into a locker whose policies, after narrowing, are at least as strict as the source's. Narrowing never widens a policy, raises a protection level or changes a Machine policy. A transfer is exempt from narrowing exactly when the caller could obtain its result with admitted instructions anyway, so the exemption adds no attack surface. A secret bound to the system never leaves it, and a bound key field is never replaced from outside.

Secrets do not linger. When a CC is destroyed, invalidated or restarted, the material that depended on it is released or zeroized; a destructive configuration instruction zeroizes before any check that could trap, so no stale copy survives in the KLF.

_Nothing stale is returned._ An output that is not produced reads as zero: an instruction that performs no operation, or stops in an Error State, zeroes the part of its output window it has not written, and an inert locker reads as zeros, so that neither an input nor an earlier output can pass for a result.

*Rationale.* This invariant is what makes least privilege enforceable: policies granted at provisioning and narrowed later can be relied upon only if no operation can widen or bypass them. For derived material, NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 §6.2.1 and SP 800-108r1 §8 require exactly this inheritance.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-concepts>> (no extraction), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-narrowing>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>>, the paragraphs after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-unrestricted>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-DRBG-implies-unrestricted>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>>; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> (zeroizing step) and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (restart) for the paragraph on lingering; <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> (zeros) for the last.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-6,P6]]
P6: _It must always be possible to configure new Cryptographic Contexts_::
+
--
Configuration instructions are admitted in every State group, and neither a Machine nor a _UsagePolicy_ may restrict them: even when all lockers are in use, a caller can reconfigure any of them. In particular, the return to _Ready_ and the exits from an Error State are always open, and a locker in a Configuration State can be cleared, re-provisioned or re-imported like any other.

*Rationale.* Avoiding any denial of service on the KLEE resources.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-opening-locker-when>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-triggers>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-to-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>> (first paragraph), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clearads>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>> (last sentence), `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-7,P7]]
P7: _Cryptographic Contexts persist: they can be stored and reloaded to survive context switches, power cycles, process and VM migrations,_::
+
--
A CC is not bound to the locker that holds it, nor to the hart, the power state or the machine: it can be saved from a locker and restored into any one, so that it survives a context switch, sleep and hibernation, and the migration of a virtual machine. What is saved is the whole CC, its MDH and Content, and restoring it yields a locker that behaves as the saved one did, including an operation interrupted at an architected interruption point; an interruption an an implementation-defined point need not survive, and the operation can always restart (<<KLEE-decision-6>>).

*Rationale.* Persistence is the counterpart to <<KLEE-Principle-6>> that makes the KLEE architecture so powerful: a CC evicted from a locker using the architecturally provided means to make room is not destroyed, only saved. Only the consumer of a CC (or a higher privileged piece of software) can decide when a CC is destroyed.

*Consequences.* The SCCs, and the mechanisms to export them from lockers and import them into lockers.
The format is defined independently of the implementation and of the ISA, so that it can be restored elsewhere (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC>>, <<Zkl-books.adoc#KLEE-separation>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-export>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-import>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-state-save-and-restore-order>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-isolation>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-8,P8]]
P8: _Cryptographic Contexts keep their confidentiality and integrity outside a locker_::
+
--
A CC outside a locker reveals nothing of its secret material and cannot be altered undetected: it is encrypted under a key that only the KLEE unit holds, and it is authenticated together with its MDH. The sealing process can be bound to a locality, a chip, a chip family or a system, and is then restorable only there.

*Rationale.* Persistence (<<KLEE-Principle-7>>) moves a CC through memory, storage and networks that the KLEE unit does not control; the guarantees of <<KLEE-Principle-5>> hold outside a locker only if the saved form carries them.

*Consequences.* Export seals the CC with an AEAD under a key derived from a locality secret and binds the MDH as associated data; import verifies the seal before anything is restored, and a failed verification leaves an Error State, not a usable locker. Metadata that only the issuing implementation can interpret is bound to that implementation.
// A locality secret must not be withdrawn while a CC bound to it may still be exported.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SCC-AEAD>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-Localities>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>; outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-locality-unconfigure>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-9,P9]]
P9: _All Machines follow one state-machine protocol_::
+
--
Software drives every Machine the same way: a state-setting usage instruction names the next State, or an operation on the current one; an execute usage instruction performs the operation of the current State. The architecture and the specification of each Machine specify which transitions exist and which usage instructions are admitted in each Valid State.

// Two conventions complete the protocol: re-entering the current State is admitted unless the implemented standard gives it no meaning, and the completion of a management operation leaves the locker in the State its MDH names, a fresh provisioning in _Ready_.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-triggers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clearads>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-10,P10]]
P10: _Every operation is atomic or prefix-complete with atomic component operations and bounded progress, and asynchrony is invisible_::
+
--
An _uninterruptible_ instruction completes, has no effect, or traps with only the effects that <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>> lists. A _resumable_ instruction processes its input serially and halts only at a prefix-complete point, recorded in `klstart`, where it has committed exactly what a shorter instruction performing the completed prefix would commit. Its units, blocks or granules, are atomic: each is performed fully or not at all, although the memory accesses that implement it need not be (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>). A precise halt does not retire the instruction; a transition to an Error State after partial progress does. A committed prefix is never recomputed: prefix-completeness protects not only the consistency of the state but the one-time use of keystream and nonces, which re-running a prefix from the committed state would break.

The KLEE unit may execute asynchronously, but the issuing hart never observes it: a conflicting access waits for the instruction to complete or halt precisely. Memory instructions are RISC-V memory instructions in every respect, and touch only idempotent memory, so that a prefix can be replayed.

A management operation spans several instructions, and the same holds for it: between its instructions the locker is in a Configuration State, the prefix-complete state of the operation, which nothing consumes partially (<<KLEE-decision-4>>).

Progress is bounded. No instruction processes a potentially unbounded input, as some software libraries and some CISC architectures do. Every wait, retry and restart has a documented bound, past which the implementation either progresses or reports the condition with the _State_ unchanged. Key material is all-or-nothing: it enters a field in one uninterruptible step of exactly the field's length, or not at all, so no locker is ever usable with a partly written key.

*Rationale.* Context save and restore need a state that the locker, `klstart`, `vstart` and the ADS describe completely; for a management operation, that state is the locker in its Configuration State. The memory model must not acquire a new kind of access. Halting and resuming is meaningful only if resumption eventually completes.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-program-order>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> (interruption points, transfer size), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>> (halt points), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>> (key item), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> (temporary failure); for management operations, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>.

[[KLEE-Principle-11,P11]]
P11: _Any misuse of a Valid locker makes it unavailable to software_::
+
--
A usage instruction that the Machine, the current State–if Valid–or the current policies do not admit on a locker performs no operation and moves the locker to _Invalid_; so does an unrecoverable failure of a resource the operation needs. The architecture keeps no history: once in an Error State, the locker is inert (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) until reconfigured (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>).

*Rationale.* Fail closed. Attempts to issue the wrong instruction on a live key may be an attack; the only safe outcome is to make the key unusable until software, which can always reconfigure, decides.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>> (second paragraph), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-same-state>> (last sentence), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> (permanent failure), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-state-after-setst>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-12,P12]]
P12: _Inert lockers let instructions fall through_::
+
--
On an inert locker, every instruction other than a destructive one performs no operation and raises no exception: the _State_ is unchanged and any output is zero. A transfer instruction leaves `klstart` as it would after a transfer with an empty window. Inspection instructions read what is there: zeros, or the MDH.

*Rationale.* A locker becomes inert asynchronously, when a handler clears or invalidates it. The instructions that follow must neither trap nor act on something else, and the original error must stay readable, so that one check at the end of the sequence suffices: fall-through is one way to implement <<KLEE-Principle-2>>. Since `klstart` does not advance on an inert locker, the length of a transfer sequence is the caller's own count, taken from the image size before the transfer, never `klstart` (Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-no-klstart-loop>>).

*Boundary.* <<KLEE-Principle-12>> governs lockers that hold nothing to act on. A misused Valid locker falls under <<KLEE-Principle-11>>, a locker in a Configuration State under <<KLEE-Principle-10>>.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-transition-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-openings>>, bullet 2 of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, second half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-can-be-reconfigured>>, inspection part of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-config-instructions-config-state>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-13,P13]]
P13: _Failures are reported by their cause, errors are recorded in the CC that caused them_::
+
--
Each CC, in particular each locker, keeps its own error state, independently of every other locker and of the hart: a failure of an operation is recorded in the _State_ field of the locker whose precondition failed, as an Error State that names the class of the cause, _Invalid_ for a sequencing error, _Privilege Violation_ for a policy, _Unsupported_ for a capability, _Expired_ for the clock, _Authentication Failed_ for a seal. An exception is raised instead only for a condition that a more privileged handler may resolve, by emulating a Machine, freeing memory, granting a right, or supplying a clock or entropy, and it names that condition; an illegal-instruction exception is reserved for programming errors.

*Rationale.* A handler, or the software that owns the locker, acts on the cause without decoding the instruction or reconstructing the sequence that led to it, and the error survives a context switch, an export and an import with the locker, since it is part of its MDH (<<KLEE-decision-1>>). Keeping the error per locker rather than per hart lets lockers fail independently: an error on one does not disturb an operation on another. The choice is fundamental and may be revisited; for the time being it fixes the format of the MDH and of the exported image, and simplifies both the implementation, which needs no separate error register, and the error handling, which needs one check per locker.

*Consequences.* The Error States are values of the _State_ field, and so part of the internal and the external format (<<KLEE-decision-3>>). A derivation records a failed check on the source, the destination or both, according to whose precondition failed. A failure of the environment is an error (_Invalid_), an outcome of the algorithm is not (_Failure_). The KLEE exception codes list conditions a handler can resolve, and Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-handler-duty>> says what a handler must do with one it cannot.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-architecture>>, <<Zkl-ISA-priv.adoc#KLEE-exception-codes>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>> (which locker transitions), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-narrowing>> (_Privilege Violation_), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> (_Invalid_, not _Failure_), <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-iobuf-configured>> (a resolvable condition); outside the GR series, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-handler-duty>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principle-14,P14]]
P14: _Exceptions are raised only for error conditions that the fall-through mechanism would misreport_::
+
--
An instruction traps only for an error condition that falling through would misreport. There are four: a malformed or reserved encoding; a sequencing error that would silently lose a CC; a use of unconfigured infrastructure, as opposed to an inert locker; and a policy violation, which the policy owner must see, not the caller. These checks precede every check that depends on the locker's _State_, so that fall-through (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) is never mistaken for an error.

*Rules.* First half of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-openings>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-mgmt-klmanagedlocker-check>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-iobuf-configured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-management-check-order>>.
--

== Design Decisions

A _Design Decision_ is a mechanism that two or more Principles select together, and from which a group of rules follows. It is not a Principle: another mechanism could satisfy the same Principles. It is stated separately because the rules derive more easily from it than from the Principles directly.

[[KLEE-decision-1,D1]]
D1: _The Metadata Header_::
+
--
A locker is described by a 128-bit header carried with its CC, the MDH, which alone determines what the locker is, what it admits and how large it is. It is the architected description that <<KLEE-Principle-1>> requires, it names the algorithm as <<KLEE-Principle-3>> asks, and it is one fixed-size header rather than a table or a set of registers (<<KLEE-Principle-4>>). Its layout minimizes the lookahead to identify a locker (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>): _Machine_, its policies, _State_ and _StateExtension_ lie in the first 32 bits, and the fields that fix every length in the first 64, so that one GPR on RV32 identifies the locker and two size it (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-length-rule>>).

Hence the _State_ field alone identifies the points software must recognize; an Error-State locker _is_ its MDH, and is exported and imported as such; an _Unconfigured_ locker reads as the one MDH value that is not an MDH; a management operation restores exactly the MDH it was given; and implementation-defined progress lives outside the Content, so that every operation can restart from the Content.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-state-identifies-cc>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-locker-is-only-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-error-state-exported-as-MDH>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-getmd-to-from-unconfigured>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-long-running-progress>>.
--

[[KLEE-decision-2,D2]]
D2: _The outcome of an instruction on a locker_::
+
--
<<KLEE-Principle-11>>, <<KLEE-Principle-12>> and <<KLEE-Principle-14>> assign one outcome to each State group; <<KLEE-Principle-10>> adds the Configuration States and <<KLEE-Principle-6>> the configuration instructions. Together they give a single rule, which the General Rules it replaces would then only refine:

====
After the checks that do not depend on the locker's _State_ (encoding, fatal error, missing CSK, locker _Off_, `klmanagedlocker`), the outcome of a KLEE instruction is determined by the locker's State group and the instruction's class:

. _Inert locker_ (_Unconfigured_, Error State, or no managed locker): every instruction except a destructive configuration instruction performs no operation and raises no exception; the _State_ is unchanged, an output operand is zeroed, and an inspection instruction returns zeros or the MDH.
. _Configuration State_: configuration instructions are admitted; a usage instruction raises a KLEE privilege-violation exception and leaves the locker unchanged.
. _Valid State_: a configuration instruction is admitted; a usage instruction that _UsagePolicy_ forbids raises a KLEE privilege-violation exception and leaves the locker unchanged; a usage instruction that the Machine, the State or _MachinePolicy_ does not admit performs no operation and transitions the locker to Error State _Invalid_; any other usage instruction performs its operation.

A malformed or reserved encoding, a `kl.mgmt` that would interleave two management operations, and a KLIOBUF instruction without a configured KLIOBUF raise an illegal-instruction exception, resp. an unconfigured-KLIOBUF exception, in every State group.
====

Leg 1 is <<KLEE-Principle-12>>, leg 2 is <<KLEE-Principle-14>>, leg 3 combines <<KLEE-Principle-14>> and <<KLEE-Principle-11>>, and the last paragraph is the part of <<KLEE-Principle-14>> that does not depend on the locker. The order of the checks within each leg stays in <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-gate-order>>.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>>, and the summary table <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-insns-on-unconf-or-error-locker>>.
--

[[KLEE-decision-3,D3]]
D3: _The Error-State architecture_::
+
--
An Error State is a value of the _State_ field, so an Error-State locker is described by its MDH alone (<<KLEE-decision-1>>): it holds nothing else, and its Content and ADS are released when it enters the State (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>). It got there by misuse or by an unrecoverable failure (<<KLEE-Principle-11>>), and it stays there, inert (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>, leg 1 of <<KLEE-decision-2>>), so that the original error remains readable until software reconfigures it, which it always can (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>). Since it is only an MDH, it is exported and imported as one, without a management operation, and a handler that cannot resolve the condition records it there by setting the Error State itself.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-state-transfer>>, the error list of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-error-architecture>>, Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-handler-duty>>.
--

[[KLEE-decision-4,D4]]
D4: _The management operation protocol_::
+
--
A management operation is one operation spanning several instructions (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>): a `kl.mgmt` opens it, transfer instructions move the image, and a `kl.mgmt` closes it. Between them the locker is in a Configuration State, the prefix-complete state of the operation, which only configuration instructions may touch (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>). The opening for provisioning or import is a destructive configuration instruction: it is admitted in every State (<<KLEE-Principle-6>>) and zeroizes the locker before any check that can fail (<<KLEE-Principle-5>>). The order is chosen for consistency: some failures, invalid Metadata, leave the locker reconfigured in an Error State, so its old content is gone in any case; zeroizing first makes every outcome of the instruction, success, Error State or exception, leave the old content destroyed and the locker named by `klmanagedlocker`. It also lets the capacity check count the locker's own allocation as free, so that a CC can be reconfigured in place, and lets a handler that takes over an unsupported Machine find its target already zeroized and named (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-handler-duty>>). At most one operation per hart is in flight, named by the managed locker (<<KLEE-decision-5>>); a second one nests by exporting the first.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-opening-locker-when>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-destructive-config>>, the `kl.mgmt` exception of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-exception-state-change>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-locker-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-nested-mgmt>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klmanagedlocker-write>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-never-clear-exporting>>.
--

[[KLEE-decision-5,D5]]
D5: _The managed locker architecture_::
+
--
At most one CC per hart is in transit at a time, together with its authentication and identification information `SIV`, `IMPQUAL` and `SIV2`: these registers exist once per hart (<<KLEE-Principle-4>>), so a second management operation must first save the first by a nested export. The pointer `klmanagedlocker` names the locker of the CC in transit, so that a management routine needs no locker number (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>). The pointer follows the CC when it moves, becomes "none" when the CC ceases to exist, is kept when the operation completes or fails so that the outcome stays readable, and is otherwise written only by software.

The instructions that take the pointer are the minimum that management routines need, since hardware complexity is added only where it brings the largest saving in code size (<<KLEE-Principle-2>>): the management and inspection instructions take it, because one routine written for the pointer replaces 32 written for fixed lockers; the usage instructions do not, because cryptographic code can address fixed lockers.

Two consequences follow with <<KLEE-Principle-12>> and <<KLEE-Principle-14>>. Opening a second operation on another locker while the pointer is in use is a sequencing error, and traps (<<KLEE-Principle-14>>). Naming the managed locker when there is none falls through (<<KLEE-Principle-12>>) for every instruction but an opening, which has nothing to open and traps (<<KLEE-Principle-14>>).

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-locker-less-transfers>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-load-mv-allowed>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-zkl-store-mv-allowed>>, pointer bullet of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-conflicting-access-rule>>. <<KLEE-decision-5>> follows from <<KLEE-Principle-4>> and <<KLEE-Principle-2>>.
--

[[KLEE-decision-6,D6]]
D6: _The resumption architecture_::
+
--
Since every operation is atomic or prefix-complete (<<KLEE-Principle-10>>) and the hart keeps a single progress register (<<KLEE-Principle-4>>), `klstart` records the prefix-complete point of whichever resumable instruction is in flight, in bytes, and `vstart` the same point in elements where a vector operand is involved. Interruption points are of two kinds. Those that the architecture defines, per instruction and per Machine State, are part of the CC: `klstart` and the State record them, and a restored CC resumes from them (<<KLEE-Principle-7>>). Those that an implementation defines, inside a long-running operation, are not: the operation records its progress in _MachineUse_ and the ADS, so that it is architected and not hidden (<<KLEE-Principle-1>>), but an import, a clone or another implementation may discard it and restart the operation from the Content. The architecture promises resumption only at its own points, and leaves the finer ones to the implementation. The issuing hart never observes the asynchrony: a conflicting access waits for completion or a precise halt, and interposed software saves and restores `klstart` around its own use.

*Rules.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klstart-vstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-CSR-klstart>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-memory-instructions>>, <<Zkl-ISA-machines.adoc#KLEE-MGR-progress-discard>>; outside the GR series, Rules <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-save>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-no-reset>>.
--

[[KLEE-Principles-residue]]
== What the Principles do not determine

The Principles constrain the following choices without fixing them; they remain rules or encodings in their own right.

* *The klmanagedlocker is kept on completion* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klmanagedlocker-updates>>). <<KLEE-decision-5>> would also allow resetting it; keeping it makes the outcome readable and lets the next opening reuse it.
* *Provisioning ends in _Ready_, with _StateExtension_ and _MachineUse_ zero* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>). A protocol convention.
* *The size arithmetic of derivations* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>>: `min`, zero-fill, divisibility of granularities). <<KLEE-Principle-10>> requires only prefix-completeness and all-or-nothing keys.
* *Freedom within one memory instruction* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-rule-memory-instruction-decomposed>>: any granularity, any order, no atomicity). <<KLEE-Principle-10>> requires only prefix-completeness at halts.
* *Which block transfers are uninterruptible* (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>: scalar `kl.mvin` and `kl.mvout` are, vector ones are resumable). Both move 128-bit blocks; the split is a choice.

== Observations for discussion

. *<<KLEE-Principle-11>>, <<KLEE-Principle-12>> and <<KLEE-Principle-14>> form a triad*: invalidate (misused Valid locker), fall through (inert locker), trap (software or policy error). A dozen rules instantiate it. Stated once, with its boundaries, it would let <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-klml-no-managed-locker>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-no-exec-in-ready>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure-cannot-be-set-directly>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-policy-narrowing-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-config-state>> each shrink to one line plus their specifics. <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-insns-on-unconf-or-error-locker>> currently summarizes the "fall through" leg only.
+
<<KLEE-decision-2>> states the merged rule.
. *<<KLEE-Principle-6>> and <<KLEE-Principle-5>> are two security Principles that the specification states nowhere as such.* The rationale of <<KLEE-Principle-6>> is a NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>>; that of <<KLEE-Principle-5>> is the paragraph after <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-unrestricted>>. Both could open their sections as normative text.
. *The unifying statement of <<KLEE-Principle-10>>, "partial execution ≡ shorter instruction", is <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-block-iterated-instructions>>*, near the end of its section. As the section's opening sentence, it would make <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>–<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-which-resumable-instructions>> read as its consequences.
. *<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>> is a one-rule section*, because it does not fit the State-based organization. Under <<KLEE-decision-1>> and <<KLEE-Principle-5>> it belongs with the derivation rules, next to <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-SKID-no-export>>. <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> was the other such section; it now closes the Rules chapter, after the resumability rules it depends on.
. *<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>> is a pointer* to the properties table and to the error architecture. Once the triad is written out, it becomes the triad's "trap" leg for usage instructions.

[[KLEE-Principles-gaps]]
== Gaps between the Rules and the Principles

This section compares each General Rule with the Principles and Design Decisions that the Coverage table assigns to it, and records what the rule states that none of them determines. Rules that follow in full are not listed. The gaps are then grouped, to see which common aspects the Principles miss.

=== Gaps, rule by rule

[cols="<1,<4",options="header"]
|===
| Rule | What the Principles and Design Decisions do not determine
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>> | Which instructions are gated. The table makes `kl.clearads` and `kl.derive` usage instructions but not `kl.restrict*`, `kl.clone`, `kl.rename` or `kl.swap`; <<KLEE-Principle-5>> explains why narrowing and copying need no gate (they elevate nothing), but not why clearing the ADS needs one. Also the "sometimes when" of the policy, i.e. policies that constrain the moment of use, has no Principle behind it.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-iobuf-configured>> | Why an unconfigured KLIOBUF is its own exception rather than an illegal instruction. <<KLEE-Principle-14>> says only that it traps.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-custom-locality>> | The choice of _ChipFamScrt_ as the weakest admissible binding; <<KLEE-Principle-8>> asks only for a binding.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-to-ready>> | "Unless the Machine explicitly forbids it". <<KLEE-Principle-6>> states that neither a Machine nor a _UsagePolicy_ may restrict a configuration transition, and the NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>> says the return to _Ready_ is allowed unconditionally. The clause contradicts both.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-same-state>> | The default that a same-State `kl.setst` repeats the entry operations. A convention of the protocol (<<KLEE-Principle-9>>), not derived.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clearads>> | Why `kl.clearads` is subject to Usage Control (see <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-success-failure>> | Why a locker in _Success_ or _Failure_ cannot be the destination of a derivation. It follows from the protocol (<<KLEE-Principle-9>>: a key field is loaded only in _Ready_ or a loading State), but the rule gives the ML-KEM motivation instead.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>> | The zeroing of the unwritten output window, and its rationale ("an in-place operation never leaves its input in place of the output it did not produce"). No Principle requires that an output that is not produced be zeroed rather than left as it was.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-management-check-order>> | That the address-misaligned exception is still raised on an empty window, and not when `klmanagedlocker` = 32; and the exact order of the state-independent checks, which <<KLEE-Principle-14>> gives only in outline.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>> | Provisioning ends in _Ready_ with _StateExtension_ and _MachineUse_ zero (already in <<KLEE-Principles-residue>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>> | Which locker transitions when a check fails: the source for item 1, the destination for items 2, 4 and 5, both for item 3. <<KLEE-Principle-11>> says a misused locker is invalidated, not which of two lockers is the misused one. Also the order of the checks.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-narrowing>> | That the failure is recorded as _Privilege Violation_ rather than _Invalid_, and on the destination. <<KLEE-Principle-5>> explains the refusal, not the name of the Error State.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> | The size arithmetic (already in <<KLEE-Principles-residue>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-idempotent-memory>> | That the fault is an access fault with `xtval` at the offending byte, and that re-execution faults before any access. RISC-V conventions, not KLEE Principles.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>> | Which block transfers are uninterruptible (already in <<KLEE-Principles-residue>>).
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-block-iterated-instructions>> | The security rationale for honoring `klstart` ("partial plaintext leakage, or multi-use pads"). <<KLEE-Principle-10>> makes prefix-completeness a correctness property; that re-running a committed prefix is a security defect is stated nowhere as a Principle.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>> | That a permanent failure gives _Invalid_ "not _Failure_". The distinction between an error of the environment and an outcome of the algorithm is a classification of Error States that no Principle states.
| <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-skid-field>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-single-skid>> | Not covered at all: they are General Rules outside the Rules chapter, and the Coverage table omits them. <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-skid-field>> follows from <<KLEE-Principle-8>> (a system-bound secret never leaves the system, so the saved form carries a reference, not the key); <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-single-skid>> is a choice of granularity for that reference.

=== Common aspects the Principles miss

The gaps fell into five groups; each has been resolved as described.

. *Failures are reported by cause, at the locker that caused them.* Several gaps were about _how_ a failure is reported rather than _that_ it is: _Privilege Violation_ versus _Invalid_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-narrowing>>), _Invalid_ versus _Failure_ (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-rbg-failure>>), the source or the destination of a derivation (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-checks>>), an unconfigured-KLIOBUF exception versus an illegal instruction (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-iobuf-configured>>). They follow one rule, now stated as <<KLEE-Principle-13>>.
. *Nothing stale is ever presented as a result.* The zeroing of unwritten output windows (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-locker-error-state>>), the zeros that `kl.mvout` reads from an inert locker (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-cleared-under-management>>), the zeroization before a failed opening (<<KLEE-decision-4>>) and the release of Content on invalidation (<<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-clear-locker-content-error-state>>) share a rule that "secrets do not linger" covered only for secrets. Now the consequence "nothing stale is returned" of <<KLEE-Principle-5>>.
. *Which instructions are gated.* The table of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-usage-policy>> decided case by case; the rule it follows, that an instruction is gated if it can alter the cryptographic state of a locker another mode may be using, while narrowing, copying and destroying are always allowed, is now a sentence of <<KLEE-Principle-5>>.
. *A re-run over committed state is a security defect, not only a correctness one.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-block-iterated-instructions>> and Rule <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-SR-klstart-no-reset>> say that resetting `klstart` after a partial execution can reuse a pad or leak plaintext; <<KLEE-Principle-10>> now says that a committed prefix is never recomputed.
. *An inconsistency and an omission.* <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-to-ready>> let a Machine forbid the return to _Ready_, against <<KLEE-Principle-6>> and the NOTE under <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-setst-in-success-failure>>; no Machine used the clause, and it has been removed. <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-skid-field>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-single-skid>> are now in the Coverage table.

The remaining gaps, the conventions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-same-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-completion-state>>, <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-derive-rule-both-fixed-size>> and <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-uninterruptible-instructions>>, the RISC-V conventions of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-idempotent-memory>>, and the check-order details of <<Zkl-ISA-unpriv.adoc#KLEE-GR-management-check-order>>, are choices that no Principle should determine, and belong in <<KLEE-Principles-residue>>.
